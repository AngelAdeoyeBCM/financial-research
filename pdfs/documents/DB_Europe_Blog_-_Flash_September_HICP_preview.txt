# Europe Blog - Flash September HICP preview

Economics Europe Blog Date 28 September 2026

September f lash inflation will be released throughout the week, starting with Spain and Belgium tomorrow (29 Sep). France, Germany and Italy will follow on Wednesday (30 Sep) before the Dutch and euro area print releases on Friday (2 Oct). We expect euro area headline inflation to rise sharply to 3.7 % YoY in September (3.2% prev.), driven mainly by another strong increase in energy HICP. Our forecast is above consensus but marginally below current market pricing. Core HICP is also expected to rise to 2.5% (2.4% prev.), as August’s weakness in travel-related services partly reverses, and core goods inflation strengthens further. Our baseline remains one further 25bp ECB hike in December, taking the terminal rate to 2.75%. Resilient activity and a further rise in inflation could lead the hawks to push for bringing the move forward to October, but, in our view, the data mix remains consistent with a quarterly pace of tightening. In the current environment, t he risk is that the ECB hikes to 3%. We believe hiking above 3% would require clear evidence of second-round effects, strong growth or a belief that nominal neutral is closer to 3% than 2%. France, Germany and Italy will follow on Wednesday (30 Sep) before the Dutch and euro area print releases on Friday (2 Oct). We expect euro area price pressures to intensify in September, with headline HICP rising sharply to 3.75% (3.25% prev.). Our forecast is above consensus but marginally below current market pricing. While we expect energy will be the main driver, we also expect core HICP to edge higher, to 2.53% YoY (2.40% prev.). Figure 1 shows our detailed forecasts across countries and components. Relative to the available consensus, we expect stronger prints in Germany and Spain. We anticipate energy HICP might have increased by 3.7% MoM in September (after 2.9% MoM in August), led by renewed fuel-price pressures – our trackers point to an increase of ~6.5% MoM in pump prices. We expect gas and electricity to add some pressure as w ell – gas will rise by c.5% MoM in France and further upside from Italy and Spain is likely. On these assumptions, we expect energy inflation would pick up to 18.6% in September from 14.3% YoY in August. Commodity prices remain under pressure, with gas and oil prices closer to the ECB’s September adverse scenario. We expect further upside from energy in the coming months: on current forward curves, energy HICP should hover around 20% YoY in Q4-26 (Figure 2). While elevated, this would be roughly half the levels observed in 2022. The acceleration in core HICP partly reflects our estimate of services inflation bouncing back to 3. 2% YoY (3.0% prev.). Travel and holiday components all surprised us to the downside in August, pulling services inflation to a four-month low. We view most of August’s weakness as noise and expect a partial rebound in September. Airfares inflation has remained subdued despite higher energy costs, although lagged pass-through remains an upside risk. We expect airfares inflation to edge higher to ~5% YoY (-15% MoM vs -19% MoM in Sep -25), with our equity analysts and dbDIG Airfares Tracker point ing to some upside risk to our forecast (Figure 3). We also expect accommodation (-5% MoM) and package holidays (- 8.5% MoM) to fall slightly less than last year. Figure 2: Direct energy pressures keep intensifying Figure 3: Our dbDIG airfares tracker pointing to a decline of “only” 12% MoM Excluding travel-related items, services HICP has been broadly stable at around 3.0% since the start of the year (vs 3.4% in 2025), consistent with moderate domestic price pressures. Our wage-sensitive HICP index has been around 2.5% for more than a year. The ECB’s forward-looking wage tracker excluding one-off payments is converging down and stabilising at levels consistent with the 2% inflation target. The are no signs of second-round effects from the energy shock in the wage data. We also expect core goods inflation to strengthen further, picking up to 1.3% YoY (1.2% prev.). We expect durable goods to remain the main source of pressure, while we see non-durables and semi-durables inflation broadly unchanged. Clothing may nevertheless add some volatility as the sales season comes to an end. Excluding energy, our high-import content HICP index shows rising but still limited imported price pressures, potentially held back by the lagged disinflationary effect from the euro’s appreciation in 2025. The recent strength in core goods has come largely from strong IT goods price momentum. Evidence of energy cost-passthrough remains limited, although inflation in high energy - intensive goods has started to rise. Upstream price pressures continue to suggest good prices will firm further (Figure 4). Finally, we expect a marginal uptick in food, alcohol and tobacco (FAT) inflation, to 1.2% from 1.1% in August, with s easonal products the main source of upside. Food PPI remains weak and has contributed to keep ing consumer food-price momentum subdued, but the unfolding energy shock could still lead to food price increases. Global agricultural prices are rising and could receive an additional boost from extreme weather conditions. We continue to expect food inflation to accelerate gradually towards 1.9% by year end and to above 3% over H2-27. Inflation outlook and the ECB Our HICP forecasts for both 2026 (+0.1pp vs last month to 3.1%) and 2027 (+0.3pp to 2.9%) have risen following the renewed pressures in energy markets. Headline inflation is now expected to plateau at around 4.0% in Q4 -26 before falling back to 3% in Q2-27 and returning to the 2% target only by late 2027. Core HICP has also been stickier than we thought at the end of last year, and we expect will average 2.4% in 2026, rising further to 2.6% -2.7% in 2027 (+0.1pp vs last month). The upside largely reflects recent and expected strength in core goods as higher energy costs gradually feed through. Underlying inflation remains broadly stable, with limited evidence so far that the energy shock has generated broad-based spillovers or second-round effects through wages. However, the longer energy prices remain elevated, the greater the risk for the inflation shock to become broader and more persistent. Our ECB call assumes one further 25bp hike in December, to a terminal rate of 2.75%. However, with inflation expected to rise quickly to 4% in Q4 -26 and economic activity proving resilient, the hawks may push to bring the hike forward to October. In our view, the absence of compelling evidence of indirect and second-round effects remains consistent with a quarterly pace of hikes. The risk is that the ECB has to hike to 3.0% if energy prices remain elevated and the perceived risk of energy inflation spilling over into core inflation rises. Hiking above 3.0% would need either clear evidence of second-round effects, strong growth or the ECB to believe that the neutral rate is closer to 3% than 2%.
