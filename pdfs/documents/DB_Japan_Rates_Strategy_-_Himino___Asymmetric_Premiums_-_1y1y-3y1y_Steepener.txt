# Japan Rates Strategy - Himino & Asymmetric Premiums - 1y1y-3y1y Steepener

27 August 2026 Deutsche Bank Research Japan Japan Rates Japan Rates Strategy Date

Himino & Asymmetric Premiums: 1y1y/3y1y Steepener

Monetising the Forward-Rate Ceiling Through Curve Steepening We believe investors could consider a DV01-neutral forward steepener comprising a receiver in 1y1y and a payer in 3y1y. An entry around the prevailing spread of 38bp as of 27 August may be considered, with an indicative target of 65bp and a stop-loss at 30bp. Static carry and roll are estimated at approximately +13bp on an annualised basis. The position offers a potential means of expressing our rates outlook—a ceiling of 1.80% for the 2y yield and an increase in the 5y yield towards 2.50%—through the forward OIS curve. It may capture two themes within a single structure: a prospective unwinding of the market’s pricing for a September rate increase and a sustained term premium in the belly of the curve. The immediate catalyst was Deputy Governor Himino’s speech and subsequent press conference earlier today. Although he provided no commitment to a September rate increase, he stated explicitly that the BoJ must be “more mindful than before of upside risks to prices”, wording that did not appear in the July Outlook Report. He also added orally that the Board would deliberate carefully at every policy meeting. This is not a timing commitment, but it is more accurately read as an explicit statement that every meeting is live rather than that the policy path is a blank slate—half a step closer to the language used ahead of the January 2025 hike. We therefore interpret the remarks as an upward adjustment to the hawkish boundary of policy expectations, without an accompanying commitment on timing. The OIS market currently assigns a probability of slightly above 80% to a September increase. That pricing may still retrace, but the decline could be shallower than in the preceding three episodes. Valuation considerations likewise indicate that forward rates are already close to their ceiling. As of 27 August, 1y1y, 2y1y and 3y1y stood at 2.03%, 2.30% and 2.41%, respectively—28bp, 55bp and 66bp above our forecasts. In particular, 1y1y already exceeds the 2.00% level under our upside scenario, while 3y1y is only 9bp below the 2.50% level under our continuation scenario, implying that this outcome is almost fully discounted. The initial market response to the speech was led by front-end paying, which compressed the spread and improved the potential entry point. From the prevailing level of 38bp, we estimate returns of +27bp under our base case, +32bp under a Deutsche Securities Inc. 2 Deutsche Securities Inc. policy-stall scenario and +7bp even under our upside scenario. Should front-loaded increases persist and produce a front-end-led bear flattening, we estimate that the spread would compress by only 3bp, to 35bp. On our assumptions, the position remains profitable across all scenarios after carry. A 30bp stop-loss could be applied as a disciplined risk-management threshold. The Deputy Governor’s press conference this afternoon did not provide explicit guidance on the timing of the next rate increase, although his live-meeting language leaves a shallower retracement in hike pricing as the principal adverse risk. Alongside this position, investors may also consider an outright receiver in 1y1y (entry: 2.03%; target: 1.80%) and a receiver in the September–October meeting-to-meeting forward. We continue to view the 5s20s cash flattener and the 30y payer tail hedge as relevant complementary positions. We remain neutral on 2y1y, which lies between our upside and continuation scenarios. Translating Our Policy-Rate Outlook into the Forward Curve Current 38 Target One potential expression is a forward OIS curve trade: receive 1y1y (Aug-2027 to Aug-2028) and pay 3y1y (Aug-2029 to Aug-2030), structured DV01-neutral. Because both legs are one-year forwards, the notional ratio is approximately 1:1 and closely matches the risk ratio. The spread is currently 38bp (27 August); an indicative framework could use a 65bp target and a 30bp stop. Static carry and roll are approximately +13bp annualised, equivalent to around +3bp over a three-month holding period. If the meeting-to-meeting receiver discussed below is a point trade targeting the repricing of a specific date, this steepener offers a broader way to consider where the ceiling in rates may be established and sustained. A potential holding period could extend through the 17–18 September meeting, the October meeting and, depending on market developments, into year-end. The spread traded in a 34–47bp range during August. Early in the month, limited front-end hike pricing and upward pressure in the belly drove it to 47bp (Figure 2). At 38bp, the current level may offer a reasonable entry point near the bottom of that range. The indicative 65bp target lies above the August high, while the 30bp stop sits just beyond the 34bp range low. A relatively tight risk threshold may be appropriate: if market developments challenge the underlying thesis, an early exit could be considered. The trade is anchored in our rates outlook. We have long assumed a 1.75% terminal rate, reached through hikes in October 2026, January 2027 and April 2027. Under this path, we expect the 2y yield to peak at 1.80%, while the 5y yield rises towards 2.50% on supply-demand dynamics: a larger free float as the BoJ scales back purchases and increased issuance in the short and intermediate sectors following the budget-request process. Translated into forward space, a capped 2y yield and a higher 5y yield imply lower front-end forwards, notably 1y1y, but firmer intermediate forwards such as 3y1y. In our 20 August rates outlook, we highlighted that our end-2028 2y yield forecast was 63bp below the market forward. Recalculated at 27 August levels, the gap remains substantial at around 55bp. Himino Raises the Hawkish Ceiling Without Providing a Date Deputy Governor Himino’s remarks at today’s meeting with business leaders in Saitama were consistent with the base case set out in our 21 August preview, “A Hand on the Door to September”: he provided no indication of the timing of a possible rate increase at the 17–18 September meeting. With respect to the timing and pace of policy adjustment, he remained within the formulation used in the July statement, under which the BoJ would assess the degree of confidence in its outlook and the associated risks. In the press conference, however, he added meeting. The precise interpretation is therefore not that policy is a blank slate, but that every meeting is live—language half a step closer to that used ahead of the January 2025 hike. He made no reference to long-term yields, JGB purchases or market-functioning considerations. His discussion of the underlying inflation trend was, however, more explicit. He broadened the focus from whether underlying inflation would rise to and stabilise around 2% to whether it could exceed 2%, adding that the Bank must be “more mindful than before of upside risks to prices”—a sentence absent from the baseline assessment in the July Outlook Report. The central message was therefore a firmer bias towards further policy normalisation, but without a commitment on timing: a higher hawkish boundary and a live-meeting signal, without a specified date. More than half of the speech was devoted to a point-by-point rebuttal of criticism surrounding the June hike, which lifted the policy rate to 1.00%, its highest in 31 years. Himino argued that “1% may in fact be a low interest rate in a non-deflationary economy”, that short- and medium-term real rates remain negative, and that policy effects take one to two years to reach maximum impact. Together, 4 Deutsche Securities Inc. these points set out a clear case for pre-emptive tightening even if headline CPI temporarily falls below 2%. His observation that the policy direction signalled at the March Wakayama press conference was implemented in June suggests that soft near-term CPI would not preclude action in September or October. He also highlighted several upside inflation factors: AI-related export prices, up 10% year on year, substantially offsetting the drag from oil-led import prices, up 17%; a return to inflation above 2% in the second half of the fiscal year; and a view that downstream pass-through has yet to run its course. The closing reference to Eiichi Shibusawa and inflation during the Satsuma Rebellion directly invoked the history of monetary financing. In our view, the Deputy Governor effectively reinforced the case for retaining insurance against the tail risk of fiscal dominance through a 30y payer. The speech and press conference have two principal implications for positioning. First, in the absence of a timing signal, the probability of slightly more than 80% assigned by the OIS market to a September rate increase remains vulnerable to the pattern observed in the preceding three episodes: expectations strengthened following a speech, subsequently retraced ahead of the meeting and were ultimately validated at the following meeting. However, the explicit statement meeting suggests that any retracement could be shallower than in those episodes. This dynamic still supports receiving positions in front-end forwards, but with less scope for repricing. Second, the explicit emphasis on upside inflation risk increases the weight of the right-hand tail in the distribution of future policy rates. Himino also acknowledged that yen weakness is contributing to inflation, while maintaining that the exchange rate is not a direct target of monetary policy. As discussed below, however, a substantial premium for that outcome is already embedded in current pricing. In our assessment, curve steepening therefore provides a more compelling relative-value expression than an outright payer position. Forward Rates Are Already Close to Their Ceiling Following the speech, 1y1y, 2y1y and 3y1y forwards stood at 2.03%, 2.30% and 2.41%, respectively. The expected policy rate consistent with our path—25bp hikes in October, January 2027 and April 2027, followed by a hold at 1.75%—is 1.75% across all three horizons. The cushions embedded in market forwards are therefore 28bp, 55bp and 66bp, respectively. The scenario comparison makes the valuation even clearer. Under our upside scenario, which adds a fourth hike in July 2027 and lifts the terminal rate to 2.00%, 1y1y is already trading slightly above the implied policy rate. Under our continuation scenario, in which rates rise towards the upper end of the neutral range at 2.50% by 2028, 3y1y is only 9bp below that level, meaning more than 90% of the scenario is priced. Current forward rates therefore reflect not simply expectations for the terminal rate but compensation for uncertainty around it—that is, term premium, as captured by our 8 August “Policy Restraint” framework. In our view, the right-tail risk highlighted by today’s speech already carries a meaningful price. The realised outcome therefore depends on how the term premium embedded in each forward evolves. The 28bp premium in 1y1y should be relatively quick to compress as uncertainty around the policy path declines. By contrast, the 66bp premium in 3y1y is supported less by policy expectations than by structural supply-demand forces, including the larger JGB free float as the BoJ reduces purchases and higher short- and intermediate-sector issuance following the budget-request process. It should therefore prove stickier. In a plateau-confirmation or policy-stall scenario, the front-end premium compresses while the belly premium remains supported, widening the spread. That is the core of the steepener: it is not a directional call on rates, but a relative-value trade on asymmetric term-premium compression. Our estimated returns under these assumptions (Figure 1, right) are favourable relative to the current 38bp spread. The position generates an estimated gain of 27bp under the pace-confirmation scenario and 32bp under the policy-stall scenario, creating meaningful upside when rates are held unchanged. Even if the upside scenario materialises and the terminal rate reaches 2.00%, the 1y1y entry level is already above that rate, leaving a 7bp gain. The loss scenario is a front-end-led bear flattening in which an early hike, including one in September, is followed by further tightening and the front-end forward rises above the expectations-consistent level. Even then, the estimated loss is limited to 3bp, with the spread ending at 35bp. On our assumptions, the position remains positive across all scenarios after annualised carry of 13bp. Nevertheless, the term-premium assumptions should be regarded as uncertain by approximately ±10bp, and the 30bp stop-loss should therefore be applied mechanically. Because the explicit live-meeting language could result in a shallower retracement and earlier policy action, adherence to the 30bp stop takes precedence over adding risk. A compression towards 34bp, the August low, may offer an opportunity to add only if the underlying thesis remains intact, while 65bp is the profit-taking level. An outright receiver in 1y1y, entered at 2.03% with a 1.80% target, is a more directional expression of the same view and may be considered by investors seeking greater exposure to the receiving leg. We remain neutral on 2y1y, which lies between the upside and continuation scenarios and offers less compelling directional value. The principal near-term event risk was the Deputy Governor’s press conference this afternoon. He avoided committing to a specific date, but his statement that the 6 Deutsche Securities Inc. Board would deliberate carefully at every meeting keeps September live and creates a risk of a shallower retracement in hike pricing and earlier policy action than in the preceding three cycles. Such an outcome would be adverse for the steepener, the meeting-to-meeting receiver and the outright 1y1y receiver; under those circumstances, the predetermined 30bp stop-loss should be observed without discretion. Although he refrained from providing guidance on timing, the established pattern of retracement in rate-increase pricing may still re-emerge as attention shifts to any remarks by the Governor following the G20, if made, and subsequently to the 17–18 September policy meeting. If the preceding three cycles provide a valid guide—pricing first retraces and the increase is then delivered at the subsequent meeting—the principal focus is likely to shift from September to October. We expect the premium embedded in 1y1y to compress as the rate increase is ultimately delivered, enabling the spread to reach our target.
