As a reminder, a potential leadership challenger would need support of 20% of the Parliamentary Labour Party i.e. 81 Labour MPs to trigger a contest. The current leader would not be required to seek nominations in the event of a challenge and would be on the ballot paper (unless there were a resignation). The potential challenger must be a Member of Parliament. If a contest is triggered, Labour members would vote using a preferential ballot. If a leadership challenge were to occur, we don’t have much clarity on fiscal plans of potential challengers. But a left-leaning candidate could consider raising public spending (for instance for unprotected departments and public services), defence, or opt for a more generous support scheme in response to the energy shock. The key uncertainty remains whether such policies, if confirmed, would be done via
1. higher taxes,
2. sticking to fiscal rules but adjusting the consolidation path to encompass higher borrowing in the near-term but greater fiscal tightening in the back end or
3. breaking fiscal rules altogether, which for us is not a foregone conclusion. More public spending can imply higher taxes or near-term borrowing followed by medium term tightening rather than breaking the fiscal rules. A potential defence carve out from fiscal rules is one option being floated by Andy Burnham in a media interview, though it doesn’t deal with current pressures on public services and unprotected department spending cuts. At the same time, a potential challenger Angela Rayner has previously suggested £3-4bn of tax rises (including raising the bank surcharge to 5% from 2%, removing inheritance tax relief for AIM shares and reinstating pensions lifetime allowance).
