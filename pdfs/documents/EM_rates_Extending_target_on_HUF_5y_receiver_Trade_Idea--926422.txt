# EM rates: Extending target on HUF 5y receiver

We extend the target on our M26 5y HUF receiver to 4.50%, from 4.90%, which we initiated in EM rates: The case for HUF receivers, dated 20 April. Since entry, continued softness in Hungary’s inflation picture and a lower country risk premium have driven the belly of the HUF curve around 90bp richer. We also trail the stop 60bp lower to 5.80%. We continue to like the disinflationary story in Hungary both from a cyclical (Figure 1) and structural perspective. Cyclically, we have had soft January repricings and the soft underlying inflation trend has continued so far, suggesting that inflation should remain within the NBH inflation target range throughout the forecast horizon. Consumer inflation expectations, as evidenced by Eurostat data, have also remained well-anchored and have meaningfully diverged from CE3 peers (Figure 2). Structurally, increased investment driven by FDI inflows and accelerating EU fund absorption can durably lift productivity and ease the persistent upward pressure on unit labour costs that has been a key source of services stickiness. The narrative around euro adoption should also provide a macro anchor that over time allows HUF rates to converge towards levels seen in Poland and the Czech Republic (see Hungary: Euro vision, dated 4 June). In addition to our 5y receiver, we maintain our front-end flattener (pay year-end against a 2y receiver) and our 10y HGB– Bund tightener to capture the convergence to the eurozone theme. We also remain short EURHUF via options (see Hungary: Election tailwinds for capital flows and HUF, dated 15 April).
