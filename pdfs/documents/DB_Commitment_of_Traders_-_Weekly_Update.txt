# Commitment of Traders - Weekly Update

Rates Commitment of Traders Date 3 April 2026

- In the week leading up to Tuesday, March 31, interest rate futures positioning showed mixed activity across tenors, with asset managers decreasing US contract exposure by $1 4.8 million in DV01, now at the lowest exposure in US since April 2024, while increasing exposures in the 5 to 10-year sectors and WN. Overall, asset manager exposure to Treasury futures decreased modestly by $1.5 million in DV01. Leveraged funds largely mirrored these moves but were tilted toward the long side as their net DV01 increased $16.1 million in DV01, potentially evidence of lighter basis positioning.
- This week saw less demand from leveraged funds for 30-day fed funds than 1M SOFR, likely due to profit-taking of positions betting on softer funding conditions amid a remarkably stable quarter-end. Net speculator positions in 30-day fed funds decreased by $ 0.8 million in DV01, while 1M SOFR saw an in crease of $ 2.7 million in DV01. Asset managers increased their short-term rate exposure by $1.7 million in DV01 overall. Figure 2: Trader Classification Source: Commodity Futures Trading Commission Reportable Positions: Clearing members, futures commission merchants, and foreign brokers (collectively called reporting firms) file daily reports with the Commission. Those reports show the futures and option positions of traders that hold positions above specific reporting l evels set by CFTC regulations. If, at the daily market close, a reporting firm has a trader with a position at or above the Commission’s reporting level in any single futures month or option expiration, it reports that trader’s entire position in all futures and options expiration months in that commodity, regardless of size. The aggregate of all traders’ positions reported to the Commission usually represents 70-90% of the total open interest in any given market. Please see here for relevant reporting levels.
