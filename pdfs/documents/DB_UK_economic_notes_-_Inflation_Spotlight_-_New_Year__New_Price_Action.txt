# UK economic notes - Inflation Spotlight - New Year, New Price Action

12 February 2026 Deutsche Bank Research Europe United Kingdom Economics UK economic notes Date

On 17 February, the ONS will publish the first set of inflation data for 2026. The good news? After a run of largely encouraging data, we expect inflation to take a big step down to start the year. The bad news? We think inflation could be a touch stronger than the Bank of England expects. Where do we see inflation starting the year? For CPI, we see headline inflation dropping to 3% y-o-y (Dec-25: 3.4%). We also see core CPI landing at 3% y-o-y (Dec- 25: 3.2% y-o-y). Services CPI we think will take a big step down as well, reaching 4.3% y-o-y (Dec-25: 4.5%). For RPI, we see price growth slowing to 3.8% y-o-y (Dec- 25: 4.2%). Looking ahead, we continue to think inflation will take a big step down through the first half of the year, landing a stone's throw away from the Bank's 2% target in Q2- 26. Thereafter, a small increase in momentum is likely - but we expect inflation to be range-bound tracking between 2-2.5% y-o-y for the remainder of the year. Breaking down our expectations for the January print We expect the ONS to have collected prices on, or around, the 13th of January (2nd week of the month). We breakdown on nowcasts below. Services inflation to see seasonal fall in January Services prices will likely take a step down in January as part of their usual discounting. On housing, we expect private rents to have picked up by 0.2% m-o-m, with social rents up 0.6% m-o-m (CPI). This would see the overall rents basket up 0.3% m-o-m. For RPI specifically, we see depreciation flatlining in January. And we expect mortgage interest payments (MIPS) to have pushed up by 0.6% m-o-m. Away from housing, there will be a lot of focus on travel and transport prices - given how volatile they have been all of last year. Given the December print, and likely choice of index day, we expect a sizeable 23% m-o-m pull back in airfares. Other travel prices, we think, will be supported modestly by stronger seafares. Turning to recreational and personal services, we expect overall price cuts to feature in the January data. Catering prices, we think, will have fallen 0.5% m-o-m (CPI). Strong discounting will have likely pulled prices lower to start the year. The latest BRC data continued to show weak price growth in several categories, with clothing prices down 0.1% m-o-m, furniture prices down 1.1% m-o-m, electrical prices down 0.2% m-o-m (registering its sixth consecutive monthly fall), and DIY/Gardening prices down 1% m-o-m. Indications of new year discounts have been plentiful, with BDO, for example, noting that increased high street sales were driven "primarily by heavy discounting after Christmas and in the new year sales" after maintaining higher stocks/inventories from a slow Christmas spending period. This was also echoed by the latest BRC reading. What are we expecting for core goods prices? Health goods prices, we expect, will rise 0.7% m-o-m (CPI). Clothing prices, we expect, will shrink 3.7% m-o-m (CPI). Furniture prices, on our models, are expected to have fallen by 4.2% m-o-m (CPI). Car prices, we expect, will have picked up 0.1% m-o-m. The latest AutoTrade Retail Price Index showed a slight increase from 0% to 0.2% on like-for-like prices. Where does this leave our projections? For CPI, our projections point to a 1.1% m-o-m fall to start the year, taking the annual rate to 0.9%. For RPI, we see core goods prices down 1% m-o-m, taking the annual rate to 2.1% (Dec-25: 2%). Survey data, however, point to some slowdown. What do survey data tell us? First, the BRC data showed a large uptick in food prices (up 1.1% m-o-m) driven by increases in both fresh food prices (0.7% m-o-m) and processed food prices (1.5% m-o-m). One word of caution here: the BRC has undershot food prices relative to the ONS over the last three months - suggesting some payback here. Second, Kantar/Worldpanel data showed grocery price inflation slowing down from 4.3% to 4% in January. Third, the Food Foundation Basket showed a 0.7% m-o-m uplift in grocery prices to start the year. And fourth, Trolley's Grocery Price Inflation tracker showed a 1.1% gain in Jan-26, after two consecutive months of declining prices. How do our projections compare to the Bank of England's? On headline, core, core goods, services and food CPI, we think price momentum will be stronger across the board. We break down our projections relative to the Bank's below:

- Headline CPI: Our projection of headline CPI sits 11bps above Bank forecasts at 3.02% y-o-y vs 2.90%.
- Core CPI: Our projection of core CPI sits 12bps above the Bank's projection at 3.03% y-o-y vs 2.91%.
- Services CPI: Our projection of services 4.26% y-o-y vs 4.14% y-o-y.
- Core goods CPI: Our projection of core goods 0.87% y-o-y vs 0.75% y-o-y.
- Food and non-alcoholic beverages CPI: Our projection of food & non-alcoholic beverages CPI sits 22bps above the Bank's projection at 3.92% y-o-y vs 3.70% y-o-y. Figure 13: DB vs BoE projections for Jan-26 CPI Source: Deutsche Bank, Bank of England The look ahead: where is inflation going from here? Looking ahead, we maintain our projections for swift disinflation over the coming months. As some of the big price rises drop out of the annual rate calculation, slower administrative price rises by and large will see inflation (particularly services) drop lower through Q2-26, settling just around target. Slower wage growth will also help temper price rises, with margins constrained by more modest underlying demand. And government policy, combined with lower wholesale costs, will see dual fuel bills cut through the spring adding to the UK's disinflation. Altogether, we see price momentum slowing from around 3% y-o-y in Q1-26 to just over 2% y-o-y in Q2-26. We expect small upward gyrations through the year but see price momentum staying range bound between 2.2% y-o-y and 2.5% y-o-y. What are the risks to our forecasts? Risks are skewed in multiple directions. For services, we see risks skewed to stickier price momentum given the large uncertainty surrounding April price resets - we will explore these further in a coming piece. For core goods, we think risks are balanced. Risks of trade diversion could coincide with higher demand, including some margins rebuild which could see prices push up. For food, we see risks skewed to lower price momentum. And similarly, despite ongoing geopolitical volatility, we think on balance risks are skewed to lower gas and oil prices.
