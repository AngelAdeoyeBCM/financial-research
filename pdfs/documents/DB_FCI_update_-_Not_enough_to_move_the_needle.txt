# FCI update: Not enough to move the needle

Economics US Economic Notes Date 6 February 2026

- In this note, we update our high-frequency version of the Fed's FCI-G to quantify the impact on expected growth from recent moves in asset prices. Over the last week, equities have sold off, the dollar has unwound some of its January depreciation, and credit spreads have widened.
- While these moves have tightened financial conditions, they have had a relatively minor impact on the Fed's measure, with both the baseline and alternative index tightening by only 3bps over the past week.
- Even with this tightening, financial conditions remain easy and would be expected to add up to a percentage point to the growth outlook over the next year. As such, a more pronounced move in markets is likely need ed to meaningfully impact the Fed outlook. FCI update In this note, we use our high frequency version of the Fed's FCI-G to provide an update on recent moves in financial conditions and quantify their impact on expected growth. As a reminder, the Fed's financial conditions index (FCI-G) is intended to translate contemporaneous and lagged changes in financial variables into their impact on growth. For more details about the construction of our daily version, see "What a difference a week makes for the Fed's FCI". Note: See Annex for explanatory information about items with asterisks. The table in Figure 1 shows how these variables have moved over the past week. Since last Friday, equities are down about 2%, mortgage and corporate spreads are about 5bps wider having not kept up with the fall in the 10y Treasury yield, Matthew Luzzetti, Ph.D. and the dollar has appreciated almost 1%, unwinding some of its slide over the second half of January. Taken together, these moves have tightened financial conditions by about 7bps over the last week. However, the presence of lagged changes in asset prices have muted the ultimate impact, with both the baseline and alternative FCI tightening by only 3bps over this period. Figure 2: Baseline three-year lookback FCI-G Figure 3: Alternative one-year lookback FCI-G Figures 2 and 3 show our daily FCI-G indices for the baseline three-year lookback and alternative one-year lookback versions as well as how they line up with the Fed's monthly series. Both indices remain very easy, and, via the Fed's internal models, would be expected to add around a percentage point to the growth outlook over the next four quarters. Financial conditions this easy likely make Fed officials slightly less concerned about a sharp deterioration on the employment side of their mandate, especially when paired with recent preliminary signs of stabilization in the labor market data. The tightening in financial conditions over the last week would either need to be sustained for a meaningful length of time or show signs of impacting market functioning for the Fed to begin signalling their concern about financial conditions. Annex This section includes some explanatory notes for our FCI. * In the Fed's construction of the FCI-G, the weights are derived from shocking variables in various macro models that are used at the Fed Board. Importantly, when shocking these variables, it holds all other variables constant, which is why decreases in the ten-year Treasury has the opposite sign than one might expect. A higher Treasury rate, holding other interest rates fixed, gives higher coupon payments to bondholders, which generates a slight positive income effect and would be considered an easing of financial conditions. We re-arrange terms in the Fed's FCI formula to put things in the more typical formulation of mortgage and corporate spreads. This gives a more natural interpretation where a lower 10y eases conditions, but would be partially offset by wider spreads if those rates did not come down as well. ** As detailed in our FCI note, we approximate current month's Zillow data using lagged movements in Redfin house prices. *** As the trade weighted dollar is daily, but updated weekly, for the recent week's data we approximate the daily moves in the Fed's trade weighted dollar index with those from Bloomberg's DXY Curncy series.
