# EM rates: Closing SORA 5s10s flattener

Chandresh Jain, EM Asia FX and Rates Strategist | MAR | Joey Li, FX and Rates Strategist, APAC | BNP Paribas Singapore Branch. Sources: Bloomberg, Macrobond, BNP Paribas Fig. 1: Change in 2y swaps vs fixing across EM Asia We close our m25, 5s10s flattener position in SORA rates for a 7.7bp (USD115.5k) loss. The position didn't move much against us, but the negative carry and roll-down costs of holding the position resulted in a loss. We initiated the trade on 23 April 2025, following a steepening of the SORA curve in tandem with US rates after a series of US Treasury sell-offs triggered by tariff announcements and concerns about the Fed's independence (see EM rates: Enter 5s10s SORA flattener). At the time, we viewed the move in SORA rates as overdone, given Singapore's AAA sovereign credit rating. Since then, back-end SORA rates have continued to track the move in US rates, as shown by how the box has largely traded in a range-bound fashion (see Figure 1). Looking ahead, we now expect the US Treasury curve to steepen due to fiscal concerns resurging (see US rates: The fiscal gulf asymmetry, dated 1 April). Similarly, we expect Asian rates to steepen, as rate-hike pricing in the front-end may be removed in a de-escalatory scenario, while subsidies across EM Asian economies will continue to weigh on the fiscal picture over time, driven by elevated energy prices. Hence, we consider it prudent to close our SORA flattener. We close the position at 39.6bp for a loss of 7.7bp (USD115.5k), including carry and roll. We are closing our m25, 5s10s flattener position in SORA rates for a loss of 8bp (USD120k). We initiated the trade in April 2025, following a steepening of the SORA curve in tandem with US rates, after a series of US Treasury selloffs triggered by tariff announcements and concerns about the Fed's independence (see EM rates: Enter 5s10s SORA flattener, dated 23 April 2025). At the time, we believed the move in SORA rates was overdone, given Singapore's AAA rating. Since then, the back-end SORA rates have continued to track the move in US rates, as seen by how the box has traded in a range-bound fashion (Figure 1). However, our outlook has changed, and we now expect the US Treasury curve to steepen due to resurgent fiscal concerns (see US rates: The fiscal gulf asymmetry, dated 1 April 2026). Similarly, we anticipate Asian rates to steepen, as the pricing of rate hikes in the front-end may be removed in a de-escalatory scenario, while subsidies across EM Asian economies will continue to deteriorate the fiscal picture over time, driven by elevated energy prices. Given this, we believe it is prudent to close our m25, 5s10s SORA flattener – we close the position at 39.5bp for a loss of 8bp (USD120k).
