# EM FX and rates: Portfolio adjustments due to Middle East events

As the outbreak of military action in the Middle East over the weekend has triggered volatility and uncertainty for markets, we are adjusting our portfolio of trades to better reflect the risks ahead. In particular, the heavier-than-expected attacks on Iran suggest the possibility of oil and gas prices staying elevated for longer than anticipated, while Tehran could deliver a more disruptive-than-anticipated response directed at energy commodities. This outcome could entail some broader de-risking across markets. We are making the following adjustments to our portfolio:

1. Taking a partial profit on our short EURBRL position. We are locking in a profit on half of the position ($5m), booking a gain of 5.38% (USD269k). The remaining half stays open with a stop-loss revised from 6.321 to 6.145 and a profit-taking level adjusted from 6.041 to 5.840. We entered the position on 13 January in anticipation of a supportive global macro environment for high-yielding EM currencies (see Brazil: Adding to our short EURBRL position).
2. Closing our Jan29 DI receiver trade. We exit and book a loss of -1.1bp (USD11k). We entered the position on 28 January on the back of two coinciding factors:
1. a backdrop of USD weakness which will probably continue to filter into downward pressure for local rates and
2. the BCB likely softening its tone today, signaling the start of the easing cycle could be approaching (see: EM rates: Enter a Jan 29 DI receiver).
3. Closing our EURPLN put seagull trade. We buy the 17 April 4.30 call in EUR20mn and sell the 17 April 4.22-4.15 put spread in EUR20mn x 30mn for net 2.8bp (4.24 spot ref; zero loss). We entered the position as a put seagull on 19 January (see EURPLN: EU fund flows support PLN; add 3m seagull), owning carry and selling elevated vol surface. However, we are following the path of prudence and reducing tail risk exposure here due to Poland’s potential exposure to energy price spikes.
4. Closing our short CADMXN trade for a small gain. We exit for a profit of 0.76% (USD38k). This position reflected the view that the CAD would underperform the MXN during trade negotiations ahead of the summer’s USMCA review. We think this will remain the case and will look to re-enter short positions in the future, but given risks of carry positions being unwound, we opt to move to the sidelines for now (see FX: Short CADMXN, dated 2 February).
