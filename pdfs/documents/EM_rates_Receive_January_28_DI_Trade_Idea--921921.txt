# EM rates: Receive January 28 DI

TRADE IDEA TRADE IDEA 0 50 100 150 200 250 300 2-Feb 16-Feb 2-Mar 16-Mar 30-Mar MX BR CO CL US 08/04/2026 1

Mario Castro, Senior Latam Strategist | MAR | BNP Paribas Securities Corp. | Guanchen Xie, Latam Strategist | BNP Paribas Securities Corp Please refer to the end of this report for important information and MAR.disclosures. We prefer to receive January 28 DI because the DI curve offers a good vehicle to harvest the premium that should fall as the temporary US–Iran truce eases geopolitical risk. The two-week ceasefire – signaling a possible de-escalation – is likely to shave some of the EM premium that has built up over the past week. Even if the market does not fully price a diplomatic settlement, the truce lowers the negative tail risk of further escalation in the conflict, at least for now. We favor January 28 receiver for three key reasons. First, the DI curve was the most affected Latam curve during the sell-off (Figure 1). Before the Middle East flare-up, the market was already long the receiver side, anticipating Banco Central do Brasil’s easing cycle. Moreover, the DI curve tends to keep a high beta to global fixed-income, especially in stressful periods. Second, the strong sell-off has created a wider gap between the policy path embedded in the curve and the path the BCB is likely to follow, given Brazil’s macro fundamentals. Despite higher oil prices, the market’s end of 2027 inflation expectations have risen only 5bp, suggesting room for the central bank to ease further. Even though higher oil prices narrow the BCB’s easing margin, we still project a policy rate of 12.50% at end- 2026 and 11.00% at end-2027. The market currently prices rate of 14.1% at end-2026 and 13.7% at end-2027. Third, January 28s remains the most distorted node in the DI curve, reinforcing the case for reversal. The node was heavily received before the conflict as it offers a good expression for the terminal rate of the economic cycle, covering the BCB’s policy horizon. Once volatility eases, we expect it to outperform the rest of the curve as the easing cycle unfolds.
