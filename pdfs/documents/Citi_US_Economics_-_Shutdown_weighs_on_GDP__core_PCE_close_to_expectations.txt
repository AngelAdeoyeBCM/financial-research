## CITI’S TAKE

Data on GDP, spending and inflation do not change the outlook for continued solid activity but slowing inflation. Real GDP increased a slower than expected 1.4%QoQ annualized in Q4 due in part to a 1.0pp drag from the government shutdown. But “underlying growth” as measured by final private domestic demand held up at 2.4%, supported by expectedly solid 2.4% consumer spending growth. Equipment investment surprisingly contributed a soft 0.2pp as a strong 0.65pp contribution from information processing investment was offset by weakness elsewhere. Core PCE inflation at 0.36%MoM in December technically rounded to 0.4% but was close to our projection for 0.33%MoM. n | Data – Real GDP advanced just 1.4% QoQ in Q4, below consensus for 2.8% and our projection for 3.1%. The Q4 government shutdown weighed on Q4 GDP by 1.0pp according to the BEA, and government spending subtracted 0.9pp from real growth. (Nominal GDP is not affected meaning the GDP deflator was stronger than is typical). Removing the effects of the shutdown and volatile trade balance, final private domestic demand advanced a solid 2.4% supported by a 2.4% increase in consumer spending. Most surprising relative to our projection was an only 0.2pp contribution from business equipment investment. Investment in computers and other equipment related to AI contributed a strong 0.65pp, but other categories of investment spending were weak. Residential fixed investment was slightly negative, reflecting the ongoing weak housing market. In monthly data, personal spending advanced a solid 0.4%MoM in December with a strong 0.7% increase in services and 0.11% pull-back in goods reflecting a decline in auto sales as well as broader weakness across other goods. Incomes were up 0.3%MoM meaning the savings rate fell from a low 3.7% to an even lower 3.6%. Core PCE inflation registered 0.36%MoM, close to our expectations for 0.33% but technically rounding up to 0.4%. Most details were known from previously released CPI and PPI. Portfolio management fees were up 1.8%MoM, airfares and healthcare prices were close to flat. Citi’s take – Q4 real GDP along with December income, spending and inflation do not significantly change the outlook. Most readings were close to our expectations and will leave in place our and Fed officials understanding that activity is holding up even as underlying inflation is slowing and hiring has been weak. Core PCE inflation is above target at 3.0%YoY and above 3% on a monthly annualized basis as well. But most of the elevation in inflation is due to stronger readings for goods inflation in prior months that should fade as firms complete the pass-through of tariff costs to consumers. Shelter inflation is slowing and with the labor market looser there should be less concern about upside risk in other services inflation. January core inflation readings have been boosted by residual seasonality in recent years, so a more “normal” reading in the next PCE release – even if still elevated – is also reassuring. The most interesting development in the GDP report was strength in AI-related investment that was offset by weakness in other categories of investment. Information processing investment added 0.65pp to real GDP and investment. Software intellectual property investment added another 0.17pp. Summing these two, AI-related investment accounted for 0.82pp, or about 1/3 of private final demand growth in Q4. This could be an early sign that strong investment related to AI is coming at the cost of investing in other areas of the economy.
