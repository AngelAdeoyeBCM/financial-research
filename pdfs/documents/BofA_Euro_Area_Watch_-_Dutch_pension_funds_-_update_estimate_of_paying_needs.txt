# Euro Area Watch Dutch pension funds: update estimate of paying needs

PFZW published its life cycle choice With €250bn of assets, PFZW is the second largest Dutch pension fund, after ABP. It is also the largest of the 24 pension funds that have switched to Defined Contribution as of 1-Jan-26. Yet, until this Jan, PFZW hadn’t communicated about their life cycle choice under the new system, ie what will be the allocations to risk-free returns vs excess returns for different age cohorts. As flagged in Dutch Pension Funds: scenario analysis, the life cycle choice is the most important input into a fund’s calculation of required fixed income & swaps exposure in the new system. PFZW will allocate 20% risk free rate exposure to members below the age of 50, rising incrementally to 100% by the age of 65. The risk-free exposure for young members is the middle of the 10-30% range seen across other large pension funds (Exhibit 1), but higher than ABP’s 10%. We estimate that this can translate into an interest rate exposure of around 45% for the fund. w/ final list of PFs that switched, we update our estimates We had previously projected net paying needs of €50-140mln/01 in total for the funds that were planning to switch as of 1-Jan-26. This range was based on a range of 40%- 55% interest rate exposure for all the funds that had not disclosed their life cycle choices, including PFZW. We now update our estimate to around €80mln/01. This also takes into account the updated list of funds that transitioned, the level of funding ratios as of end of Nov, and is based on the information available for individual pension funds’ hedge ratios as of 3Q25. As funding ratios likely moved higher in Dec, the paying needs may be somewhat lower. Additionally, other factors need to be accounted for when it comes to determining flows in the near term, and especially so by tenors (see below). Not just lower paying needs that may have limited flows The 10s30s EUR swaps curve flattened c.1.5bp today with a focus on PFZW’s life cycle, suggesting investors may have been anticipating a lower allocation to risk free rates for young members in the PFZW life cycle. However, the curve has also been under flattening pressures over the last few weeks already, as Dutch pension fund paying flows have been limited, with a skew towards receiving instead. In the next page, we discuss five potential explanations for that. We continue to forecast that 10s30s can reach c.40- 45bp this year but acknowledge that it can be quite volatile with risks of flattening moves as positioning in steepeners is likely still large. We still express the steepeners in conditional bullish format, as opposed to outright. In terms of tenors, we are favouring 5s20s expressions and are very cautious on 30s50s, where the paying needs are much more uncertain. ## GLOBAL RESEARCH

Lack of Dutch pension fund paying: what could be the reasons? There is a possibility that paying needs for the funds that transitioned on 1-Jan-26 are lower than the €80mln/01 we now estimate. Funding ratios have likely increased in December, and hedge ratios may have adjusted somewhat lower relative to 3Q25 levels. But we also see 5 potential reasons why Dutch pension fund paying flows have been limited, with a skew towards receiving side, over the last few weeks:

1. The most obvious reason: funds are making use of the flexibility they are allowed to adjust their portfolio & risk exposure over a period of up to 12 months. PFZW and PMT had communicated their intention to spread the flows over months.
2. Distribution of required flows not as clear as some expected. The theoretical adjustments for a fund that is currently hedged uniformly across the curve include receiving needs in the sub 15-20y part and paying needs beyond that (with net paying in total). However, as discussed in Dutch Pension Funds: scenario analysis, those needs may end up spread differently, with some needing to receive in ultras: a. because they may not have had many received positions in that part of the curve (this was something we had noted for the largest pension fund ABP for which information was available, but it may also apply to other large funds) / those positions were not enough to match the allocation to risk free rates chosen in the life cycle for young members. b. because of a change in the distribution of compensation across age groups (more compensation to young members following the sharp increase in funding ratios). This is a change that was publicly announced by PMT.
3. Receiving flows first: Considering the higher rates even in the 10y sector, some funds may have considered fulfilling their receiving needs first. This is something however that may not be possible for funds that require a reduction in their interest rate exposure in the new system, as it would aggravate the deviation from the required exposure (temporarily higher rate hedge).
4. Funds that have not transitioned yet may have been using the higher rates to receive; either to
1. adjust for the impact of the indexation they announced for 2026,
2. to adjust to the life cycle requirements in ultras, if positions are limited, or
3. to lock in their strong financial position as was done by others in 2023-2024. The fact that the Jan-26 wave of pension funds were able to switch at very high funding ratio levels and can thereby provide elevated compensations to their members (5- 20% increases for retirees depending on funds) may be creating additional pressure on the next wave of funds to maintain their currently strong financial position to be able deliver similar increases in pensions.
5. More concerning for long-dated swap spreads: funds could be selling their LT bonds before (or instead of) unwinding their swaps positions. We have not been expecting such sequencing as the concentration of supply in Q1 does not favour it.
