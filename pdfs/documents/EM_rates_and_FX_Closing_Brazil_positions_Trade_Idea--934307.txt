# EM rates and FX: Closing Brazil positions

We exit our long Brazil positions after the surprise reelection of President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva in Sunday’s run-off vote. Both positions crossed their stop-loss levels. We now expect markets to look ahead to consider the point at which the sharp sell-off could retrace. Assuming the USD remains stable or weakening, the BRL should offer more opportunities than rates, in our view. In fact, the market will likely prepare for the continuation of the status quo of high rates (with the BCB constrained from cuts) and appreciation in FX (due to a soft USD and BRL’s high carry). By contrast, regarding rates, we see an immediate comeback as unlikely, particularly in the belly and at the long end. The focus of opportunity will be short tenors, should the market price in hikes for the coming months (rate hikes are unlikely, in our view). We entered both long Brazil positions after the first round on 4 October as we saw room for a rally given a better-than-expected performance from Lula’s rival Flávio Bolsonaro (see and receive DI on bullish first round election results, dated 5 October). We downsized both positions ahead of the run-off to better manage the P&L impact in case polling that suggested a probable Bolsonaro victory was proved wrong (see EM rates and FX: Resizing Brazil positions ahead of the run-off, 22 October).
