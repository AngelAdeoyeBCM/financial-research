# Japan Monetary Policy Watch - BoJ June MPM preview

8 June 2026 We expect a 0.25% rate hike at the June meeting The Bank of Japan (BoJ) will hold its Monetary Policy Meeting (MPM) on June 15-16. We expect the BoJ to decide on an additional 0.25% rate hike, raising its target for the uncollateralized overnight call rate to around 1.00%.

- The primary reason for this forecast is the strong sense of urgency Governor Ueda expressed in his June 3 speech about the risk of "getting behind the curve." The Governor explicitly stated his desire to avoid a situation where a delayed response would necessitate sharp rate hikes later. We believe this forward-looking risk management stance shortens the timeframe we had previously assumed the BoJ would take to "carefully scrutinize data," thereby increasing the probability of a preemptive rate hike. Furthermore, the domestic corporate goods price index and inflation expectations, which the Governor is closely watching, are already indicating upside risks. These objective facts support the Governor's sense of urgency and could serve as a rationale for a rate hike decision at the June meeting.
- This rate hike will be a significant step, bringing the policy rate closer to the lower bound of the neutral rate. A critical focus will be whether this move is accompanied by a revision to the forward guidance, as this will be pivotal in determining the flexibility and direction of future policy management. 1. The vote count If a rate hike is decided, the vote count will be a key focus. It is important whether the decision is unanimous or if there are dissenters. In particular, whether board member Asada, appointed by Prime Minister Takaichi, will vote in favor of the hike will be a crucial piece of information for gauging the future course of monetary policy.
2. Possibility of a 50bp rate hike proposal Another point of interest is whether any board member will propose a 50bp hike. The minutes of the March MPM revealed that one member wished to consider the magnitude of rate hikes when discussing them. Amid growing concerns about upside inflation risks, the possibility of opinions favoring a larger hike cannot be dismissed.
3. Publication of a "mini" outlook report This is an interim meeting without the release of the full "Outlook for Economic Activity and Prices" (Outlook Report). However, if the BoJ decides to raise rates citing heightened upside risks to inflation, it may publish a concise analysis (a so-called "mini" outlook report), similar to the December 2025 meeting. In such a case, it would also be important to see if there is any difference between the members who approve the mini-report and those who vote for the rate hike itself, as it would offer insights into the range of views within the Policy Board. The focus is on the revision of forward guidance We believe the next rate hike could be a significant turning point, meaning more than just raising the policy rate to 1.0%. The key question is whether the forward guidance will be revised simultaneously, and specifically, whether the BoJ will alter its "pre-committed" stance on future hikes.
1. The limits of "adjusting the degree of accommodation" The BoJ has so far maintained its stance of "continuing to raise the policy rate and adjust the degree of monetary accommodation." However, with this upcoming rate hike, the phase of "adjusting the degree of accommodation" will effectively reach its final stage, weakening the rationale for maintaining this stance. A policy rate of 1.0% would be nearly aligned with the 1.1% lower-end estimate of the nominal neutral rate calculated by the BoJ. While it is strictly 0.1 percentage point lower, considering the uncertainty of the estimate and that underlying inflation is slightly below 2%, it is reasonable to assess that the rate has almost reached the lower bound of the neutral rate. The crucial part is what comes next. If the BoJ maintains its current forward guidance, it would imply that the next rate hike is also an "adjustment of the degree of accommodation." However, the subsequent hike would bring the policy rate to 1.25%, exceeding the 1.1% lower bound of the neutral rate. At that point, monetary policy could hardly be described as "accommodative," and the phrase "adjusting the degree of accommodation" might begin to seem inconsistent with reality. Here, the BoJ faces a crucial communication crossroads. If it maintains the guidance of "continuing to adjust the degree of accommodation," it will face the new challenge of justifying a future hike to 1.25%. On the other hand, revising the guidance now would increase future policy flexibility but also risks being perceived by the market as an unintendedly dovish signal. How the BoJ navigates this 3 dilemma will be a major focus of this meeting.
2. Expecting a shift to a data-dependent stance We anticipate that the BoJ, considering future communication costs, will choose to secure policy flexibility in advance and will revise its forward guidance, which currently presumes continued rate hikes. Specifically, we envision the following change: [April Outlook Report] "As for the conduct of monetary policy, given that underlying CPI inflation has been approaching 2 percent and real interest rates are at significantly low levels, the Bank will continue to raise the policy interest rate and adjust the degree of monetary accommodation, in response to developments in economic activity and prices as well as financial conditions. In this regard, it will consider the timing and pace of adjustment, while closely monitoring the impact of the future course of the situation in the Middle East on Japan's economic activity and prices and examining the likelihood of realizing the baseline scenario of the outlook for economic activity and prices and the risks to the outlook. With the price stability target of 2 percent, the Bank will conduct monetary policy as appropriate from the perspective of sustainable and stable achievement of the target." [Envisioned new guidance at the June meeting] "Regarding the conduct of monetary policy, the Bank will appropriately adjust the policy interest rate in accordance with economic activity and price and financial conditions. In doing so, the Bank will examine the timing and pace of adjustments while watching developments in the Middle East situation and their impact on Japan's economic activity and prices, and checking the likelihood of achieving the Bank's baseline scenario and the risks. The Bank of Japan will appropriately conduct monetary policy from the perspective of the sustainable and stable achievement of the 2 percent "price stability target." This shift—deleting the "pre-committed" wording and moving to a data-dependent stance where decisions are made on a case-by-case basis—is perfectly consistent with Governor Ueda's intention, expressed in his June 3 speech, to carefully scrutinize data such as the "second-round effects" on inflation. It is important to note that the market impact of this forward guidance revision is ambiguous. The removal of the pre-committed wording on rate hikes could be interpreted by the market as a dovish signal that the hiking cycle is nearing its end, and the risk of a temporary yen depreciation and lower interest rates cannot be denied. However, we place more importance on the fundamental implications of this change rather than its superficial interpretation. We consider this change to be hawkish for two main reasons. First, the phrase "adjusting the degree of monetary accommodation" implicitly suggested that policy would remain in "accommodative" territory. Removing this wording is a clear signal that this implicit constraint is gone, indicating that future policy may not necessarily be accommodative. Second, it enhances the BoJ's "policy flexibility," enabling it to respond swiftly and flexibly—at times even exceeding market expectations (e.g., by accelerating the pace of rate hikes)—depending on future data. These two fundamental shifts—namely, the "de-commitment from an accommodative stance" and "enhanced policy flexibility"—are the hawkish aspects, and they will be a critical focal point for gauging the flexibility and direction of future policy management. Page 4 watch at the Governor's press conference At his press conference, Governor Ueda will likely reiterate the criteria for policy judgment based on the logical framework of his June 3 speech, distinguishing between two cases:
1. when the likelihood of the baseline scenario materializing increases, and
2. when upside inflation risks heighten before that likelihood increases. Additionally, if questioned by reporters, we would focus on the Governor's view on the relationship between underlying inflation reaching 2% and the achievement of the "price stability target." This is crucial for understanding the evolving nature of the BoJ's policy reaction function. Key issue: Interpretation of the "price stability target" and the asymmetry of the policy reaction function The Governor currently states that underlying inflation is "approaching" 2%. The recent rise in crude oil prices could push this figure up, leading to it reaching 2% while also posing an upside risk. In this context, answers to the following long-standing questions we have are awaited:
1. If underlying inflation reaches 2%, can it be considered consistent with achieving the "price stability target"?
2. If underlying inflation exceeds 2%, does it become inconsistent with the target?
3. Alternatively, is the target considered consistent if underlying inflation averages 2% with some fluctuations, meaning temporary overshoots are permissible? While it may have been premature to discuss these points before, now is the time for a full-fledged debate, as the BoJ anticipates reaching a state consistent with the target from the second half of fiscal 2026 onwards. This issue is important for understanding the asymmetry of the policy reaction function. In the past, during the process of exiting deflation, a deliberate "behind-the-curve" strategy while underlying inflation was below 2% had a certain rationale, as it helped push inflation toward 2%. However, now that Governor Ueda has clearly signaled his concern about the risk of "getting behind the curve," we should consider this policy stance as having already shifted. We believe that once underlying inflation reaches 2%, or even when its path toward 2% becomes foreseeable, a similar strategy would risk an inflation overshoot and threaten the achievement of the "price stability target." In other words, we understand that the BoJ's policy reaction function is changing to become more forward-looking as inflation approaches 2%, and the need to respond more swiftly to upside risks is increasing. A clear explanation from the Governor on this point is essential for fostering the market's understanding of the BoJ's policy reaction function and for stabilizing expectations regarding future monetary policy. Enhancing the transparency of dialogue with financial markets is, by extension, a textbook example of central bank communication that contributes to economic and price stability. Future policy path and JGB purchases
1. Rate hike path: Maintaining a quarterly pace Regarding the subsequent rate hike path, we expect further 0.25% hikes in October 2026, and January and April 2027, bringing the policy rate to 1.75% in April 2027. 5 This quarterly pace is supported not only by the reasons we pointed out in our previous report (ensuring tightening effects, controlling inflation expectations, coordinating with overseas central banks, and changes in the Policy Board's composition) but also from the perspective of achieving the price stability target. As discussed above, from October onwards, when the situation could become consistent with the "price stability target," the need to raise rates will increase in response to upside risks to underlying inflation.
2. JGB purchase plan: Expecting purchases to remain at JPY 2.1 trillion per month Regarding the JGB purchase plan, which will be discussed concurrently, we expect no changes to the plan already decided for the period up to Q1 2027. Altering it would reduce the predictability of JGB purchases and could introduce unnecessary volatility into the JGB market. The focus will be on the plan from Q2 2027 onwards, for which we expect no further reduction in purchases; that is, the monthly purchase amount will be maintained at JPY 2.1 trillion. This is inferred from the minutes of the Bond Market Group Meeting and Governor Ueda's June 3 speech. However, we recognize that at the Bond Market Group Meeting, requests from market participants varied widely, from accelerating the reduction to continuing it or halting it, with the latter being a minority view. In such circumstances, if the BoJ decides to halt the reduction, it will be accompanied by a responsibility to provide a very thorough explanation. A decision to halt the reduction can find its own economic rationale. This is because, in a rate hike phase, curbing excessive rises in long-term interest rates and heightened volatility can be considered a legitimate policy objective to ensure financial market stability. However, the timing is the problem. Amid media reports suggesting the government would condone a rate hike on the condition that the BoJ halts the reduction of its JGB purchases (e.g., Nikkei on May 28, TV Tokyo's "Morning Satellite" on June 4), a simultaneous decision to raise rates and halt the reduction could, regardless of its true intent, be perceived by the market and the public as a "political deal with the government." This carries a serious risk of creating doubts about the BoJ's independence, which is the cornerstone of monetary policy credibility. Thus, while halting the reduction of JGB purchases has the legitimate objective of "market stabilization," a simultaneous decision with a rate hike presents a dilemma with the serious side effect of "doubts about independence." We consider this a non-negligible hurdle for a June rate hike and would urge the BoJ to approach this contradictory challenge with extremely careful communication.
