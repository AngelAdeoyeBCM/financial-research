# FX: Trail stop on short USDKRW

. 07/01/2026 1

RKE. We initiated our long KRW trade idea in November, as we saw scope for the flow outlook to turn more supportive, alongside assistance from FX authorities, cheap valuations, favourable positioning, and more robust fundamentals (see FX: Fading KRW weakness, sell USDKRW 6m NDF, dated 21 November 2025). With the position having performed, generating a 1.3% gain at the time of writing, we now trail the stop to the entry level of 1,460.50. We maintain our medium-term bullish view on the KRW, as the narrative stays intact: the policy-driven repatriation, rotation from overseas back to domestic assets, and favourable positioning (with FX investors having only built modest KRW longs at a score of +11, on a +/-50 scale according to BNP Paribas Global FX Positioning Tracker). Since we initiated the trade idea, USDKRW initially traded in 1,470-1,485 range until late December, when USDKRW subsequently dropped by 2.4%, following an FX intervention, reports of NPS conducting strategic FX hedging, and the announcement of new measures to reshore domestic capital from overseas(see MoEF, dated 24 December 2025). These measures include:

- Temporary tax relief for retail investors with Reshoring Investment Accounts (RIA) – Capital gains tax on overseas equities sales (normally 22% including 2% local income tax) will be reduced for those which convert gains into KRW for a year and conduct long-term investment in domestic equities, with 100% relief if repatriated back by Q1 2026, 80% if back by Q2 2026 and 50% if back in H2 2026, with a maximum limit of KRW50mn per investor.
- Promoting FX-forward products for individuals – The government will work with major brokerage firms to roll out forward-selling instruments quickly, addressing the current lack of FX-risk-management tools among retail investors. It will also give a 5% capital gains tax discount (up to KRW5mn) for investors using these products for their overseas investments.
- Tax-exemption upgrade – To eliminate double taxation on dividends paid by overseas subsidiaries to domestic parent companies, the dividend-income-exclusion ratio will be raised from the current 95% to 100%. We believe the measures aim to increase USD liquidity in the domestic market, thereby correcting the KRW–USD imbalance. The initiative mirrors the drivers of KRW strength in H1 2023, when the currency was buoyed by dividend repatriation, triggered by corporate dividend-tax reforms (see North Asia FX: South Korea-Taiwan divergence appears, dated 13 August 2023). Though retail investors are a bit cautious on using RIA – given the long lock-in period and their bias toward holding USD investments – we see this as a positive start to the reallocation of domestic capital back to South Korean assets. We expect the government to continue providing support with the aim of promoting financial stability and pushing the KOSPI toward the 5000 level.
