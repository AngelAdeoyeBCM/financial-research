# US Economic Perspectives - The AI view of the Fed - Hawks fly together

Economics US Economic Perspectives Date 10 September 2026

- In this piece, we update our AI-based scoring of Fed communications leading up to the September FOMC meeting. This builds on our prior work seen here: Using DB AI to ID Fed hawks and doves. Our AI tool continues to identify a hawkish trend in Fed communications since our last update in July, with the overall sentiment score for all officials increasing by 0.6 to 7.2, creating a record high and marking the most hawkish level within our sample which started from October 2021. Similarly, the average score for the 2026 voting members rose to 7.2 from 6.5 in July, surpassing the Covid inflation period and reaching its most hawkish point over the past 5 years.
- The distribution of scores is uniformly hawkish, all but one official in hawkish territory (i.e., above 5). Among the 12 voting members, nine experienced a shift toward more hawkish levels. This group includes Chairman Warsh, whose score rose marginally to 8.2, making it the third-highest among all voting members. Governor Waller was the only voter to register a decline, with his score slipping by 0.3 points to 6.5. This prevailing hawkish distribution aligns with recent Federal Reserve communications, which have signaled a lower threshold for near-term rate hikes, even if some suggested meaningful sensitivity to this week’s inflation data (see Factions of the Fed’s Family Fight). Fed communications most hawkish over the past 5 years In our ongoing analysis of Federal Reserve communications, we again employ our proprietary LLM tools to quantify the hawkishness or dovishness of officials’ speeches and prepared remarks (see Using DB AI to ID Fed hawks and doves). This method assigns a score on a scale of 0 (most dovish) to 10 (most hawkish) to each communication. In this piece, we continue this AI scoring exercise and update our analysis for speeches leading up to the communication blackout period for the September FOMC meeting. Since our previous update prior to the July FOMC meeting, Chairman Warsh delivered a notably hawkish speech at Jackson Hole (see Jackson Hole recap: Working 9 to 5 to return inflation to target). Our AI tool assigned this address a score of 8.8, approximately half a point higher than his prior speeches. This hawkish assessment stems from his predominant focus on elevated prices, downplaying of recent positive inflation data, assessment of financial conditions as non-restrictive, a nd his emphasis on traditional monetary policy tools. Most importantly, the speech indicated a clear bias toward proactive policy action until inflation shows tangible and sufficient progress toward the target. Our AI also highlighted that this speech remo ved ambiguity regarding the inflation target, characterizing it as a resolute and disciplined declaration focused squarely on price stability. Warsh’s score ranks the third highest among all voting members. Matthew Luzzetti, Ph.D. Chaitali Patil Research Associate This hawkish sentiment appears broadly shared across most of the Committee. Almost all officials saw their scores rise, reflecting a greater openness to potential rate hikes. The average score for 2026 voters, as well as the average for all 19 officials, increased by 0.5 points to reach 7.2 —the highest level observed in our five-year sample. This marks the first time the average score has risen above 7.0, surpassing even the peak of the pandemic-era inflation period, which topped out at 6.8. This sustained upward trajectory over the past nine months underscores a collective hawkish shift in the Committee’s risk assessment and an increasing perceived need for further policy action. The overall distribution of scores is heavily skewed toward the hawkish end of the spectrum, with 18 of the 19 officials scoring above the neutral threshold of 5.0. The sole exception is Governor Bowman, who registered a dovish score of 3.8; however, this may not fully reflect her current stance, as her speeches since May have primarily focused on topics other than monetary policy. Similarly, Governor Powell’s score remained unchanged at 5.5 due to a lack of public comments since May. Meanwhile, officials near the center of the Committee—including Vice Chair Jefferson, New York Fed President Williams, Philadelphia Fed President Paulson, San Francisco Fed President Daly, and Boston Fed President Collins —all experienced modest to moderate score increases. The scores for Williams, Jefferson, and Paulson rose marginally to 5.6, 6.7, and 6.8, respectively. Daly’s score rose more notably by 1.4 points to 6.1, reflecting her comments that the probability of a persistent inflation scenario is now “much closer to” her modal forecast, which could warrant “substantial” policy recalibration. Collins’s score rose by more than a full point to 8.6, representing the fourth-highest level among all officials. This shift was driven by the lower threshold Collins set for policy tightening, noting that a mere absence of continued disinflation, rather than outright evidence of accelerating inflation, could justify a rate hike. On the most hawkish end of the spectrum, Cleveland Fed President Hammack, Minneapolis Fed President Kashkari, and Kansas City Fed President Schmid maintained the highest scores, with all three rising to or above 9.0. Hammack recorded a score of 9.8, the mo st hawkish in the current Committee and the highest in our historical dataset. Among other voting members, very hawkish scores were also registered by Dallas Fed President Logan (7.9), Governor Cook (8.1), and Governor Barr (8.0). Both Barr and Cook experienced the sharpest score increases since July, driven by expressed concerns over stalling inflation and a readiness to act if price pressures do not moderate “sufficiently” in the near term (Figure 3). This was the largest decline among all officials, matched only by Cheryl Venable, the interim Atlanta Fed President who assumed office on March 1. Waller’s softer score aligns with his recent communications advocating a “hawkish hold” stance—urging patience to wait for further disinflation while remaining fully prepared to hike rates if incoming inflation data runs hot. This represents a slight moderation from his hike-biased stance in July. Reg arding the Atlanta Fed, we scored two speeches by interim President Venable, yielding moderately hawkish scores of 6.0 in May and 5.5 in August, with her more recent remarks offering a more balanced economic assessment. Overall, the distribution of policy sentiment remains hawkish, with almost all officials firmly in hawkish territory. While the upcoming CPI report will carry weight in determining the outcome of next week’s FOMC meeting, current communications signal a relatively low bar for near-term rate hikes. As previously analyzed (see Fed Notes: Factions of the Fed’s Family Fight), an implied core PCE print of greater than 20 basis points for August would likely lean the Committee toward a rate hike.
