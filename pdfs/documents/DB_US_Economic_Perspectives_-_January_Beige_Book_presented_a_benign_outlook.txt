# US Economic Perspectives - January Beige Book presented a benign outlook

Economics US Economic Perspectives Date 15 January 2026

We have previously leveraged our proprietary LLM tools to analyze the Beige Book, assessing economic sentiment across key macroeconomic variables: growth, the labor market, inflation, and perceived recession probabilities. The derived sentiment scores were able to capture economic dynamics over the past decade (see From qualitative to quantitative: decoding Beige Book with AI). In this piece we continued this exercise and updated it to the latest Beige Book from January 2026. As a reminder, the score is based on a scale of 0 to 10, a score of 0 is associated with a lower expectation for the future, and a score of 10 is associated with higher expectation. A score of 5 is neutral expectation (see Appendix for prompt for full details). Note that for the last topic on recession, a score of 0 would suggest that the recession is not expected in next 12 months, while a score of 10 would suggest that the recession is nearly certain in next 12 months. Our data spans from January 2015 to the latest Beige Book in January 2026. The latest Beige Book presents a more sanguine outlook than last year, indicating a healthier labor market, falling inflationary pressures, and stronger growth expectations (Figure 1-4). The labor market score rose to 5.2 in January, having remained at below-neutral level for most of last year. This aligns with the recent decline in the unemployment rate, which had assuaged some concerns about the downside risks to labor market (Figure 5). The inflation sentiment scores also fell to 5.2 from an elevated lev el at 7 last November, as firms anticipated some moderation in price growth, albeit from high price levels. Encouragingly, the growth sentiment score surged to 6 in January, reaching its highest level since just prior to Liberation Day. Businesses cited greater consumer spending during the holiday season and expressed a “mildly optimistic” outlook, anticipating slight to modest activity growth. Similarly, the discussion generally reflected reduced concerns about a recession, with the score dropping to 1.5 – its lowest level since 2021 – after hovering at or above 5.0 throughout 2025. This score corresponds to an estimated 15% recession probability, consistent with our model-based estimates (see A post mortem on recession probability models). In conclusion, the latest scores aligned with recent economic data showing a lower unemployment rate, softening yet still elevated inflation, and improved growth prospects (see December CPI recap: Distortions and outliers and December employment report: Stabilizing slack supports steady Fed). This also supports our higher estimate of the neutral rate, and our baseline outlook of solid growth, a stabilizing labor market, and lower but still-above target inflation (see R-star update lends some weight to Fed hawks). With tensions remaining between the Fed’s dual mandates, the latest Beige Book provides supporting anecdotal evidence that downside risks to the labor market have receded some, Matthew Luzzetti, Ph.D. allowing the Fed to await more data and clarity on the inflation outlook before further easing.
