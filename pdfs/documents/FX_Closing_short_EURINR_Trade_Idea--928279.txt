# FX: Closing short EURINR

. 16/07/2026 1

B N P PA GL OB ET S A PP The trade idea we opened in FX: Sell EURINR on 1 July has reached its stop-loss, and we close it for a loss of 2.07% (USD 207k). We initiated the idea because we expected the flow backdrop to improve for the INR following the Reserve Bank of India’s measures (see FX: RBI, BI try different paths to fight depreciation, dated 12 June). Furthermore, lower oil prices after the signing of an MOU between the US and Iran, coupled with suggesting scope for longs to build, made INR longs attractive, in our view. We favoured funding the position with the EUR, given it’s a low yielding currency and our view that it remained vulnerable if the market priced in further Fed tightening. The cross faced setbacks on both legs due to:

1. renewed Middle East tensions and damage to Russian oil infrastructure, which pushed oil prices higher, impacting India as a large net energy importer, particularly from Russia and the Middle East;
2. media reports of smaller-than-expected FCNR deposit inflows, which limit the RBI’s capacity to intervene against currency weakness;
3. the aforementioned factors driving an unwind of INR longs – suggests INR longs have pared from a score of +20 in mid-June to +1 now; and
4. a softer US CPI report this week, which has likely prompted some unwind of USD longs. These unwinds were particularly concentrated against G10 currencies, with EUR shorts reaching an extreme score of -32 (on a +/-50 scale), according to Tracker. In turn, the c ross has reached its stop-loss, so we close the position at 111.63 for a 2.07% loss (exit – USDINR 05Oct26 NDF: 97.03, EURUSD 05Oct26 forward: 1.15043, 11:35 GMT+8). However, we still believe our view will perform over the medium term and may look to re-engage the position at better levels. Even though the situation in the Middle East remains fragile, our base case assumes prolonged US–Iran negotiations, coupled with improving flows through the Strait of Hormuz. Additionally, the R BI appears to be taking steps to resolve issues regarding FCNR deposits, with media reports suggesting it has been holding talks with banks in India and abroad. Finally, while recent US data was slightly weaker than we expected, the pace of jobs growth is still sufficient to tighten the labour market, and we view the CPI reading as an outlier rather than a signal of an underlying trend, so we continue to expect three consecutive rate hikes starting in December, although an earlier move remains a distinct possibility.
