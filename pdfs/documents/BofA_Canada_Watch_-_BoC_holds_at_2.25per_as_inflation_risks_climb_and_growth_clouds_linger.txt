# Canada Watch - BoC holds at 2.25per as inflation risks climb and growth clouds linger

Canada Watch BoC holds at 2.25% as inflation risks climb and growth clouds linger Key takeaways

- BoC held at 2.25% with a modest hawkish tilt as upside inflation risks increased on higher oil prices.
- We still expect a prolonged hold as trade uncertainty caps growth, but risks now skew to the upside, particularly for 2027.
- Rates: CAD rates higher, driven by front-end repricing. FX: USDCAD moderately lower, but still risks to upside. The BoC maintains hold at 2.25% The BoC (Bank of Canada) held its policy rate at 2.25% (E: 2.25%, BofA: 2.25%) at its September 2 meeting, in line with our expectations (see our BoC preview). This marks the seventh consecutive hold since December. The BoC highlighted rising inflation risks from persistently high oil prices, while noting that new US tariffs have added uncertainty to the growth outlook. BoC sees higher inflation risks... The statement and press release flagged increasing upside risks to inflation, mostly from higher and potentially more persistent oil prices tied to the ongoing Middle East conflict, but also from a further escalation of the US-Canada trade war. While the BoC does not yet see evidence of higher oil prices spilling over into other goods and services, it is concerned that a prolonged period of elevated oil prices could turn into more persistent inflation. Inflation currently sits at 3%, with core at 2% (see). Our forecasts have inflation reaching 3.2% in 2H 2026 with core near 2%, but we agree that upside risks to the inflation outlook have increased. ... but more uncertain growth prospects The Canadian economy rebounded sharply in 2Q, in line with BoC expectations. However, the recent escalation of the US-Canada trade war “make[s] growth prospects more uncertain” and “pose[s] risks to the sustainability of the rebound in economic activity.” In our view, while the direct impact of higher tariffs is likely limited given the narrow export coverage, the larger concern is that prolonged trade uncertainty weighs on investment, hiring, and confidence, while making a USMCA renewal more difficult (see report). BoC on hold for an extended period We continue to expect the BoC to remain on hold at 2.25% for the rest of this year and into next. Despite higher upside inflation risks, we believe the trade shock caps the recovery and puts hikes further out of reach. With uncertainty lingering and core inflation close to 2%, we think the central bank will continue to look through tariff- and oil-driven pressure on headline inflation. That said, with the BoC flagging increasing upside risks to inflation, we now see the balance of risks to our call as skewed to the upside, particularly for next year. Canada Economics BofAS Global Economics Team BofAS 2 | 02 September 2026 Rates: CA rates sold off on hawkish statement language Despite the in-line decision, markets perceived the BoC statement as hawkish. As a result, CA rates sold off, driven by the front-end. The curve flattened as markets repriced risk of higher near-term inflation. Market pricing of BoC hikes in ‘26 rose from 61bp priced to a high of 95bp before partially retracing back down to 85bp priced, as of this writing. The market now prices in nearly 3.5 hikes by July 2027 in CA vs 2.5bp in the US. We continue to believe the market overprices the near-term risk of a BoC hike given stable core inflation and downside risks to growth and investment from elevated trade uncertainty. Gov Macklem also acknowledged in the press conference that there has been some spillover from higher yields globally into CAD rates which has led to some tightening in financial conditions. This should allow the BoC to be patient on rate hikes. Near-term, we see further upside risk to CAD rates, especially if the Fed hikes at their Sept meeting. CAD: USDCAD moderately lower, but still risks to upside USDCAD moved lower in reaction to initial headlines that noted concern around upside inflation risks. In general, rate differentials remain a core driver in terms of FX markets, and factors increasing the relative prospects of BoC action have been CAD-positive, particularly given currently moderate core inflation levels. The BoC statement noted that “The Canadian dollar has appreciated slightly on US-dollar weakness”. Inflation commentary during the press conference also nudged USDCAD lower as well. Our outlook over the rest of the year looks focused around a 1.40-type level for USDCAD, but ultimately we still see risks to the upside given our views for the BOC to stay on hold over the medium-term. The ongoing trade war plays a core factor in our expectations, as well as specifically our US Economics team’s view that the Federal Reserve will hike 25bp at each of their three remaining meetings for the year.
