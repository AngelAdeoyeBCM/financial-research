# UK economic notes - BoE Recap – Marching On

5 February 2026 Deutsche Bank Research Europe United Kingdom Economics UK economic notes Date Today's decision was not really about a rate cut - though this was a lot closer than we anticipated. Instead, today's meeting was about positioning within the MPC. Trade-offs across the economy are rising. And this is adding to the MPC's consternation around how restrictive policy remains. Indeed, while headline growth has been resilient, underlying GDP growth remains modest. Despite slightly stickier pay settlements, the labour market remains fragile. And despite survey data pointing to firming price pressures, CPI looks poised to return to near the Bank's 2% target around spring. Put simply, the February decision was about managing trade-offs, as Bank Rate approaches the committee's estimate of the neutral rate. What did we learn today? First, a rate cut was a closer decision than we anticipated. With the vote tally coming in at 5-4, this was a finely balanced decision. Put simply, many on the MPC still see Bank Rate as overly restrictive amidst the current economic conjecture. Second, Bank projections back the dovish tilt from the MPC. Bank staff lowered their CPI projections meaningfully through the forecast horizon – suggesting that CPI is on course to remain at, or near, target through the medium-term, with headline price momentum expected to slow to 2% by spring this year. Bank staff also raised the peak in unemployment projections, with the jobless rate now expected to peak at 5.3%. In the near-term, GDP growth projections were also slashed. Bottom line, the MPC now sees a larger accumulation of spare capacity in the economy, suggesting that policy remains restrictive. Third, risk management considerations are factoring into the MPC's thinking. With the labour market remaining fragile, and wage growth on a downtrend, the MPC at large is starting to acknowledge more downside risks to activity as opposed to upside risks to inflation. Wage settlements of 3.4% - as confirmed by Bank Agents - now track closer to the Bank's revised target-consistent levels. Importantly, forward guidance from the Bank was explicit: "Bank Rate is likely to be reduced further". Governor Bailey noted that "there was scope for some further easing of policy". Deputy Governor Breeden noted the "case for taking out some insurance against these downside risks to inflation and think policy should be eased a little faster than implied by the current market path." And Deputy Governor Ramsden concluded that "policy should be less restrictive in order to meet the 2% target BoE Recap – Marching On 5 February 2026 Page 2 Deutsche Bank AG sustainably in the medium term." What next? We continue to think that further rate cuts are coming. We stick to our call for the next Bank Rate cut to come in March and a final rate cut to come in June taking Bank Rate to 3.25% - broadly consistent with our estimate of neutral. What do we need to see for a Q1-26 rate cut? Data coming broadly in line with the Bank's projections. Risks are still skewed to a slower pace of rate cuts. But we remain confident that Bank Rate will be cut twice this year. The February MPR: key highlights from the decision We summarise the key developments as part of the Bank's updated outlook. In short, near-term growth projections were cut, alongside the Bank's inflation projections. The jobless rate is projected to rise a touch further. And more spare capacity is expected to build over the medium-term.

- Growth: The BoE expects GDP growth to have expanded by 1.4% in 2025 (Nov MPR: 1.5%). Thereafter, the Bank expects growth to slow - at least relative to its previous projections. GDP growth in 2026 is now projected to drop to 0.9% (Nov MPR: 1.2%), 1.5% in 2027 (Nov MPR: 1.7%), and 1.8% in 2028 (Nov MPR: 1.8%). The uptrend in GDP growth reflects the gradual easing of monetary policy restrictiveness to date, as well as the further easing implied by the market path for interest rates. The Bank also expects household savings rate to normalise, leading to stronger household consumption growth. Accommodative financial conditions and increasing support from the housing market are also expected to support activity over the forecast period.
- Inflation: In what was one of the biggest cuts in inflation projections ever between forecast updates, the Bank redrew its CPI projections following on from the Budget and recent inflation outturns. CPI is projected to return to around the 2% target in Q2-26 (Nov MPR: 2.9%) and is expected to remain close to that level over the forecast period. The projected near-term decrease in CPI inflation is largely attributed to a reduced impact from energy prices, a result of the government's energy bills package and falling wholesale gas costs. By the second quarter of 2026, the influence of administered prices and the pass-through of NICs-related costs are also expected to fade. Additionally, import prices are forecast to decline over the year, partly because trade diversion is expected to exert downward pressure on world export prices.
- Labour market: Subdued growth in output is expected to feed through to weaker labour demand. The unemployment rate is projected to peak in the second half of 2026, at around 5.25%, before declining gradually. Unemployment remains above Bank staff's assumed medium-term equilibrium rate by the end of the forecast period (the Bank judges NAIRU to sit at 4.75%). On wage growth, AWE Private Regular Pay is expected to have slowed to 3.4% in Q4-25 (Nov MPR:), before dropping to 3.3% in Q4-26 (Nov MPR: 3.2%), 2.7% in Q4-27 (Nov MPR: 2.9%), and 3% in Q4-28 (Nov MPR: 3.2%). Importantly, Bank Agents noted that private sector pay settlements were now expected to track at 3.4% this year (a mild improvement from its initial assessment of 3.5%).
- Output gap: Putting it altogether, the Bank's projections point to more spare capacity in the economy. The output gap is projected to widen to 1% of GDP this year (Nov MPR: -0.8%), before dropping to only 0.8% in 2027 (Nov MPR: -0.5%) and staying in negative territory in 2028 at -0.4% (Nov MPR: -0.1%). Put simply, spare capacity in the economy is projected to be higher across the Bank's forecast horizon. Why? The MPC at large has started to put more weight on risk management considerations, owing to the fragile labour market and projected drop in CPI to 2% by spring. Bottom line, the Bank thinks that price dynamics are likely to return to more 'normal' levels in the coming years as wage bargaining power remains tepid. The MPC is looking at productivity growth and import prices to justify above 3% wage settlements. While the MPC has put more stock on the absolute level wage settlements as a measure for target-consistency in the past, it has expanded its framework to include import prices, labour productivity, and firms' margins. With the MPC now assuming import price inflation of 1%, import intensity of 25%, 0% margins contribution, and 1% productivity growth, the Bank now thinks target consistent wage growth is likely closer to 3.25% - a slight upgrade from before. This has narrowed the gap with the Bank Agents 3.4% private sector pay settlements expected this year. Productivity growth may be on an uptrend. Respondents to the latest DMP Survey expected AI to increase their firms' productivity by around 0.6% per year on average over the next three years. The MPC expects productivity growth to track around 1% in the medium-term. This would add to the level at which wage growth would remain target-consistent. Overall supply growth is expected to outpace the Bank's GDP projections, adding to spare capacity. Fiscal policy will weigh on the output gap. The MPC expects the combined effect of the announced measures to directly reduce CPI inflation by 0.5pp at its peak in Q2-2026, bringing CPI to 2% in that quarter. While this hasn't changed the Bank's judgement on second-round effects, the faster drop in CPI is expected to pull down on inflation expectations. The MPC noted that "the risk of greater inflation persistence is judged to have continued to diminish, in part due to the lower near-term outlook for inflation". Equally, despite fiscal policy measures announced in the Budget expected to add to GDP growth, the overall fiscal stance remains tight. In the MPC's central projection, the fiscal stance weighs on the output gap by around one percentage point by the end of the three-year forecast period. 5 February 2026 Page 4 Deutsche Bank AG Policy rules point to lower Bank Rate too. In the Bank's central projections, a forward-looking Taylor rule (something Governor Bailey ascribed to in the November forecast update), had Bank Rate slowing to 3.1% by the end of the year. In a scenario of inflation persistence, Bank Rate is estimated to drop to 3.2% under the forward-looking Taylor rule. And in a weak demand scenario, Bank Rate is expected to drop to as low as 2.7% (using the same policy rule). Overall, even looking at the stickier 'optimal policy paths' estimated by Bank staff, Bank Rate is estimated to drop below current levels. The MPC's forward guidance is clear - Bank Rate will likely reduce further in the coming months/quarters. Beyond the more finely balanced split vote (5-4), the MPC's tone was clear: more rate cuts are coming – notwithstanding some uncertainty around the timing of future rate cuts. Looking at the centrists, the case for further rate cuts remains - or indeed has strengthened. Governor Bailey noted that there was "scope for some further easing of policy" - though cast some uncertainty on the timing of rate cuts, noting effectively that every meeting was 'live'. Deputy Governor Sarah Breeden noted that downside risks were rising, and the "case for taking out some insurance against these downside risks to inflation" was building. She even explicitly challenged market pricing, stating that "policy should be eased a little faster than implied by the current market path". Deputy Governor Dave Ramsden noted that with his judgement of neutral at "around 3%", "policy should be less restrictive in order to meet the 2% target sustainably in the medium term". Even external member Catherine Mann signalled that a shift in policy stance could be coming: "[new] analysis and current developments have moved the appropriate time for a cut in Bank Rate closer." Put simply, while future rate cuts may be a close call, divisions in the MPC may be narrowing. Our call: Marching On The odds for a Q1-26 rate cut have picked up strongly in our view. We stick to our call for March and June rate cuts, bringing terminal rate to 3.25% - broadly in line with our estimate of neutral policy. In our preview, we looked at the conditions that would keep March in play. Those conditions have been met, with a more finely balanced split vote. And despite a change in guidance with the Bank moving away from its previous message of a "gradual downward" Bank Rate path, firmer language around the path for Bank Rate ("Bank Rate is likely to be reduced further"), and clearer guidance from individual members that Bank Rate may be too restrictive add to our confidence that multiple rate cuts remain likely. What do we need to see for March to remain basecase? Spot CPI (including services), wage growth (AWE Private Regular Pay), and the jobless rate moving broadly in line with the Bank's central projections. The forthcoming data will be very important in this regard. Risks are still skewed to a slower pace of rate cuts, but we continue to think that the Bank will cut twice more before calling an end to the easing cycle.
