# FX: Long EURUSD

FX: Long 1: EU reliance on US for exports has declined We hold a bullish view on EURUSD and forecast 1.20 by year-end. A revival of US–EU trade tensions and the subsequent risk-off has done little to weigh on EURUSD with the pair holding up well just above 1.16. This is in spite of tit-for-tat escalation as a Greenland-related US tariff threat to EU states was met by an EU threat to go back to a tariff list prepared last year, and roll out of its anti-coercion instrument to limit US companies’ EU market access. In the past the market has suggested bilateral tariffs are bullish for the USD as the US suffers less economically as an importer. However, we see some unique circumstances suggesting escalating tensions may be bullish for EURUSD. US tariffs on the EU may be perceived as less credible by the market than in the past given the risk that those implemented under the IEEPA are struck down. Even if these latest threats materialise, EU exports to the US as a share of total exports have declined to just 10% (Figure 1). If implemented as stated, our preliminary estimates suggest that the tariffs would shave off around a little less than 0.1% from 2026 eurozone GDP. We also see a number of paths through which the Greenland issue may prove bullish for EURUSD. Specifically, if it results in more EU integration, joint issuance or more defence spending outside of Germany. Additionally, if EURUSD continues to trade well in the face of risk-off concerns around the USD, then hedging flows from eurozone investors are likely to come back to the fore (see What drives FX hedge ratios?, dated 3 July 2025). If there is an FX transaction attached to any deal, whereby the US pays either Greenlanders, Denmark or both this is also likely to prove bullish EURUSD. At the same time, relative data, both in terms of surprise and the absolute level of strength, have moved in favour of EURUSD recently, and yet the pair has lagged. This suggests to us some room for catch-up (Figures 2 and 3).

## KEY MESSAGES

We add a long EURUSD trade as we think a revival of EU–US trade tensions could prove bullish for the pair. Relative data surprises and strength have moved in favour of EURUSD. E M – YE S FO IE W 219/01/2026 We acknowledge that with the ECB likely on hold this year the transmission mechanism for better European data to the EUR is less clear – but still, we think the near-term trajectory for European data is for outperformance with the impact of German fiscal stimulus likely to soon show up in survey releases. Federal Reserve credibility concerns provide another left-hand tail for the USD and markets will be closely watching this week’s oral arguments as SCOTUS looks at Trump’s proposed ousting of Fed board member Lisa Cook. However, on balance we do not expect a near-term decision on Cook, nor a rapid announcement of the next Fed chair, which should make Fed independence risk a less imminent (albeit still important) threat to the USD. The BNP Paribas FX Positioning Tracker sees EURUSD positioning as largely flat currently, with longs having reduced since the start of the year (Figure 4). There is also currently no strong valuation angle with EURUSD largely fairly valued on STEER. This suggests room for longs to rebuild, although we acknowledge the case is not as compelling from a quant perspective as we feel it is from a fundamental perspective. Our EURUSD long adds to our existing bullish EUR exposure through long EURAUD and long EURGBP, which is partly balanced by our short EURBRL trades. Additionally, we continue to hold a EURUSD higher, S&P lower dual digital (see Safe haven shift? EURUSD higher in US equity selloffs, dated 21 November 2025).
