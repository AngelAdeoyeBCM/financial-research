# Fixed Income Blog - EUR HICP market preview

Rates Fixed Income Blog Date 28 September 2026

We expect September HICP (ex-tobacco) at 3.75% (3.72%; 3.70% unrevised) y/y, marginally below market fixings. The expected pickup from August is driven mainly by energy prices, although we also forecast higher core and food inflation. Looking further ahead, we expect headline inflation at around 4.0% over the coming months, while core inflation accelerates towards 2.6/2.7% a round the turn of the year. M arket pricing remains above our forecasts over the 1-year horizon, with HICPxT peaking at 4.3% before falling back below 3.0% in H2 2027. From a market perspective, we remain neutral EUR inflation for now, while acknowledging the embedded risk premium in HICP valuations, with 1y HICP currently ~30bp above our forecasts and forwards close to the upper end of this year’s range. EUR HICP forecasts We expect euro-area headline HICP inflation to rise by ~50bp in September, reaching 3.75% year-on-year, its highest rate since September 2023. This would be above the Bloomberg consensus average of 3.62% (median 3.7%). We forecast HICP ex-tobacco at 3.72% (3.70% unrevised), marginally below market pricing. Energy is the main driver of the expected increase, with both core and food inflation also forecast to rise. Energy prices are likely to have risen strongly again in September, with our forecast at ~3.7% month-on-month, following increases of 2.90% in August and 2.68% in July. Higher petrol and diesel prices should be the main drivers, while further increases in natural gas prices are also likely to have pushed up household energy tariffs (Figure 1). Despite the downward impact of scheduled fuel tax cuts, current market prices point to a further rise in HICP energy in October, driven particularly by gas. Figure 1: Natural gas likely to have pushed up household energy tariffs in both in September and October Figure 2: Seasonal food inflation expected to accelerate CRE EC Food inflation continued to decelerate in August and is now 1.5pp lower than at the start of the year and more than 2pp below its July 2025 level. We expect the first increase since April in September, driven by an acceleration in seasonal food inflation (Figure 2). Risks to unprocessed food inflation excluding seasonal items remain tilted to the downside however—farm-gate meat prices and meat PPI have continued to ease —while trends in processed food inflation remain subdued. Further ahead, the increases in energy costs and agricultural prices since February point to some reacceleration into 2027, and we expect food inflation to average well above 2% next year. We expect core inflation to rebound after last month’s downside surprise (2.53% year-on-year vs 2.40% in August), with increases in both services and core goods. Core goods inflation has risen this year, from ~0.5% in Q425/Q126 to 1.24% in July, driven primarily by persistent pressure from IT goods (~5% of core goods) and a one-off increase in (German) pharmaceutical prices (~4% of core goods) in July. Excluding these two categories, core goods inflation has shown no clear trend this year (Figure 3), although volatility in clothing may be obscuring emerging upward pressures elsewhere. IT goods price momentum remains strong and marked increases in commodity production costs as well as accelerating PPI (Figure 4) point to a broader pick-up in core goods inflation into 2027. For September, we see particular upside risks in clothing and durables. Figure 7: Market pricing is currently higher than our forecasts over 1-year horizon Figure 8: Market expecting HICPxT to peak H2 2027 Valuations and market view Rising commodity prices and resilient economic data - with data surprise indicators remaining at their highest levels since early 2023 - point to a period of relatively elevated euro area inflation and remain supportive of inflation markets. Our forecasts see core inflation at ~2.7% on average next year, and market implied ex-energy inflation is around 3% in H2 2027 (Figure 9), an upward revision of more than 1pp since February. Valuations incorporate much of the supportive macro backdrop; at the 1y point, market pricing is around 30bp above our HICP forecasts, suggesting some embedded risk premium, while front-end forwards are close to the upper end of this year’s range. We are neutral on EUR inflation for now.
