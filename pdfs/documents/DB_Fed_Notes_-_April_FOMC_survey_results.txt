# Fed Notes - April FOMC survey results

Economics Fed Notes Date 27 April 2026

This note summarizes responses to our pre-FOMC investor survey, which is designed to understand expectations for the meeting and how financial markets and market expectations are likely to respond to various potential outcomes. This edition also included a question on the timing of Warsh’s fi rst FOMC meeting as Fed chair. Responses to this survey were submitted between Tue 4/21 and Thu 4/23. Expectations for policy at this week’s meeting (Figures 1-4)

- Respondents attach an average probability of 87% to no change in the policy rate at this week’s meeting. There is some variation across asset classes and roles, with fixed income investors (9 4%) and those in research (93%) more confident about a hold than other roles.
- A significant majority (75%) expect Chair Powell in his press conference to sound neutral relative to market pricing of the policy rate path through the June meeting. The remainder skew hawkish, with 20% expecting Powell to sound hawkish versus only 5% expecting a dovish tone.
- Respondents were split on whether the FOMC statement would adopt explicit two-sided guidance on future rate decisions, with a slight majority (55%) expecting no inclusion of this language. - In the event of a surprise 25bp cut at this week’s meeting, the probability distribution of the expected year-end fed funds rate shifts significantly, with the PDF-implied mean dropping from 3.4% to 3.0%.
- Headline CPI is expected to be slightly higher over both the near and long term if there were to be a surprise cut. Headline CPI over the next 12 months is expected to be 0.1 ppt higher at 3.1% while longer-run CPI is expected to be 0.2ppt higher at 2.4%.
- There were no differences in the expected year-ahead unemployment rate (4.4% median) nor the probability of a recession (20% median) across the two scenarios. - In the baseline expected scenario of no rate cut, the median expectation is that yields would be flat while the S&P 500 rises 0.3%.
- In the alternative surprise-25bp-cut scenario, the median expectation is for a steeper curve, with the 2y UST yield falling 15bps and the 10y declining by 5bps. In this scenario, the S&P 500 would be expected to rise 1.5%.
- When asked to rate the importance of various channels for the reaction of 10y UST to a surprise cut, respondents saw the Fed’s responsiveness to inflation and growth conditions as the most important factors. - A majority (63%) viewed June as the most likely first meeting with Kevin Warsh as the Fed chair, with July (26%) viewed as the next most likely. Note that responses were collected prior to the announcement that the DOJ investigation into Powell will be dropped, which increases the likelihood of an earlier confirmation and likely clears Warsh’s path to the seat by June. Background on respondents (Figures 12-14)
- There were 20 respondents (though not all answered every question).
- Most respondents are based in the US (40%) or Europe (35%).
- The overwhelming majority have a multi-asset (45%) or fixed-income (40%) focus.
