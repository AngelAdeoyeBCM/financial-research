# USA - GDP Growth Below Consensus Expectations, Partially Reflecting Shutdown Drag; December Core PCE Slightly Above Consensus Expectations

20 February 2026 | 12:15PM EST BOTTOM LINE: Real GDP rose 1.4% annualized in the fourth quarter, below consensus expectations but partially reflecting a 1.15pp drag from federal government spending as a result of the shutdown. The details of the Q4 GDP report were close to our expectations, though somewhat weaker inventory accumulation in Q4 has positive implications for growth in Q1. We will launch our Q1 GDP tracking estimate after assessing the impact of the Supreme Court’s tariff ruling on our forecast. Core PCE increased 0.36% month-over-month and 3.0% year-over-year in December, slightly above consensus expectations. Both the S&P manufacturing and S&P services PMIs declined in February, somewhat below expectations. New home sales increased by more than expected in November and December. 1. Real GDP rose 1.4% annualized in the fourth quarter, below consensus expectations but partially reflecting a 1.15pp drag from federal government spending as a result of the shutdown that will turn into a boost of similar magnitude in 2026Q1. Real consumer spending and private domestic final sales both increased 2.4% in Q4. Business fixed investment increased 3.7% annualized, reflecting strong growth in intellectual property products (+7.4%) and equipment (+3.2%) investment but a drag from structures investment (-2.4%). Housing investment declined 1.6%. Net trade boosted GDP growth by 0.1pp, and inventory accumulation boosted growth by 0.2pp in Q4. Inventory accumulation contributed somewhat less to Q4 GDP growth than we expected (0.2pp vs. our expectation of 0.3pp), which has positive implications for Q1 growth. Real GDP increased 2.2% on a Q4/Q4 basis in 2025.
2. Personal spending increased 0.4% in December, somewhat above expectations. Real personal spending increased 0.1%, reflecting a 0.5% decline in real goods spending but a 0.3% increase in real services spending. Personal income increased by 0.3% in December, in line with expectations. The saving rate edged down to 3.6% in December from an upwardly-revised rate in November (+0.2pp to 3.7%).
3. The core PCE price index rose 0.36% month-over-month in December, and the year-over-year rate increased to 3.0%, slightly above consensus expectations. Core PCE was revised up by 1bp in November and 2bp in October. Headline prices rose 0.36% month-over-month and 2.90% year-over-year. Both market-based core PCE and core services PCE excluding housing rose 0.34%. The GDP price index rose by +3.6% in Q4 (quarter-over-quarter annualized), above expectations and reflecting an acceleration in the consumption (+2.9%), imports (+2.3%), and government spending (+7.2%) deflators.
4. The Supreme Court this morning struck down the Trump administration’s tariffs imposed under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA). We will launch our Q1 GDP tracking estimate after assessing the impact of the Supreme Court ruling on our component-level forecasts.
5. The S&P Global US services PMI edged down by 0.4pt to 52.3 in February, slightly below consensus expectations. The underlying composition was weak, as both the new business (-0.5pt to 52.7) and employment (-0.3pt to 50.2) components decreased. Both the input prices (+0.5pt to 60.2) and output prices (+1.6pt to 58.3) components increased. The future output index increased by 4.0pt to 66.8 (NSA), its highest level since January 2025.
6. The S&P Global US manufacturing PMI declined by 1.3pt to 51.2 in February, below consensus expectations. The underlying composition was weak, as the output (-2.9pt to 52.3), employment (-1.2pt to 50.2) and new orders (-2.3pt to 49.6) components all decreased. Both the input prices (-0.4pt to 61.9) and output prices (-3.2pt to 55.3) components declined. The future output index increased by 1.4pt to 69.7 (NSA), its highest level since June 2025.
7. Sales of new single-family homes rose by 6.9% on average between November and December to a seasonally-adjusted annualized level of 745k units, somewhat above expectations. New home sales were revised down by 81k to 656k in October. On average across November and December, new home sales were flat in the Northeast but increased in the West (+25.4%), Midwest (+14.7%), and South (+1.7%).
