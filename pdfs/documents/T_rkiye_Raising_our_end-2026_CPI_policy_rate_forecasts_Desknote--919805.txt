# T rkiye Raising our end-2026 CPI policy rate forecasts Desknote

FOCUS 03/03/2026 1 Türkiye: Raising our end-2026 CPI, policy rate forecasts Yasemin Basyigit, Türkiye Economist | Türk Ekonomi Bankasi A.S. | Jeffrey Schultz, Head of CEEMEA Economics | BNP Paribas London Branch. Note: BNPP estimates on seasonally adjusted figures Source: Macrobond, BNP Paribas Fig. 1: Seasonally adjusted CPI run rate – elevated in headline, more encouraging in core Revising up our end 2026 CPI estimate: Persistent strength in food prices and energy-related shocks prompt us to raise our forecast for end-2026 CPI inflation from 23.0% to 24.0% and our year-end policy rate forecast from 28.0% to 30.0%. We think the CBRT may need to keep monetary conditions tighter for longer to anchor expectations and offset the impact from the food and energy sectors. Encouraging core momentum: The February headline CPI was up 2.96% m/m versus market expectations of 3.0%, but the y/y figure saw its first increase since 2023, rising from 30.7% in January to 31.5%, primarily on unfavourable base effects. Food inflation was again the primary driver, and at 36.5% y/y was well above the CBRT year-end target of 19%. Clothing and footwear provided some seasonal relief, but transport and food pushed headline CPI higher. Annual core inflation eased from 29.8% to 29.5%, with the monthly pace slowing sharply to 1.53% from 4.55% in January. Seasonally adjusted figures (due Wednesday) will likely show a stable run-rate for core CPI around a 2% m/m pace. Services showed signs of deceleration: annual services inflation slipped to 39.7% and monthly rent growth eased to 3.5% from 5.3%, suggesting some impact from (tight) monetary policy measures. But large energy price risks in the pipeline on geopolitical flare-ups: Energy inflation eased to 28.2% in February from 30.2% but is vulnerable to upside risks from rising geopolitical tensions. We think the government may use state-owned utilities or tax adjustments, to absorb wholesale energy price spikes and limit pass-through to households. The CBRT estimates a 10% jump in energy prices may add a full percentage point to headline CPI (with almost half materialising in the first quarter of the shock). Second-round effects on food, and via a weaker currency, could push the impact north of 2pp over the coming months based on current oil and gas prices. A recent CBRT inflation report assumed a 2026 average Brent price at USD60.9/bbl, implying upside risk to its forecasts (see a recent CBRT blog for more).

## KEY MESSAGES

We revise our 2026 year-end CPI inflation forecast for Türkiye from 23% to 24% and lift our year-end policy rate forecast to 30% from 28% previously. We expect the central bank to keep its policy rate unchanged at the March MPC meeting with growing risks from energy prices and elevated food inflation remaining a concern for now. We expect the CBRT to adopt a 'wait-and-see' approach to the April meeting with any rate cuts dependent on FX movements and global risks. Inflation data: February's headline CPI was reported at 2.96% m/m, largely in line with market expectations of 3.0%. However, the annual figure experienced its first increase since 2023, rising from 30.7% to 31.5%, primarily due to unfavourable base effects. The food group remained the primary driver, contributing a notable 1.7pp to the monthly headline figure. With monthly food inflation exceeding 6% for two consecutive months, annual food inflation has increased to 36.5%, surpassing the CBRT's year-end target of 19%. Although the clothing and footwear group provided some seasonal relief, the momentum in transport and food prices continues to exert upward pressure on the headline figure. In contrast to the volatile headline data, the Core CPI (C-based) figure has demonstrated greater stability and is exhibiting a downward trend. Annual core inflation eased from 29.8% to 29.5%, with the monthly pace slowing significantly to 1.53% from 4.55% in January. Seasonally adjusted figures (to be released tomorrow) will likely show a stable run rate for core CPI around 2% monthly pace.The services sector is showing signs of deceleration, with annual services inflation slipping to 39.7% and monthly rent growth easing to 3.5% from 5.3%, suggesting that the high inflation environment is beginning to feel the impact of current monetary policy. Risks from energy prices: Energy inflation moderated to 28.2% in February from 30.2% but remains vulnerable to upside risks from escalating geopolitical tensions. The government may rely on state-owned utilities such as BOTAS and TEDAS or on tax adjustments to absorb wholesale energy price spikes and limit the pass-through to household electricity and gas bills, helping to contain overall inflation pressures. The central bank is estimating that a 10% jump in energy prices can push headline CPI around a full percentage point higher (with almost half of this jump materialising within the first quarter of the shock). Adding in second round effects of higher energy into food and via a weaker currency, the impact could be north of 2pp to headline CPI over the coming months assuming the current level of oil and gas prices. CBRT's recent inflation report assumed 2026 average Brent price at $60.9, implying upside risk to its forecasts as well (see recent CBRT blog here). March MPC decision: In light of rising annual inflation and heightened geopolitical uncertainty, we anticipate that CBRT will adopt a cautious approach. At the MPC meeting on 12 March, we think the policy rate will remain unchanged at 37%. The bank will remain in a 'wait-and-see' approach into the 22 April meeting and the outlook for cuts will depend on how the exchange rate and global risks evolve in the interim period. Reflecting the persistent strength in food prices and energy-related shocks, we have updated 2026 projections. We revise our year-end CPI forecast from 23.0% to 24.0% and 2026 year-end policy rate forecast from 28.0% to 30.0%.We think CBRT may need to keep monetary conditions higher for longer to anchor expectations and offset the deviations in the food and energy sectors. At the 12 March MPC meeting, we expect it to hold the policy rate at 37%, then take a 'wait-and-see' approach into the 22 April meeting, where the outlook for cuts will depend on how FX and global risks evolve in the interim.
