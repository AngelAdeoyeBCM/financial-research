# Canada Watch - Trump warns 100per Canada tariffs; impact limited, USDCAD likely higher

Canada Watch Trump warns 100% Canada tariffs; impact limited, USDCAD likely higher Tariff warning and political context In a post on Truth Social on Saturday January 24, US President Donald Trump warned to impose “a 100% tariff against all Canadian goods and products coming into the U.S.A.” if Canada makes a deal with China. He added that “If Governor Carney thinks he is going to make Canada a ‘Drop Off Port’ for China to send goods and products into the United States, he is sorely mistaken.” Canada–China agreement and likely responsive motive Trump’s remarks appear to be a response to the recent rapprochement between Canada and China and Prime Minister Mark Carney’s high‐profile Davos speech arguing that the rules‐based trade order is facing a rupture, not a transition. Canada has already reached a preliminary agreement with China that allows up to 49,000 Chinese EVs at a 6.1% MFN tariff—down from 100% —while China will reduce tariffs on Canadian canola to about 15% and remove duties on several agricultural products (see report, Diversifying to negotiate). Implementation unclear and the role of USMCA It remains unclear how a blanket 100% tariff would be implemented. If imposed under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA), the measure could be subject to judicial review. It is also uncertain whether such a tariff would apply to goods compliant with USMCA rules of origin. The current 35% blanket tariff applies only to non‐USMCA‐compliant goods, limiting the effective tariff rate to roughly 5%. If a 100% tariff followed the same structure, we think the trade impact could be limited. USMCA review: two interpretations The implications for the July 1 USMCA review are mixed. One view is that USMCA is not functioning as intended given existing US tariffs on Canada and Mexico. Another view is that the agreement is functioning as designed because most qualifying trade still faces zero tariffs. In this sense, USMCA appears both alive and dead. Our baseline is that USMCA survives the review in its current “alive/dead” state, likely with more frequent future reviews. Even so, risks of a breakdown have risen at the margin. US Senate pushback on Canada tariffs Recall that the US Senate blocked Trump‐era tariff orders on Canada twice in 2025— once in April, just after Liberation Day, and again in October following a dispute over TV commercials. These actions could be interpreted as tariffs on Canada being unpopular among Republican senators, in particular in an electoral year. Limited impact for USDCAD despite short-term upside Markets are already alert to upside risks for USDCAD following Carney’s Davos remarks, and earlier this month we noted that topside USDCAD calls offered a cost‐effective tail‐ risk hedge (see report, Overlooked FX tail risk hedges).
