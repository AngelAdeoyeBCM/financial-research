# Global FX Strategy USD bulls could face a challenging week

## CITI’S TAKE

Despite a strong USD rally this week, we see risks for next week should drone launch counts from Iran start to fall. This could then lead market participants to re-focus on the weak US labor market report. USD reaction to CPI, though, is still likely to be conditioned on the geopolitical backdrop. While the labor market issue is a temporary headwind to our bearish EURUSD view, some big picture developments do reinforce our conviction. n | We remain USD bulls into summer; however, we do not want to get too excited about recent dollar strength. This USD rally was driven by geopolitical events rather than cyclical outperformance, which makes it susceptible to a reversal on US-Iran de-escalation. While timing there remains uncertain, a scenario is possible where Iran drone capability is degraded quickly. Citi Commodities see $80-90/bbl Brent for at least the next 1-2 weeks, before seeing prices moderate in 2Q. This reflects heavily disrupted flows for the next 1-2 weeks before flows gradually return in the latter part of March (Global Commodities - Energy market outlook: Strait of Hormuz closure - when a wildcard becomes the reality). In that context, the amount of Iranian drone launches needs to be watched. While the US and Israel have largely degraded Iran’s missile capabilities, its drones remain a threat (WSJ March 5, 2026). This is reflected in daily UAE data on such launches (Figure 1), which show missile attacks have fallen sharply while drone launches have plateaued. Secretary Hegseth and General Caine noted in a recent briefing that they are taking greater control of airspace, and will be accelerating the pace of attacks as well as the method, from “stand-off munitions to stand-in munitions” (Department of War). Should we see drone launches start to fall, it could be the first signal we are getting closer to the point of Hormuz opening. We watch this data closely as a leading indicator, and it would not surprise us if there is already some progress by Monday. However, if that is correct, it could see a reversal of recent USD strength. It should also see NOKSEK lower, for which we went short earlier this week (current spot ref 0.9579 at 5pm ET 6 March 2026). And if we were to see such a development, it could lead to market participants to re-focus on the very weak US labor market report. We have been – and remain of the view – that US growth re-acceleration in H1 could drive USD strength. However, the latest data did not support our view. It is only one data print – albeit quite a surprising one – but it is not enough to change our view especially as we still see positive aspects in leading indicators such as Challenger Job Cut Announcements (which suggests future layoffs remain low; Figure 2) and ISM Services and Manufacturing Employment which points to improving business sentiment around hiring (Figure 3). Still, this was not helpful to our view and further labor market reports consistent with the latest will significantly limit the ability for a bigger USD rally (though USD could still stay supported while inflation expectations remain elevated given the challenge this presents to the Fed). Any asymmetries for USD are likely conditional on the backdrop. In other words, if geopolitical tensions are increasing with spiking oil prices and a higher USD, then a weak CPI will not do much to impact dollar strength while a strong CPI could accelerate dollar buying. If, on the other hand, geopolitical tensions are easing and the USD is selling off, then a strong CPI is unlikely to have material affect while a weak CPI could see the USD sell-off accelerate. Bigger picture, we do see two implications. First, as we have been flagging, USD/SPX correlations are normalizing (back to negative; Figure 4). A structural headwind for the USD is that uncertainty around the USD “safe haven” status could lead foreign equity investors to increase their FX hedge ratios (sell USD); however, quantitatively, the correlation breakdown appeared to be temporary rather than a structural shift. While this relationship continues to normalize, it could weaken the case for an increase in FX hedge ratio adjustments. Second, this overall situation is a reminder that Europe remains vulnerable given its external reliance on energy and underinvestment in defense. In world of increasing fragmentation and greater geopolitical tensions, we would be cautious on regions lacking self-reliance. These keep us bearish EURUSD over the coming months. Figure 4.
