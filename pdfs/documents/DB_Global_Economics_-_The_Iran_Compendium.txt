# Global Economics - The Iran Compendium

13 March 2026 Deutsche Bank Research Global Middle East Economics Global Economics Date With the conflict in the Middle East following strikes against Iran about to enter its second week, in this note we've compiled the most topical research pieces from DB's macro & thematic research teams, covering the economic, geopolitical and market implications of the crisis. The situation remains volatile. Geopolitically, we assess that the Iran crisis will likely worsen before improving, although de-escalation remains plausible at some point over the next few weeks. The oil disruption represents a stagflationary supply shock for the global economy, but the exact nature and extent of impact will vary considerably across countries. Asia is more exposed to potential disruption given its reliance on oil from the Middle East, though for China risks are mitigated by a diversified energy mix and substantial oil reserves. Other EM countries, including across CEEMEA, will also be exposed to the stagflationary effects. Unlike 2022, Europe is set to mostly see a price shock. This will also weigh on growth but with the extent of the drag mitigated by improved energy system resilience. As a net energy exporter, the US is more shielded from the negative growth effects, but is more exposed than many DM countries to the near-term spike in inflation. Rates markets have seen a significant hawkish repricing of central bank expectations. With several major central bank meetings next week (including Fed, ECB, BoJ and BoE) we expect most central banks to adopt a wait-and-see approach in the near term. Rate cuts are likely to be delayed for those still in their easing cycle (BoE) and potentially also for those now hiking (BoJ). Topical Deutsche Bank Economic & Thematic Research publications on Iran crisis US economics Fed Notes: Could the Fed hike rates in 2026? (March 13) Despite recent hawkish repricing, we think a 2026 Fed rate hike is unlikely. We see this requiring a re-tightening labor market, core PCE noticeably above 3%, and rising long-run inflation expectations. US Economic Perspectives: How big of an oil shock can the Big Beautiful Bill handle? (March 10) We find that while the OBBBA's tax cuts would still exceed the impact of sustained $100/bbl oil on US consumers, $150/bbl oil would more than wipe out these benefits, severely threatening consumer spending. Fed Notes: What does history tell us about the Fed's response to oil price shocks? (March 3) History shows the Fed's response to oil price shocks depends on the economic context. With inflation high, the Fed has focused on inflation; otherwise, it has prioritized growth. US Economic Perspectives: Impact of oil price shocks a "frack"-tion of what they once were (Mar 2) Due to US shale production, the economic impact of an oil shock is now more balanced. Fed models suggest even a large shock would have only a modest impact on growth and core inflation, but the main risk is unanchored inflation expectations, reinforcing a wait-and-see Fed approach for now. Euro area Focus Europe: Iran shock and the case for fiscal intervention: 2022 vs 2026 (March 12) Despite parallels to the 2022 energy crisis, a similar large-scale fiscal intervention today is unlikely without a significant deterioration in the outlook. Public finances are weaker and growth is slower, meaning intervention would only occur if the economy faces a severe recessionary shock. Focus Europe: ECB Preview: Contingency, jeopardy and a commitment to price stability (March 12) The ECB will likely hold policy steady at its upcoming meeting, focusing instead on its communications. We expect a strong commitment to price stability to anchor inflation expectations amidst the uncertainty and jeopardy created by the energy shock. Focus Europe: Jeopardy: Energy prices and the fragility of inflation expectations (March 13) After the recent period of above target inflation, inflation expectations are likely to be more fragile and sensitive to energy price shocks. Our analysis of the 2008 and 2022 crises shows a strong correlation between energy prices and changes in inflation expectations, suggesting energy poses a de-anchoring risk that the ECB will monitor closely. Focus Europe: Iran Shock: A macro impact scenario analysis (March 6) We present two scenarios for energy prices. Under the moderate scenario ($80/bbl and gas at EUR50/MWh) that the ECB could 'look through' the shock. A more adverse scenario ($120/bbl and gas at EUR75/MWh) could trigger a recession and persistent inflation overshoot, forcing a policy response. Focus Germany: Cutting our growth forecast (March 11) We have cut our forecast for Germany's 2026 growth from 1.5% to 1.0% as the Iran war's energy price surge and geopolitical uncertainty will meaningfully delay recovery, impacting trade, consumption, and competitiveness. Germany Schnappschuss: Oil price shock likely to cause little damage to industry (March 2) We assess that despite recent oil price hikes from military strikes, German industry remains largely insulated from significant damage due to reduced oil dependency. Other DM UK economic notes: BoE: What next? Policy Path In Flux (March 10) Middle East events have clouded the BoE's policy path, making the UK's disinflation trajectory highly uncertain. We shift our rate cut call to June, contingent on war resolution or labor market deterioration. UK Weekly Digest: What the Middle East War Means for the UK Economy? (March 6) Middle East events introduce UK economic uncertainty. We assess the impact of potential scenarios on GDP, inflation, and unemployment. A negative scenario could take 0.75pp off GDP. Japan Economic Notes: Impact of rising crude oil and LNG prices on the Japanese economy (March 3) Rising oil/LNG prices from Middle East tensions will hit Japan's GDP, with limited, transient CPI impact. This cost-push pressure complicates BOJ rate hikes, likely prompting a "wait-and-see" approach. Australia Macro: Iran Conflict: Positive terms of trade shock sets Australia apart from peers (March 3) A sustained spike in energy prices would boost Australia's terms of trade and therefore national income. But because this boosts the fiscal position, it matters less for monetary policy. Emerging Markets Asia Macro Insight: All about the Strait of Hormuz (March 10) Asia's high reliance on Gulf energy via the Strait of Hormuz creates significant stagflation risk. South Korea, Taiwan and Thailand are particularly exposed; central bank responses will depend on oil price severity and currency impacts. China Macro: Iran Shock and China (March 3) While China's economy is exposed to oil from the Middle East, minimal oil-reliant power generation and substantial strategic reserves should limit the impact of the oil supply shock. We expect reflationary effects on CPI/PPI. India Economics Weekly: Special Report: India macro outlook in a 100 Dollar oil scenario (March 12) We expect that elevated oil prices will raise India's FY27 CPI to 4.55%, widen the current account deficit, and pressure the rupee. This creates fiscal risks, but we see no rate hikes due to potential growth shocks. Insight CEEMEA: Navigating the Iran Shock (March 11) The geopolitical and energy shock creates significant uncertainty for the CEEMEA region. While initial conditions differ from 2022, rising oil prices imply stagflationary pressures and tighter policy. A $150/bl oil scenario would mean a 1% growth hit and 1.4% inflation surge. Middle East Pulse: Middle East: What's at stake? (March 11) We assess the implications of recent Middle East conflicts, highlighting varied impacts on the oil sector across Gulf states due to attacks and re-routing capabilities. Non-oil sectors like tourism and logistics also face risks, with overall effects depending on conflict duration and escalation. Thematic & Oil The House View: The Impact of the Middle East (March 10) Middle East tensions risk higher Euro area inflation and potential ECB hikes. The US is less exposed, while China could see reflation. Asia faces significant oil supply disruption risks. Thematic Research: Iran Crisis: From Despair to Optimism and Back Again (March 12) We judge that the Iran crisis will likely worsen before improving, with near-term escalation leading to ongoing market volatility and a more unpredictable region with persistent threats to navigation, although de-escalation remains plausible at some point over the next few weeks. 13 March 2026 Page 4 Deutsche Bank Securities Inc. Thematic Research: The oil shocks of recent decades: A reference guide (March 12) We review historical oil shocks – 1970s, 1990, 2022 – which caused recessions, inflation spikes, and hawkish central bank action, and consider how their lessons are relevant today. Capital Markets Blog: Markets and geopolitical risk: debunking some myths (March 9) Contrary to common myths, markets are not immune to geopolitical risk. Historical data shows S&P 500 returns vary enormously by the nature of the shock, with different countries reacting differently. Thematic Research: Iran: its politics, economy, and impact (March 4) In this Iran 101 chartbook, we present a brief overview of Iran's politics, history, military, economy, and energy assets. We also highlight the key issues at play for the global economy, including the dependence of different economies on oil and gas and potential impacts on inflation and GDP growth. Hsueh On Oil: Nothing else matters (March 9) In moving beyond $100/bbl, the oil market is pricing in a more than 1-month disruption at the Strait of Hormuz. Nothing matters as much as when the Strait can be safely reopened; policy responses like SPR releases are temporary palliatives. FX Special Report: Don't forget geography (March 11) Middle East oil market risks are asymmetric. While a price shock for the West, a Hormuz closure could create an output shock for Asia, given its high energy import reliance. Energy independence adds safe-haven premium on the USD for now. Thematic Research: The threshold for an Oil-Driven risk-off move: A Historical Guide (March 3) We find that geopolitical events impact markets significantly only if they trigger substantial, sustained oil price spikes (50-100%), cause broader macro damage, or force hawkish central bank responses.
