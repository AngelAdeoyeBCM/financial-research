Today’s Q3 CPI print was in line with consensus, rising 1.0% q/q (unrounded: 0.997%), while the monthly trimmed mean rose 0.26%, also meeting expectations. This print aligns with both market expectations and the RBA’s own outlook, as explicitly noted by the Deputy Governor during this morning’s Senate Hearing. We have been received in the RBA’s November meeting, predicated on our view that back-to-back rate hikes remain unlikely, a view we continue to hold (see AUD rates: Receive Nov RBA as back-to-back rate hikes appear unlikely, dated 28 October). This view was based on

1. mixed domestic economic developments, specifically softening in the labor and housing markets alongside depressed consumer sentiment;
2. a softer inflation trajectory following the July upside surprise – with m/m trimmed mean rising at an annualized rate of 3% over August and September; and
3. the merit in observing the transmission of previous rate rises, which are still flowing through the economy – all reasons that continue to hold after today’s CPI print. A key difference between our view and the market lies in our interpretation of the RBA’s hawkish stance. We believe the RBA is maintaining its hawkish rhetoric to keep financial conditions tight – avoiding a repeat of last year’s premature easing. In an environment where energy prices remain elevated and domestic productivity growth remains sluggish, we expect this hawkish bias to persist, irrespective of the actual timing of the next rate hike. Consequently, we have also maintained a paid position in the Dec/Feb RBA spread, as the RBA’s hawkish stance will keep the terminal rate debate “live” and hedge against upside data surprises. Should the RBA surprise markets with a “hold” at its November meeting, (similar to the RBNZ’s surprise hold today), we expect the decision to be unanimous. A non-unanimous hold would risk appearing uncredible or overly dovish, inadvertently loosening financial conditions. Therefore, we anticipate the RBA will use its press conference to frame any disagreement as a matter of “timing” or “different weights placed on mixed economic signals” rather than a change in the policy path, thereby preventing a rally in front-end AUD rates and FX weakness. For markets participants who hold a different view and interpret the RBA’s hawkish comments as signal for an immediate hike at the next meeting, today’s CPI and hawkish comments at the Senate Hearing would have likely served as confirmation that November hike is a done deal. Consequently, November RBA has now moved to 22.5bp priced at the time of writing. We, however, believe this move has improved the risk-reward for receiving this meeting. Therefore, we are increasing our position in November RBA receivers from USD 15k/01 to a total allocation of USD 30k/01. Trade idea: Receiver another USD15k/01 in November RBA at 4.82% for a total allocation of USD30k/01. Target: 4.60%. Stop: 4.85%.
