# US Economic Perspectives - April Beige Book - costs of Middle East conflicts adding to uncertainty

Economics US Economic Perspectives Date 16 April 2026

We have previously leveraged our proprietary LLM tools to analyze the Beige Book, assessing economic sentiment across key macroeconomic variables: growth, the labor market, inflation, and perceived recession probabilities (see From qualitative to quantitative: decoding Beige Book with AI). The derived sentiment scores were able to capture economic dynamics over the past decade. In this piece we continued this exercise and updated it to the latest Beige Book from April 2026. As a reminder, the score is based on a scale of 0 to 10, a score of 0 is associated with a lower expectation for the future, and a score of 10 is associated with higher expectation. A score of 5 is neutral expectation (see Appendix for prompt for full details). Note that for the last topic on recession, a score of 0 would suggest that the recession is not expected in next 12 months, while a score of 10 would suggest that the recession is nearly certain in next 12 months. Our data spans from January 2015 to the latest Beige Book in April 2026. While the latest Beige Book continued to reflect a stable labor market as observed in February, it featured a substantial discussion about the economic impact of the conflicts in the Middle East, presenting challenges to the growth outlook and upside risks to the inflation (see Figure 1-4). The labor market score remained steady at 5, at neutral and consistent with a low (Figure 5). While the inflation sentiment score also stayed unchanged from February at an elevated level of 7, the most notable difference is the sharp rise in energy and fuel costs cited by all Districts in the April report, directly attributed to the Middle East conflicts. This points to more pronounced and widespread input cost pressures than th e general "rising nonlabor input costs" and tariff concerns mentioned in the February report. The April report also explicitly states input cost increases outpaced selling price growth, squeezing margins. unemployment of 4.3% and a stabilizing pace of payroll gains over recent months The growth sentiment score remained strong at 6 and the April report indicates a slightly broader base of modest growth (eight districts) compared to February (seven districts). However, the latest report also highlighted the Middle East conflicts as a new, major source of uncertainty dampening business decision - making across various economic aspects including hiring, pricing and investment. Energy producers, in particular, are exercising caution regarding increased drilling due to the uncertain pe rsistence of higher oil prices. These factors collectively contribute to downside risks for growth and led to a small increase in the recession score to 4 from 3 in February. This score on average corresponds to an estimated 10% recession probability, consistent wi th our model-based estimates (see A post mortem on recession probability models). Matthew Luzzetti, Ph.D. Overall, the latest scores for the April Beige Book continue to depict a strong economy with a balanced labor market and still elevated inflation. However, the report offered initial indications of the potential economic consequences stemming from the Middle East conflicts, highlighting intensifying and widespread cost pressures, alongside slower growth attributed to heightened uncertainty. As businesses adopt a cautious approach to pricing and investment decisions, the ultimate economic impact hinges on the duration of the energy shock, a point emphasized by many Fed officials in their recent communications. This situation further reinforces the Fed's inclination to maintain its current stance until concrete evidence emerges necessitating further policy adjustments, especially given that current policy might not be as restrictive as initially anticipated (see Is the Fed r(*)estrictive?). Indeed, we have made relatively modest adjustments to our forecast with slightly weaker growth and modestly higher inflation, seeing the Fed on an indefinite hold near neutral (see Outlook update: The (oil) drumbeat of war).
