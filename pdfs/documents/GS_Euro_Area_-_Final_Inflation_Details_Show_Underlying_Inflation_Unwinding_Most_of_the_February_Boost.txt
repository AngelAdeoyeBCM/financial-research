# Euro Area: Final Inflation Details Show Underlying Inflation Unwinding Most of the February Boost

17 April 2026 | 10:08AM BST BOTTOM LINE: The final release of inflation for March showed headline inflation at 2.55%yoy, revised up from the flash reading of 2.52%yoy, and core inflation at 2.29%yoy, up 3bp from the flash print. Services inflation remains the main driver of headline inflation but its contribution went down to 1.49pp in March, while the contribution from Energy moved up to 0.48pp, its highest reading in more than 3 years. Our summary indicator of sequential underlying inflation ticked down in March to 0.19%mom after increasing notably in February, partly unwinding the Olympics-induced tourism- and hospitality-related boost. The change in our sequential summary measure reflects a sequential deceleration across most of the metrics we track, with the biggest moves in the supercore (-0.26pp), HICPXX (-0.16pp) and domestic (-0.13pp) measures. We continue to see year-over-year core inflation at 2.4%yoy in 2026, peaking at 2.5%yoy in Q3 and then falling to 2.4%yoy by end-2026 and to 2.2% by end-2027. As for headline inflation, we see it averaging 2.9%yoy in 2026, peaking at 3.2%yoy in Q2, and at 2.0%yoy in 2027. The risks, however, remain to the upside. KEY NUMBERS: Core HICP (Mar, Final): 2.29% vs. Flash 2.26%, last 2.41%, all %yoy. Headline HICP (Mar, Final): 2.55% vs. Flash 2.52%, last 1.89%, all %yoy. MAIN POINTS:

1. The final release of inflation for March showed headline inflation at 2.55%yoy, revised up from the flash reading of 2.52%yoy, and core inflation at 2.29%yoy, up 3bp from the flash print. Energy inflation was revised up to 5.1%yoy from 4.9%yoy reported in the flash print. Using our seasonal adjustment methodology, aimed at closely replicating the ECB’s, and removing the Easter adjustment for the whole services basket, we estimate that seasonally adjusted sequential core inflation was 0.08%mom in March, down notably from 0.33%mom in the February reading, close to the ECB’s estimate of 0.09%mom. Within core inflation, we estimate that seasonally adjusted sequential core goods inflation went down to -0.10%mom in March, while sequential services inflation declined to 0.18%mom. The main positive contributions to the seasonally adjusted sequential pace in March came from catering and airfares, while accommodation services and garments and footwear provided the most notable negative offset.
2. Services inflation remains the main driver of headline inflation but its contribution went down to 1.49pp in March. This could be attributed, in part, to hospitality-related services (restaurants and accommodation), miscellaneous Economics Research services, insurance and financial services and health and leisure services (recreational services) still contributing more than their historical averages (Exhibit 1). The contribution from NEIG went down to 0.13pp in March. The contribution of Food, Alcohol and Tobacco (FAT) went down to 0.45pp, while the Energy contribution moved up to 0.48pp, its highest reading in more than 3 years. A notable increase in the fuels component (0.53pp) brought the transport contribution up to its highest level since February 2023.
3. Our summary indicator of sequential underlying inflation has been broadly stable over the past quarters, but ticked down in March by 8bp to 0.19%mom after increasing notably in February, partly unwinding the Olympics-induced tourism- and hospitality-related boost. The change in our sequential summary measure reflects a sequential deceleration across most of the metrics we track, including HICPXX, domestic, weighted median, supercore, 25% trimmed mean, persistence-weighted and our proxy for the ECB’s PCCI measures. At the same time, wage-sensitive measures ticked up slightly on the month (Exhibit 1). The biggest moves were recorded in the supercore (-0.26pp), HICPXX (-0.16pp) and domestic (-0.13pp) measures.
4. Updating our medium-term inflation infrastructure with the final details, another month of underlying summary measure, our medium-term path continues to show core inflation at 2.4%yoy in 2026, peaking at 2.5%yoy in Q3 and then falling to 2.4%yoy by end-2026 and to 2.2% by end-2027. The path now looks somewhat firmer on a stronger-than-expected underlying pace and an upside revision in the final data, driven mostly by core goods. As for headline inflation, we continue to see it notably above target this year. We see it averaging 2.9%yoy in 2026, peaking at 3.2%yoy in Q2, and at 2.1%yoy in 2027, using our commodities team’s latest baseline path for gas and oil prices. The risks, however, remain to the upside.
5. Eurostat will publish the April flash HICP release on Thursday, 30 April.
