# UK Economics February GDP: strong, but overstated

## CITI’S TAKE

February’s GDP print was stronger than expected, but the headline figures masks a weaker underlying picture. The upside that we saw better reflected a rebound in some of the more volatile services components, which itself appeared to a form of mean reversion rather than ‘new’ growth, and weather-sensitive elements within industrial production. While this is a large improvement on the January figures, momentum to us looks uneven and fragile with a still-notable underperformance in manufacturing, which remains exposed to headwinds in the final print of the quarter. Headline strength masks mixed underlying momentum. GDP grew by 0.5% m/m in February, well above the 0.1% consensus expectation and marking a sharp reversal from January’s stagnation. The upside surprise was driven almost entirely by services output, which also rose 0.5% m/m. Beneath the headline, however, part of this strength reflects a snap-back in employment services following an unusually large contraction the prior month. Employment activities rebounded by 2.5% m/m after falling 6.6% in January, contributing a material share of overall services growth. The scale of January’s decline here was difficult to reconcile with labour-market indicators and we think likely reflected data capture or timing effects rather than a genuine, sudden deterioration in hiring conditions. February’s rebound therefore only partially unwinds that earlier weakness and looks more like mean reversion in our view rather than an acceleration that signals either resurgent labour demand or near-term momentum. That said, services do look robust outside of this idiosyncrasy. Twelve of the fourteen subsectors expanded in February, which is a meaningful improvement in breadth relative to January, with multiple categories pointing to a more stable underlying services backdrop that was suggested by last month’s data. That said, some of the most cyclically informative consumer-facing segments remain uneven so while it’s difficult to argue that services are stalling outright, momentum does look fragile. Industrial production boosted by volatility, not cycle. Industrial production also rose in February, providing a secondary lift to headline GDP. However, this increase was driven primarily by energy-related components, notably electricity, gas, and mining, which are inherently volatile being sensitive to weather conditions and commodity price fluctuations. As such, these gains offer limited information about the medium-term industrial cycle and should be treated with caution when extrapolating forward. Significantly, manufacturing provided no underlying support to growth. Output contracted on the month, continuing the pattern of subdued and uneven performance seen since late 2025. This weakness is notable given that it predates the commodity price shock set to feed into the data in coming months, suggesting that the sector remains exposed to rising cost pressures and soft demand conditions. While rolling three-month measures still benefit from base effects related to earlier disruptions in motor vehicle production, the monthly profile reinforces the view that manufacturing is under-performing the rest of the economy even going into the energy shock. A strong print, but an overstated one. Taken together, we believe February’s data points to an economy that is performing better than feared but not as strongly as the headline suggests. The 0.5% m/m GDP gain reflects a combination of mean reversion in volatile services components and energy-driven strength in production, neither of which provides a firm foundation for sustained momentum. Underlying growth appears positive but modest, and the balance of risks remains tilted toward renewed softness as higher energy prices and lingering weakness in manufacturing feed through. As such, while February represents a welcome improvement relative to January, the strength of the print should be treated with caution – particularly ahead of the March data, which will be more informative about whether services momentum is genuinely durable or once again distorted by volatility.
