# Fixed Income Blog - The term premium is at a decade high. But is it too high

Rates Fixed Income Blog Date 30 March 2026

No. Some commentators have argued that the UST10Y is cheap because measures of term premia are high relative to recent history. It is factually correct that term premia are close to a decade high. The DB term premium (5s10s slope + 20% short in 2s) has indeed risen from below 50bp in 2022, when we argued that term premia were likely to be on a secular uptrend, to above 100bp today. But that does not mean that the term premium is too high. Indeed, in an environment with persistent negative supply shocks and structurally easier fiscal policy, term premia should increase. During negative supply shocks, bonds are not good hedges for equities as higher inflation is bad for both bonds and equities. As the hedging properties are eroded, bonds should have a higher risk premium. Separately, significant upward pressure on deficits will reduce excess savings and thereby put upward pressure on term premia and equilibrium rates. Bottom line: in an environment where the negative supply shock to oil is persistent and there is very significant latent demand for fiscal policy, it remains more likely that term premia will rise than decline from current levels. For reference, the DB term premium was 25-100 bp higher in the arguably more benign environment of the Arab Spring in 2011 and the First Gulf War in the early 90s.
