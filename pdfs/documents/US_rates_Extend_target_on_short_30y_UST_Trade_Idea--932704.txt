# US rates: Extend target on short 30y UST

## OUR KEY VIEWS

- Strong economic data, higher energy prices, and weak demand for Treasury debt have contributed to further upward pressure on long-end rates.
- Fiscal concerns and CTD switch risk still pose significant risks to bond market stability.
- Trade: Extend short 30y UST. Entry: 5.36%. New target: 5.80%. New stop: 5.40%. Size: $50k/01. Following the FOMC's hawkish September meeting, we initiated a short position in 30y UST (see Removing a "dose of accommodation", dated 16 September). Over the past couple of sessions, price action has been driven by robust economic data, hawkish Fed rhetoric, weak Treasury auctions, underwhelming buyback announcements/operations, and volatile oil prices. The market has responded accordingly, where we are now 10bp from our target (5.60%) (see Don't fade this, dated 23 September). 30y yields have breached 5.50% with most points on the curve exceeding 5% yields. We therefore raise our target and stop for our short 30y position, as we still believe this is not the time to fade the move yet. Fiscal concerns appear underpriced ahead of the midterm elections, particularly as rate hikes materialize and the Treasury's heavy reliance on T-bills front-loads its interest burden (see The Debt Knight Rises, dated 22 September). Furthermore, we are within 5bp of a CTD switch in the USZ6 bond futures contract. Such a switch could induce a 10%+ duration extension for unhedged asset managers, potentially triggering negative convexity-driven selling flows and further exacerbating the rise in yields.
