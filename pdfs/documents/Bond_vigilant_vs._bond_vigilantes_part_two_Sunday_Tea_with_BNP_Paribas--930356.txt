# Bond vigilant vs. bond vigilantes part two Sunday Tea with BNP Paribas

Sunday Tea with BNPP: Bond vigilant vs. bond vigilant es...part two

## OUR KEY VIEWS

- Despite Treasury buybacks, we think long-end Treasury yields will continue to rise.
- Incoming growth and inflation data keep a September ECB rate hike on track.
- In US equities, we are bullish on volatilit y, but symmetric on delta. With long-end Treasury yields rising un-anchored in line with our view (see High Fives for 30y USTs, 12 May), the US Treasury intervened on Wednesday, by raising long-end Treasury buybacks. However, after a brief pullback, yields are back near their recent highs. This is bond vigilantes vs. bond vigilant administration – part two. Part one was in 2025 (see Bond vigilant vs. bond vigilantes, 10 February 2025). Treasury secretary Scott Bessent stated in early 2025 that they were “watching the 10y”, and had a large “toolkit” to address Treasury market issues. Through a combination of
1. biasing issuance in bills (see, T-bill and chill),
2. buybacks,
3. regulation changes, and most importantly,
4. controlled fiscal deficits (helped by tariffs), the bond vigilant administration prevailed over bond vigilantes in 2025. But the current setup in 2026 is different, and we think bond vigilantes have the upper hand this time. Fed credibility is being questioned by the market, a wave of hyperscaler issuance is adding to duration supply concerns, amid strong growth, sticky infl ation, and worsening deficits – all driving yields higher. Additionally, we see buybacks only removing a small fraction of the net duration supply in Treasuries (see Figure 1). Raising buybacks may have felt necessary but was not going to be sufficient to quell the rise in yields. Fed credibility concerns are a key part of the price action in US yields, with 2y yields falling since the July FOMC, and 30y yields rising – a K shaped bond market (see Figure 2). Such markets are rare, with only six such instances since the GFC. The most comparable episode is Q3 2023, where 30y yields rose by nearly 100bp in Q3 20 23. We continue to suggest positioning for higher long-term yields via 6m30y 1x1.5 payer spreads. Vis-à-vis credibility, investors will be closely watching the Jackson Hole appearance from Chair Warsh next Friday (10:00 EDT), though we don’t expect much, given that the theme of this year’s Jackson Hole symposium is “Financial Innovation: Implications for Payments and Policy”. If Warsh does speak on monetary policy, we think the language is likely to be similar to the June and July press conferences. For views on monetary policy, we think that listening to the wider FOMC is more informative. The Fedspeak docket is sparse as of writing, but we expect it to fill out next week. Another key data point will be core PCE (Wednesday 8:30 EDT), our estimate is on a knife’s edge at 0.246%, rounding to 0.2%. We think that:
1. there could be upward revisions to June based on upwardly revised PPI services prices; and
2. July and August prints are unlikely to change the overall narrative of sticky, moderately above-target inflation. As we’ve previously written about, the upcoming BEA revisions in September will likely lower PCE inflation on net. Regarding a key inflationary driver – the Middle East conflict – we observe that while crude prices have strengthened, the primary pressures on the energy system reside in gasoil and non-US natural gas. With gasoil and jet cracks at historically elevated levels, it appears that demand destruction is being driven through product pricing rather than crude oil prices to balance the system amid supply constraints. We don’t think the end of the rise is near, which is why we bought the Oct26-Dec26 ICE gasoil crack spread. EU gas prices show similar strains, having risen above EUR65/MWh on Friday. This is mainly driven by a need for European operators to buy LNG to refill historically low storage levels before winter, while Middle Eastern LNG remains scuppered. We see furthe r upside in the next two months and see a relative easing after that, given warm, wet and windy weather forecasts and more US LNG coming to the market. Last week’s PMIs bolster confidence in eurozone momentum, with manufacturing surging – notably in Germany – driven by defence spending and data center buildouts. Additionally, the Indeed wage tracker rose to 3.3% in July, up 100bp since March. Expected inflation releases this week will likely signal rising pressures, particularly in Spain, where we forecast 4.7% y/y, up from 3.9% in July. Overall, this data reinforces our conviction in a September hike by the ECB. Against this backdrop, Isabel Schnabel’s Jackson Hole appearance is critical. While we expect her to maintain her overall hawkish stance, it will be telling to see whether recent data has heightened her concerns regarding potential second-round effects. In our recent publication we highlight how changes to the Solvency II framework for eurozone insurers may incentivise them to shift their bond portfolios longer on the yield curve. We estimate eurozone insurers could switch up to 60mn DV01 from 20y into 25y and 30y bonds, mainly d uring H1 2027, whilst increased stress test scenarios should also boost demand for long-dated, out-of-the-money receiver swaptions. In US equities, w e are bullish on vol, but our delta view is now more symmetri c. Positioning is now rich (72/100), leaving us now neutral on delta. The catalyst calendar is full of traps. NVDA earnings (Aug 26) and Jackson Hole (Aug 28) this coming week; followed by nonfarm payrolls (Sep 4), CPI (Sep 11), and FOMC (Sep 16) during September expiry week. Price seasonality turns more challenging after the NVDA earnings release. We expect 2027 estimate upgrades to be a tailwind for stocks, but more so into year-end. For the micro “tail” trades: NVDA stock’s implied move of 4.5% scans ‘fair’ given the two-year average of 3.7%. NVDA spot has had a historical downward bias on earnings, but Semis’ price action into earnings warrants a more symmetric view. We like SMH upside, for a spot up / vol up catch up trade. For the macro “tail” trades: RTY vol remains attractive as a ‘rates wall of worry’ and high nominal growth trade. At the same time, low implied vol also scans well for hedges. Colombia’s GDP confirmed the upside bias suggested by high frequency indicators and increased 1.3% q/q in Q2, and 3.5% y/y – close to our above-consensus call (BNPP: 3.7% y/y). This result leaves growth carry-over for the year at 2.3%, already very close to our 2.5% forecast for 2026 average growth. The high carry-over for the year coupled with strong domestic consumption and the optimism boost from the new government would su ggest a strong upside bias to our growth forecast for the year – but the earthquake in August suggests a hit to both drivers in the short term, possibly keeping the pace of growth more moderate.
