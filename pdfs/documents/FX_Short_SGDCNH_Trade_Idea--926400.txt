# FX: Short SGDCNH

Parisha Saimbi, EM Asia FXLM Strategist | MAR | BNP Paribas Singapore Branch | Ann Jiang, APAC Markets Strategist | BNP Paribas Hong Kong Branch. MAS unlikely to tighten in July: With the US and Iran reaching a deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and current inflation expectations remaining anchored, the Monetary Authority of Singapore may hold off tightening its monetary policy at the July meeting, limiting investor appetite to enter long SGD into the meeting. Likely softening safe-haven flows: As geopolitical tensions ease, we believe investors have less incentive to park large cash balances in Singapore. As many regions where inflation remains elevated or central banks are more hawkish offer higher yields than those in Singapore, we think investors are likely to shift towards higher-yielding assets elsewhere rather than keep funds in a low-yield, safe-haven currency like the SGD (Figure 1). Dividend seasonality to weigh more on SGD: We expect dividend outflows to see SGD underperform into August, the next large dividend month for Singapore (see FX: Catching Asia’s dividend wave, dated 6 May). China’s dividend season tends to peak between June and July, but we believe dividend outflows will have a lower impact on the CNH this summer due to regulators’ push for more frequent payouts and the slow-bull outlook for Chinese equities this year, which may lead to a larger proportion of payouts being reinvested. Lower USDCNY fixing pattern confirms likely RMB appreciation: The People’s Bank of China has been lowering the fixing amid the move lower in spot. This suggests to us that the PBoC might be comfortable with the current pace of appreciation. The RMB index at 101.386 broke a previous high of 100.8404, suggesting that the central bank could be open to more CNH strength against the basket. CNH strength to be supported by exporters’ settlement demand: Exporters’ FX settlement has been the main driver of CNH appreciation this year, and we expect the trend to persist given the still-large monthly trade surplus and prevailing expectations of further RMB appreciation. While we anticipate a strong USD ahead of rate hikes from the Fed, we believe only a prolonged period of a stronger dollar would be sufficient to alter exporters’ behaviour.

## KEY MESSAGES

We initiate a short SGDCNH trade idea, as we see limited investor appetite to enter long SGD here with the Monetary Authority of Singapore unlikely to tighten its monetary policy in July and safe-haven inflows into SGD assets likely reducing following the US–Iran deal. We also find that dividend seasonality tends to weigh on the SGD in August, while dividend earnings in China may be reinvested amid the bullish outlook for Chinese equities this year. The CNH should outperform due to strong demand from exporters to settle FX and authorities guiding the fixing lower, in our view.
