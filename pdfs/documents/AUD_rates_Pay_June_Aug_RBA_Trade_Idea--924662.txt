# AUD rates: Pay June/Aug RBA

Joey Li, FX and Rates Strategist, APAC | MAR | Chandresh Jain, EM Asia FX and Rates Strategist | BNP Paribas Singapore Branch. 1: Hours worked increased in April We are adding a paid position in the June/Aug RBA spread, following today’s rally in AUD rates, as we believe inflation concerns will keep the Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA) in its hiking cycle. Rally in AUD rates driven by labor force data: AUD rates rallied significantly today, driven by the release of April’s labor market data, which showed a slight decline in employment numbers and a rise in the unemployment rate to 4.5%. This, combined with lower oil prices overnight and softer inflation expectations, led to an 8- 10bp move lower in front-end AUD swaps. Besides the inherent volatility in the monthly series, we are not convinced that the recent labor market data represents a significant loosening in the labor market, and prefer to fade the rally for several reasons: Firstly, the underemployment rate tightened from 5.9% to 5.8%, while hours worked per employed person increased (Figure 1), suggesting a more nuanced picture rather than a broad-based loosening of the labor market. Secondly, the rise in the unemployment rate is also due to seasonal adjustments, as April has historically been a strong month for the labor market. The trend measures of employment and unemployment rate remained steady from March to April, indicating no significant change in the labor market (Figure 2).

## KEY MESSAGES

We believe the Reserve Bank of Australia remains in a hiking cycle and see today’s rally in AUD rates as an opportunity to add a paid position in June/Aug RBA spread. Today’s jobs data did not indicate a material loosening in the labor market to us, as indicators of labor-market tightness other than the headline unemployment rate present more nuanced and mixed picture. At its last meeting in May, the RBA’s scenario analysis continued to suggest a slow timeline for underlying inflation returning to target, despite assuming a 4.7% terminal rate this year. Model-based estimates also suggest that the same level of the cash rate is less restrictive than before, supporting a higher terminal rate. We think Australia’s economy is likely to remain resilient, bolstered by elevated energy prices, its position as a net energy exporter, and fiscal support. Oil prices in the range of $80-90/barrel are unlikely to trigger demand destruction, yet still pose significant upside risks to inflation in the coming months, as producers pass on cost increases to consumers. With upcoming CPI prints for March and April scheduled to be released ahead of the RBA’s May and June meetings respectively, we expect the RBA’s hiking cycle is likely to continue. The current market pricing of 24.5bp over the next two meetings (May and June) appears overly optimistic to us, particularly given that the May RBA meeting is a SoMP meeting featuring updated forecasts, making it challenging for the RBA to justify a hold in the cash rate at a level they do not have confidence to be restrictive, amidst a worse inflation outlook.

Risk: The primary risk we see to this trade is that the RBA holds the cash rate steady in May, potentially due to increased uncertainty in the Middle East conflict resulting from further escalation or a breakdown of current talks. This could lead some board members to adopt a more cautious approach in May, as they did in March. However, we consider this outcome unlikely, as further escalation or a derailment of peace talks would likely drive up oil prices and exacerbate near-term inflationary pressures regardless of the level of demand, which was the board’s rationale for hiking in March. Even if the RBA does pause in May though, we expect it to maintain a hawkish tone, keeping the June RBA meeting live. The central bank has recently demonstrated its willingness to hike at non-SoMP meetings, and with April’s CPI report scheduled for release ahead of June RBA, we believe it will be challenging for markets to price in less than 15bp of rate hikes for June, thereby limiting potential losses on the trade. If the RBA does hike in May, we expect it to remain hawkish to keep inflation expectations anchored and financial conditions restrictive, which would likely result in June RBA pricing in a further additional 5–10bp of rate hikes.
