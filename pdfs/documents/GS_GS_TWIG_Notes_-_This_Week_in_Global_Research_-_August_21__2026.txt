# GS TWIG Notes: This Week in Global Research - August 21, 2026

21 August 2026 | 12:33PM EDT macro issues we’re watching Sluggish growth & employment + low inflation = no hikes Jan Hatzius reiterates our view that the Fed is very unlikely to hike rates at its September meeting or even again in this cycle in “They’re Not Hiking.” Why? Growth is slowing. Look for 2H26 real spending growth to slow to 1-1.5% as the stimulus from tax cuts wanes. See also Ronnie Walker’s “Earnings Season Takeaways: Consumer Strength Before the Slowdown” and Megan Peters’ “G10 Consumer Dashboard: July 2026: Spending Starts to Slow.” The labor market may be worse than it looks. Underlying job growth is only 5K per month now and the 40bp drop in the unemployment rate we have seen this year is a product of a dwindling population of job seekers and not the result of more people finding work. AND core inflation is hard to find. Rising fuel prices are putting pressure on some consumers, but outside of energy prices, inflation is well contained. Digging further into evolving Fed policy, Allison Nathan sits down with a slew of experts to discuss whether less transparency from the central bank is good or bad for monetary policy and/or markets in “Assessing a less transparent Fed.” Bottom-line: reduced transparency leads to increased market volatility, but the question of whether this just increases the market’s noise or also improves the market’s signal remains. See also Friedrich Schaper’s note on the US Treasury in “Fed Communication And Term Premium” and Joseph Briggs’ note exploring central bank transparency across the world in “What Has the World Learned About Central Bank Communication?.” See also Lina Thomas’ latest note on gold, “Gold and Central Banks: Storage Dilemma; Buying Trend Picks Up” and Stuart Jenkins FX note, “Finding Factors in FX Markets.” Focus on: labor Global AI-related hiring headwinds still appear confined to a narrow set of industries and workers writes Sarah Dong in “Is AI Impacting Global Labor Equity Research Markets?” Overall, most developed markets are adopting AI at the same pace — around 20% of companies have embraced the new technology. But we find little evidence that AI is leading to lower employment, yet. Separately in Germany, rising real wages amid stagnant productivity have weighed on employment writes Niklas Garnadt in “Germany—A Cyclically Loose but Structurally Tighter Labour Market.” Looking ahead, an aging workforce coupled with immigration control should drive a 0.4% annual contraction in labor supply over the next decade – a drag on growth but not necessarily the unemployment rate. Positioning corner: a rough July for some, but that may have cleaned us up for a better forward Ben Snider and Ryan Hammond update our quarterly positioning analysis across both hedge funds and mutual funds in two notes this week, “Hedge Fund Trend Monitor: Down but not out” and “Mutual Fundamentals: Rebalancing act.” Hedge Funds had a strong 2Q, but then struggled as AI momentum unwound in July. Our Hedge Fund VIP list of the most popular long positions suffered its worst 1-month underperformance vs. the S&P 500 in more than 20 years of history, and July marked one of the sharpest hedge fund de-grossing episodes of the past decade. The good news: US equity long/short hedge funds have returned 10% through mid-August and the crowding we saw in 2Q is much less pronounced post-July’s sell-off. As for Mutual Funds, more funds than usual (41% vs. 37% on average) are outperforming their benchmarks somewhat helped, perhaps, by an inability to keep up with the weight of AI in indices. See also Snider’s update on 2Q26 earnings season from last Friday, “What Q2 earnings reports signaled about the state of corporate AI adoption.” the important issues our sector analysts are watching Government matters: navigating stimulus and regulation around the world In China, the Ministry of Commerce (MOC) announced a new initiative to stimulate consumption at the local level this week, and Leaf Liu highlights how this new policy should drive growth across food & beverage and other value-based retailers, in particular, in “China: MOC announced policy guideline to support county level consumption.” See also Ronald Keung’s “eCommerce Tracker: July industry online retail GMV +3%” for more on China consumer activity. In the US, Pennsylvania announced new regulations requiring datacenter developers to follow stricter rules, but Carly Davenport believes the actual impact on high-quality, large-scale data center developments will be minimal in “Power: PA follows Texas in tightening Data Center rules, focused on local community approvals.” Buy the dip in: TLN. See also Adam Bubes’ update on datacenter construction around the country in “GS Data Center Construction Spending Tracker,” and Davenport’s “Power: Levelized Cost of Energy Update.” Nick Cash weighs in on what lower import duties on Canadian steel could mean for the Steel market in “Tentative Trade Deal Could Reduce US Steel/Aluminum Tariffs on Canada; Further Details Not Yet Finalized.” Turning to Financials, James Yaro highlights how an SEC proposal issued this week n (Regulation Crypto Assets) could drive new use cases for digital assets outside of crypto trading, within tokenization and decentralized finance (DeFi) in “Brokers & Crypto: SEC innovation exemption proposal for digital assets.” Drug innovation conflagration: another cancer vaccine and GLP-1’s go generic in Canada and Brazil Positive results from a drug trial from MRNA and MRK to create a personalized vaccine for people who have melanoma or a form of lung cancer showed very promising results and Salveen Richter and Asad Haider weigh in on what it all means in two notes: “MRNA: Intismeran Phase 3 success in melanoma unlocks the oncology vertical” and “MRK: Incorporating mRNA Cancer Therapy Update as Another Growth Vertical Gets Derisked and Priced.” The result supports potential FDA approval discussions and strengthens confidence in MRNA’s broader cancer pipeline beyond vaccines, while giving MRK a new potential growth driver beyond Keytruda. Separately, GLP-1s (semaglutide) to treat Type 2 diabetes in Canada and Brazil are going off-patent this year, creating an opportunity for generic drug manufacturers to boost volumes even as the treatment price erodes aggressively writes Shayam Kotida in “The Obesity Evolution: Framing the generic semaglutide opportunity in Canada and Brazil.” Best positioned: Sandoz. Learnings from Consumer Earnings WMT and TJX both reported sharp slowdowns in comparable sales growth, AAP noted a deceleration in demand for DIY autoparts during the final four weeks of the quarter, and KTB indicated that retailers are taking a more cautious stance on inventory accumulation – suggesting that the US consumer may be wavering as Kate McShane and Brooke Roach discuss in a series of single stock notes this week: “WMT: Q2 top line slows sequentially, while 2026 guidance is raised,” “TJX: F2Q27 Earnings Review,” “AAP: DIY to remain a headwind in the near term,” and “KTB: 2Q26 Earnings Review.” Not every retailer is struggling, however. Off-price leader ROST reported 10% same-store sales growth writes Roach in “ROST: F2Q26 Earnings Review.” And big box hardware store HD saw an acceleration in same-store sales growth writes McShane in “HD: Ticket-led comp beat.” See also Roach’s “Apparel Retail: Refreshing our off-price trackers and highlighting key debates.” Seeking alpha in Japan, the US, Canada, and beyond In Japan, Norohiro Miyazaki forecasts above-guidance growth for shipping container company profits as US port congestion coupled with restocking demand is driving pricing power in “Japan Shipping/Airlines earnings outlook set to move up another notch.” Separately, Airlines are benefiting from Tech shipment demand and stabilizing jet fuel costs. Best positioned: MOL, ANA, and Japan Post HD. Ryan Nash forecasts tepid-but-consistent loan growth of 1% across Regional Banks for 2H26, with net interest income (NII) seeing a 130bp acceleration in growth to +3.7% in “Regional Banks Handbook.” Best positioned: HBAN, KEY, RF, CFG (on CL), FITB. Turning to Energy stocks, Neil Mehta leans into idiosyncratic E&P’s as the favorable refinery outlook is starting to be priced in and oil beta trades are difficult amidst so much uncertainty in two notes: “E&P: Highlight Capital Efficient Growth Outlook and Flexibility of Capital Returns Through Volatile Macro Environment” and “Strong Canadian Oil Fundamental Backdrop Continues in Both Upstream and Refining.” Buy: DVN and CVE. We rode the Magic Bus around Silicon Valley, then caught up with Manufacturers in India Companies and academics on our Silicon Valley AI Field Trip highlighted improving model capabilities, expanding agent monetization, and the growing importance of proprietary data, workflow ownership, and verification, write George Tong, Gabriela Borges, and Matt Martino in “Takeaways From Our 3rd Annual Silicon Valley AI Field Trip.” Best positioned: IRM, MCO, MSCI, SPGI, and TRI in Business & Information Services, and MSFT (on CL), MDB and RBRK in Software. Separately, in India, Day 1 of our Aerospace & Precision Machining Field Trip to Bengaluru reinforced our belief that Hindustan Aeronautics’ engine delivery issues appear to be easing, highlighting the importance of execution of manufacturing platforms, writes Amit Dixit in “Field Trip Day 1 highlights.” 3 Buys and 2 Sells for a Summer Weekend Luxshare.
