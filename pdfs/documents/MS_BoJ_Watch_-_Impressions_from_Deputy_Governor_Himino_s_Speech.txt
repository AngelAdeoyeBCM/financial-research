# BoJ Watch - Impressions from Deputy Governor Himino’s Speech

BoJ Watch | Japan

Himino’s speech maintained a hawkish tone and suggested openness to more nimble policy adjustments: At the meeting with local leaders in Saitama on August 27 at 10:30 a.m. JST, Deputy Governor Ryozo Himino said that “we should pay greater attention to the upside risk to prices than in the past,” while emphasizing that “it is important to stabilize underlying inflation at a level around 2 percent.” He also stated that “the timing and pace of adjustment should be chosen by examining the likelihood of the baseline scenario of the outlook for economic activity and prices being realized, and the risks to the outlook, including the impact of the situation in the Middle East, the expansion in AI-related demand, and developments in foreign exchange rates.” Overall, his remarks appeared to leave room for greater flexibility in determining the timing and pace of policy adjustment, suggesting openness to a more agile approach to monetary policy. Indeed, Himino stated: “For a long time, we have asked ourselves whether underlying inflation is moving up and whether it will reach and stabilize at 2 percent. However, given the movement of these indicators, I believe we are entering a phase where we will also need to bear in mind the possibility of underlying inflation exceeding 2 percent in addition to these considerations when making policy decisions.” This suggests that the focus of policy deliberations may be broadening beyond whether underlying inflation will reach and stabilize at 2 percent to encompass the risk that it could exceed 2 percent. Himino also assessed that “the AI buildout is partially offsetting the deterioration in Japan’s terms of trade caused by the energy price hike,” which had been viewed as a potential source of downward risk on underlying inflation. He further noted that “firms and households may find that, in terms of the way interest rates affect their business or life plans, a 1 percent interest rate in a non-deflationary environment is in fact lower than zero or negative rates in a deflationary environment.” Referring to financial institutions’ aggressive lending stance and the pickup in the growth rate of bank lending, he added: “These indicators show that financial conditions remain accommodative. That said, the speech did not appear to go materially beyond the July meeting: At the same time, the speech did not appear to move materially beyond the policy framework presented at the July Monetary Policy Meeting and in the July Outlook Report. Himino reiterated the need to pay greater attention to upside risks to prices and the assessment that the AI buildout is partially offsetting the deterioration in Japan’s terms of trade. We had the impression that he did not offer pushback against market expectations for early rate hikes but at the same time, in our view, the speech did not deliver a clear new signal ahead of the September meeting. With relatively little additional data due before the September meeting, the speech did not place materially greater emphasis on shifting from a conventional data-dependent approach toward a more forward-looking policy framework. In this respect, the remarks may have been somewhat moderate for market participants who had expected clearer groundwork for a September rate hike. Nevertheless, market attention remains focused on whether the BoJ will signal greater willingness to accelerate the pace of rate increases. The near-term focus will be on whether there is any further change in the BoJ’s communication, including around the G20 meeting that Governor Ueda is scheduled to attend. In particular, markets will be watching for any shift in communication regarding the pace of future rate increases.
