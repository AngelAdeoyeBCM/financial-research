# Metal Matters LBMA annual conference and recent marketing takeaways

## CITI'S TAKE

In this note we share market color gathered at the LBMA annual conference last week as well as meetings with corporates, investors and traders across Africa, Asia and Europe over the past weeks.

- **Long-term constructiveness vs short-term cautiousness on gold** – The market participants we met were still broadly constructive on the long-term gold outlook on themes including the debasement trade, CB buying, geopolitical fragmentation, etc. That said, the short-term stance was more cautious on concerns that still high energy prices and sticky inflation will lead to continued Fed hawkishness which has been a major headwind for gold. European clients in particular have shown the most pessimism on prospects of dealmaking with whether US-Iran or Russia-Ukraine.
- **Strong dip buying interest vs muted upside participation for now** – Many market participants indicated interest in dip buying. The $4K/oz level, which was a solid support during summer trading, remains a psychologically important level many are watching and looking to add exposure around. However, recalling the pain trading the headlines in the early days of the US-Iran war and considering the quick collapse of the first MoU, market participants are now requiring a higher bar (e.g. concrete steps towards deescalation) before going all-in on the gold trade again.
- **Fading monetary debasement vs growing fiscal debasement** – The fear of Fed losing political independence was the most important driver of the January gold rally, leading to a collapse in US$ and all-time high gold prices. Market concerns on monetary debasement faded as the Fed has been hawkish on inflation since Warsh took over, and the debasement trade recently shifted to fiscal problems. However, fiscal debasement has a less straightforward impact on gold. While gold demand should theoretically benefit from debasement hedging, higher government bond yields (still considered risk-free practically) lead to higher opportunity costs of holding gold, a zero-couple non-yielding asset. As rates rise though, the US government may be more and more tempted to debase fiscally by buying bonds.
- **Gold vs other precious metals** – We sense a lot less market interest on silver and PGMs than last year as the price outperformance against gold reversed and as market fundamentals turn quiet (on PGMs)/bearish (on silver substitution/thrifting). We stick to our longstanding view that silver and platinum will continue to track gold in direction with high beta, while palladium will continue to lag given its negative long-term fundamental outlook. We continue to favor the risk-reward profile on gold in the current low-4K trading range. In our base case, we expect SoH reopening and Yemen/Saudi deescalation by year-end, which will see substantial upside potential in gold as lower oil prices and a less hawkish Fed drive up investment demand, and under this Middle East assumption we stick to our 0-3m point-price target of $4,800/oz. If oil prices get stuck here with no deescalation in sight, we still see limited downside risk with solid dip buying interest around 4K. Even in the unlikely scenario of a major reescalation leading to much higher for longer energy prices, we expect gold to initially take a hit amid broader de-grossing but eventually outshine as a hedge against stagflation. n
