# EM rates: Take profit on paid THOR 5y

Chandresh Jain, EM Asia FX and Rates Strategist | MAR | Joey Li, Markets Strategist, APAC | BNP Paribas Singapore Branch. Sources: Bloomberg, Macrobond, BNP Paribas Fig. 1: Rates initially rallied due to the BoT's surprise rate cut, but have since sold off as oil prices surged higher We take profit on our paid position in M26, 5y THOR at 1.75%, capturing 27bp (USD405k) on the trade. We had initiated this trade idea after the Thai general elections, which resulted in a win for the Bhumjaithai Party (BJT) and Anutin Charnvirakul. We expected the outcome to bring some stability to the Thai economy and a return to fiscal spending (see EM rates: Paying THOR 5y after Thai election results, dated 9 February). Despite the Bank of Thailand's surprise rate cut in February, which initially triggered a sharp rally in Thai rates, the ongoing escalation in the Middle East has driven oil prices higher, prompting a renewed sell-off in Asian rates, with Thailand being particularly affected due to its large net oil imports. While markets are primarily focused on the inflationary impact of rising oil prices, we believe Thailand's situation may differ somewhat from other large oil-importing countries in Asia such as India or South Korea. Although higher oil prices do contribute to inflation, we think growth concerns take precedence in Thailand, which does not have an inflation problem – both headline and core inflation have largely undershot the central bank's target of 1–3% over the last few years. Instead, the surge in oil and fuel prices, combined with concerns over the conflict, has led to a decline in air traffic, which will likely weigh heavily on tourism flows, a key driver of Thailand's economy. The BoT rate cut last month increases the risk that the Monetary Policy Committee may have a lower bound for its policy rate in mind, particularly if tourism is negatively impacted, in our view. Given this, we deem it prudent to take profit and potentially re-enter at more favorable levels. We close our paid M26, 5y position at 1.75%, capturing a profit of 27bp (USD405k). We are taking profit on our paid position in M26, 5y THOR at 1.60%, capturing 12bp (USD180k) on the trade. We had initiated this trade after the Thai general elections, which resulted in a win for the Bhumjaithai Party (BJT) and Anutin Charnvirakul, expecting the outcome to bring stability to the Thai economy and a return to fiscal spending(see EM rates: Paying THOR 5y after Thai election results, dated 9 February). Initially, the Bank of Thailand's surprise rate cut in late February, which was not priced in by the market, led to a sharp rally in THOR rates. However, the recent escalation of Middle East tensions has caused oil prices to surge, resulting in a significant selloff across Asian rates, with Thailand being particularly affected due to its large net oil imports. While markets are primarily focused on the inflationary impact of rising oil prices, we believe Thailand's situation may differ somewhat from other large oil-importing countries in Asia such as like India or South Korea. Although higher oil prices do contribute to inflation, we think growth concerns take precedence in Thailand, which does not have an inflation problem – both headline and core inflation have largely undershot the central bank's target of 1–3% over the last few years. Instead, the surge in oil and fuel prices, combined with concerns over war and strikes, has led to a decline in air traffic, which will likely weigh heavily on tourism flows, a key driver of Thailand's economy. The Bank of Thailand's surprise rate cut last month increases the risk that the Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) may have a lower bound for its policy rate in mind, particularly if tourism is negatively impacted, in our view. Given this, although we maintain our view that the election results will be supportive for the Thai economy and expect this year's increase in net bond supply to put upward pressure on rates, we think it's prudent to take profit at these levels and re-enter at more favorable levels. We close our paid M26, 5y position at 1.60%, capturing a profit of 12bp (USD180k) on the trade.
