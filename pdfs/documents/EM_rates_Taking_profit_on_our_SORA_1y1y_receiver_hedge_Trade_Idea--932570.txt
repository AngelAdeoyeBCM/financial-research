# EM rates: Taking profit on our SORA 1y1y receiver hedge

We have been running a SORA m27,1y receiver, hedged with a payer on the FOMC Oct/Jan spread (see EM rates: Shifting our SORA 1y1y receiver hedge forward, dated 1 0 September, and for the original trade idea, see EM rates: Receive SORA 1y1y against paid FOMC Dec/Mar, dated 13 July). Taking profit on our hedge, as risk–reward looks less attractive: The Oct/Jan FOMC payer has largely worked as intended, hedging our received position in SORA rates across various sell-offs in US rates, including around the September FOMC meeting (Figure 1). Following another move higher in US rates overnight, markets are now pricing more than 32bp in total for the December and January FOMC meetings. At these levels, we think risk–reward for maintaining the hedge is no longer appealing and prefer to take profit on the hedge. Transitioning to outright receivers in SORA rates: Turning to Singapore, SORA rates have sold off by 40–50bp over the past month, driven by the move in US rates. We think it may be attractive to run our SORA receiver outright at these levels, as the curve has steepened significantly relative to front-end SORA fixings (Figure 2). Although the loan-to-deposit (LTD) ratio has risen, it remains low relative to historical levels; notably, the MAS assesses the current 69% LTD ratio as stable (see MAS Financial Stability Review 2026, published 22 September). Additionally, despite the Fed rate hike at its September FOMC meeting, SORA fixings remain in their YTD range, suggesting that systemic liquidity remains ample. As such, we think risks are skewed to wards a rally in SORA rates, if SORA fixings do not rise substantially higher from here. Take profit on US hedge: Close Oct/Jan FOMC payer at 32bp. Entry: 21bp. Allocation: USD30k/01. Keep SORA receiver: Remain received in SORA m2 7, 1 y outright. Current: 2.16%. Allocation: USD15k/01.
