# EM rates: Shifting our SORA 1y1y receiver hedge forward

We are running a SORA M27,1y receiver with a payer in FOMC Dec/Mar spread (see EM rates: Receive SORA 1y1y against paid FOMC Dec/Mar, dated 13 July). We now shift our FOMC Dec/Mar payer to an Oct/Jan payer, as the risk of an earlier start to the Fed hiking cycle has risen. We now forecast three rate hikes from the Fed, starting in September (Sep, Dec and Jan), although the upcoming PPI/CPI prints remain a key risk (see Sunday Tea with BNP Paribas – CPI of the storm, dated 6 September). The existing Dec/Mar spread payer has hedged unfavorable rises in SORA rates, while our received position in SORA rates has earned about 8bp in carry and rolldown since entry (Figure 1). However, with the possibility of the Fed starting its hiking cycle in September, we see a risk that our existing Dec/Mar FOMC hedge may not be the best hedge. As markets front-load rate-hike expectations, the Dec/Mar spread could flatten, undermining its protection against further sell-offs in SORA rates. To mitigate this, we believe it is prudent to roll our hedge forward to the Oct/Jan FOMC spread. This Oct/Jan spread covers the December and January FOMC meetings, which occur right after the US mid-term elections. This spread is trading at 21bp and has more scope to steepen with Fed starting the hiking cycle soon, in our view. We opt to exclude the September FOMC meeting, as the risk-reward appears less attractive at current levels – markets are already pricing in 15–16bp for the meeting, while August CPI remains a risk. Continue to favor front-end SORA receivers: We continue to like our receiver in SORA rates, as the curve remains steep. Although the loan-to-deposit ratio has increased in recent months, it has not translated into higher SORA fixings, which suggests that systemic liquidity remains ample. Given that the rise in front-end SORA rates over the last two months has been largely driven by the increase in USD rates (Figure 2), we still favor front-end SORA receivers at these levels, hedged with US rates.
