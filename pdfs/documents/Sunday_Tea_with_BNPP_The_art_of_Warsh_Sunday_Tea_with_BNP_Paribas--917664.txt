# Sunday Tea with BNPP: The art of Warsh

. 01/02/2026 1

RKE.

## KEY MESSAGES

- We see the Fed on hold for all of 2026, even if Kevin Warsh is confirmed as Chair Powell’s successor.
- There is risk that the Treasury hints at coupon cuts this week. We are long 30y spreads into the refunding announcement.
- We expect a hold with dovish risks from the BoE and therefore go into the event received March MPC and long EURGBP. We revised our Fed outlook last week and no longer expect any rate cuts this year. This is for four reasons:
1. we expect the data to bear out Chair Powell’s optimism on growth and employment over the year;
2. we see inflation proving stickier than the FOMC now expects;
3. we believe the FOMC will increasingly assess its policy stance as non-restrictive or stimulative; and
4. regardless of who succeeds Powell, policy will probably follow a traditional reaction function. President Trump announced Kevin Warsh as his nominee for Fed Chair last Friday. Warsh previously served on the Fed’s Board of Governors from 2006 to 2011 and was in contention in 2017 to head the central bank before the job ultimately went to Powell. Warsh’s nomination should at the margin ease concerns over Fed independence. He has historically fiercely supported the institution’s autonomy, which should aid his confirmation process and remove some risk premium from the USD. Warsh has also been hawkish on inflation in the past and critical of the size of the Fed’s balance sheet. In recent times, however, Warsh has been more open-minded toward rate cuts, based on expectations for a disinflationary, AI-fueled productivity boom. As such, the markets will likely keep pricing for a move in June elevated. But if US economic data holds up this year (as we expect), there is little scope for more easing, even if the new Chair wants this. On the data front, the January nonfarm payrolls report scheduled for release on Friday should support this notion. We forecast an above-trend and above-consensus 105k headline payrolls gain, aided by seasonal factors. Less intense holiday hiring late last year (due in part to the federal government shutdown, and to post-pandemic workforce adjustments) will result in fewer-than-normal job cuts in January, we think. While we forecast a 4.4% unemployment rate, risks skew toward a decline to start the year. Indeed, with the US population aging and immigration policy tight, our call for January’s payrolls would be roughly double what is required to absorb new labor supply. Fig. 1: Payrolls have tended to surprise to the upside in recent Januarys Sources: Bloomberg, BNP Paribas That said, the probability that the data is released on schedule has diminished, given that the federal government is partially shut down over disagreement on funding for the Department of Homeland Security. This episode is expected to be much shorter than the shutdown late last year. And data collection for the January jobs report is complete. But if the reopening does not occur early this week, we doubt that enough time will remain to put the final touches on the report for on-time delivery. This will put even more focus on the week’s other key risk event – the Treasury’s quarterly refunding announcement. The administration appears to be intent on lowering mortgage rates this year to quell affordability concerns going into the midterm elections. We think the most potent, logical and practical pathway to reduce long-end yields is by decreasing long-end duration supply. The Treasury could cut. 01/02/2026 2 10y, 20y and/or 30y coupon auction sizes or increase buyback sizes for the 10-20y and 20-30y sectors. It could also consider abolishing the 20y point, which has drawn only lukewarm demand since its launch in 2020. Or officials could hint at taking any of these steps during the year. Any of these possibilities would likely depress term premiums and weigh on the back end of the UST curve. Accordingly, we head into the refunding long 30y swap spreads. In US equities, Q4 earnings are in full swing, with ~30% of SPX by market cap reporting this week, including heavyweights GOOG and AMZN. Of the ~40% of SPX that ha s already reported, results have been strong with 78% po sting headline EPS beats, though sector and single-name price action has been mixed. Rotation within the broader Tech sector has been extreme to start the year with semiconductors (SMH) outperforming software (IGV) by >25% YTD. Into the second half of earnings season, we remain constructive but see a compelling argument to own SMH downside convexity as a left tail hedge for the AI narrative. We are not bearish on the delta, but convex hedges on the ETF look attractive following rece nt spot outperformance. SMH has one of the largest liquidity-adjusted short gamma profiles from levered ETFs. And SMH’s biggest weights are at the epicenter of the global AI story. A structure we continue to like is selling a Jun26 Put Ratio (buying the wing). The trade is long convexity and gamma, with a smaller cost of carry and less premium outlay than an outright long in downside vol. Fig. 2: Options-implied 1d SPX moves through earnings Sources: Bloomberg, BNP Paribas In Japan, early projections from the major media suggest that the ruling LDP could secure an outright majority in the lower house election scheduled for 8 February. Fiscal policy seems likely to stay expansionary under Prime Minister Takaichi, who now advocat es a lower consumption tax. Recent fiscal developments have added upside risk to the medium-term inflation outlook, and the latest BoJ meeting signaled a more hawkish stance from the policy board. Consequently, we have advanced our forecast for the next rate rise to April from June and expect additional hikes roughly every four to five months thereafter, ending at a terminal rate of 2% by end-2027. Domestic pension funds rebalancing and a partial recovery in the value of the JPY ha ve helped to settle long-end JGBs for now. But large uncertainty over the fiscal outlook, the pace of BoJ hikes and the eventual terminal rate suggests that Japanese bonds will continue to remain in a volatile and unstable environment. A number of key central bank meetings will also be taking place this week. For the RBA decision on Tuesday, the market is pricing in a 70% probability of a 25bp rate hike. We have taken profit on our Feb/May RBA payer spread and switched to a paid position in Feb/Mar RBA, which now has a better risk-reward profile, in our view. Furthermore, we have shifted our overall view on the AUD from bearish to bullish as we see superannuation fund flows tilting to net positive for the currency. In Europe, the ECB and BoE both meet on Thursday. We think markets will focus more on the latter, where there is greater uncertainty on
1. the timing of the next rate cut and
2. the terminal rate level, whereas risks around the ECB are well priced, in our view. While we expect the BoE to remain on hold with a 7-2 vote split, we expect other aspects of the meeting to reinforce the easing bias and provide more clarity on the path ahead. The updated inflation projections should show a more benign outlook, including a return to target by Q3 2026 (70bp lower than in the November MPR). And while not our base case, there are two other dovish risks: policymakers could signal a conditional bias to cut in March or suggest that inflation risks have tilted to the downside. Therefore, with just 5bp of cumulative cuts priced in by the March meeting, we remain received March MPC, long EURGBP, and long a EURGBP higher / SX5E higher dual digital.
