# FX: Closing EURBRL and USDPEN on Middle East events

As developments in the Middle East continue to trigger volatility and uncertainty for markets, we are closing the remainder of our Latam FX portfolio of trades to better align our position with risks that may lie ahead. In particular, the more severe-than-expected attacks on Iran indicate a potential for oil and gas prices to remain higher for longer, as Tehran may respond in a more disruptive manner, specifically targeting energy commodities. This scenario could lead to increased risk aversion across various markets. We therefore make the following adjustments to our portfolio:

- Take profit on our remaining short EURBRL position. We are locking in a profit on the remaining half of the position (USD5mn), booking a gain of 3.6% (USD178k). We entered the position on 13 January in anticipation of a supportive global macro environment for high-yielding EM currencies (see Brazil: Adding to our short EURBRL position).
- Closing short USDPEN NDF position as it has touched the stop loss level, and book a loss of 1.5% (USD301k). We entered the trade on 3 February, on the back of Peru’s record-high terms of trade (driven by the surge in copper and gold prices), and in anticipation of seasonal corporate-tax liquidity (FX: Entering a short USDPEN NDF trade) Our short USDPEN NDF position has touched the stop-loss level, and we therefore exit the trade and book a loss of 1.2%(USD243k). The USDPEN moved sharply after the weekend’s developments in the Middle East, and, like many other Latam currencies, the PEN continues to trading weaker as risk sentiment deteriorated. Nevertheless, the PEN’s fundamentals remain sound and we still see upside potential. We will therefore watch for an opportunity to re-establish a bullish PEN position, which could be further supported by the seasonal lift typically seen during the upcoming local corporate-tax seasons. To recap, we entered the trade on 3 February, on the back of Peru’s record-high terms of trade (driven by the surge in copper and gold prices), and seasonal corporate-tax liquidity to appear in the coming weeks (see EM rates: Entering a COP basis payer).
