# Sunday Tea with BNPP: An unstable status quo

. 03/05/2026 1

B N P PA GL OB ET S A PP.

## KEY MESSAGES

- The Middle East conflict is extending into its third month with scant sign of a durable resolution on the horizon. Each additional day of disruption takes the global economy closer to a potential tipping point after which more significant damage will be inflicted.
- At the same time, the ongoing – even if elusive – prospect of a resolution, a strong US earnings season and momentum in recent economic data has helped to support risk assets despite the disruption.
- Should the stalemate continue, the path of least resistance right now remains for higher energy prices. But provided incoming data remains solid – and we think this week’s US payrolls data will be just that – this bifurcated, somewhat unstable status quo can persist. More of the same: In Global Outlook Spring 2026: Looking beyond the war, dated 24 April, we argued that the conflict in the Middle East was already sufficiently extensive to inflict some damage to the global economy and deliver a sizeable inflationary impulse. At the same time, we suggested that a resilient starting point and tailwinds from AI and defence spending would prevent a recession and, in turn, keep risk assets supported and the dollar under pressure while pushing yields higher. Incoming data and market moves over the past week have been broadly consistent with our core views, with markets appearing to give the benefit of the doubt to the idea that the disruption will not lead to a significant hiking cycle or a global recession. Indeed, the S&P500 has continued to climb despite oil prices briefly touch ing a four-year high of more than USD125/bbl. From flows to fundamentals: In equity markets, we think the baton was passed last week from flows to fundamentals. The massive inflows that drove the rapid move higher from the lows are begin ning to be replaced by a steady, but slower, rate of incremental buying. The best performing equity sectors YTD are those with the strongest earnings revisions. Price action from the Mag -7 names reporting last week wa s mixed; META, NVDA, MSFT fell, but GOOGL, AAPL and AMZN moved higher post earnings. The read for the capex beneficiaries was bullish. The net effect was volatility dampening at an index level, but still bullish. Our view is that whil e flows and positioning no longer suggest the addition of risk, the earnings momentum picture is so strong it will likely continue to support a bullish outlook for US equities. An unstable status quo: The challenge, however, is that disruption in the Middle East is entering its third month and, at least at the time of writing, there was little to suggest a resolution is imminent. The fact that Qatar Energy extended the force majeure on its LNG supplies by another month into mid-June may signal pessimism in the region about the possibility of a speedy de-escalation. The path of least resistance for oil prices is higher, should the status quo continue. After all, each day of reduced flow through the Strait of Hormuz implies a reduction in accumulated oil inventories that cannot be recouped. It is a matter of accounting that, absent a change in the supply dynamic, prices will have to be high enough to create the demand destruction that will bring the system into balance. While the UAE’s surprise exit from OPEC suggests a potential expansion in production that may help to lower prices in the medium term, we don’t expect any impact in the near term. Although the Strait remains closed and the Fujairah pipeline is at or near capacity – we expect more supply from the UAE from 2027 onwards (see UAE leaves OPEC – limited short-term impact, more supply in 2027, dated 28 April). This uneasy tension between the relative optimism of markets and the frozen geopolitical situation, cannot persist forever. At some point there will be a reconciliation. Either a diplomatic solution that puts the global economy on a pathway to gradual normalisation will materialise, or an undeniable and significant economic impact will arise that is harder for markets to look through. Our base case assumes an eventual resolution, but not yet. The unstable status quo can continue for a while. Central bank waiting game: Against this backdrop, last week’s central bank meetings largely confirmed our expectations that the shock has delivered a sufficiently extensive inflationary impulse to warrant some form of response. The Fed retained its easing bias in form only, but in practice it will likely follow a symmetric bias and we see a growing risk of a hike this year if the unemployment rate declines and the Strait remains closed (see April FOMC: The end of an era, dated 29 April).. 03/05/2026 2 We expect this week’s US jobs market data to be solid – we look for a 65k rise in non-farm payrolls, a steady unemployment rate and firmer wage pressures. This should support a wait-and-see stance from the Fed. But it would only take a few more months of above - trend job growth to push the unemployment rate to 4% or below, a level at which we think wage-price dynamics could creep back into the Fed’s thinking. With markets pricing a symmetric bias and then some, we think the market has caught up to our views. At current levels, we see risk –reward favouring a small long bias in the front end – which we maintain via 2s30s steepeners. The ECB signalled a hike in June, our base case, as the default option, while the BoE took cuts off the table, which opens the way to rate hikes (see ECB: When, not if and Bank of England: Hikes on the horizon, dated 30 April). In both cases, we expect 50bp of hikes to be delivered this year. Meanwhile, the Bank of Japan delivered a ‘hawkish hold’ and we expect it to deliver a hike in June as well. The BoJ is already behind the curve, in our view. Additional pressure on USDJPY in recent days has, according to press reports, resulted in finance ministry intervention. This merely buys time, in our view, and should the policy mix of ‘rates on hold, yen-buying intervention and expansionary fiscal policy’ persist, the underlying pressure on the currency will likely continue to build, raising the risk that eventually a more rapid policy tightening becomes unavoidable. (see Japan: What does the MoF’s FX intervention imply for BoJ policy, dated 1 May). Given poor fundamentals we would expect the impact of intervention to be short lived and thus we like using this opportunity to buy the dip in the pair (see FX: Buying the dip in USDJPY, dated 30 April). Policy rate meetings in Poland and the Czech Republic this week (both seen on hold) will be watched closely for any evidence of central bankers becoming less comfortable with the ‘wait-and-see’ approach communicated up to now. The large upside surprise in Polish inflation last week, despite government support measures, has likely lowered the bar to more hawkish communication this week, while the risk of a rate hike in July or September (versus our central case of rates on hold) has also increased. We think the CNB’s quarterly forecast update this week could show some disconnect between the generally more hawkish research department (that has flagged upside risks to inflation) and monetary policy council members who have continued to pour cold water on the prospect of rate hikes later this year and downplayed the need to respond to an ECB that looks likely to raise rates from June. Elsewhere, we expect Banxico to deliver a final 25bp cut of the cycle (to 6.5 0%) this week, before embarking on a long pause. We are now positioned through a MBONO 38s ASW tightener, as the trade could benefit from the current upward trend in oil prices (triggering stronger sell off in TIIE s than in MBONOs) and from a more hawkish Banxico that will limit the downward pressure on TIIE s. In Colombia we expect local curves to react with a mix of bull/bear steepening to the surprising hold decision by BanRep (+60bp priced in). While the market will reduce its end -2026 policy rate expectation (including the possibility of another hold in June), premium 2027 onward shoul d increase reflecting policy/inflation uncertainty (see Colombia: Credibility on hold to contain wage-inflation spiral, 30 April). We expect the COP to underperform regional ly as election day approaches (31 May) and believe risks for FX are asymmetric on the negative side for the event. UK local elections with national implications: Finally, after an extended period of political volatility, UK voters will head to the polls on 7 May for England’s local elections and national parliamentary elections in Scotland and Wales. While Thursday’s polling test will not directly impact the composition of the House of Commons or the national of the government of the UK, that doesn’t mean it comes without national implications. Polling institutes project widespread losses for the ruling Labour Party, even in constituencies that have traditionally been strongholds, as pressure builds from both sides of the political spectrum. If such losses materialise, there is likely to be a resurgence in political uncertainty as concerns about a change in leadership return to the forefront. Media reports have suggested that key Labour figures, including Angela Rayner and Wes Streeting, are still deciding whether to launch a challenge in the weeks after the election. Whether through a resignation or a challenge, a change in leadership risks reigniting concerns about the UK’s fiscal position. As UK political risk premium is embedded, this could add to pressure on gilts and GBP. Treasury QRA: Another chance to push affordability? With midterms approaching, and the UST 30y yield at ~ 5% - mortgage rates are not supportive of ‘affordability’ push from the administration. We think the Treasury can attempt to push long-end yields lower by delivering indirect or direct long-end coupon cuts at the next QRA on Wednesday. We suggest tactical long 30y swap spreads into next week (see Spreading it short and sweet, 28 April).
