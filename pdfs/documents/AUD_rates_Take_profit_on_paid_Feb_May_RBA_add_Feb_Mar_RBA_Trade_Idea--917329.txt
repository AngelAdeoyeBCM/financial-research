# AUD rates: Take profit on paid Feb/May RBA, add Feb/Mar RBA

Joey Li, Markets Strategist, APAC | Chandresh Jain, EM Asia FX and Australia’s inflation continues to run above target We have maintained a paid bias in front-end AUD rates since Q4 last year, as inflation continued to surprise to the upside and remains above Reserve Bank of Australia’s (RBA) target (Figure 1). We initiated a paid position in Feb/May RBA ahead of the Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA December 2025 meeting, citing the risk of a more hawkish stance due to upside surprises in inflation (see AUD rates: Pay Feb/May RBA spread, dated 5 December 2025). The central bank did adopt a more hawkish tone at its December meeting as we had expected, ruling out rate cuts and warning of potential rate hikes if the inflation momentum proved more persistent than expected. Since then, front-end AUD rates have exhibited asymmetric pricing behavior, with a greater tendency to price in additional hikes on strong data while responding in a more muted fashion to weak data, and the spread has widened over time (Figure 2). Q4 CPI print to decide Feb RBA: With the Q4 CPI release scheduled for Wednesday (28 January), markets are expecting a 0.9% q/q print in the trimmed mean, exceeding the RBA’s November forecast of 0.8% q/q. However, economist estimates vary widely, ranging from 0.7% to 1.0%. As a result, Feb RBA remains only 60% priced, at 15bp. Currently, market consensus seems to be that the RBA will hold if the print comes in at 0.8% q/q, but will hike if it reaches 0.9% q/q. ## KEY MESSAGES

We are rotating our paid position in Feb/May RBA to a paid position in Feb/Mar RBA, as risk/reward looks more attractive to run the latter into Australia’s Q4 CPI release tomorrow. We maintain our paid bias in front-end AUD rates, as inflation has been running hot, and markets’ reaction function remains skewed to the upside. At current levels of 4bp, Feb/Mar RBA looks more attractive, as we think markets should price in a greater likelihood of back-to-back rate hikes if Q4 CPI comes in strong. Restructuring our position this way locks in some of our gains and limits our losses in downside scenarios, while remaining positioned for further upside amid a potential Reserve Bank of Australia hiking cycle. ## KEY MESSAGES

We continue to maintain our paid bias in front-end AUD rates, as inflation has been running hot, and markets’ reaction function remains skewed to the upside. We take profit on our paid position in Feb/May RBA, as we think risk-reward is no longer attractive to run it into the Q4 CPI release tomorrow. At these levels, the Feb/Mar RBA looks more attractive, as we think markets should price in a greater likelihood of back-to-back rate hikes if Q4 CPI comes in strong. Restructuring our positions this way locks in some of our gains, limits our losses in downside scenarios, while remain positioned for further upside amidst the RBA’s potential hiking cycle. Unless Q4 CPI prints at 1.0% q/q, this spread is unlikely to price in more than 25bp. On the other hand, a downside surprise could cause the spread to quickly retrace towards 10–12bp. Feb/Mar RBA spread looks more attractive: The Feb/Mar RBA spread, however, remains compressed at 4bp (Figure 1), as markets tend to place more weight on the RBA’s Statement on Monetary Policy meetings. While this was true in 2025, with the RBA remaining cautious in delivering rate cuts, we think this dynamic can change if Q4 CPI surprises the RBA to the upside again (i.e. a 0.9% q/q or higher print), as markets will have to price in a higher probability of back-to-back rate hikes. On the other hand, if CPI comes in soft, the loss on the position is limited to 2-3bp. Alternative scenario: There is a possibility that Q4 trimmed mean CPI comes in moderately strong (e.g. 0.85% q/q), and the market remains split between a hike and a hold. In such a scenario, if the RBA eventually chooses not to deliver a hike in February, we think rate-hike pricing for February should shift to the next two meetings. With how little is priced in March RBA versus May RBA, we think the Feb/Mar spread stands out as the most to gain, especially if markets start to view the RBA as behind the curve. As such, we rotate our position in front-end AUD rates, shifting from a paid position in Feb/May RBA to Feb/Mar RBA. Although both positions are likely to move in the same direction as the data evolves, restructuring the trade this way allows us to lock in some of our existing gains, while also improving the risk/reward profile. By doing so, we limit our potential losses in the event of a downside surprise while maintaining exposure to potential further upside in the event of a hiking cycle by the RBA.
