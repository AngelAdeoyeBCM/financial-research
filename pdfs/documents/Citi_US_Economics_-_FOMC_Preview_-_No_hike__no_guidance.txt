# US Economics FOMC Preview - No hike, no guidance

## CITI'S TAKE

Expectations for Wednesday's meeting are low with no hike expected and no guidance likely to be delivered. Attention will focus on Chair Warsh's general tone and how sensitive (or not) policy will be to declining equity prices. n | No policy action is expected at this week's FOMC meeting, there will be no summary of economic projections and Chair Warsh has pledged not to give guidance. That leaves expectations low for what can come out of this meeting. Warsh's read of inflation should remain hawkish. August core inflation surprised to the upside ahead of the September meeting, but that was due to an aberrant spike in cell phone services prices. September core inflation also surprised higher and can't be as easily dismissed with stronger shelter prices, a key contributor. Meanwhile, labor markets have been "stable. " Warsh will likely repeat that the committee will act to tighten policy until inflation is declining at a sufficient speed. He may also repeat that the committee has removed only a "dose" of accommodation which would seem to suggest more policy rate hikes to come. Most interesting will be how he discusses the recent volatility in markets. With the S&P 500 only slightly off its highs, Warsh may be reluctant to say much about financial conditions having "tightened. " In the past he has said that he seeks to take signals from markets which could sound dovish if repeated as interest rates are now reflecting less hawkish policy expectations. In our base case we expect the FOMC to raise rates another 25bp in December. But an additional rate hike is now dependent on whether the equity sell-off continues. We expect the Fed to begin cutting rates in June of next year at which time clearer evidence should have emerged that inflation is slowing to target.
