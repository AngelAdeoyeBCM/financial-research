# Japan: Supply constraints suppress growth

FOCUS 16/02/2026 1

Ryutaro Kono, Chief Economist, Japan | Hiroshi Shiraishi, Senior Economist, Japan | BNPP Securities (Japan) Limited. Sources: Cabinet office, BNP Paribas Fig.1: GDP (sa, JPY trn) Softer-than-expected Q4 GDP: The preliminary report for Q4 2025 showed that Japan’s real GDP grew lower-than-expected at just 0.1 % q/q (0.2 % saar) after an outright contraction in Q3 (−0.7 % q/q or –2.6 % annualized). Compared with our own forecast, business investment and public investment were softer than expected, while inventory investment provided a larger-than-expected negative contribution. The negative growth in Q3 2025 was driven by the pull-back of front-loaded shipments to the US and a plunge in residential investment caused by the tightening of regulations. In Q4, exports essentially stopped falling and residential investment rebounded, which turned growth back to positive. However, the recovery lacks vigor. Polarized consumption: Personal consumption grew modestly by 0.1 % q/q in Q4 after a 0.2 % increase in Q3. The tepid increase was partly due to higher-than-average temperatures, which suppressed sales of apparel and other weather-sensitive items. Worker compensation increased firmly in Q4, rising 1.0 % q/q in nominal terms and 0.5 % q/q in real terms, helped by larger winter bonuses at major firms. With the rising stock market also generating some wealth effects, sentiment among high-income households is likely to hold up. That said, rising prices continue to be a headwind for low- and middle-income households, and personal consumption increasingly seems to be getting polarized in Japan. Supply-side constraints: Business investment recorded an increase of just 0.2% q/q in Q4 following a 0.3 % contraction in Q3. The Bank of Japan’s December Tankan survey indicated that the corporates’ capex appetite remains strong. Still, the high-investment plans partly highlight the higher construction costs and capital-goods prices caused by the JPY’s depreciation and rising labour costs. Overall, the muted real GDP growth reflects, in no small part, a low potential growth rate and the economy already close to full employment, in our view. On a nominal basis, GDP grew by a solid 0.6 % q/q (2.3 % q/q), and the GDP deflator remained strong at 3.4 % y/y in Q4 (versus 3.5 % in Q3).

## KEY MESSAGES

We now expect Japan’s real GDP to grow at 0.6% y/y in 2026, down 0.1pp from our previous forecast, and 0.9% y/y in 2027, up 0.1pp from our earlier expectation. The downward revision to 2026 forecast is due to the lower base effect resulting from the weaker-than-expected Q4 2025 GDP. The lower-than expected real GDP print in Q4 2025 reflects, in no small part, a low potential growth rate and the economy already close to full employment, in our view. We are not pessimistic about the cyclical outlook in the near term, as external conditions are likely to hold up, while domestic fiscal policy stays expansionary. However, we expect a severe labour shortage to remain a drag on real growth. E M – L O CA L E YE IEW 216/02/2026 Cyclical outlook is not looking bad in the near term: While deteriorating Japan–China relations are likely to keep inbound tourism consumption subdued for a while, the January manufacturing PMI pointed to a rebound in exports and production. Manufacturing activity will likely stay propped up by the AI-investment boom mainly in the US. We forecast the spring wage negotiations to yield a roughly 5 % pay rise (a base-up of over 3 %). At the same time, we expect food-price inflation to moderate, reducing the risk of a sharp slowdown in household consumption. Fiscal policy to stay loose: The e xpansionary fiscal stance under the current administration should continue to lift aggregate demand. At the end of last year, the Takaichi administration decided to introduce a gasoline tax cut and raise the minimum taxable income. The government also drafted a supplemental budget of JPY18.3trn. In the FY2026 budget proposal, primary spending (i.e., spending other than debt-servicing expenses) is slated to increase by over JPY4trn compared with the initial FY2025 budget. In addition, a consumption-tax reduction will likely be implemented sometime next year. Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi has said that deficit-financing government bonds will not be issued, but we do not expect the entire tax cut to be funded by cutting spending and raising other taxes. We expect the government to draw on non-tax revenues so that, net of all measures, the policy would amount to a meaningful fiscal stimulus (see Japan: Consumption tax cut plan - fiscal food for thought, dated 16 February 2026). Labour shortage to drag down real growth: Nevertheless, a severe labour shortage is likely to remain a drag on real GDP growth. While firms are likely to keep investing, mainly in labour-saving equipment, a significant expansion of domestic production capacity is unlikely, in our view. Amid this backdrop, we think the impact of expansionary fiscal policy will not substantially boost the real growth rate; a significant part of its impact will probably show up as upward pressure on prices. New forecasts: We now expect Japan’s real GDP to grow 0.6% y/y in 2026, down 0.1pp from our previous forecast, and 0.9% y/y in 2027, up 0.1pp from our earlier expectation. The downward revision to 2026 is due to the lower base effect resulting from the weaker-than-expected Q4 2025 GDP. We have revised up quarterly growth profile for the coming years, reflecting the expansionary shift in fiscal policy under the Takaichi administration, but we did not make large revisions given the above-mentioned supply-side constraints. Note that we have yet to factor in a consumption tax cut as its funding source and the timing of its implication remain uncertain at this point. However, we do not intend to make large upward revisions to the real GDP growth rate due to the above-mentioned supply-side constraints.
