# G10 FX: Entering a long NZDCAD trade

Ishan Gurnani, G10 FX Strategist | BNP Paribas Securities Corp |. Sources: Macrobond, BNP Paribas. Fig. 1: First principal components of surveys in New Zealand vs Canada highlight divergence in sentiment We are adding a long NZDCAD spot trade to our strategy portfolio, targeting a move to 0.82. The position captures two core views:

1. divergence in domestic sentiment and
2. the CAD’s vulnerability to elevated trade uncertainty. On the latter, we were long USDCAD via options given little premium in the vol surface but took a profit on the trade due to Fed independence concerns. Engaging in NZDCAD instead helps to remove broader USD risk. NZDCAD as an RV expression: We would characterize Canada and New Zealand as “late-cycle economies” given aggressive easing by their central banks thus far, and markets now pricing the next move as a rate hike for both. The RBNZ is priced for 44bp by year end, and the BoC for 16bp. Easier financial conditions appear to be feeding through well to soft data in New Zealand. Forward-looking indicators are rebounding, suggesting that the worst might be in the rear-view mirror for the economy, and thus we approach the NZD with cautious optimism. In contrast, despite easier financial conditions in Canada as well, we are more doubtful about seeing a similar economic rebound. This is because of trade uncertainty acting as a drag, which we suspect will be evident through to the USMCA review on 1 July at the very least. In his preliminary assessment of USMCA, the USTR Ambassador Greer said that “the shortcomings are such that a rubberstamp of the agreement is not in the national interest” and President Trump said the accord is “no real advantage to us, it’s irrelevant really.” We interpret this as political posturing ahead of negotiations, but when assessing rhetoric about withdrawing from the agreement versus splitting it into bilateral treaties, we think this period of uncertainty will be reflected in future Canadian data. This suggests continued CAD underperformance on crosses, and we think the contrast with the data in New Zealand sets up an attractive RV expression. Finally, we remain on the lookout for any news on the US Supreme Court IEEPA tariff decision. If tariffs are struck down, we expect the Trump administration to swiftly proceed with alternative levies, which our prior analysis highlights disproportionately hurt Canadian exports (see US: SCOTUS vs. IEEPA, dated 5 November 2025).
