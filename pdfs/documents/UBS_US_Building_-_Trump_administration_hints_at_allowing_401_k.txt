# US Building Trump administration hints at allowing 401(k

First Read

withdrawals for home down payments Hassett hints at plan to allow for 401(k) withdrawals for home down payments Earlier today, Kevin Hassett (Director of National Economic Council) hinted the Trump administration could announce that homebuyers will be eligible to utilize 401(k) savings (without penalty) to help fund home down payments. Further detail is expected in the context of the President's broader housing plan when he speaks on Wednesday from 8:30am – 9:15am ET at the World Economic Forum in Davos, Switzerland in a session titled " Special Address by Donald J. Trump, President of the United States of America." We also expect incremental headlines and ideas to emerge throughout the week. A potential positive for homebuyers We believe this proposal could be a positive for potential home buyers struggling to fund down payments. According to the National Association of Realtors, 33% of buyers aged 26-34 (prime first-time buyers) indicated that saving for a down payment was the most difficult challenge of the home buying process. Importantly, Fidelity research (link) suggests that average 401(k) balances hit an all-time high of $144K in 3Q25. We also believe the $68tn generational wealth transfer (link) occurring in the US will benefit young homebuyers. In fact, survey work conducted by the National Association of Realtors (NAR) indicated that >25% of homebuyers aged 26-34 who made a down payment received help from a family or a friend. A potential step in the right direction, but what else could help? As we have discussed in previous reports (see below for links), we believe there are numerous avenues the Trump administration can pursue to support the housing market. In the short term, the administration can leverage the GSEs to subsidize mortgages, purchase more MBS for their own portfolios ($200bn already announced) and reduce guarantee fees (G-fees), the latter of which equate to over 60bps on a 30 year mortgage. Additionally, the administration could reduce tariffs which we estimate at ~ $9k per home (link). In the intermediate/longer term, the administration could open more federal land for building, cull more timber from federal land and convince younger generations to pursue careers in the construction trades. Finally, in the event of a housing emergency, the administration could exert more pressure on the states to loosen land restrictions, although most of these decisions are made at the local/ municipal level. However, we continue to believe the most powerful long-term, structural solution to the housing shortfall and affordability challenges in the US is infusing technology into the construction process (link).
