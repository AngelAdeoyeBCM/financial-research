# Fed Notes - R-star update lends some weight to Fed hawks

Economics Fed Notes Date 15 January 2026

A longstanding feature of our Fed forecast has been that the nominal neutral rate is higher than the assumptions reflected in the Fed’s SEP. Based on our dashboard, which consolidates estimates from thirteen different indicators, we have built our Fed outl ook around a view that r-star has been in a relatively tight 1.5-1.75% range over the past two years, consistent with a nominal neutral rate of 3.5 -3.75% over the long run when inflation is at target. That estimate is meaningfully above the 3.0% median nominal neutral rate in the Fed’s SEP and all but 4 of the 19 individual projections. One compelling pushback to our view has been that the most widely cited estimates of r-star – estimates from NY Fed President Williams and co-authors that are known as LW and HLW -- have been substantially lower than the average we publish. 1 HLW, in particular, has been the lowest estimate we track, stuck below 1% in recent years. However, recent updates and revisions to LW and HLW move those estimates closer to our view. Updated for Q3 2025 (the last available), estimates of HLW and LW rose to 1.1% and 1.6%, respectively. In addition, recent estimates of HLW have been revised higher. Figure 1: The latest update shows HLW and LW r-star estimates on the rise, with some upward revisions 1 See here: Luzzetti, Ph.D. A key driver of the upgrade to r-star has been the upward revision to the “other determinants” component, which we have referred to as “dark matter” in past publications (see “Dark matter” and “The dark matter determining debt stability”). These are the factors tied to the balance of supply versus demand for savings that are unrelated to potential growth. Indeed, the average revisions between the initial and current vintage of LW and HLW estimates have been +61bp and +50bps over their respective available samples (since 2005 for LW and 2015 for HLW). We, therefore, would not be surprised to see further upward revisions to these data series over the coming quarters if the economy evolves broadly in line with our forecasts – solid growth, labor market stabilization, and lower but still above-target inflation. For Fed officials, the level of neutral has become an increasingly important determinant of near-term policy decisions after the Fed completed another round of rate cuts in December. Dovish-leaning officials point to a 3% estimate of the nominal neutral fed funds rate as an argument for why further rate cuts should be expected over time. Conversely, hawkish-leaning officials have argued that policy is already close to neutral, providing minimal scope for further rate cuts, particularly with inflation still above target. These updates of canonical estimates of r-star provide some support for the latter view.
