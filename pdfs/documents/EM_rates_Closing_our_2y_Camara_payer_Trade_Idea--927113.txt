# EM rates: Closing our 2y Camara payer

We close our 2y Camara payer position as it has touched its stop-loss level, booking a loss of 42.5bp (USD425k). We exited after the Camara curve was repriced lower—a move driven by a rally in core rates, weak Chilean activity and inflation data, and declining oil prices. Moreover, the June monetary-policy report (IPoM) from the BCCh reiterated the market’s view that no rate hikes are expected in the foreseeable future. From a multi-month perspective, we still think the market is overlooking the possibility of the BCCh tightening due to second-order inflation impacts linked to the conflict-driven spike in oil prices and the potential for Fed rate rises as soon as December (for our base case, see US: Changing our Fed call to rate hikes, dated 5 June). To recap, we opened the 2y Camara payer (switching from a Camara-US spread payer) on 1 June in the belief that the existing hiking premium was modest compared with upside inflation risks. We also expected the June IPoM to serve as a pivotal catalyst that could lead the market to price in additional hiking premium (see EM rates: Switching into outright 2y Camara payer).
