## KEY MESSAGES

We expect USDINR to break and sustain a move below 90 after the reduction in US tariffs on India from 50% to 18%. We view a test of 88 as feasible over the coming months. India’s improving economic outlook, driven by the US tariff reduction and recently signed FTAs, slower fiscal consolidation, and the passthrough from previous rate cuts may encourage portfolio inflows, in our view. High vol-adjusted carry value and corporate repatriation flows into the tax year end could also support the rupee. We prefer to sell USDINR on rallies, as we see some near-term USD support and our risk premium indicators signal caution on adding risk. We favour adding long INR on crosses though. Unless a time frame shorter than five years is imposed for the US goods purchases, they shouldn’t materially deteriorate India’s trade balance, in our view. Lower US tariffs, alongside the recent FTAs and slower fiscal consolidation plans (as per the Union Budget), could help economic sentiment recover in India. This could encourage foreign equity inflows to return, especially considering the INR is about 12% weaker (on a NEER basis) than it was a year ago. BNP Paribas Equity Research also holds a constructive view on local equities – see Budget FY 27: Tailwinds for consumption as well as capex now (Kunal Vora, 1 February). USDINR has a strong relationship with foreign flows, whereby a USD10bn foreign inflow over a 3m period could spur a ~2.5% decline in USDINR (Figure 2). Carry value, potential corporate repatriation flows and improving economic data also supportive: We note the INR offers higher carry value on a vol-adjusted basis than many global peers (Figure 3), which could also encourage inflows over the medium term amid a supportive global risk environment. We also see scope for repatriation flows from corporates, as the tax year end approaches in March, which could provide some support to the rupee (Figure 4). Further, during our recent meetings with local banks and corporates in India, our impression is that there is an FX backlog of exporters which refrained from selling US dollars in the last couple of months with the FX volatility. Therefore, stabilisation in USDINR now could trigger more exporter USD selling flows into INR. Further, we believe policy rate cuts from last year and liquidity added by the RBI should spur economic growth momentum, with some lag. We are already seeing a pick-up in credit growth, industrial production, car sales and airline passenger movements. This should also support a rise in companies’ earnings growth and encourage foreign equity inflows, in our view. Preferred trades: We see some near-term USD support, and our risk premium indicators are signaling caution on adding risk now, so we are biased to sell USDINR on rallies. We favour adding INR on crosses though. In FX: Five key drivers for 2026 (19 January), we flagged long INRIDR as one of the trades to consider in Q1. Fiscal risks and an easing bias from policymakers, may continue to weigh on the IDR, considering IDR’s carry value has diminished. Hence, IDR may benefit less in a risk-supported carry environment.
