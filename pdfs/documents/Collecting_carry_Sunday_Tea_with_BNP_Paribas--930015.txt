# Collecting carry Sunday Tea with BNP Paribas

Sunday Tea with BNPP: Collecting carry

## OUR KEY VIEWS

- Recent economic data are unlikely to tip the scales in either direction for the Fed.
- With earnings season nearly over and no tier -1 data until next month, we expect volatility to trend lower for the next few weeks.
- Our playbook for the rest of the summer is to remain long risk, collect carry and sell risk premium. Last week’s US inflation prints also failed to do what the latest employment print could not: tip the market in either direction regarding the outlook for the Fed. Indeed, we had written that the pivot point on inflation would be around 0.25%, with cooler numbers likely pushing back expectations for a rate hike, and hotter numbers bringing them forward. What happened left us in limbo; the results of the CPI and PPI prints together currently map to a 0.25%m/m reading on core PCE to be released later this month. At face value, the latest inflation prints should tilt the scales in favor of a hike next month. Indeed, if presented with this number last month, Governor Waller would have arguably voted for a hike. And the number is higher than the 0.2%m/m average reading that NY Fed President Williams has cond itioned his on-hold view upon. However, much of the upside in July PCE is expected to be driven by portfolio services – a category that is being redone, and likely biased lower, by the release of August’s PCE report. What this means is that the Fed will likely remain data-point dependent, with the next employment and inflation numbers heavily influen cing market pricing of the Fed. Unfortunately, those data releases don’t come until next month, with no tier -1 economic data releases scheduled for the rest of August. Additionally, almost all of US Q2 earnings season is behind us (except for NVDA on 26 August). And with the US administr ation appearing unwilling to launch a large-scale attack in the Middle East, geopolitical headline risk is increasingly having less of a market impact. Meanwhile, we remain positive on global economic growth. The US economy is expected to grow by more than 8% nominally in Q3. And although this week’s eurozone activity surveys could be negatively affected by recent extreme hot weather, it would not change our constructive view on Europe. The underlying momentum of the eurozone economy remains robust, as suggested by Q2 GDP numbers. Consequently, we think the playbook for the remainder of the summer is to remain long risk, collect carry, and sell risk premium. In US equities, single-name implied volatility remains elevated and skew is flat. We continue to prefer mega-cap c all ratios as upside expressions. Overall equity positioning is starting to rebuild but remains near neutral territory (BNPEPUS: 61/100). From a flow perspective, system atic (CTA & Vol target) flows are skewed to ward buying in the immediate term. These flows have a self-reinforcing effect in a low volatility spot trend that we model growing to +$ 15bn/week. The period into mid - September also coincides with the peak in S&P 500 corporate buybacks, where we model an additional $30bn a week of equity demand. With lower vol begetting more inflows, we like structures that play for a grind higher in stocks over the next month. Indicatively, a QQQ US Sep26 750/780 Call Ratio 1x1.5 offers 4.4x max payout. In rates, despite our structural call for higher yields, we see scope for a tactical rally in Europe through the end of the summer. We are entering the lowest week of EGB supply and, based on recent moves in equities and bonds, Dutch pension fund rebalancing flows are likely to be on the receiving side. Meanwhile in the US, we hold short vol bias. We like to short atm vol on 2y5y, 3y5y, 5y10y and 10y10y, and short receiver skew (e.g. 50bp lower) on 3y2y and 2y2y. These vols all screen as relatively rich in RV. In FX, we are long AUDJPY. AUD screens as cheap relative to its short-term fair value (-1.7 z-scores, according to our STEER TM model), while having the largest coefficient to global equities. The trade also adds to our short JPY delta (via a USDJPY call EKO) as without any shifts in fundamentals, the case for the JPY to further unwind its post-intervention gains remains strong. We also remain short CLPCOP. Not only does the trade carry very well, but it reflects our multi-month bullish fundamental outlook on the COP. In the vol space, FX pairs have outperformed implied breakevens over recent US event risks, reflecting market sensitivity to US monetary policy risks. In addition, heavy gamma positioning around spot in EURUSD, where the strike map indicates high gamma positioning between 1.14-1.16, with a particularly large upcoming expiry in the 1.15 bucket, may further anchor spot in the current range.
