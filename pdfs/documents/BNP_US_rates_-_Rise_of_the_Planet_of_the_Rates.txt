# US rates: Rise of the Planet of the Rates

## OUR KEY VIEWS

- Futures CTD switch risk could exacerbat e the rise in long-end yields. If fiscal concerns rise, and the curve bear steepens, the CTD switch risk could drive an even bigger rise in yields.
- We expect the unemployment rate in the US to decline to 4%, exerting upward pressure on yields across the curve.
- Trade: Extend stop on short 30y UST. Entry: 5.36%. Target: 5.80%. Stop: 5.50%. Size: $50k/01. On Friday, we extended our short 30y UST target by 20bp to 5.80%. This follows last week's 15 – 20bp sell-off, driven by robust economic data, hawkish Fed rhetoric, lackluster Treasury auctions, underwhelming buyback announcements/operations, and volatile oil prices (see Extend target on short 30y UST, dated 25 September). While no critical data prints are scheduled for the remainder of the month, the sell off appears to be holding momentum, exhibiting greater acuity than oil price action. A primary mechanism previously identified is the cheapest-to-deliver (CTD) switch risk. The negative convexity profile inherent in long-end futures contracts can induce further duration selling during selloffs (see Don't fade this, dated 23 September). As we are within a few basis points of a switch, the sharp widening in the cash-futures basis may prompt preemptive hedging (or selling) by certain investors, which in turn drives a further selloff. Furthermore, Figure 1 illustrates that the negative convexity profile of the US contract can be exacerbated by a steepening yield curve. As an example, a 10bp parall el selloff can induce a 1 4% duration extension in the USZ6 contract. Meanwhile, the same selloff on top of 6bp of steepening in the USZ6 futures basket curve (15-25y maturity range) can induce a 28% duration extension. Should fiscal concerns resurface, driven by the Treasury's expanding interest expense (see The Debt Knight Rises, dated 22 September), a bear steepening of the curve could simultaneously accelerate and expand the futures duration extension. Regarding upcoming macro data, payroll estimates sit at 90k (see US jobs preview, dated 25 September). However, Trump's immigration policies have put more focus on the unemployment rate. Therefore, even a downside surprise in payrolls could still pose downside risks for the unemployment rate given the low breakeven rate required. Notably, 10 of the 15 last September nonfarm payroll prints resulted in downside surprises for the unemployment rate (see Figure 2). Our baseline is for the unemployment rate to fall to 4%, which should put upside pressure on the 'rate expectations' component of back-end yields. Trade: Extend stop on short 30y UST. Entry: 5.36%.
