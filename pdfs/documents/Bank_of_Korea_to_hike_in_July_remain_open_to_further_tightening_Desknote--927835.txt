# Bank of Korea to hike in July, remain open to further tightening

. 09/07/2026 1

RKE.

## KEY MESSAGES

- We expect the Bank of Korea to raise the policy rate by 25bp to 2.75% at the Monetary Policy Board meeting on 16 July, marking the first hike since January 2023. The decision is likely to be unanimous.
- While the BoK will not release new economic forecasts or forward guidance at this meeting, the central bank will closely monitor recent developments, including stronger GDP growth, the broadening of inflationary pressures amid economic recovery, a rise in financial stability risks, and a surge in financial market volatility, in our view.
- We anticipate the BoK will maintain its current tone and remain open to further tightening. After July, we continue to project another hike to 3.00% in October, followed by a prolonged hold, though risks remain tilted toward further rate hikes in 2027. 25bp rate hike highly likely: We expect the Bank of Korea to raise the policy rate by 25bp to 2.75% at the Monetary Policy Board meeting on 16 July. This would be the first rate hike since January 2023, when the BoK hiked by 25bp to 3.50%. The decision is likely to be unanimous.
- Several factors in play: While the BoK will not release new economic forecasts or forward guidance at this meeting, the central bank will closely monitor recent developments, including stronger GDP growth, the broadening of inflationary pressures amid economic recovery, a rise in financial stability risks, and a surge in financial market volatility.
- GDP growth: We believe the BoK sees upside risks to its 2.6% GDP growth forecast for 2026, supported by upward revisions in Q1 GDP data. The central bank could judge that robust semiconductor exports, large corporate investment pledges, and a potential stabilisation of Middle East tensions, despite ongoing uncertainty, pose upside risks.
- CPI inflation: We think the BoK will maintain its cautious tone. While Brent oil prices have stabilised since the May meeting, falling considerably below the BoK’s projection of USD95/bbl for H2 2026, uncertainties persist. Potential demand-side inflationary pressures cannot be ignored amid ongoing corporate and minimum wage negotiations. Relatedly, the BoK noted in its Parliamentary address on 9 July that the price diffusion index, which tends to rise when domestic demand is recovering, had risen to its highest level since 2010, excluding the pandemic period. FX pass-through risks also linger due to USDKRW volatility. As such, we think the BoK will continue to flag the risks of prolonged high inflation.
- Household debt: Two concerns remain. First, Seoul metropolitan area housing prices continue to accelerate, which could lead to a further increase in mortgage loans. While the government has introduced new macroprudential measures and is considering tax changes, and banks have tightened lending, the effectiveness of these steps in cooling the market has yet to be seen. Second, ‘other loans’ have continued to rise within household lending, suggesting increased household risk appetite for the domestic equity market. Overall, we believe the BoK could be worried about an increase in household leverage in such a volatile environment.
- Financial markets: We think the BoK will view the FX market with caution, given uncertainties in global central banks ’ monetary policies, including the risk of Fed rate hikes, developments in the Middle East conflict, and foreign investor behaviour in the domestic equity market. We think the BoK is likely less concerned about the recent correction in equities and more focused on financial stability, especially after a recent surge in retail participation. The central bank may worry about the aggressive risk-seeking behaviour of domestic investors and the resulting repercussions of a sharp rise or fall in equities. BoK to remain open to additional rate hikes: We see few reasons for the Bank of Korea to change its overall tone from the previous meeting. We expect its forward guidance to be along the lines of: “The board will decide the timing of further rate hikes while assessing the extent of the increase in inflationary pressures, the improvement in the domestic economy, and financial stability.” We do not think the BoK will fully rule out back-to-back rate hikes, although it is not our base case. After July, we expect another hike to 3.00% in October, followed by a prolonged hold, though risks remain tilted toward further tightening in 2027. Jeeho Yoon, Senior South Korea and Taiwan Economist | BNP Paribas SA Seoul Branch
