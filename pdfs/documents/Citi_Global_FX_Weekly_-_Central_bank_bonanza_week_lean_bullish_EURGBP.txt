# Global FX Weekly - Central bank bonanza week lean bullish EURGBP

Global FX Strategy

## CITI’S TAKE

This week sees five G10 central bank meetings (Fed, ECB, BoE, BoJ, and BoC), though little is expected as officials await further inflation data and as uncertainty persists around the Strait of Hormuz. While into the Fed, we think follow-through for USD will be limited without an SEP to recalibrate market expectations and with Senator Tillis no longer blocking Kevin Warsh’s confirmation. Elsewhere, we favor EURGBP upside on potential central bank divergence and political/fiscal risks in the UK, and watch for MoF intervention warnings should the BoJ disappoint market pricing and USDJPY break above n | Global FX Strategy This week sees five major G10 central bank meetings (Fed, ECB, BoE, BoJ, and BoC), though in all cases little action is expected as bankers await further data and as uncertainty persists around the Strait of Hormuz (Figure 1). Rather, markets will be focused on any potential guidance towards future meetings, especially as many ensuing June meetings are “live.” Generally, markets are pricing in some potential for hikes in June, though Citi house views are for none of these banks to hike by then except for the ECB. Expectations for upcoming G10 central bank meetings Source: Citi Research, Citi Velocity Overall, we continue to see inflation risks in the coming months, with inflation concerns likely to be increasing into these meetings given the Strait of Hormuz remains closed (Citi Commodities has pushed back its base case for the Strait re-opening to late May from mid-April and has raised its oil price forecasts higher for the year). Still, we do not expect central bankers will want to commit to action this week, which could cause some marginal disappointment in hawkish expectations for June meetings. However, market pricing may not react much to dovish disappointments as inflation data is still to come—this remains the primary factor for central bank expectations, and uptick in headline inflation more broadly over Q2 (Figure 2). Ultimately, we like leaning long EURGBP into this week. Both central banks are similarly priced through the coming months; however, as we note below, we see a different reaction function between the ECB and the BoE going forward. Commentary from ECB members suggests risk of a more hawkish path—even compared to current pricing. Meanwhile, the BoE may push back on hawkish pricing and could fail to deliver hikes even in light of upcoming inflation data. We forecast EURGBP higher over the year—partially on relative central banks, but also on political/fiscal risks for the UK (Figure 3), while fiscal policy is supportive for the EU. While we do not expect the meetings this week will be a major driver of EURGBP higher (given limited impact from the meetings on relative rate pricing), on the margin they should reflect the more hawkish lean from the ECB versus the BoE, which should still give some support to the pair. With the market short USD and pricing the Fed as the lone G10 central bank to cut rates this year (Figures 4 and 5), we similarly think asymmetry tilts in favor of USD, at least on any marginal shift in characterizing the economic backdrop. We also found the themes in Fed Governor Waller’s most recent speech noteworthy, and such themes could become part of this meeting’s deliberations. Namely, a lower NFP breakeven rate implies low jobs growth “may not be the warning sign” it once was, and a series of inflationary supply shocks makes the standard “look through” argument more problematic. We think it is possible that Powell could reflect such framing in his press conference remarks. Follow-through for USD, however, might be muted. The main reasons are:

1. there is no updated SEP to recalibrate market expectations;
2. the DOJ is dropping the Powell investigation, allowing Senator Tillis to support Kevin Warsh’s nomination and keeping a dovish premium in the Fed curve;
3. we do not expect Powell to announce he is staying on the Board at the presser—though this will likely be asked and could drive market reaction;
4. we expect FX to quickly revert to trading US- Iran headlines where markets remain optimistic despite our skepticism; and
5. Citi Markets expects a strong USD-sell signal around month-end FX hedging flows. As a reminder, big picture, we flipped neutral USD and lowered our DXY forecast during last week’s update, as USD battles crosscurrents around relative rates (USD-negative) versus terms of trade and US-Iran reescalation risk (USD-positive). A dovish premium is embedded in the Fed curve relative to other G10 central banks Source: Citi Research, Bloomberg Source: Citi Research, Bloomberg ECB Citi Econ Take: European Economics - ECB: Wait and Warn now, Hike Later Citi Economics has increased its modal expectation for the ECB, from staying on hold this year to seeing two 25bps hikes, in June and July respectively. This change is a function of an increasing risk of a prolonged energy disruption combined with communication by members of the Governing Council which points to a very low level of patience should the supply shock last a bit too long. We expect language at this meeting to focus on uncertainty, with the ECB remaining patient as it awaits more inflation data before making any decisions. However, there could be a hawkish lean to the commentary related to inflation expectations in light of multiple inflation shocks (a similar comment was made by Waller; see Fed section). Last meeting, Lagarde noted: “inflation expectations have a lot to do with the memory... now the memory is rather fresh because people have seen inflation. So the reaction function that they will have in terms of investment, in terms of wage negotiations and in terms of consumption is going to be informed by a fresher memory of inflation” (ECB 03/19/26). We have been flagging that global central bankers may remain more hawkish than expected—despite knowing that hiking into a commodity supply shock could be a policy error—due to concerns around de-anchoring inflation expectations as we move into yet another inflation shock (i.e., do consumers begin to worry that there is something structurally driving inflation higher, rather than this being another idiosyncratic inflation shock?). In that context, and given the Strait of Hormuz remains closed, there is some marginal hawkish risk for the ECB. But again, we do not think they would go so far as to commit to action in June, which would limit how much markets can really adjust already hawkish June meeting pricing (which would limit EUR upside). Global FX Strategy BoE Citi Econ Take: UK Economics and Rates – BoE: the case for holding Citi UK Economics is calling for no BoE hikes this year; rather, they expect the policy rate will remain on hold at 3.75%. Citi UK Rates expects Governor Bailey could once again push back on market pricing for hikes this year—as he did on 1 April. A 9–0 or an 8–1 vote split for no hike could further reinforce the MPC is likely to remain on hold for the foreseeable future. We have sympathy for this dovish potential, and relative central bank expectations are one of the factors causing us to forecast EURGBP higher over 2026 (other factors include UK political and fiscal risks, and positive fiscal impulse in the EU later in the year). In line with Econ and Rates views above, we see marginal dovish risk into the BoE meeting this week also given the weaker starting point for the UK pre-conflict (BoE was already being priced dovishly) and higher starting point for policy rates (e.g., more room to cut into neutral territory). On the margin, this could see a weaker GBP, but we also see limited market impact from guidance ahead of clarifying inflation data releases in the coming months (a BoE staying on hold in light of new higher inflation data would be a clearer, market-moving dovish signal, in our view). BoJ Citi Econ Take: BoJ Preview – Expecting hawkish hold, voting behavior again in focus The long-held base case of Citi Economics has been a rate hike once every six months, with the next hike coming in July. The two most recent public appearances by Governor Ueda offered no clear signal for an imminent rate hike, and market pricing for a hike has fallen sharply. That said, even if the policy rate is unchanged at this meeting, the hold is likely to be hawkish given concerns about further JPY depreciation and the risk of falling behind the curve. We will be closely watching the voting behavior of board members to gauge the degree of hawkishness. At the March meeting, the number of dissenting votes did not increase, which we interpreted as a signal that the BoJ intends to maintain its wait-and-see stance. An increase in the number of dissenting votes increase at this meeting could be interpreted as the BoJ laying the groundwork for a rate hike in the near future. Notably, despite recent USD weakness during the deescalation phase of the US- Iran conflict, USDJPY has remained elevated and EURJPY has climbed to a new all-time high. If the BoJ leaves its policy unchanged at this week’s meeting, USDJPY could well climb past 160. In this case, we think the Japanese government would likely intervene to buy the JPY and try to push USDJPY back to around 155. BoC Citi Econ Take: Canada Economics Weekly – Core inflation subdued with soft demand While we had forecast modest upside for USDCAD in the coming months, the pair has primarily traded to US equities (risk-on) and relative data momentum, which have pushed USDCAD lower along with optimism around PM Carney’s push to attract investment (with the latest development being the creation of a sovereign wealth fund; see Bloomberg, 4/27 /26). Such momentum could continue with tomorrow’s Spring Economic Update (e.g., to the extent higher oil prices improve Global FX Strategy the revenue outlook or a new stimulus is announced) and with Citi Economics expecting modest hawkish risks for the BoC compared to communications in March. Growth and employment data since then have improved, and the latest CFIB survey showed a jump in average price plans, typically a leading indicator for Canadian inflation. Still, Citi Economics maintains their base case for cuts over H2 (September/October), in contrast to current market pricing of roughly 30bps in hikes by year-end. One dovish risk is if the April MPR indicates the neutral rate could be lower (e.g., around updated estimates of potential growth), which would mean current policy is less accommodative than originally thought. We expect any subsequent repricing could eventually weigh on CAD, as would focus on more structural headwinds facing Canada’s economy that weigh on the outlook for growth. But these may take time to materialize, and BoC communication for now is unlikely to be a CAD headwind, in our view.
