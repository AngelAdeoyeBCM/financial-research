# Euro Weekly Digest - Still waiting for signs of second-round inflation pressures

Economics Euro Weekly Digest Date 16 October 2026

Week in review

- Final September HICP – Headline and core confirmed at 3.8% and 2.5%. Services driven by holiday-related items, core goods driven by clothing. Still no sign of the energy shock broadening out.
- EA IP – Another weak monthly print. On track for a contraction in Q3. Nowcast GDP mode ls point to +0.1% q/q in Q3 as stronger survey data are offset by softness in hard data.
- German natural gas reserve – Media reports state the Government is planning a strategic reserve. For more see our blog post.
- EU summit – EU leaders Focus on China, ETS, the budget, and competitiveness.
- French budget – Public debate is underway, but the Government is expected to invoke Article 49.3 or executive ordinances to pass the budget.
- ECB Commentary – No change in messaging. More GC members express calm about the situation in France. Week ahead
- October flash PMI (Fri) – PMIs have been showing strength recently, but also rising cost pressures from energy and wages.
- National surveys (Tue-Thu) – French, German, and the European Commission survey data.
- CES inflation expectations (Fri) – Our own survey showed a strong rise in inflation expectations. A similar rise in the ECB's data we believe would be a sign for concern. Week in review Final September HICP The final September HICP print showed both headline (3.8%) and core (2.5%) were unrevised from the flash print. The details of the final print confirming that the rise in services inflation (from 3.0% in Aug to 3.2%) was entirely driven by holiday-related items, with package holidays and accommodation reversing more than the August declines. Recreational services, which were unusually weak in August, also rebounded. The decline in core goods (from 1.2% in Aug to 1.1%) can be explained by an unexpected drop in clothing, largely driven by Spain. Durables inflation Kuhumita Bhattacharya Research Associate continues to push higher, with IT equipment rising further to 2% YoY, the highest level on record. Evidence of indirect energy effects continues to remain limited. Our high energy-intensive index has risen back to its July levels (3.0%), but as this largely reflects the movement in travel-related components, we read it more as seasonal noise than a clear signal of broadening price pressures. Our domestic inflation indicator holding steady at 3.5%, reinforces this view. Euro area August IP Euro area industrial production fell 0.5% m/m in August. This headline included a sharp 3.1% m/m decline in Irish production; excluding Ireland, the fall in activity is lower by 0.3% m/m. At the country level, industrial activity exhibited widespread softness during August. Output contracted across almost all major member states: Italian production fell sharply by 1.3% m/m, Spanish output declined by 0.7% m/m, and France recorded a 0.3% m/ m. This broad-based contraction underscores the persistent cyclical and structural headwinds facing European manufacturing, leaving the region's industrial sector on a highly fragile footing. Germany was the sole outlier, with headline production surging by 2.0% m/m to an 18-month high. However, this spike was overwhelmingly driven by a massive 9.3% m/m jump in construction, which was fuelled by a unique one-off completion of a major battery storage infrastructure project in Leuna. Looking at the components, consumer goods production has now contracted by an average of 2.4% m /m for two consecutive months, whil e energy output declined by 0.7% m/m in August for the euro area. Although private consumption continues to show resilience, the impact of rising energy prices is beginning to leave its mark on both hard and soft economic indicators. Industrial production has been weak for two consecutive months in Q3-2026. Should output remain at these levels for the remainder of the period, IP excluding construction is projected to contract by 0.6% q/q in Q3 2026, reversing the robust 0.8% q/q expansion recorded in Q2-2026. Our nowcasting model now points to a GDP growth of +0.1% q/q for Q3-26 with positive picture seen in Q3-26 surveys partially offsetting the softness seen in the hard data. Bear in mind, euro-area growth could undergo a technical correction following the sharp rise in Irish GDP in Q2 2026. France – Budget Debates, Sovereign Friction, and Polling Shifts Public debates on the 2027 budget commenced in France. While recently adopted parliamentary amendments — particularly regarding pensions — are generally pushing the projected deficit higher, most of these expansionary measures are unlikely to feature in the final bill. This is because the government is widely expected to ultimately activate Article 49.3 or resort to executive ordinances, the latter of which would bring the initial, unamended budget draft into force by enacting the budget directly by executive authority. More details on the French budget process and its potential timelines can be found in our dedicated note. In other news, Marine Le Pen has reiterated several of the party's core foreign policy stances, explaining that the Rassemblement National (RN) intends to renegotiate France's net contribution to the EU and reduce financial support to Ukraine. While we observe a strategic shift toward domestic fiscal orthodoxy from the RN leadership, European and international relations remain key areas where significant policy friction is likely going forward. Detailed analysis of the RN's platform on Europe, the mechanics of EU decision-making, and how the party could disrupt these processes can be found in our recent Push and Pull series. Turning to the latest presidential election polling, the numbers suggest that Raphaël Glucksmann (Socialist Party, PS) is gaining significant momentum following his victory in the Socialist primary. Glucksmann has crossed the 15% threshold in first-round surveys, placing him within reach of a potential second - round run-off. His ascent appears to be coming primarily at the expense of potential centre-right candidate Édouard Philippe (Horizons), who has lost ground in first-round polling over the past two weeks. German natural gas reserve According to media reports, the German government plans to establish a national gas reserve as a step for energy security during exceptional crises, such as sabotage, extreme weather, or rare major incidents. The reserve is initially expected to amount to 24 terawatt hours (TWh), equivalent to roughly 10% of Germany's gas storage capacity. The draft bill, proposed by the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Energy, outlines the creation of additional physical gas stockpiles that can only be released during an officially declared state of emergency. This safeguarding mechanism is specifi cally designed to prevent market distortion, ensuring that the strategic reserve does not disrupt commercial winter-preparedness measures or lead to lower commercial storage levels. For more details, see our Germany Blog. EU summit (Oct 15-16) Key takeaways from the October 15-16 EU summit
- EU-China trade relations: As expected, EU leaders tasked the Commission to strengthen the bloc's trade defence toolbox. New instruments could include a new diversification instrument, a more easily applicable deterrent for trade dispute escalations and potentially the launch of safeguard investigations into the chemical and machinery sectors. The Commission will likely present proposals for such new tools ahead of the December 17-18 summit.
- EU ETS: Leaders remained divided over carbon pricing, notably ETS2 (covering the heating and transport sector). Italy and several CEE countries pushed for a postponement beyond 2028. No such delay was agreed, with the compromise instead calling for a fresh Commi ssion impact assessment of ETS2.
- EU budget 2028-34: Leaders failed to bridge major differences over the next multiannual budget and postponed the negotiations to a special summit on 26 November.
- Competitiveness: France and Germany reached a compromise combining tighter "Made in Europe" provisions under the Industrial Accelerator Act with greater flexibility on EU car emissions rules. The 2035 CO2 reduction target would be lowered to 90% (from 100%), with another 1 0pp of flexibility for investments in alternative fuels. ECB Commentary. ECB commentary
- Future rate hikes: Stournaras said the ECB might stick with raising rates gradually. Wunsch said the ECB would probably have to hike again (and did not exclude anything on the timing of the next hike). Simkus said the data suggests more tightening may be needed. Kazaks said there is no immediate need to rush on rates and the trade-offs for monetary policy are getting trickier.
- Second-round effects: Lagarde, Moulin, and Simkus said we are not seeing any effects yet.
- Inflation: Moulin said inflation is being driven by energy, and core inflation remains quite stable. Pereira said he is not seeing inflation broadening out beyond inflation.
- France: Wunsch said some market pressure is good for governments, and they should work on reducing debt. Lagarde said that bond yields are rising across the world, and France's bond situation is linked to fiscal policies. Moulin said increase in yields are putting pressure on financial conditions, and France must show commitment to budget restrained. He noted France has reduced the deficit quite significantly in the pass and it is totally possible for France to reduce the current deficit. Simkus said French yields are rising due to economic developments, and he doesn't see a role for the ECB at present. Kocher said we are not at the stage where the ECB discusses bond-market tools. Week ahead October Flash PMI (Fri) We have the October flash PMI on Friday. The latest PMI data continues to signal robust growth for the euro area, with the current reading representing the strongest expansion observed in three and a half years. The services sector continues to outperform manufacturing, thereby reinforcing the narrative of domestic economic resilience. Nevertheless, escalating input cost pressures remain a key concern. Most member states are experiencing a sharp rise in input prices, driven predominantly by escalating fuel and energy costs alongside emerging wage pressures. Although output prices have al so risen, firms are currently absorbing a portion of these cost pressures, which temporarily mitigate the risk of the energy shock broadening. This containment is particularly significant given that the ECB remains highly attentive to rising wage pressures and potential second-round inflation effects. Notably, the ECB's forward-looking wage tracker has yet to indicate any such second-round effects. National surveys (Tue-Thu) Ahead of the PMI releases, the October survey calendar includes several key data, starting with the ZEW economic sentiment on Tuesday, followed on Thursday by the French INSEE business confidence and the European Commission's flash consumer confidence indicator. French INSEE business confidence is currently hovering close to its long-term average, supported by the own-production outlook indicator, which has maintained a strong upward trajectory in recent months. Conversely, the European Commission's consumer confidence indicator delivered a some what weaker signal last month; consumer confidence fell by a full point in September, reversing the recovery observed over the preceding months. This decline underscores the fact that consumer sentiment remains highly sensitive to shifts in inflation and household purchasing power. September CES inflation expectations (Fri) The ECB will publish their measure of household inflation expectations from the Consumer Expectations Survey (CES) on Friday. We have our own proprietary measure of household inflation expectations which was released at the start of the month. Our data showed that after having been steadily declining for a number of months now (since the initial shock of the war in Iran), b oth short- and long-term expectations saw a significant rise across the euro area, with 5–10-year expectations up c.30bp to 2.31%. Short term expectations increased by a similar amount to 2.50%. The increase in inflation expectations seen in the dbDataInsights poses a risk for the ECB. Not only was the increase large in size, but the rise in average expectations was driven by a broad shift in the distribution of expectations, and no real decline in inflation expectation confidence – suggesting this is not a story of uncertainty, but a rise in expectations the average respondent believes in. There are differences in the timing of the CES and dbDataInsights surveys, so we will be watching closely to see if the ECB's data shows them a similar rise to what we have seen. Other data German PPI (Tue), EC construction output and current account (Tue) followed by German import prices (Fri). European Commission's to publish annual fiscal data for 2025 The C ommission will be publishing the annual government finance statistics (debt-to-GDP and deficit ratios) for 2025 for European Union countries on Wednesday.
