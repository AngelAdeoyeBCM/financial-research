# AUD rates: Take profit on paid March RBA

Joey Li, Markets Strategist, APAC | Chandresh Jain, EM Asia FX and Rates Strategist | MAR | BNP Paribas Singapore Branch. We take profit on our remaining paid position in March RBA trade idea at 4.105%, capturing a profit of 21bp (USD210k). This trade is part of our ongoing paid bias in front-end AUD rates, a view we had since Q4 last year due to upside inflation surprises that exceeded the RBA’s target (see AUD rates: Pay Feb/May RBA spread, dated 5 December 2025). Ahead of the Q4 CPI release in January, we took profit on our Feb/May RBA payer and added a Feb/Mar RBA payer, as we believed the latter offered better risk–reward (see AUD rates: Take profit on paid Feb/May RBA, add Feb/Mar RBA, dated 27 January 2026). We anticipated markets would continue to exhibit an asymmetric reaction function, with a greater tendency to price in rate hikes on strong data and hawkish rhetoric from the RBA than to remove them in response to weaker data or dovish signals. Since then, strong macro data and hawkish comments from officials continued to support our trade. In early March, we took partial profit on our paid position in March RBA, as key macroeconomic data had been released, and pricing seemed somewhat fair for a meeting that does not coincide with the release of the Statement on Monetary Policy. We chose to retain some paid exposure, however, as we expected high oil prices could increase the risk of a back-to-back rate hike (see AUD rates: Taking partial profit on paid March RBA, dated 3 March 2026). Indeed, oil prices have remained elevated around USD100/bbl. Moreover, RBA Deputy Governor Andrew Hauser last Tuesday said that the recent flow of data reinforces the central bank’s view that the economy has limited supply capacity and affirmed our concern that elevated oil prices present an upside risk to inflation. Consequently, the consensus from market economists began to shift towards a rate hike in March, rather than May. On Tuesday, the RBA delivered a rate hike, citing a material risk that inflation may remain above target for longer, as short-term inflation expectations have already risen due to rising fuel prices. We close the rest of the paid position for a profit of 21bp (USD210k), bringing the total profit for our paid Feb/Mar RBA trade to USD330k. Although we maintain our paid bias in front-end AUD rates, the recent sell-off across global rates has led markets to price in a significant amount of rate hikes from the RBA this year.
