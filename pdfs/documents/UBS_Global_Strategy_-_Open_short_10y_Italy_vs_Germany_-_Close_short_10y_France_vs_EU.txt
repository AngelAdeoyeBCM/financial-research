# Global Strategy Open short 10y Italy vs Germany - Close short 10y France vs EU

No cycle like this one Global rates are increasingly behaving as they did in the 2000s. Oil prices remain elevated and volatile with dated Brent physical crude rising ~6% to to $ 127/bbl on 1 Oct. Higher inflation risk, higher term premia, political and fiscal uncertainty are now reinforcing one another in Europe as the US capex cycle powers on. Food prices add a further upside risk to the inflation and rates outlook. ECB does not signal imminent TPI ECB rhetoric offers little support for expectations of a near-term activation of the Transmission Protection Instrument (TPI). At the September press conference, president Lagarde did not want to comment on the widening of spreads. In an interview with La Croix, the president said that “debt close to 120% of GDP and not on course to be brought under control, it’s a serious matter.” The president also added that “governments are competing with sizeable private financing needs, particularly related to artificial intelligence.” Bundesbank President Nagel also said that the ECB has several other tools aside from the TPI, but “has nothing to do with maybe certain spread levels or things like that as the focus remains price stability.” Take profit - short 10y France vs EU at 84 bps (open: 52) We take profits on our short 10y France vs EU at 84 bps (entry: 52 bps) opened on 11 Sep after the sharp moves after the French government presented its 2027 budget. The budget counts on higher revenues, with real spending growing at 0.6% ex inflation but slower than the economy’s rebound of 1% in real growth. This should nudge the spending-to-GDP ratio slighty lower from 57.1% to 56.9%. Open short 10y Italy vs Germany with 150 bps target (entry: 119, stop: 100) A cycle like no other with resilient growth, energy price risks and a broadening demand for capital will put segments of the rate complex under pressure while demand for safety could increase. We go short 10y Italy vs Germany at 119 bps with a target of 150 bps. We have long been sceptical of the view that Italy can sustainably decouple from France. Instead, the greater risk is that Italian spreads move closer to French spreads versus German bunds (currently at 141bps).
