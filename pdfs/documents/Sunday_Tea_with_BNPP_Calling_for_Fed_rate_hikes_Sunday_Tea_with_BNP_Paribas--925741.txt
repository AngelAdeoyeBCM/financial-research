# Sunday Tea with BNPP: Calling for Fed rate hikes

. 07/06/2026 1

RKE.

## KEY MESSAGES

- We expect the Fed to hike rates at three sequential meetings, starting in December. The risk is that this happens sooner.
- We see US yields and the USD trading higher due to our new Fed call.
- With the Fed in play and the SpaceX IPO (with its related flows) on the horizon, we like owning equity vol. On Friday morning, we were the first sell-side bank to call for Fed rate hikes this year. Since January, we have projected rates on hold, given our view of robust US growth and sticky inflation. But last week’s solid payrolls release tilted the scales for us. We now expect the Fed’s next policy move to be a hike, aimed at starting to withdraw the monetary stimulus provided by 2025’s three “insurance” cuts. This year’s strong growth, gradually tightening labor market and persistently high inflation strike us as markedly different from what officials expected at the time of last fall’s easing, and we expect monetary policy to adjust accordingly. We think those “insurance” cuts were modeled on similar moves in 1998 after the collapse of Long-Term Capital Management, and with the US economy now similarly proving stronger than anticipated, the Fed will probably follow its 1999 playbook to withdraw th em: three successive rate hikes. We see this tightening most likely beginning at the December meeting (i.e. after the midterm elections), although an earlier start is possible depending on the course of the Middle East conflict and the labor market’s trajectory. We believe the main argument for eventual tightening will be an assessment that the Fed’s policy stance is miscalibrated. This stance was set last fall against expectations for a cyclically weakening US economy and for inflation that would cool to near 2% thi s year. With inflation now closer to 4%, policy i s much more stimulative in real terms than was expected (Figure 1), and with the economy demonstrating remarkable stability, the need for cyclical stimulus seems much smaller than policymakers had expecte d. Fed officials also seem increasingly concerned that the neutral rate is higher than they believed last fall, meaning policy may have been m ore stimulative than was intended from the start. Fig. 1: The real policy rate is lower than the Fed expected Sources: BEA, FRS, Macrobond, BNP Paribas While we acknowledge that new Chair Warsh will likely seek to avoid hiking rates in his early days of leading the Fed, our view is that regardless of who heads the central bank, monetary policy will be determined by the economic outlook and by the FOMC’s existing policy goals and strategy, in whatever direction the data leads it. In his confirmation testimony, Warsh pointed to the trimmed mean PCE measure of inflation as close to 2%. We believe the public use of this measure to argue that current elevated inflation is transitory is consistent with our expectation for rate hikes to come with a substantial delay but then to proceed sequentially to catch up. In June 2021, the Fed similarly relied on trimmed mean measures (at that point printing at 1.8%) amid the demand shock of the pandemic reopening to argue that rapidly rising inflation was driven by “special factors” and that a policy response was not required. Policy tight ening followed months later, in March 2022.. 07/06/2026 2 Following the strong employment report, we see this week’s inflation numbers printing equally hot, likely prompting the market to keep moving in our direction. We expect May US inflation to continue reflecting conflict-driven energy prices, sticky non-shelter services inflation and a returning trend of softer housing prints (headline CPI est: 0.6%m/m; core CPI est: 0.3%). And it could get worse. Depleting energy inventories mean that the market is running out of time – US crude stocks fell by 8 million barrels for the week and are now 3% below the five-year average, as reported by the EIA. A prolonged Middle East conflict with no resumption in Strait of Hormuz shipping flows would likely see physical shortages felt more urgently by the end of Q2/early Q3. If the conflict persists until August, we see oil and gas prices rising to USD140/bbl and EUR60/MWh respectively. We will also get the New York Fed and University of Michigan inflation expectations prints on Monday and Friday, respectively. We look for UMich 5-10y inflation expectations to push even higher to 4.2%. For reference, the May data rose from 3.4% to 3.9% in the final read, and last year’s Liberation Day high was 4.4%. The Fed appears to be increasingly concerned by the length of the inflation overshoot and the possibility that it may run for several more years. Notably, a recent lecture by Governor Waller (entitled “Policy Risks Have Changed”) contained a comprehensive discussion of evidence from asset prices and surveys of rising inflation, along with a Bayesian theoretical framework holding that after a succession of otherwise-“transitory” positive inflation shocks, it is reasonable to expect the public to perceive high inflation as the “new normal”. Other policymakers have made similar arguments in recent weeks, and we see Waller – who was earlier among the most skeptical that LTIE was at risk – as signaling an emerging sea change in the Fe d’s thinking. With the unemployment rate forecast to hit 4% this year, and inflation expectations forecast to rise above 4% this week, there are multiple pathways for the Fed to hike, in our view. Fig. 2: Higher inflation already on consumers’ minds Sources: FRBNY, UMich, Macrobond, BNP Paribas Accordingly, we have been positioned for the market pricing in additional hikes via SFRH7 1x1.5 put spreads. We have, with our changed Fed call, also revised up our Treasury yield forecasts. We now see 10y yields ending this year at 4.75% and 30y yields at 5.35%. We also see 2y yields ending this year at 4.35%, consistent with 75bp of hikes through March 2027. As the market moves to price in Fed hikes, this will also impact the FX market. Policy divergence among central banks can become a dominant theme in FX, especially given that the Fed repricing reflects stronger fundamentals, while that of peers is due to the energy shock. We are now entering the period where the USD will perform best: the currency tends to rally as markets price in hikes, rather than when tightening is delivered (Figure 3). Our STEERTM model suggests that the pair should be trading closer to 163. We suspect the JPY scans as rich here most likely because the market remains concerned over intervention by official authorities. However, fundamentals – elevated energy prices, risks of Fed tightening after strong US data, and the BoJ falling behind the curve – suggest that the pair can appreciate further. We immediately bought the dip after the last intervention episode, and last week we locked in profits after USDJPY traded to our previous 160 target. From here, we remain long USDJPY via a 160 call with 163.50 EKO and a 163 digi call KO.. 07/06/2026 3 We still expect high-yielding EM FX to trade well, but any rallies from here are more likely to be grinds. We’re less inclined to chase rallies but remain amenable to buying the dip in currencies where local fundamentals are strong. Hungary is an example. Our latest research assesses the positive feedback loops into HUF term premia, FX performance, and fiscal and monetary policy stemming from a meaningful resumption in EU fund flows and the new government’s push toward EUR adoption in the next five years. We favour being long 10y HGBs versus Bunds; we remain short EURHUF via options and maintain our front-end flatteners in IRS. On the theme of rate rises, we expect the ECB to re-engage with tightening this week for the first time since 2023. With a hike widely expected after hawkish commentary, the focus will be on whether this is just part of a recalibration of policy in the face of the energy shock (our current stance, with a second hike expected in September) or the start of a longer-lasting cycle. For now, we expect a data-dependent approach to prevail. While the new projections will shed some light on the ECB’s current thinking, we do not expect them to signal a more hawkish policy path than is currently priced in. As such, we think the market will keep hovering in a range of two to three hikes for the time being and see 2.43% on 2y ESTR as a floor. Further out on the curve, June will mark (with May) the highest monthly net supply of the year, which should keep pressuring up term premium, especially from local lows reached at the end of last week. Finally, the equity market’s focus this week will be the SpaceX IPO. This will be the largest IPO ever. The deal is headline-grabbing, but more importantly from a flows perspective, it will be a call on liquidity. It comes at a time of very strong wealth effects f or US retail investors, driven in part by robust recent gains in S emis. Rule changes for prominent equity indices have also fueled headlines about large potential passive buying flows. This implies significant selling elsewhere. IPOs have often exhibited high volatility and fat tails. With the confluence of flows expected around SpaceX’s flotation, we think this time will not be an exception. What is unprecedented is that volatility in this stock will be coupled with the largest m arket cap ever seen in a US IPO. While the standalone SpaceX flows might be digestible, many of these are potentially same-way and additive. With the SpaceX free float reported to be close to $75bn on IPO, it’s easy to see how $30bn of passive buying, a r etail investor chase, and levered ETF / option inflows could quickly become challenging for the stock’s liquidity. With the Fed in play, the SpaceX IPO (with its related flows) on the horizon, and VIX and VVIX coming off year-to-date lows, we like owning equity vol.
