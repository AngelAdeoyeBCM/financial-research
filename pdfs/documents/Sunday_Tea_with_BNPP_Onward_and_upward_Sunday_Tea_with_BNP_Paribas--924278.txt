# Sunday Tea with BNPP: Onward and upward

. 17/05/2026 1

B N P PA GL OB ET S A PP.

## KEY MESSAGES

- We see increasing chances of a Fed rate hike, starting at the end of the year.
- Risks to the long end of the Treasury curve look evident, given underpriced US fiscal deterioration.
- We remain long EURGBP to position for more discounting of UK fiscal and political risk premium. Since January, we have looked for the Fed to keep rates on hold well into 2027 based on expectations for resilient US growth, a stable labor market and sticky inflation. Over the course of th is year, and especially since the start of the Middle East conflict, the challenges the FOMC will face in returning inflation to its 2% target have become more evident. While we see monetary policy as poorly positioned to deal with the economic outlook as it stands, our longstanding view is that the FOMC would hike rates only if unemployment seemed likely to decline below 4%, a level at which wage-price amplification becomes easier to envision, or if long-term inflation expectations (LTIE) showed further evidence of becoming de-anchored. For now, we forecast the unemployment rate to remain unchanged over 2026 but given strong recent labor data and the increasing level of real monetary stimulus entering the US economy, another ‘boom-y’ monthly payrolls print would likely lead us to expect unemployment to follow a declining trajectory over the year, potentially reaching 4% by year end. We also think that if the Strait of Hormuz remains effectively closed past midyear, this would generate a sustained level of inflation including further entrenchment into core services, which the FOMC might judge as an unacceptable risk to LTIE. Were rate hikes to come, we think they would be aimed at reversing last year’s ‘insurance ’ cuts. We would expect the FOMC to follow its playbook for the 1999 unwinding of ‘insurance’ cuts that follow ed the failure of hedge fund Long-Term Capital Management. Like the current environment, policymakers subsequently faced an unexpectedly resilient economy amid the internet boom, with a tightening labor market and upside inflation risks. The initial response (after a delay) was to withdraw those three ‘insurance ’ cuts at sequential meetings. If the FOMC opted to hike now, we believe this would most likely kick off at the December meeting. At the April press conference, then- Chair Jerome Powell expressed a desire to cycle through various forms of bias language in the meeting statement – first to a formal neutral bias, then to a tightening bias – prior to raising rates. If taken literally, this would place the earliest live meeting for a hike in the autumn. Moreover, President Donald Trump has long viewed with suspicion the timing of the 50bp rate cut in September 2024, shortly before the presidential election, and we think a Fed under new leader Kevin Warsh would likely try to avoid repeating this. While markets have gradually been pricing the risk of a rate hike next year, they seem unprepared for a 1999-style scenario of ‘insurance’ cuts being reversed in succession. We think the options market offers an attractive structure to play for this scenario. Late last week, we bought a SFRH7 a/a+40bp 1x1.5 put spread – a trade that has a maximum payout 40bp above the market’s current pricing by next March’s Fed meeting. In addition to seeing risks to the US front end, we believe the back end can continue trending higher as well, with the 30y yield having moved decisively above the key 5% level. This is for five reasons:
1. After 30y yields broke the 4% resistance level in 2023, long-end yields de-anchored. We see similar parallels with the recent break of 5%.
2. The ‘Treasury put’ appears to be struck much higher than 5%.
3. We see scope for term premiums to rise with deficit concerns becoming more acute.
4. Holders of long-end Treasuries are increasingly more price-sensitive.
5. Pension fund rebalancing is less supportive versus recent history. Accordingly, we also added ATM +25 / ATM +50 USU6 1x1 put spreads to the strategy portfolio last week. Consistent with the move higher in US yields, the USD strengthened broadly last week with EURUSD trading back to around 1.16 and high-beta FX bearing the brunt of the USD rally in light of risk-off sentiment. Tactically, the move helped our long USDJPY trades across spot and options. We note that Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent’s recent visit to Japan did little to dent the pair’s upward trajectory. Our view remains that USDJPY can grind back to or above pre-intervention levels. Elsewhere in FX, our AUDZAR and NOKSEK shorts are performing poorly as a result of oil prices moving higher, but we continue to think these crosses can have an asymmetric reaction to the downside if crude prices turn.. 17/05/2026 2 UK politics also driving markets: The focus in the UK has turned to the pathway for Andy Burnham, a former Labour MP and now mayor of Greater Manchester, to replace Keir Starmer as prime minister. Last week, Burnham cleared the first two obstacles: the resignation of Labour MP Josh Simons enabled Burnham to announce his intention to stand in the upcoming by-election, and the party’s National Executive Committee approved Burnham’s participation. However, Burnham still faces one hurdle before returning to Parliament: winning the by-election. A typical timeline would suggest 18 June is the earliest possible voting date, but Burnham’s prospects here remain uncertain. Polls from the constituency had indicated a strong performance by the Reform UK party at the next general election. Only when Burnham is again an MP can he launch a challenge against the prime minister. We see room for continued underperformance of UK assets as the market prices in some probability of a change of prime ministe r and a shift to a policy agenda that may be deemed less fiscally prudent. We stay long EURGBP to position for this. In the rates space, gilt yields have risen to levels close to the risk scenario we outlined in UK: Succession scenarios on 13 February. As of 15 May, 10y gilt yields were just shy of our projection of 5.15%. In the short term, headline risk could extend these moves. Beyond politics, the coming week will bring a deluge of data (e.g. labour market, PMIs, inflation) providing further insight on the UK economy’s performance through the energy price shock and domestic political uncertainty. We expect a picture of resilience, with a steady performance from the labour market and signs of stickiness in services inflation (BNPP April CPI forecast: 3.1% y/y, services CPI: 3.8% y/y). Ongoing resilience in data would challenge the narrative that the economy is too weak to precipitate second-round effects and add fuel to the fire for rate rises. We expect the Bank of England to hike twice this year, starting in June. The two-day summit between Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping in Beijing appeared to deliver on the relatively low expectations heading into the event. While the tone of subsequent official announcements supported our view of an extended detente between the two powers, in particular on the trade front, it stopped short of any announcements with macroeconomic significance, including on reopening the Strait of Hormuz. Oil and gas markets gain ed last week as the stalemate in the Middle East persists. At the time of writing, there was little news from the Trump-Xi talks that might make a de-escalation more likely. Therefore, we remain in oil and gas markets that will in our view progressively tighten as Hormuz flows stay disrupted, despite a few tankers finding their way through.
