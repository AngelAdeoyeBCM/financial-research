# FX: Long SGDNEER ahead of April MAS meeting

Chandresh Jain, EM Asia FX and Rates Strategist | Joey Li, Markets Analyst, APAC | Parisha Saimbi, EM Asia FXLM Strategist | BNP Paribas Singapore Branch. 1:: S$NEER vs Input Prices We favour adding long SGDNEER exposure ahead of the Monetary Authority of Singapore’s meeting in April, via a proxy NEER basket optimised for accessibility and carry. Risks for hawkish MAS shift: Singapore’s growth has remained robust, while inflation has bottomed out from 2025 lows. Since January, we have flagged a risk of the MAS reducing its policy accommodativeness this year, as Singapore goes through a reflationary phase (see Singapore: Reflation set to change MAS and market views, dated 7 January). Historically, the MAS has usually done this by increasing the SGDNEER slope (Figure 1), which we estimate has a long-term average of 1.0% and is currently at 0.50%. The recent Middle East conflict has added to inflation concerns, given Singapore’s reliance on oil imports and limited reserves (FX: Asia’s sensitivity to supply-driven energy shocks, 10 March), increasing the likelihood of a hawkish move from the MAS in April. Recentering risk: Historically, the MAS has responded to prolonged periods of high inflation, caused by high oil prices in 2011 and 2022, by re-centering the SGDNEER mid-point upwards. This allows scope for the SGD to appreciate quickly, mitigating the impact of high inflation (Figure 2). While re-centering the policy band is typically a measure MAS reserves for extreme circumstances, given the current sharp increase in oil prices, we believe MAS may consider re-centering the band upwards. This measure would provide a faster means of reducing imported inflation compared to adjusting the SGDNEER slope. We think the market is underestimating the likelihood of a re-centering from MAS and therefore recommend running long SGDNEER. If oil prices were to remain high, exacerbating growth concerns, we expect the government to respond with fiscal measures to support the economy, allowing monetary policy to focus on maintaining price stability. The current risk-off sentiment driven by the Middle East conflict has led to broad USD strength, which saw the SGDNEER initially decline, as the USD holds a significant weight in the index.

## KEY MESSAGES

We favour going long the SGDNEER ahead of the Monetary Authority of Singapore’s meeting next month. We maintain our view that Singapore is undergoing a reflationary phase, which is likely to prompt the MAS to reduce the accommodativeness of its monetary policy. Elevated oil prices due to the Middle East conflict add a risk of the MAS re-centring the policy band upwards or increasing the SGDNEER slope – which would reinforce the bullish SGD view. The growth risk will be mitigated by fiscal measures from the government, in our opinion. We have created a proxy version of the SGDNEER basket, optimized for accessibility and carry, while maintaining a strong correlation with the original basket. Notional: USD 20mn.

USD, EUR, CNY, JPY, AUD, and GBP. We are going long SGD against a modified version of its SGDNEER basket, ahead of the Monetary Authority of Singapore’s (MAS) meeting in April. Singapore’s growth has remained robust, while inflation has bottomed out from 2025 lows. Since January, we have flagged a risk of the MAS reducing its policy accommodativeness in FX and rates this year, as Singapore goes through a reflationary phase (see Singapore: Reflation set to change MAS and market views, dated 7 January). Historically, this is usually done via an increase in the SGD NEER slope, which has a long-term average of 1.0% and is currently at 0.50%. Additionally, adjustments in FX policies have typically been accompanied by moves in SORA rates, as seen in the last two policy cycles (a tightening cycle in 2022 and a loosening cycle in 2025). The recent Middle East conflicts have added to inflation concerns, given Singapore’s reliance on oil imports and limited reserves (see FX: Asia’s sensitivity to supply-driven energy shocks, dated 10 March), increasing the likelihood of a hawkish move from the MAS in April. Normally, this would lead to a bullish SGD outlook, with market participants adding long SGD positions ahead of MAS meetings. However, the current risk-off sentiment driven by Middle East conflicts has led to a strengthening USD, causing the SGDNEER to decline, as the USD holds a significant weight in the index (Figure 1). However, with the MAS meeting a month away, we believe this presents a buying opportunity for the SGD against the NEER basket. Historically, the MAS has responded to prolonged periods of high oil prices by re-centering the SGDNEER mid upwards, especially if it’s trading near the top of the band. This allows the SGD to appreciate sharply, mitigating the impact of higher oil prices to imported inflation (Figure 2). While typically a measure reserved for extreme circumstances, the current Middle East situation have started to evolve towards the tail risk scenario we’ve previously outlined, where oil prices surge towards $130/barrel (see Global macro: Crude reality (4), dated 4 March). As such, we think investors need to factor in the risk of a re-centering from the MAS, which adds to the bullish SGD view. Optimising the SGDNEER basket: To long the SGDNEER, we need to buy the SGD against a weighted basket of currencies that make up the NEER index, broadly comprising currencies from Singapore’s major trading partners and other key currencies in FX. Using our proprietary model, we derive the weights of these currencies and simplify the basket by removing those with minimal weights. As long SGDNEER exposure is typically a negative carry position, due to Singapore having one of the lowest interest rates globally, we also exclude the INR from the basket to reduce the negative carry associated with the position (Figure 3). Currently, long SGDINR has one of the largest negative carry costs among the remaining basket currencies. After rescaling the weights on the remaining currencies so that they add up to 1, we verify that the performance of our modified SGDNEER basket closely tracks the original basket (Figure 4). By analysing the weekly returns of both baskets, we find that they exhibit a high degree of correlation on a 6-month rolling basis, with only occasional deviations due to sudden movements in excluded currencies, such as the sharp appreciation of the TWD in May 2025. Our analysis shows that the 6- month rolling correlation between the two baskets is around 80%, indicating that our modified basket is a reliable proxy for the actual SGDNEER (Figure 5). The risk to our position is a material escalation in the Middle East conflict, triggering a further surge in oil prices, e.g. to around $150 per barrel, morphing into a global growth shock. In that environment, the SGDNEER index could decline on the back of USD strength. Additionally, concerns about the global economic outlook might prompt the MAS to adopt a cautious approach and keep policy unchanged at this meeting. The MAS may only seek to avoid policy tightening in a recessionary environment, but we are far from that, in our opinion. CNH (17%), JPY (14%), AUD (11%) and GBP (8%). Target on total return: 1.5%.
