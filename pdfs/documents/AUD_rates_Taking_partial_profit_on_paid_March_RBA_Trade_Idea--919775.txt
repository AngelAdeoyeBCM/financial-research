# AUD rates: Taking partial profit on paid March RBA

Joey Li, Markets Strategist, APAC | Chandresh Jain, EM Asia FX and Rates Strategist | MAR | BNP Paribas Singapore Branch. Sources: ABS, Macrobond, BNP Paribas Fig. 1: Australia inflation continues to run above target We take partial profit (40k out of 50k dv/01) on our paid position in March RBA at 7.5bp, for a profit of USD120k. Since Q4 last year, we have maintained a paid bias in front-end AUD rates, as inflation continued to surprise to the upside – and remain above the Reserve Bank of Australia's target (Figure 1). Ahead of the Q4 CPI release, we booked profit on our paid position of Feb/May RBA and paid Feb/Mar RBA spread, as we believed it provided better risk–reward (see AUD rates: Take profit on paid Feb/May RBA, add Feb/Mar RBA, dated 27 January 2026. For the original trade idea see AUD rates: Pay Feb/May RBA spread, dated 5 December 2025). The RBA delivered a unanimous 25bp rate hike in February, exceeding market expectations of +18bp and adopting a more hawkish tone. The updated forecasts in the Statement of Monetary Policy (SoMP) indicate that underlying inflation will remain above target until June 2028 even after incorporating further rate hikes, suggesting that rate hikes will likely remain on the table in the foreseeable future. We chose to keep our position in paid March RBA, as we believe the market's reaction function remained asymmetric, with a greater tendency to add rate hikes on strong data rather than reduce them on weak data. Since then, key macroeconomic data have been surprising to the upside, with the unemployment rate remaining at 4.1% for January versus market expectations of a 4.2% print. Inflation also surprised to the upside, rising 3.4% y/y in January versus market expectations for a 3.3% print. Although the full Q4 GDP data has yet to be released, partial indicators (public demand, net exports contribution, private capital expenditure) have all surprised to the upside, setting the stage for a strong Q4 GDP release tomorrow. Today, RBA Governor Michelle Bullock responded in a Q&A that markets should consider each meeting as "live" and warned that it may become increasingly challenging to overlook prolonged supply-side oil shocks, given that inflation in Australia is already high, especially if it de-anchors near-term inflation expectations. With the March RBA now pricing in 8.5bp of rate hikes and a cumulative 27bp by the May meeting (Figure 2), we believe it's prudent to take partial profits on our position and reduce our exposure from 50k to 10k dv/01, as most of the key macroeconomic data have essentially been released. Although the RBA's March meeting is a non-SoMP meeting, which reduces the likelihood of a back-to-back rate hike, we're maintaining a small paid exposure to capture the potential for a rate hike, particularly if elevated oil prices persist due to ongoing US-Iran tensions.
