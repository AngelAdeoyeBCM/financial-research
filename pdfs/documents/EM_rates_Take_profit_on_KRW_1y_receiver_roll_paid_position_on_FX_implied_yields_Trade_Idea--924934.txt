# EM rates: Take profit on KRW 1y receiver, roll paid position on FX implied yields

Chandresh Jain, EM Asia FX and Rates Strategist | MAR | Joey Li, FX & Rates Strategist, APAC | BNP Paribas Singapore Branch | Franco Hsu, APAC Markets Strategist | BNP Paribas Hong Kong Branch | We are currently running two received positions in the front-end of KRW rates: 1. a tactical KRW 1y receiver that we initiated earlier this month when the market was pricing in over four rate hikes from the Bank of Korea May 2026), and 2. a long KTB position, funded by FX implied yields, as a carry trade we established approximately a year ago when implied yields were low compared to bond yields (see EM rates: Long 2y KTB with FX implied funding, dated 6 June 2025). Risk event ahead: We view the upcoming BoK meeting as a potential risk event, as we believe the BoK may signal a shift in their stance from dovish to hawkish. This could potentially trigger another round of sell-off in the market, in our opinion, even though current valuations already appear overstretched. This is consistent with our observation over recent months that front-end KRW rates have sold off after every major hawkish pivot from the BoK, regardless of whether market pricing was already stretched relative to fundamentals. KRW rates have rallied over the weekend, buoyed by optimism over the Middle East conflict. We think it is tactically wise to take advantage of this rally to reduce/hedge our front-end receivers ahead of the BoK's meeting this week, and look to re-enter again if we get to attractive levels. To do this, we are taking profit on our 1y receivers, while rolling our paid position in 12m KRW FX implied yields to hedge out the remaining duration risk on the bond. As the trade still generates positive carry, we expect the positive carry to offset the negative PNL in the trade from the selloff in rates, ultimately resulting in a positive final PNL at maturity. Take profit on KRW 1y receiver: We take profit on our KRW 6-May-26, 1y receiver at 3.22% for a 9bp (USD180k) profit. Roll FX implied yield: Pay KRW 6-Jun-26 x 10-Jun-27 onshore FX implied yield at 2.90% for USD100mn to hedge our existing long position in KTB 2.125% June 2027. USDKRW spot reference: 1509.57 (26 May 2026, 09:42 GMT+8). Maintain paid position in 1s3s10sfly: We continue to view the market's pricing of rate hikes over the next 12 months as excessive. However, we expect the front end of the curve (1s2s, 1s3s) to steepen over time, driven by a combination of wealth effects, fiscal stimulus, and the ongoing AI cycle. Meanwhile, we anticipate the long end to remain flat, primarily due to World Government Bond Index (WGBI) flows. As such, we continue to like our paid position in the KRW 1s3s10s fly (see South Korea: More reasons to hike rates, dated 18 May 2026). We initiated the trade in early May, following another sell-off in KRW rates that led markets to price (BoK) over the next 12 months, a scenario that believed was not supported by fundamentals May). Our recent meetings with economists and policymakers in South Korea reinforced our view that inflation remains a top priority, and we brought forward our call of 2 rate hikes from the BoK to Q3 and Q4 this year, previously Q4 and Q2 next year (South May). However, we still believe that market pricing of 4-5 hikes over the next 12 months is excessive, and expect the front-end of the curve (1s2s, 1s3s) to steepen over time. At the same time, we expect the long-end to remain flat due to World Government Bond Index (WGBI) flows, and have thus added a paid position in KRW 1s3s10s fly on the back of that. Having said that, our expectation is for the BoK to signal impending rate hikes at its May meeting this week, and we think there is a risk that it triggers another round of sell-off in the market, even though current levels already appear overstretched. This is consistent with our observations over recent months, where front-end KRW rates have sold off at every major pivot from the BoK, regardless of whether market pricing was already stretched relative to fundamentals. Although the spot 1y rate is still trading near our entry level, our position has earned positive carry and is now trading at 3.25%. Given this, we believe it is tactically wise to take profit on this trade and lighten up on front-end receivers ahead of the meeting this week. We close our KRW 6-May-26, 1y receiver at 3.25% for a 6bp (USD120k) profit, while continuing to hold our paid position in the KRW 1s3s10s fly. KRW 1y receiver: We initiated this trade in early May 2026, following a sell-off in KRW rates that led the market to price (BoK) over the next 12 months, which we believed was not supported by fundamentals May). Our recent meetings with economists and policymakers in South Korea have reinforced our view that inflation remains a top priority, leading us to revise our forecast and bring forward our expected timeline for two rate hikes from the BoK to Q3 and Q4 this year, from our previous prediction of hikes in Q4 2026 and Q2 2027 (see South May). However, we maintain our call for a total of 2 rate hikes and continue to believe that the market's current pricing of 4-5 hikes over the next 12 months is excessive. Long KTB trade: This position was established as a carry trade approximately a year ago, when implied yields were low relative to bond yields. At that time, KTBs stood out among EM Asian markets for their solid credit rating, while still offering an attractive carry. The market consensus back then was that the BoK would continue to cut rates, supporting a long position in the bonds. Since then, KRW rates have experienced several bouts of sell-offs, with market sentiment shifting from delayed rate cuts to rate hikes – both the bond and our paid position in implied yields have sold off 80~100bp as a result (Figure 1). Due to the bond's larger duration (dv01) compared to our paid position in implied yields, however, the current PNL of the trade is negative, despite the positive carry earned over time.
