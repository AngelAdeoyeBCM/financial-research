# China Market-Wise - UK GDP - Start Looking Outwards

Exhibit 1: A beat Industrial Production (%M) 1.1% 0.3% 0.2% 1.3% Index of Services (%M) 0.3% 0.0% 0.1% -0.30% Monthly GDP (November, %M) 0.3% 0.0% 0.1% -0.1% Actual Our Forecast Consensus Last Source: ONS, Bloomberg, Morgan Stanley Research forecasts

UK GDP surprised to the upside in November, with manufacturing and white-collar services countering weakness in consumer-facing services and construction. Domestic weakness is driven by UK policy settings, while externally-facing sectors look primed to benefit from more robust global growth, removing some of the downside tail risks. With lessened downside risks to growth, the BoE can probably, as the market is pricing, wait for the likely progress in disinflation before cutting rates next. Detailed reading: Manufacturing output surged, flattered by a delayed normalisation in auto production (10.7%M), following the cyber attack on one of UK's largest car producers in September. It is challenging to read UK manufacturing data in 4Q, given the impact of this distortion, but it is important to flag that industrial production data across Europe seems to have accelerated into year-end, and that sentiment in the sector looks to have improved. UK is a small, open economy, and more robust global growth is an important upside risk to our forecasts for next year, as outlined here. That said, global growth giveth, restrictive rates taketh, however, as construction activity looks to be in free-fall, declining by 1.3%M, following a 1.2%M contraction in October. Construction, of course, is one of the most rate-sensitive parts of the economy. Netting all this out leaves services as the driver of the November beat. Consumer-facing services were lacklustre (-0.2%M), while white-collar services output surged. This was the main driver of the beat vs. our forecast. Perhaps there are some Budget boosts at play - to tax advisory in particular. Also, with data as volatile as monthly GDP in the UK, one should be careful not to over-interpret just one reading. We do see some likely payback in December, and will judge the sustainability of the bounce-back based on the incoming survey data. But overall, white collar services sector, too, tends to do well when global growth is on an upswing. The sector's exposure to the US economy in particular has been an important buffer for UK growth in this cycle. Overall, we are now tracking a 0.1%Q pick up to UK GDP in 4Q. With UK payrolls likely to contract over 4Q, and economy likely to have grown, cyclical productivity pick-up in the UK is showing little signs of letting up. BoE implications: The thus far muted market reaction to a pretty decent beat confirms, we think, that the market's firm consensus was that the UK economy probably was faring better under the hood, than suggested by the very weak September and October data. Not much, in any, case is priced for the BoE in the very near-term, with further cuts seen as likely to come once there is more certainty around this year's disinflation, and as Fed resumes its easing cycle. Considering the hawkishness in the December minutes, it is hard to argue with this view, even if the labour market continues to weaken - we expect the jobless rate to tick up in next week's reading, and high-frequency vacancy indicators are at their lows again as of mid-January. With this, we reiterate hawkish risks to our own BoE call (cuts in Feb, April and June). We will review the call following next week's data.
