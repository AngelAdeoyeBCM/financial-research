# FX: Extending target and trailing stop on long USDTHB

We extend the target and trail the stop on our long USDTHB (15Dec26 forward) trade idea, which we initiated in FX: Long USDTHB as rising oil prices poses upside risks on 11 September. To recap, our rationale was predicated on elevated oil prices shifting momentum in USDAsia pairs to the upside, weighing on the trade balance and raising fiscal concerns; elevated capital goods imports needed for data centres; widening US-TH rate differentials with the Bank of Thailand likely to remain on hold while the Fed begins raising rates; and Fed hikes weighing on gold prices in the near-term, to which the currency is closely correlated. Additionally, dividend seasonality in September, rich THB valuations and heavy short USDTHB positioning made it attractive to build USDTHB longs. Since then, the trade has performed in our favour – the THB has been the second worst-performing currency (behind the IDR) in EM Asia, reflecting the aforementioned factors, which have since materialised, in our view. We see scope for the pair to rise further, and favour extending the target on our trade idea to 34.50, from 33.94, for four reasons:

- From a policy perspective, we anticipate two further Fed rate hikes in December and January, and see a material risk of an October hike should upcoming non-farm payrolls and US CPI data remain strong.
- We expect a seasonal uptick in demand for crude and refined products in Asia. With prices likely to remain elevated (see Chinese product export curbs to support USDAsia pairs, dated 1 October), Thailand’s trade and fiscal balances are likely to stay under pressure.
- Recent flooding in Thailand could act as an additional drag on domestic economic activity and the government’s fiscal position. Combined with the elevated oil prices, this may also weigh on tourism, just as the season is due to get underway in Thailand.
- While short positions in the THB have built to a score of -32, according to BNP Paribas Global FX Positioning Tracker (on a +/ -50 scale), we see scope for them to turn more extreme against this backdrop. Given the strong performance of our short THB position to date, we believe it is prudent to protect current gains. With dividend seasonality now over and the risk of a pullback in capital goods imports following the government’s pause on data-centre project approvals and construction, we favour trailing our stop from 32.20 to 33, locking in a minimum gain of 0.15% on the trade.
