# EM rates: Take profit on paid 5y MYR swaps

We take profit on our paid position in the m26, 5y MYR swap at 3.71% for a profit of 22.5bp (USD450k), as the trade has reached our target (Figure 1). We initiated the trade in April, anticipating that strong economic growth and fiscal pressures from energy subsidies would exert upward pressure on the curve (see EM rates: Pay 5y MYR rates, dated 23 April). At the time, this was a non-consensus position; markets were received in MYR rates, relative to paid positions elsewhere, driven by the view that Malaysia had minimal exposure to energy supply shocks and a bullish outlook on the currency (see FX: Asia’s sensitivity to supply-driven energy shocks, dated 11 March). However, we believed MYR rates were trading too low and too flat relative to history and fundamentals. Since then, Malaysia’s economic growth has remained robust. While inflationary momentum has remained contained, the government’s decision to maintain petrol subsidies, coupled with the addition of diesel subsidies, has continued to weigh on the fiscal balance. Additionally, recent tightness in money markets also pushed the 3m Klibor higher (Figure 2), putting further upward pressure on front end rates. Finally, re-escalating tensions in the Middle East over the last two weeks have also supported the trade, driving oil prices back towards the USD100/barrel mark. Looking ahead, our core view on MYR rates remains unchanged. We maintain a paid bias on MYR rates, supported by growth, inflation, and fiscal dynamics, and plan to re-engage the trade in the future at a better level.
