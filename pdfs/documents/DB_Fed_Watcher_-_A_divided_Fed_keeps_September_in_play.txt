# Fed Watcher - A divided Fed keeps September in play

Fed Watcher Fed Watcher Date 24 August 2026

Fed communications over the past week showed the Committee remains nearly evenly divided on the appropriate policy path ahead. The minutes from the July FOMC meeting showed broad support for holding rates steady, alongside a clear conditional bias from "many" officials toward further tightening if inflation fails to moderate. Indeed, while most participants expected disinflation over the remainder of the year as their baseline outlook, many officials highlighted risks of greater inflation persistence amid p rolonged supply-chain pressures stemming from a protracted Middle East conflict and emergent AI-related price pressures. Part of the reason for the stability in views between the June and July meetings was the presence of only a few additional data points, setting up the September meeting as a possibly more important date for a policy re-think. The language from the minutes was broadly consistent with recent remarks from SF Fed President Daly, whose modal outlook has not changed significantly despite recent jobs and inflation data. Similar to NY Fed President Williams, she described current polic y as well-positioned at a slightly restrictive level, saw no evidence warranting preemptive hikes, and expected transitory inflation shocks to dissipate. However, she cautioned that compounded shocks could generate more concerning inflation dynamics, indic ating that such an outcome would necessitate tighter policy. In contrast, St. Louis Fed President Musalem remained firmly on the hawkish side of the debate. He reiterated his preference for a rate hike in July, characterizing the current policy stance as neutral or even accommodative. Musalem favored earlier, gradua l rate increases and expressed a desire to bring inflation back to target within 18 months, noting that underlying inflation —currently running at 2.5% to 3.0%—remains inconsistent with a timely return to the price stability goal. On balance, the Fed appears positioned to remain data-dependent, but the burden of proof is increasingly on inflation to moderate sufficiently to prevent further policy tightening. While our baseline forecast continues to call for two rate hikes this year —at the September and December meetings —market attention is now focused on Chairman Warsh's upcoming speech this Friday, which has the potential to offer either a broad, big-picture framework discussion or more immediate, near-term policy guidance (see Jackson Hole preview: Staying on the summit or trekking a path back to target?). What Fed officials said Musalem (08/20)

- Monetary policy is neutral or accommodative, and financial conditions are pretty accommodative. Recommended raising rates in July; earlier, gradual increases are preferable which is less disruptive and could avoid more aggressive action later. Would not prejudge September.
- Underlying inflation is 2.5% –3%, too high, and the number one concern in his district. Inflation is high due to shocks and persistent demand; wants inflation at 2% within 18 months and sees a lower probability of reaching 2% at current rates.
- Productivity recovery is pushing the neutral rate higher. No inflation pressure is coming from the labor market.
- Forward guidance is useful when rates are at zero; communicating a framework differs from making a commitment. Daly (08/20)
- Fed policy is in a good place and slightly restrictive; see no evidence calling for preemptive rate hikes. Rising global long-term yields do not give a clear policy signal.
- Modal outlook is that overlapping shocks will dissipate and inflation will return to target, but compounded effects could make the total greater than the sum of its parts. Wary, though not seeing that worrying inflation dynamics now. If that happens, need to treat with tighter policy.
- Supportive of the July hold and continue to watch for more information between now and next meeting. Recent prints on inflation and labor market did not change the picture.
- The labor market is stable, with no signs of faltering or contributing to inflation.
- Not seeing broad inflation spillover from the AI buildout.
- Attention is on inflation, and the Fed must focus on achieving its inflation target regardless of Treasury action. Minutes (08/19)
- Most supported holding rates. Many said tightening would likely be necessary if inflation did not decline, and some judged financial conditions insufficiently restrictive. Incoming information would remain key; rate changes should remain the primary policy tool.
- Most participants expected inflation to step down as tariff and earlier energy effects wane, but many saw risks of greater inflation persistence and indicated that a protracted Middle East conflict could prolong supply-chain pressures.
- Significant uncertainty around the timing, magnitude of AI's impact on inflation, labor market, and productivity gains. Participants generally expected solid near-term real GDP growth, supported by AI investment and household spending.
- The labor market had strengthened modestly, though low job-finding and long-term unemployment showed softness. Overall wage growth was moderate; widespread AI layoffs had not materialized.
- Various officials noted that their overall assessment of the economy were little changed between June and July meeting.
- Chairman asked for input from the Committee on holding 6 scheduled meetings per year though no decisions were made. Chairman affirmed that any change in practice would not affect the 2026 schedule. Our takes on the 5 task forces
