# FX: Reserve managers bought USD in Q1 (IMF COFER

FOCUS 03/07/2026 1

Joshua Wilcock, FX Strategist, G10 | Alex Jekov, Head of G10 FX Strategy, Europe | Sam Lynton-Brown, CFA, Global Head of Macro Strategy | London Branch | Ishan Gurnani, FX Strategist, G10 | BNP Paribas Securities Corp.. Fig. 1: Reserve manager allocation to USD rose in Q1 2026 USD buying: The IMF COFER (Currency Composition of Official Foreign Exchange Reserves) data for Q1 2026 shows reserve manager allocations to the USD increased from 56.4% to 57.1%. This is a data point that counters the long-term trend of reserve managers reducing USD holdings (see Figure 1). The data shows an FX-valuation-adjusted increase in USD reserve holdings of approximately USD70bn, during a period when the USD was rangebound (see Figure 2). We find (see overleaf) that this valuation-adjusted increase in USD holdings was mostly driven by reserve managers actively selling JPY into weakness, while reserve manager active buying of EUR into weakness was negligible compared to the valuation effect. Last year, we found that amid USD weakness, active buying of USD by reserve managers was insufficient to offset the valuation effect, allowing USD allocations to fall. Now, the opposite is true; reserve managers actively bought the USD on aggregate in a rangebound environment for the USD such that USD allocations increased.

## KEY MESSAGES

The IMF’s COFER data for Q1 2026 suggests foreign exchange reserve managers bought USD, and, as a result, reserve manager allocations to the USD increased. Q1 2026 was therefore counter to the long-term trend of reserve managers reducing USD holdings. Notably reserve managers sold around USD 15bn JPY per month in Q1, such that JPY allocations decreased from 5.8% to 5.4%.
