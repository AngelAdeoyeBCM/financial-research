Any asymmetries for USD are likely conditional on [the geopolitical backdrop]. In other words, if geopolitical tensions are increasing with spiking oil prices and a higher USD, then a weak CPI will not do much to impact dollar strength while a strong CPI could accelerate dollar buying. Bigger picture, [the author does] see two implications. First, as [the author has] been flagging, USD [or] SPX correlations are normalizing (back to negative; Figure 4). A structural headwind for the USD is that uncertainty around the USD “safe haven” status could lead foreign equity investors to increase [foreign equity investors'] FX hedge ratios (sell USD); however, quantitatively, the correlation breakdown appeared to be temporary rather than a structural shift. While [USD or SPX correlations] continues to normalize, [that USD or SPX correlations are normalizing] could weaken the case for an increase in FX hedge ratio adjustments. Second, [the geopolitical backdrop] is a reminder that Europe remains vulnerable given [Europe's] external reliance on energy and underinvestment in defense. In world of increasing fragmentation and greater geopolitical tensions, [the author] would be cautious on regions lacking self-reliance.
