# FX: Long EURUSD

FX: Long 1: EU reliance on US for exports has declined [The author holds] a bullish view on EURUSD and forecast 1.20 by year-end. A revival of US–EU trade tensions and the subsequent risk-off has done little to weigh on EURUSD with [EURUSD] holding up well just above 1.16. [That a revival of US–EU trade tensions and the subsequent risk-off has done little to weigh on EURUSD] is in spite of tit-for-tat escalation as a Greenland-related US tariff threat to EU states was met by an EU threat to go back to a tariff list prepared last year, and roll out of [the EU's] anti-coercion instrument to limit US companies’ EU market access. In the past the market has suggested bilateral tariffs are bullish for the USD as the US suffers less economically as an importer. However, [the author sees] some unique circumstances suggesting escalating tensions may be bullish for EURUSD. US tariffs on the EU may be perceived as less credible by the market than in the past given the risk that [US tariffs on the EU] implemented under the IEEPA are struck down. Even if [the Greenland-related US tariff threat to EU states] materialise, EU exports to the US as a share of total exports have declined to just 10% (Figure 1). If implemented as stated, [the author's] preliminary estimates suggest that the tariffs would shave off around a little less than 0.1% from 2026 eurozone GDP. [The author also sees] a number of paths through which the Greenland issue may prove bullish for EURUSD. Specifically, if [the Greenland issue] results in more EU integration, joint issuance or more defence spending outside of Germany[, the Greenland issue may prove bullish for EURUSD]. Additionally, if EURUSD continues to trade well in the face of risk-off concerns around the USD, then hedging flows from eurozone investors are likely to come back to the fore (see What drives FX hedge ratios?, dated 3 July 2025). If there is an FX transaction attached to any deal, whereby the US pays either Greenlanders, Denmark or both [an FX transaction attached to any deal] is also likely to prove bullish EURUSD. At the same time, relative data, both in terms of surprise and the absolute level of strength, have moved in favour of EURUSD recently, and yet [EURUSD] has lagged. [That relative data, both in terms of surprise and the absolute level of strength, have moved in favour of EURUSD recently, and yet EURUSD has lagged] suggests to [the author] some room for catch-up (Figures 2 and 3).
