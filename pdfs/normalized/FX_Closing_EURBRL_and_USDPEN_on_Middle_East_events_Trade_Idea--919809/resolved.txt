# FX: Closing EURBRL and USDPEN on Middle East events

As developments in the Middle East continue to trigger volatility and uncertainty for markets, [the author is] closing the remainder of [the author's] Latam FX portfolio of trades to better align [the author's] position with risks that may lie ahead. In particular, the more severe-than-expected attacks on Iran indicate a potential for oil and gas prices to remain higher for longer, as Tehran may respond in a more disruptive manner, specifically targeting energy commodities. [The potential for oil and gas prices to remain higher for longer] could lead to increased risk aversion across various markets. [The author therefore makes] the following adjustments to [the author's] portfolio:

- Take profit on [the author's] remaining short EURBRL position. [The author is] locking in a profit on the remaining half of [the remaining short EURBRL position] (USD5mn), booking a gain of 3.6% (USD178k). [The author] entered [the remaining short EURBRL position] on 13 January in anticipation of a supportive global macro environment for high-yielding EM currencies (see Brazil: Adding to [the author's] short EURBRL position).
- Closing short USDPEN NDF position as [the short USDPEN NDF position] has touched the stop loss level, and book a loss of 1.5% (USD301k). [The author] entered [the short USDPEN NDF position] on 3 February, on the back of Peru’s record-high terms of trade (driven by the surge in copper and gold prices), and in anticipation of seasonal corporate-tax liquidity (FX: Entering a short USDPEN NDF trade) [The author's] short USDPEN NDF position has touched the stop-loss level, and [the author] therefore exit [the short USDPEN NDF position] and book a loss of 1.2%(USD243k). The USDPEN moved sharply after the weekend’s developments in the Middle East, and, like many other Latam currencies, the PEN continues to trading weaker as risk sentiment deteriorated. Nevertheless, the PEN’s fundamentals remain sound and [the author still sees] upside potential. [The author] will therefore watch for an opportunity to re-establish a bullish PEN position, which could be further supported by the seasonal lift typically seen during the upcoming local corporate-tax seasons. To recap, [the author] entered [the short USDPEN NDF position] on 3 February, on the back of Peru’s record-high terms of trade (driven by the surge in copper and gold prices), and seasonal corporate-tax liquidity to appear in the coming weeks (see EM rates: Entering a COP basis payer).
