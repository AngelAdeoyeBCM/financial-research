# United States: Inflation Markets Volumes speak volumes

Ahead of the April CPI release, [the author flags] that front-end TIPS activity has been extremely elevated, even after controlling for risk events and intra-month seasonality. It is likely that some of [front-end TIPS activity] has exacerbated front-end valuations, pushing breakevens higher and tightening iotas. Exacerbating and sufficient US inflation markets have had a dramatic couple of weeks. Since the 5y TIPS auction (which was tied for the largest TIPS auction on record), 1y1y CPI swaps exploded higher, jumping nearly 25bp into early May, breaching 2.7%. 5y CPI swaps touched 2.8% for the first time since March 2023. As glimmers of an end to [the war in Iran] emerged last week, [the rally in forwards] partially retraced, but valuations remain stretched. Indeed, [The rally in forwards] occurred despite the energy commodity curves implying higher forward deflation (Seriously, but not literally, April 30, 2026), a slowdown in some of the high-frequency measures of rent inflation potentially portending additional drag on core services inflation relative to [the author's] baseline (Shelter from the geopolitical storm, April 23, 2026), and an April FOMC meeting that saw four dissents (three of which were hawkish relative to the bulk of the committee, Hawks break formation, April 29, 2026). From a systematic lens, seeing inflation forwards rally coming out of supply is consistent with previously observed behavior (Seasonality in forward ZCIS and real rates, January 21, 2026), and [the author's] sense is that the flow dynamics were exacerbated during [the rally in forwards] by investors wanting to pile into front-end TIPS ahead of an extremely strong carry period in May and June due to the strong realized [or] expected CPI prints in March and May (Awkward optics, April 9, 2026). While a rally in 1y breakevens and CPI swaps made sense given the risks of higher-for-longer energy prices (Shifting gears, May 1, 2026), the rally in forwards was a bit more puzzling and seemed to reflect a meaningful rise in inflation risk premium as markets widened the range of potential outcomes. In addition, the potential for a non-linear acceleration higher in energy prices came to the fore as inventories began to approach depletion (Living off the insurance, May 4, 2026). [The author also wants] to add one additional exacerbating factor: flow [or] volumes. Total daily TIPS volumes reached [total daily TIPS volumes'] second-highest level on record on April month end ($88.1bn, only exceeded by month-end on January 2026 at $120bn), with the caveat that daily data are only available since February 2023 (Figure 1). 5y on-the-run volumes on April 30 were the highest on record for that series at $32bn (Figure 2), while activity in the off-the-runs was not particularly notable (Figure 3). TIPS volumes exhibit significant intra-month seasonality reflecting liquidity events (including supply), month-end, and CPI, for example (discussed in more detail in Volatile Volumes, August 22, 2024). As such, if [the author models] "expected" TIPS volumes, controlling for factors such as nominal coupon activity, event effects (CPI, FOMC, auction dates, month-end), month fixed effects, and day of week fixed effects, and them compare the modeled outcomes with realized volumes, [the author gets] a better sense of whether activity was running above or below what [the author] would have expected, all else equal. Doing so shows that activity in the front end has been extremely robust, much more so than [the author] would have otherwise expected (Figure 4). Breaking out volumes into on-the-run and off-the-run shows that the bulk has been driven by off-the-runs (Figure 6). While [that activity in the front end has been extremely robust] is not necessarily surprising given the current geopolitical backdrop, it is also telling that volumes have risen significantly. TIPS 0-5y off-the-run The consequences of [front-end TIPS activity] are pretty straightforward, and [the author sees] [front-end TIPS activity] as having sharply richened the front end of the TIPS curve with most forward 1y breakevens above 2.5% on a headline basis, and closer to 2.7% ex-energy. Moreover, [front-end TIPS activity] has likely contributed to the tightening of iotas in the sub-5y sector (breakevens outperforming CPI swaps). Intuitively, at some point, it may be justified to fade the flow pricing distortion, though of course [that it may be justified to fade the flow pricing distortion] is always prone to the reverse of the "catch a falling knife" dynamic, and the geopolitical path inherently depends on what happens with the war in Iran. Directionally, [the flow pricing distortion] reduces [the author's] conviction to be long 5y TIPS, though [the author continues] to recommend that position from a more fundamental perspective, whereas the tactical dynamic for the front end going into [the extremely strong carry period in May and June] appears to have already largely play out. As always, the market pendulum sentiment swings, and this positioning [or] volume context is key going into the April CPI release this week.: Inflation Markets
