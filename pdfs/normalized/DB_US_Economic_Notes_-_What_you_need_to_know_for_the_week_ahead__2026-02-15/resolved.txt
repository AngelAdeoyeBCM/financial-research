Commentary for Tuesday: This week’s holiday-shortened data docket will consist of mostly re-scheduled releases that were delayed by the government shutdown toward the end of last year. Friday’s advance Q4 GDP release and December personal income [or] consumption reports will provide an important baseline for current-quarter expectations. Regarding Q4 real GDP growth (+2.5% forecast vs. +4.4% previously), [the author anticipates] at least a 70bps drag on inflation-adjusted output growth from the government shutdown – the longest on record (see “Everything you didn’t want to know about a government shutdown”). Though net exports are expected to once again contribute over a percentage point to Q4 growth due mostly to continued weakness in imports, Thursday’s international trade balance (-$56.0bn vs. -$56.8bn) report for December will inform that forecast. The advanced goods trade data released on Thursday will also inform [the author's] expectations for inventories last quarter, which [the author currently expects] to subtract roughly 60bps from growth. More important to [the author's] 2026 outlook will be Q4 private final sales to domestic purchasers (PDFP), a key gauge of underlying demand and a favorite indicator of Fed Chair Powell. Though [the author expects] PDFP growth to slow to 2.0% compared with 2.9% in Q3, risks are weighted to the upside – particularly if Wednesday’s December durable goods orders (-1.7% headline [or] +0.3% ex-transport [or] +0.3% core) outperform. Regarding consumer spending, [the author expects] real PCE growth (+2.5% vs. +3.5%) to slow from a heady pace over the past couple of quarters but still indicate healthy momentum heading into this year. On the inflation front, Friday’s personal income (+0.3% vs. +0.3%) and spending (+0.3% vs. +0.5%) report will provide the latest reading on the core PCE deflator, the Fed’s preferred inflation metric (+0.4% vs. +0.2%). Should [the author's] forecast hit the mark, the year-over-year rate will pick up a tenth to 2.9%. In terms of what [the author] learned from last week’s CPI data, the u pdated seasonal factors could slightly dampen short-term trends over the second half of 2025. However, despite January headline and core CPI coming in weaker than [the author's] expectations, the readthrough to January core PCE is less benign, implying another 0.4% increase. Depending on how strong medical services, airfares, and portfolio management are in the January PPI data on February 27th, it is entirely possible to see an 0.5% gain in January core PCE, which could push the year-over-year rate up to 3.1%. For [the author's] recap of the January CPI data, see “Every rose has its thorn”. Other data points this week will provide clues about current-quarter factory sector activity, which may have been weighed down by adverse winter weather across large swaths of the country in recent weeks. Though Wednesday’s January industrial production (+0.4% vs. +0.4%) should be boosted by utility output, weather may drag on Tuesday’s NY Fed Empire Survey (+0.5 vs. +7.7) and Thursday’s Philadelphia Fed (+5.5 vs. +12.6) indices.
