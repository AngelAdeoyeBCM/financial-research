# EM rates: Closing our 2y Camara payer

[The author] close [the author's] 2y Camara payer position as [the author's 2y Camara payer position] has touched [the author's 2y Camara payer position's] stop-loss level, booking a loss of 42.5bp (USD425k). [The author] exited after the Camara curve was repriced lower—a move driven by a rally in core rates, weak Chilean activity and inflation data, and declining oil prices. Moreover, the June monetary-policy report (IPoM) from the BCCh reiterated the market’s view that no rate hikes are expected in the foreseeable future. From a multi-month perspective, [the author still thinks] the market is overlooking the possibility of the BCCh tightening due to second-order inflation impacts linked to the conflict-driven spike in oil prices and the potential for Fed rate rises as soon as December (for [the author's] base case, see US: Changing [the author's] Fed call to rate hikes, dated 5 June). To recap, [the author] opened the 2y Camara payer (switching from a Camara-US spread payer) on 1 June in the belief that the existing hiking premium was modest compared with upside inflation risks. [The author] also expected the June IPoM to serve as a pivotal catalyst that could lead the market to price in additional hiking premium (see EM rates: Switching into outright 2y Camara payer).
