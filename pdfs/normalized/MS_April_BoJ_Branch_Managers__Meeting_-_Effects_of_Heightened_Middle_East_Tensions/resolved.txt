# April BoJ Branch Managers’ Meeting: Effects of Heightened Middle East Tensions

BoJ Watch | Japan April BoJ Branch Managers’ Meeting: In [the Bank of Japan's] “Regional Economic Conditions Viewed from Each Region” report summarizing the outcomes of the April 6 branch managers’ meeting, the Bank of Japan stated that while some signs of weakness have emerged, overall assessments were maintained across all regions compared with the previous Jan meeting. On the other hand, [the Bank of Japan] noted that “amid heightened tensions in the Middle East, several regions reported that the surge in oil prices and disruptions to logistics have begun to lead to rising input costs and reductions in operating rates due to constraints on the supply of raw materials.” Looking ahead, [the “Regional Economic Conditions Viewed from Each Region” report] added that “under heightened uncertainty, there are growing concerns about rising prices—particularly energy prices—and the resulting negative impact on corporate profits and private consumption, as well as the possibility that supply constraints could spread across the entire supply chain. Depending on how developments unfold, these factors could weigh on regional economic activity, and thus warrant close monitoring.” The Sakura Report included a boxed section on “Impact of Heightened Tensions in the Middle East” in the “Key Voices from Firms” section, highlighting caution among companies across regions. [The Sakura Report] included comments such as, “Due to heightened tensions in the Middle East, the outlook for raw material procurement has become uncertain, prompting us to lower factory operating rates and adjust production since mid-March (Osaka [Chemicals]),” and “We are on alert that a disruption in the supply of resin packaging materials could lead to a suspension of product delivery (Head Office [Food Products]).” At the same time, [the Sakura Report] pointed to positive views on capex and exports [or] production, supported by AI-related demand etc., and noted that following the spring wage negotiations, many firms—both large and small—are planning wage hikes in FY2026 at roughly the same level as in FY2025. Taken together, the “Regional Economic Conditions Viewed from Each Region” report and the Sakura Report suggest that the BoJ may be beginning to place relatively greater emphasis on the economic impact of supply constraints, etc., factors that did not appear to have been fully incorporated into policy discussions at the March meeting.
