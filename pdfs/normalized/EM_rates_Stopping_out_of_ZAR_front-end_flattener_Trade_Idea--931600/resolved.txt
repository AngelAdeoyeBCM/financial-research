# EM rates: Stopping out of ZAR front-end flattener

[The author's] ZAR year-end vs 1y1y flattener has reached [the author's ZAR year-end vs 1y1y flattener's] stop-loss and [the author] close [the author's ZAR year-end vs 1y1y flattener] for a 21bp loss. With the start of the war in the Middle East, the South African Reserve Bank’s initial reaction function was to remain proactive and deliver policy tightening in response to the energy price shock. Hence, [the author] initiated a front-end flattener trade idea ahead of the May MPC meeting (see South Africa: Proactive SARB to flatten front-end rates, dated 6 May). Despite May’s rate hike, the SARB did not follow through in July and kept rates unchanged despite market pricing a full hike ahead of [the July meeting]. [The author still expects] the SARB to revert to a more hawkish reaction function on 23 September (see South Africa: SARB stays on hold, but likely to hike in September, 23 July). However, the curve is struggling to price front-loaded hikes after the dovish surprise in July, and the FRA curve remains steep. As a result, [the author's] year-end vs 1y1y flattener (dates: 5 February 2027 start 3m vs 5 May 2027 start 1y) has reached [the author's ZAR year-end vs 1y1y flattener's] stop-loss level. [The author] close [the author's ZAR year-end vs 1y1y flattener] with a 21bp loss (or USD210,000 for USD10,000 DV01) at 11bp.
