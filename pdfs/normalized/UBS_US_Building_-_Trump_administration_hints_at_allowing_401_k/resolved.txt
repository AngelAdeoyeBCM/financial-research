A potential positive for homebuyers [The author believes] [the plan to allow for 401(k) withdrawals for home down payments] could be a positive for potential home buyers struggling to fund down payments. According to the National Association of Realtors, 33% of buyers aged 26-34 (prime first-time buyers) indicated that saving for a down payment was the most difficult challenge of the home buying process. Importantly, Fidelity research (link) suggests that average 401(k) balances hit an all-time high of $144K in 3Q25. [The author also believes] the $68tn generational wealth transfer (link) occurring in the US will benefit young homebuyers. In fact, survey work conducted by the National Association of Realtors (NAR) indicated that >25% of homebuyers aged 26-34 who made a down payment received help from a family or a friend. A potential step in the right direction, but what else could help? As [the author has] discussed in previous reports (see below for links), [the author believes] there are numerous avenues the Trump administration can pursue to support the housing market. In the short term, [the Trump administration] can leverage the GSEs to subsidize mortgages, purchase more MBS for [the GSEs'] own portfolios ($200bn already announced) and reduce guarantee fees (G-fees), the latter of which equate to over 60bps on a 30 year mortgage. Additionally, [the Trump administration] could reduce tariffs which [the author estimates] at ~ $9k per home (link). In the intermediate [or] longer term, [the Trump administration] could open more federal land for building, cull more timber from federal land and convince younger generations to pursue careers in the construction trades. Finally, in the event of a housing emergency, [the Trump administration] could exert more pressure on the states to loosen land restrictions, although most of these decisions are made at the local/ municipal level. However, [the author continues] to believe the most powerful long-term, structural solution to the housing shortfall and affordability challenges in the US is infusing technology into the construction process (link).
