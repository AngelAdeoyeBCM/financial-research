# EM rates: Extend target and trail stop on paid INR 5y

Chandresh Jain, EM Asia FX and Rates Strategist | MAR | Joey Li, Markets Strategist, APAC | BNP Paribas Singapore Branch. sold off significantly due to rising oil prices [The author] extend the target on [the author's] paid M26, 5y INR NDOIS trade idea (see EM rates: Pay 5y INR as growth picks up, dated 5 February) from 6.50% to 6.80%. The recent surge in oil prices due to rising Middle-East tensions has led to depreciation pressures on [the Indian currency] and a significant bear-steepening of the rates curve, driven by inflation concerns. Despite the Reserve Bank of India's announcement of INR1trn in OMOs last Friday, 5y OIS has risen by over 30bp in the last two trading sessions (Figure 1). Although [the author's paid M26, 5y INR NDOIS trade idea] has moved substantially, [the author continues] to like [the author's paid M26, 5y INR NDOIS trade idea], as growth, inflation and bond-supply dynamics suggest to [the author] that INR rates should still be traded with a paid bias. However, given the high uncertainty and fluid conditions, [the author thinks] it is prudent to trail the stop on [the author's] paid M26, 5y position from 6.00% to 6.30%, while extending [the author's] target to 6.80% (current level: 6.475%). [That it is prudent to trail the stop on the author's paid M26, 5y position from 6.00% to 6.30%, while extending the author's target to 6.80%] is because further escalations or prolonged disruptions to oil flows through the Strait of Hormuz could drive energy prices even higher – see Global macro: Crude reality (4), dated 4 March. Additionally, the government may provide further subsidies to mitigate the impact of surging oil prices, which could exacerbate fiscal deficits and steeper the rates curve further.
