# Bank of Korea to hike in July, remain open to further tightening

. 09/07/2026 1

RKE.

## KEY MESSAGES

- [The author expects] the Bank of Korea to raise the policy rate by 25bp to 2.75% at the Monetary Policy Board meeting on 16 July, marking the first hike since January 2023. The decision is likely to be unanimous.
- While the BoK will not release new economic forecasts or forward guidance at [the Monetary Policy Board meeting on 16 July], the central bank will closely monitor recent developments, including stronger GDP growth, the broadening of inflationary pressures amid economic recovery, a rise in financial stability risks, and a surge in financial market volatility, in [the author's] view.
- [The author anticipates] the BoK will maintain [the Bank of Korea's] current tone and remain open to further tightening. After July, [the author continues] to project another hike to 3.00% in October, followed by a prolonged hold, though risks remain tilted toward further rate hikes in 2027. 25bp rate hike highly likely: [The author expects] the Bank of Korea to raise the policy rate by 25bp to 2.75% at the Monetary Policy Board meeting on 16 July. [The 25bp rate hike] would be the first rate hike since January 2023, when the BoK hiked by 25bp to 3.50%. The decision is likely to be unanimous.
- Several factors in play: While the BoK will not release new economic forecasts or forward guidance at [the Monetary Policy Board meeting on 16 July], the central bank will closely monitor recent developments, including stronger GDP growth, the broadening of inflationary pressures amid economic recovery, a rise in financial stability risks, and a surge in financial market volatility.
- GDP growth: [The author believes] the BoK sees upside risks to [the Bank of Korea's] 2.6% GDP growth forecast for 2026, supported by upward revisions in Q1 GDP data. The central bank could judge that robust semiconductor exports, large corporate investment pledges, and a potential stabilisation of Middle East tensions, despite ongoing uncertainty, pose upside risks.
- CPI inflation: [The author thinks] the BoK will maintain [the Bank of Korea's] cautious tone. While Brent oil prices have stabilised since the May meeting, falling considerably below the BoK’s projection of USD95/bbl for H2 2026, uncertainties persist. Potential demand-side inflationary pressures cannot be ignored amid ongoing corporate and minimum wage negotiations. Relatedly, the BoK noted in [the Bank of Korea's] Parliamentary address on 9 July that the price diffusion index, which tends to rise when domestic demand is recovering, had risen to [the price diffusion index's] highest level since 2010, excluding the pandemic period. FX pass-through risks also linger due to USDKRW volatility. As such, [the author thinks] the BoK will continue to flag the risks of prolonged high inflation.
- Household debt: Two concerns remain. First, Seoul metropolitan area housing prices continue to accelerate, which could lead to a further increase in mortgage loans. While the government has introduced new macroprudential measures and is considering tax changes, and banks have tightened lending, the effectiveness of these steps in cooling the market has yet to be seen. Second, ‘other loans’ have continued to rise within household lending, suggesting increased household risk appetite for the domestic equity market. Overall, [the author believes] the BoK could be worried about an increase in household leverage in such a volatile environment.
- Financial markets: [The author thinks] the BoK will view the FX market with caution, given uncertainties in global central banks ’ monetary policies, including the risk of Fed rate hikes, developments in the Middle East conflict, and foreign investor behaviour in the domestic equity market. [The author thinks] the BoK is likely less concerned about the recent correction in equities and more focused on financial stability, especially after a recent surge in retail participation. The central bank may worry about the aggressive risk-seeking behaviour of domestic investors and the resulting repercussions of a sharp rise or fall in equities. BoK to remain open to additional rate hikes: [The author sees] few reasons for the Bank of Korea to change [the Bank of Korea's] overall tone from [the May meeting]. [The author expects] [the Bank of Korea's] forward guidance to be along the lines of: “The board will decide the timing of further rate hikes while assessing the extent of the increase in inflationary pressures, the improvement in the domestic economy, and financial stability.” [The author does] not think the BoK will fully rule out back-to-back rate hikes, although [back-to-back rate hikes] is not [the author's] base case. After July, [the author expects] another hike to 3.00% in October, followed by a prolonged hold, though risks remain tilted toward further tightening in 2027. Jeeho Yoon, Senior South Korea and Taiwan Economist | BNP Paribas SA Seoul Branch
