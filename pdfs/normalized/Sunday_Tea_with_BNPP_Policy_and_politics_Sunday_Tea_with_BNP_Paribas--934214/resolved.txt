## OUR KEY VIEWS

The Fed, ECB, and BoJ announc e rate decisions, nearly half of the SPX by market cap reports Q3 earnings, the Brazilian elections conclude and the UK announc es [the UK's] budget. And early the following week, results from the US midterm elections will arrive. While central banks have been dominating macro news flow in recent months, all the major central banks are likely in wait-and-see mode this week. A hold from the Fed will come as no surprise, given the steady stream of Fed speakers in recent weeks dissuading the markets from pricing in an October hike. [The author believes] the FOMC is attempting to balance two risks: the risk of a loss of credibility on inflation; and the risk of an excessively rapid tightening in financial conditions. [The author's] base case is that the Fed resumes tightening in December, and hike s at least three times in total this cycle (see FOMC preview: Skating in circles for now, 23 October). In Europe, the ECB looks set to keep policy rates on hold at [the ECB's] 29 October meeting, while keeping the door open to further increases as [the ECB] balances high energy prices with benign underlying inflation (see ECB preview: Words, but not yet action, 22 October). [The author expects] another hike in December. A further rise beyond that cannot be ruled out but is not [the author's] base case given the tightening in financial conditions and [the author's] expectation of a fall in energy prices into 2027. Recent bond market volatility has also raised the question of whether the ECB should intervene. [The author thinks] ECB President Christine Lagarde is likely to stress that [the ECB] has the tools [the ECB] needs, and stands ready to act to counter disorderly market dynamics. Relatedly, [the author believes] that the widening of OAT spreads over the past week has been largely driven by sentiment rather than fundamentals, amid limited flows. [The widening of OAT spreads over the past week] has seen risk premium take prices to oversold levels, in [the author's] view. Historical analysis of the credit spline and previous episodes of sovereign stress suggest that risk –reward now favors being long OATs. Indeed, at current levels, the market is already pricing [the author's] most bearish outcome. On Friday, [the author] entered a trade to buy 10y OAT versus 6m Euribor (Dec -26 contract). In Japan, w e expect the BoJ to keep rates on hold too (see BoJ preview: A pause despite 2% underlying inflation, 22 October). In the Outlook report, [the BoJ] is likely to finally acknowledge that underlying inflation has reached about 2% but indicate that more time is needed to confirm that [underlying inflation] has become ‘anchored ’ at that level. [The author does] not, therefore, anticipate a significant shift in the BoJ policy stance. [The author's] baseline scenario is that rate hikes will continue at a quarterly pace for the time being, with the next increase in December. Q3 US earnings season is now in full swing. Thus far, while EPS ha s been strong, with excellent beat [or] miss ratios, the market reaction has been more risk-off, driven by a heavy rotation out of tech. Indeed, Meta kicked off earnings for the megacaps last Wednesday, and closed 7% lower versus a positive close for the equal weight S&P500 on the same day. This week will be the busiest for earnings with nearly half the SPX by market cap reporting, including a few of the ‘Magnificent 7’. The following week, the results of t he US midterm elections will arrive on 3 November. The equity market’s reaction to the midterms will be path-dependent, in [the author's] view. Should the major earnings disappoint this week, the market will likely be more sensitive to a strong showing by the Democratic Party in the midterms. [The author sees] minimal risks of broad anti-AI federal regulation in the next couple of years – no matter the makeup of government – but [the author does] see two major risks to sentiment. The first is that if the Democrats take control of the House of Representatives, there could be more investigations and subpoenas of AI companies and [AI companies'] CEOs, fuel ing public opposition to the AI buildout in an environment where the issue is increasingly bipartisan. Associated with [a Democratic sweep of both houses of Congress], markets may price higher risks of anti-AI regulation from 2028 onward, which [the author thinks] could adversely impact current valuations. As such, [the author likes] owning SPY Nov26 put spreads, which capture a majority of earnings season as well as the midterms. Meanwhile, though a substantial degree of legislative gridlock is likely in all midterm scenarios, [the author sees] risks tilted to the upside for deficits – especially in a blue sweep. History shows that divided governments under Democratic presidents are often defined by fiscal restraint. But the same is not true under Republican presidents. Must-pass legislation to fund the government and the defense sector and raise the debt limit could lead to compromise. And compromise might result in more spending, not less, contrary to conventional wisdom. [The author thinks] thi s is a risk currently underpriced by markets. In [the author's] upside fiscal risk scenario for next year (a federal deficit of 8% of GDP on average in 2027–28 versus a 6.3% baseline), [the author's] modeling suggests fair value for US 10y yields could rise by about 65bp (see US midterms: AI and fiscal risks, dated 22 October). Elsewhere in politics, Brazil’s presidential election will be decided later on Sunday. While recent polls suggest a narrowing of the gap between Flávio Bolsonaro and President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, the best performing polls from the first round still point to an average lead of 2 points to Bolsonaro. Under a Bolsonaro victory [the author] would expect to see further relevant premium reduction in the local curves. [The author's] expectation is for additional rally of about 100bp over the following days as the market will likely price in an aggressive rate-cutting cycle with a terminal rate around 11%. The BRL is also likely to rally another 5% towards year-end, outperforming peers on expectations of fiscal consolidation and market friendly reform. Despite [the outlook that Brazilian rates and FX could continue to rally significantly should Bolsonaro win Sunday’s presidential election run-off], [the author] reduced the size of [the author's] Jan 29 DI receiver and short EURBRL positions late last week to better manage the P &L impact should an unexpected outcome materialize. And finally, in the UK the Autumn Budget will be announced on 28 October (see UK budget preview: A budget of continuity... for now, 15 October). UK assets appear strikingly resilient, performing well despite [the UK Autumn Budget] – a meaningful departure from budget-related jitters in the last few years. Broadly, [that UK assets appear strikingly resilient, performing well despite the UK Autumn Budget] is consistent with [the author's] expectations for [the UK Autumn Budget]. [The author thinks] the Chancellor of the Exchequer, John Healey, will seek to limit near-term policy volatility by introducing only targeted, temporary support measures and modest tax increases. [That the Chancellor of the Exchequer, John Healey, will seek to limit near-term policy volatility by introducing only targeted, temporary support measures and modest tax increases] should deliver fiscal headroom of about GBP18bn while maintaining the tightening trajectory of fiscal policy.
