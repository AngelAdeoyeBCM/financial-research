Long KTB trade: [The long KTB position] was established as a carry trade approximately a year ago, when implied yields were low relative to bond yields. At that time, KTBs stood out among EM Asian markets for [KTBs'] solid credit rating, while still offering an attractive carry. The market consensus back then was that the BoK would continue to cut rates, supporting a long position in [KTBs]. Since then, KRW rates have experienced several bouts of sell-offs, with market sentiment shifting from delayed rate cuts to rate hikes – both [KTB 2.125% June 2027] and [the author's] paid position in implied yields have sold off 80[approximately] 100bp as a result (Figure 1). Due to [KTB 2.125% June 2027]'s larger duration (dv01) compared to [the author's] paid position in implied yields, however, the current PNL of [the long KTB position] is negative, despite the positive carry earned over time.
