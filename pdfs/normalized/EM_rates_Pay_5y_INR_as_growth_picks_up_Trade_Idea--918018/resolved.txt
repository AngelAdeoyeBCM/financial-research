# EM rates: Pay 5y INR as growth picks up

Chandresh Jain, EM Asia FX and Rates Strategist | MAR | Joey Li, Markets Strategist, APAC | BNP Paribas Singapore Branch. Sources: Reserve Bank of India (RBI), Macrobond, BNP Paribas Fig. 1: Credit growth has started to recover across sectors We favour paying INR rates, as we believe India's economic growth has bottomed-out and recent deals with the EU and the US lower the downside risks to growth. Additionally, the gap between bond supply and demand appears wide this year. Economic growth improving: Several macroeconomic indicators suggest that economic growth has bottomed out towards the end of 2025. Credit growth increased across sectors in recent months (Figure 1). Industrial production, a measure of real output, also surprised to the upside in the last two months of 2025 (6.7% y/y in November versus 2.9% consensus; 7.8% y/y in December versus 5.9% consensus). Other higher-frequency indicators, such as automobile sales and volume of air travel, which proxy for urban demand, also improved towards the end of 2025 (Figure 2). Trade deals: India has been actively pursuing bilateral trade agreements with [India's] partners. The recent EU–India trade deal reduces or eliminates tariffs on most of the Indian goods. [The recent EU–India trade deal] benefits labour-intensive sectors (such as textiles, leather, chemicals, gems and jewellery), comprising more than USD33bn of annual exports to the EU and subjected to import duty between 4% and 26%. Post-deal, [the import duty between 4% and 26%] will drop to zero. Similarly, the recent US–India trade deal reduces US tariffs on Indian goods to 18% from 50%.

[The recent trade deals with the EU and the US] should boost India's export growth, which has been struggling due to hefty US tariffs. Budget FY2026–27 projects high bond supply: India announced a larger-than-expected budget for FY2026– 27, with record expenditures on infrastructure projects (INR12.2trn, about a 9% y/y increase) and to support manufacturing and defense spending. Further, gross government borrowing is planned to increase to a higher-than-expected INR17.2trn (roughly 18% increase y/y), compared with the anticipated INR16–16.5trn range. The estimated short-term borrowings have also increased by INR1.3trn in [the Budget FY2026–27], adding upward pressure on short-term yields. In recent years, state government issuances have been on the rise (Figure 3). In [the Budget FY2026–27], the tax share for states has been maintained at 41% for 2026–31, despite requests from several states to increase [the tax share for states] to 50%. Without a substantial increase in tax revenues, states will probably continue to face an increased issuance burden, contributing to a heavy bond supply this year. Overall, [the author estimates] the gross bond supply for FY2026– 27 for the central government and states combined will exceed INR31trn this year (Figure 3). Demand for bonds is weak: India's insurance industry growth has slowed down to around 6% y/y from double-digit growth in the last few years, due to weak economic momentum and regulatory changes. India's Employees' Provident Fund Organisation is also decreasing [India's Employees' Provident Fund Organisation's] bond holdings and increasing [India's Employees' Provident Fund Organisation's] investments in equities to boost the overall return of the fund, thereby reducing the demand for bonds from [India's Employees' Provident Fund Organisation]. Meanwhile, the demand from banks has also fallen due to a pickup in credit growth and less demand from the held-to-maturity (HTM) book. Previously, banks were permitted to shift investments to and from the HTM book once a year at the beginning of the accounting year. However, such transfers are now tightly regulated and restricted to exceptional circumstances, which has further reduced demand for bonds from banks. Finally, foreign investors remain on the sidelines, as the rate-cutting cycle is probably over. Combined with high taxes and elevated FX-hedging costs, [that the rate-cutting cycle is probably over] leaves minimal potential returns for [foreign investors]. Risk of hawkish [Reserve Bank of India] meeting: At the previous [Reserve Bank of India] meeting, the central bank cut policy rate by 25bp and maintained a neutral stance. Most economists expect the central bank to hold rates steady at the policy meeting on Friday. [The author thinks] that the [Reserve Bank of India] could state that economic growth data has improved, the deals with Europe and the US will also help growth, and that [the Budget FY2026–27] will bolster infrastructure-led growth in India, sending a potentially hawkish message to the market. Therefore, [the author believes] that a hold decision could lead investors to conclude that the [Reserve Bank of India] is done with [the Reserve Bank of India's] easing cycle, and INR rates could subsequently sell off. Recent rally in rates: The announcement of the US– India trade deal, which reduced tariffs on Indian exports to 18% from 50%, has cooled selling pressure on the INR. [That the announcement of the US– India trade deal, which reduced tariffs on Indian exports to 18% from 50%, has cooled selling pressure on the INR] led to a bull flattening in INR rates, as markets took [that the announcement of the US– India trade deal, which reduced tariffs on Indian exports to 18% from 50%, has cooled selling pressure on the INR] as a signal that the [Reserve Bank of India] will no longer have to intervene as much in the currency markets, thereby easing the tightness in INR liquidity. [The author thinks] [the bull flattening in INR rates] offers an attractive entry to pay INR rates. Trade idea: 6.17% and roll: –2bp/month. Risk: The key risk to [the Pay 5y INR NDIRS] would be further open-market operations from the [Reserve Bank of India], which are less likely. As the [Reserve Bank of India] does not need to intervene as much in the FX market after the reversal in USDINR, the need for OMOs will also likely decrease, in [the author's] view. FX: [The author has] initiated a long INR trade idea, as [the author sees] scope for USDINR to break below 90 in the coming months with economic sentiment improving, encouraging portfolio inflows.
