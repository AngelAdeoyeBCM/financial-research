# Canada Economics Comment: Headline Inflation Surprises to the Upside; Preferred Core Measures In Line With Expectations

22 June 2026 | 4:15PM BST BOTTOM LINE: Headline CPI increased by +3.2% yoy in May, above consensus expectations for a smaller 3.0% increase. The average of the preferred measures (CPI-Median and CPI-Trim) moved sideways at +2.1%, with both CPI-Trim and CPI-Median in line with consensus expectations. Excluding food and energy, monthly inflation accelerated to +0.3% in May (vs. +0.1%). Monthly services inflation accelerated to +0.5% in May (vs. -0.4%), driven by payback in the volatile travel services category (+3.1%), an uptick in intercity transportation costs (+2.9%, reflecting higher jet fuel prices) and higher prices for communications (+1.6%, following four months of price declines amid a price war between major telecoms companies). Sequential core goods inflation slowed to +0.1% in May (vs. +0.7%, mom top-down GS sa), reflecting a large swing in clothing inflation and a steep price decline for household equipment (-2.2%). [The author estimates] that sequential inflation for non-shelter wage-sensitive categories edged up by 0.1pp to +0.2%. Despite today’s headline surprise, underlying inflation data looked reasonably soft, consistent with [the author's] view that spillovers from higher commodity prices to the rest of the inflation basket will remain limited. [The author continues] to expect that the BoC will remain on hold at 2.25% in 2026 and see two-sided risks to the policy rate outlook. KEY NUMBERS: CPI (yoy) +3.2% for May, median forecast +3.0%, GS +3.0%, prior +2.8% CPI ex food and energy (yoy) +1.6% for May, median forecast +1.5%, GS +1.4%, prior +1.5% CPI-Trim (yoy) +2.0% for May, median forecast +2.0%, prior +2.0% CPI-Median (yoy) +2.1% for May, median forecast +2.1%, prior +2.1% CPI (mom sa) +0.5% for May, prior +0.4% CPI ex food and energy (mom sa) +0.3% for May, prior +0.1% MAIN POINTS:

1. Headline CPI increased by +3.2% yoy in May, above consensus expectations for a smaller 3.0% increase. Inflation accelerated on a year-over-year basis for four out of eight major categories. The largest increases were in transportation (+1.4pp to +9.0%)—largely reflecting higher prices for gasoline and intercity transportation, itself driven by higher prices for jet fuel—and recreation (+1.8pp to +2.8%)—largely reflecting payback in the volatile travel services category. The largest declines in year-over-year inflation were in household operations and furnishings (-0.6pp to -0.2%) and health and personal care (-0.6pp to +2.7%). Excluding food and energy, Economics Research CPI inflation ticked up to +1.6% in May (vs +1.5%), above consensus expectations for a sideways move. On a yoy basis, CPI-Trim and CPI-Median both moved sideways at +2.0% and +2.1% respectively.
2. On a three-month average annualized basis, CPI-Median ticked up by 0.2pp to +2.4% and CPI-Trim ticked up by 0.7pp to +2.2%. On a month-over-month annualized basis, CPI-Median moved sideways at +2.2% but CPI-Trim accelerated to +2.8% (vs. +1.7%).
3. On a seasonally adjusted monthly basis, headline CPI inflation increased in four out of eight major categories. The largest increases was in recreation (+2.0pp to +1.3%), while inflation accelerated by 0.3pp for food (+0.4%)—the release noted that prices for fresh fruit and vegetables increased due to reduced supply (particularly from Mexico due to poor weather and lower planted acreage following US tariff implementation) and higher fuel costs—and alcohol and tobacco (+0.3%). Inflation declined the most for clothing (-1.2pp to +0.1%)—following a steep uptick in April—and transportation (-0.8pp to a still elevated +1.2%). Seasonally adjusted monthly CPIX inflation, which excludes eight of the most volatile components (including fresh fruit and vegetables, gasoline, fuel oil and intercity transportation) and the effect of indirect tax changes, ticked up by 0.1pp to +0.2%. On a year-over-year basis, CPIX inflation edged up by 0.1pp to +2.2%.
4. Sequential core goods inflation slowed to +0.1% in May (vs. +0.7%, mom top-down GS sa), reflecting the large swing in clothing inflation and a steep price decline for household equipment (-2.2%). Monthly services inflation accelerated to +0.5% in May (vs. -0.4%), led by payback in travel services (+3.1%), intercity transportation (+2.9%) and communications (+1.6%, following four months of price declines amid a price war between major telecoms companies). Shelter inflation turned negative again in May (-0.1%), reflecting a decline in homeowners’ replacement costs (-0.4%), unchanged mortgage interest costs, and a slight increase in rental costs (+0.1%). [The author estimates] that sequential inflation for non-shelter wage-sensitive categories edged up by 0.1pp to +0.2%.
5. Today’s data were firmer than past months, however strength was largely driven by payback in volatile categories (e.g. travel services and clothing) and components that are directly exposed to energy price increases (including gasoline and intercity transport). The average of the preferred measures, which trim out [the volatile categories and components that are directly exposed to energy price increases], remains close to 2% on both a year-over-year and sequential basis. Today’s data support [the author's] view that spillovers from higher commodity prices to the rest of the inflation basket will remain limited. [The author continues] to expect that the BoC will remain on hold at 2.25% in 2026 and see two-sided risks to the policy rate outlook. Megan Peters
