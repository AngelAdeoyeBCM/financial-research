# China: Q4 mini stimulus package to provide mild boost

## OUR KEY VIEWS

- [The author expects] new special LGB quotas totalling RMB500bn to form the backbone of the Q4 mini stimulus package signalled by China’s State Council, along with the roll-out of a further RMB300–500bn in quotas for new policy-based financial instruments, if needed.
- [The author estimates] the mortgage rate subsidy will save homebuyers about RMB22bn a year, providing only a small lift to home sales and household consumption.
- The People’s Bank of China will keep [the People’s Bank of China's] policy rate on hold for the rest of 2026, in [the author's] view, counting more on structural facilities to provide targeted support to policy-favoured sectors. Measured stimulus likely in Q4 to bridge growth gap: China’s State Council hinted at a mini stimulus package in Q4 at [China’s State Council's] 28 September meeting, which aligns with [the author's] expectations: [the author anticipates] a measured incremental stimulus in late September or early October as the activity data for July and August was disappointing (see China: Soft growth raises prob ability of measured stimulus in Q4, dated 15 September). The purpose of the Q4 stimulus would be to bridge the gap between underlying growth momentum and the lower end of the 4.5−5.0% annual growth target. At Monday’s meeting, [the author thinks] policymakers likely had a rough idea about the growth rate in Q3, which might have fallen below the 4.3% pace needed for H2 to meet the annual target. [The author estimates] GDP growth slowed to about 4.2% y/yin July and August, which means the gap to the target is narrow. Therefore, [the author thinks] a mini stimulus package should be sufficient for a mild pickup in growth in Q4, especially considering the more favourable base effect for Q4 and that part of the proceeds from government bond issuance in Q3 has yet to be disbursed to specific projects. Potential fiscal stimulus via special LGBs and new policy-based financial instrument quotas: [The author believes] incremental fiscal stimulus remains the most effective lever to counter short-term downward pressure on growth. The State Council has called for “making good use of carry-over local government debt quotas” (defined as the debt ceiling minus the current balance). In 2026, carry-over quotas for general local government debt stand at RMB557bn, while [carry-over quotas] for special local government debt are RMB1.1trn, suggesting sufficient fiscal headroom. Following the pattern seen in 2025, [the author anticipates] additional RMB500bn quotas for special LGBs to be announced for Q4. Furthermore, while the RMB800bn in new policy-based financial instruments has yet to be fully deployed, the rapid implementation capability of [the new policy-based financial instruments], coupled with the fact that increasing [the quotas for new policy-based financial instruments] requires no additional approval, suggests that policymakers may roll out a further RMB300–500bn quota in Q4 if needed. Monetary policy to focus on structural facilities rather than rate cuts: The State Council meeting emphasized timely adjustments to policy tools, mirroring the language used by the Politburo meeting in late July. But [the State Council meeting] added a call to “increase relending quotas for scientific and technological innovation, agriculture and small businesses”. The People’s Bank of China followed up today with an increase of RMB700bn in relending quotas for technological innovation, agriculture and small businesses, while cutting the pledged supplementary lending rate by 25bp and expanding the scope to cover projects under the Six Networks initiative (comprising water, power grids, computing power, telecoms, underground pipelines and logistics). [The author continues] to expect the PBoC to keep the policy rate on hold throughout this year due to unabated pressure on banks’ net interest margins, the shift from broad-based deflation to mild reflation and the prospect of the US Fed hiking interest rates further. Mini stimulus to target investment rather than consumption: [The Q4 mini stimulus package] looks set to be directed mostly towards infrastructure investment and emerging industries. The meeting readouts specifically mentioned stepping up the construction of major projects included in China’s 15th Five-Year Plan and the Six Networks initiative, alongside upgrading aging reservoirs and grain depots. Interest subsidy for mortgage loans to provide gentle lift to property sales: The Ministry of Finance announced that [the Ministry of Finance] will provide nationwide interest-rate subsidy for commercial mortgage loans (housing provident fund loans are excluded). [The mortgage rate subsidy], which is set to begin on 1 October and last for a year, is applicable to house purchases with the following criteria:
- the property is the first home purchase, in the primary as well as secondary market;
- the area is no greater than 120sqm;
- the total home price is no greater than RMB1.5mn; and
- the subsidy is 1% for a maximum loan value of RMB1mn for no more than five years. [The author estimates] China’s annual home sales (primary as well as secondary) at 11 million units, of which about 40%, or 4.4 million units, may satisfy all criteria. If [the author assumes] an average loan value of RMB500k, the annual interest subsidy will cost the MoF RMB22bn, which is quite small relative to China’s household disposable income of RMB63trn. Therefore, compared with a 100bp outright reduction in the existing mortgage rate, which would result in RMB360bn interest cost savings for Chinese households, [the mortgage rate subsidy] will result in a smaller consumption lift. Nonetheless, [the mortgage rate subsidy] is likely to provide as some relief to banks, which have been seeing tepid mortgage loan demand due to a relatively high mortgage rate of about 3%. As for property sales, tier-1 cities – where prices have stabilised in 2026 – are unlikely to benefit much due to the price cap at RMB1.5mn. For lower-tier cities, [the mortgage rate subsidy] – while not as potent as an outright mortgage rate cut – might provide a gentle lift to home sales. [The author] don’t think [the mortgage rate subsidy] will be sufficient to turn around the property sector in the immediate future. Still, [the author sees] the incremental policy support as a good sign that policymakers are taking measures to stabilise [the property sector] (see China new property policies: Limited impact, b utpositive signal,dated 31 August). Limited impact on China rates: China rates have remained stable and have shown little correlation with the global rates sell-off, reflecting weak domestic demand, banks’ ongoing bond-buying activity and an accommodative monetary stance. [The author expects] a modest stimulus package to be insufficient to meaningfully lift yields or break the current range, as [the Q4 mini stimulus package] would primarily aim to keep the economy near the lower bound of this year’s growth target, and the funding is likely to be directed towards supply-side measures. Instead, [the Q4 mini stimulus package] may help to set a floor for declining yields, consistent with the PBoC’s preference to avoid overshooting risks. [The author thinks] there could be a slight widening in the IRS–bond spread in the long end, since IRS may receive only a temporary sentiment boost while bond-buying demand might remain strong. Still prefer dip-buying A-shares: China’s A-shares experienced a tech-heavy sell-off on Monday, mainly due to the approaching National Day long holiday, the lack of major surprises from the Trump –Xi summit and fears of a US ban on Chinese optical transceivers, as well as external factors such as elevated oil prices and US long-end Treasury yields. However, [the author still likes] dip-buying opportunities in A-shares, as onshore equity ETFs continued to see net inflows in September, and valuations have dropped to more reasonable levels. In addition, the incremental policy measures – including increased relending quotas for technological innovation and technical upgrades, as well as initiatives to stabilise the real estate market – should help support an improvement in fundamentals.
