# Thematic Research - Next week...this week

w Cross-Discipline Thematic Research Date 22 May 2026

Inflation will be the main theme next week with reports due in the US (PCE), Europe and Japan (Tokyo CPI). From central banks, there will be the ECB’s account of the April decision as well as the RBNZ’s policy decision. In corporate earnings, highlights include Dell, Salesforce and Costco. The main release next week will be the US April PCE report due Thursday, and [the author's] US economists expect core PCE to come in at +0.3% MoM, unchanged from March. In addition, personal consumption is forecast to moderate to +0.3% MoM (from +0.9%), with income rising by +0.4% MoM. Another release on Thursday will be durable goods orders and [the author's] team forecast a +0.1% MoM rise. Finally, the Conference Board’s consumer confidence is due Tuesday which is seen by [the author's] economists declining to 91.9 in May (92.8 in April). On the calendar front, Monday will be a quiet day for data globally with the Memorial Day holiday in the US (markets closed) and a bank holiday in the UK. Moving on to Europe, the focus will be on the May flash inflation prints across the Eurozone, with Germany, France, Italy and Spain amongst those reporting on Friday and the Eurozone-wide measure due the following Tuesday. [The author's] European economists expect inflation at 2.82% YoY in Germany, 2.83% in France and 3.42% in Italy and 3.59% YoY in Spain. See more in [the author's European economists'] full inflation chartbook here. Another highlight in Europe will be the ECB’s account of [the ECB's] April meeting due Thursday, and [the ECB] will also release [the ECB's] Financial Stability Review on Wednesday. Over in Asia, the focus will be on Japan, where the Tokyo CPI on Friday will provide an early read on national inflation trends. Economic activity data is also due Friday, featuring the April industrial production and retail sales indicators. In terms of forecasts, [the author's] Chief Japan economist sees core CPI ex. fresh food speeding up to 1.6% YoY (1.5% in April) and core-core ex. fresh food and energy to 2.0% (+1.9%). For industrial production, [the author's Chief Japan economist] expects a +0.2% MoM increase. Finally, [the author's Chief Japan economist's] week-ahead here with full forecasts also mentions that the Ministry of Finance will announce the results of [the Ministry of Finance's] forex market interventions conducted from 28 April to 27 May on Friday. Elsewhere in the region, notable releases include industrial profits in China, Australia’s April CPI and the RBNZ policy decision all due Wednesday. [The author's] economists look for a 0.3% MoM headline print for Australia inflation report (full preview here) and expect RBNZ to keep the cash rate unchanged at 2.25%. Rounding out with corporate earnings, next week’s highlights revolve around tech names including Dell, Marvell and Salesforce, with consumer firms Costco and Dollar Tree also reporting results.
