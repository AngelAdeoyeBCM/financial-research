# Fed Chatterbox: March Edition

13 March 2026 | 11:53AM EDT The Fed Chatterbox highlights notable comments on key topics from Fed officials since the last FOMC meeting. A few themes stand out: Since the FOMC’s January meeting, most participants have shared New York Fed President Williams’ view that “monetary policy is currently well positioned to support the stabilization of the labor market and return inflation to our 2% goal.” A few participants have echoed Governor Barr, who said that “it will likely be appropriate to hold rates steady for some time.” Governor Miran, who dissented in favor of a rate cut at the January meeting, said instead that four cuts in 2026 would be “appropriate,” adding that [Governor Miran] would “rather get them sooner than later.” Most participants have shared Vice Chair Jefferson’s view on inflation that “some upside risks remain, but I expect the disinflationary process to resume this year once increased tariffs pass through more fully to prices.” However, a few participants agreed with Governor Barr, who said that “there are many reasons to be concerned that inflation will remain elevated,” and characterized “the risk of persistent inflation above our 2% target” as “significant.” A few participants commented on the impact of the war with Iran on inflation before the start of the blackout period. While Governor Waller argued that [the war with Iran] “is unlikely to cause sustained inflation,” President Goolsbee warned that “if you’re now going to pile on a quite large energy and commodity shock, this might be big enough to be comparable to some of the big supply shocks in the past.” Prior to the release of the February employment report, President Williams noted that “in recent months, there have been promising signs of stabilization” in the labor market. Several other participants have shared [the view that in recent months, there have been promising signs of stabilization] but acknowledged that, as Governor Cook said, “the lack of dynamism in the labor market likely makes it susceptible to downside shocks.” After the release of a weaker-than-expected February employment report, a few participants have shared Governor Bowman’s concern that “the labor market is deteriorating.” President Daly also noted that “the hopes that the labor market was steadying— maybe that was too much—and we really have to keep our eye on the labor market.” Governor Bowman added that the labor market “could use some support from our policy rate,” a view shared by Governor Miran. Some participants have agreed with President Collins’ argument “for maintaining policy rates at their current, mildly restrictive levels for some time.” But other participants disagreed with [President Collins’ argument]. A few have shared President Goolsbee’s view Economics Research that “it is not obvious that our interest rate policy is even restrictive.” Others including Governor Waller have instead argued that “monetary policy is still restricting economic activity” and, as Governor Bowman said, that “absent a clear and sustained improvement in labor market conditions, we should be ready to adjust policy to bring it closer to neutral.” The Policy Outlook Bowman There is a trend—that the labor market is deteriorating.
