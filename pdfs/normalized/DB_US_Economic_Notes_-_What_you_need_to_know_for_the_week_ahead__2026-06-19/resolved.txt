# US Economic Notes - What you need to know for the week ahead

Economics US Economic Notes Date 19 June 2026

Commentary for Monday: As market participants digest last week’s FOMC meeting, as well as the Iran/U S MOU, focus will be on New York Fed President Williams, as well as Chicago’s Goolsbee on Thursday, both of who m will appear after seeing the latest reading on the core PCE deflator, the Fed’s preferred (for now) inflation metric earlier that morning. As [the author] noted last week (see “Fed Notes: June FOMC recap: Rock Chalk, Warsh-hawk “), new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh immediately put his stamp on the Committee. In a return to 1990s-style communications, he significantly shortened the post-meeting statement – it was cut from 341 words to 130 – and removed forward guidance. He also did not submit forecasts for the Summary of Economic Projections (SEP). Those projections, completed by his colleagues, skewed undeniably hawkish, and Warsh did not distance himself from this signal in his press conference. The median dot for 2026 showed half a hike, with nine officials projecting higher rates this year (six projecting 50bps of hikes or more). The median dot for next year moved up to 3.625%, consistent with the current fed funds rate. The shift higher was motivated by a reassessment of inflation persistence, with core PCE revised up to 3.3% for 2026 and 2.5% for 2027. In his press conference, Warsh leaned hawkish, emphasizing the importance of restoring price stability, and noting that the Committee has work t o do on inflation. He argued that the evidence is mixed on how restrictive policy currently is. Warsh also presented a positive picture of the labor market and growth and did not emphasize dovish points (e.g., disinflationary forces from AI or trimmed mean) In keeping with his promise of “regime change”, Warsh also announced the establishment of task forces in five areas:

1. communications,
2. the balance sheet,
3. the use and reliance on data sources,
4. productivity and jobs in the age of AI, and
5. the Fed’s inflation frameworks. [New Fed Chair Kevin Warsh] referenced [the task forces in five areas] repeatedly in [Warsh's press conference] and expects most of [the task forces in five areas'] work to conclude by year-end. In recent weeks, [the author] built a case that the Fed could well need to raise rates (see “Five doubts about the US disinflation story”, “What’s keeping inflation above 2%? Almost everything” and “Overinsured?”). With tentatively positive news on the geopolitical front – i.e. de-escalation in Iran – as well as so me greater clarity on the Fed’s potential reaction function under Chair Warsh, [the author] update [the author's] Fed view (see “US Economic Perspectives: Yield to Warsh: 50bps of hikes in 2026 “). We now expect the Fed to act against sticky inflation, which we have revised up to 3.2% and 2.5% for core PCE for end -2026 and 2027, respectively. Our base case is that the Fed raises rates twice this year, in September and December, bringing the fed funds rate to 4.1%. Our forecast then sees the Fed on hold in 2027 before returning policy to neutral (3.5-3.75%) with 50bps of rate cuts in H1 2028 (penciled in for March and June). Matthew Luzzetti, Ph.D. In this respect, we are keen to hear from New York’s Williams and Chicago’s Goolsbee on Wednesday. As we also outlined last week, we believe Williams to be one of the nine Fed officials not projecting a rate hike this year while we see Goolsbee forecasting 50bps of rate increases (see “Fed Notes: Who’s who in the June 2026 dot plot “). Recall that Williams also serves as the Vice Chair of the FOMC, thus we are keen to hear how his outlook may be evolving and whether his messaging is in line with that of Chair Warsh. To be sure, market participants will scrutinize both Williams’ and Goolsbee’s comments for any potential insights into how the Committee’s reaction function may be evolving. Though traditionally Williams has said little about Committee deliberations, Goolsbee may possibly provide more color on Committee dynamics. Both officials will appear after a full morning of data releases includ ing May personal income (+0.4% vs. Unch.) and consumption (+0.6% vs. +0.5%), which will provide the latest reading on core PCE inflation (+0.37% vs. +0. 24%). As we recently outlined (see “US Economic Notes: May inflation recap: (CPI-PCE) Wedge-ing the door open for hikes “), though May CPI data was somewhat positive given weaker core goods prices, portfolio management and foreign airfares in the PPI more than offset that leaving our tracking estimate of core PCE inflation somewhat higher than CPI. If our forecast is close to the mark, the year-over-year growth rate of the Fed’s preferred inflation metric would rise by another 15bps to 3.44%, the highest since October 2023. In summary, the Warsh e ra began with a promise to restore price stability. He noted “But the commitment to deliver is strong, unanimous, and unambiguous. And that’s an important message we’ve missed for five years. And we’re going to fix that.” [The author believes] the Fed will need to back [the Fed's] strong language with action sooner rather than later.
