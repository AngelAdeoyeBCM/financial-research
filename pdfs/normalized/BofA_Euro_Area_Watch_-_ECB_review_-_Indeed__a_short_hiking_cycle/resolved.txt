# Euro Area Watch ECB review: Indeed, a short hiking cycle

One and more soon [The author] expected the ECB to hike all three policy rates by 25bp. And [the well-telegraphed hike] is what [the author] got. The ECB delivered the well-telegraphed hike this week. And that came, also as expected, with no pre-commitment on the trajectory, a data-dependent approach, but still an implicit steer that absent very good news (on energy prices) or very bad news (on the economy), [the ECB] is not done here. Indeed, Lagarde took [the author] back to the watchers speech when [Lagarde] described three broad scenarios depending on the size and the persistence of the shock. And [Lagarde] implied that [the author is] in a scenario that needs a measured response (not a forceful one). [That the author is in a scenario that needs a measured response (not a forceful one)], together with forecasts that include more than one hike, while still having inflation back at target, means the ECB is likely not done yet. [That the ECB is likely not done yet] means a short hiking cycle in line with [the author's] base case of two hikes, but a third one is possible if there are additional surprises in the data. [The author still feels] comfortable with [the author's] call for 50bps in total. Even the adverse scenario could be consistent with a total of 50bp needed to bring inflation back to target by the end of 2028. But the balance of risks has shifted somewhat. A small change in the balance of risks Indeed, the balance of risks is shifting towards a third hike being more likely than “one and done.” For starters, one could argue that even in the mild scenario from the ECB, two hikes would still be needed to bring inflation back to target by the end of 2028. And, while above [the author argues] that the adverse scenario could be seen as consistent with two hikes also, [that the adverse scenario could be seen as consistent with two hikes also]’s not a clear case, and a third could be needed. More importantly, [the author] would push back on the idea of the ECB doing a “one and done” or an “insurance” hike. Lagarde also pushed back against that this week. “One and done” is not unthinkable, but [“One and done”] is a scenario [the ECB] would arrive at by accident, not something [the ECB] has in mind today. If the ECB were to skip July and things evolved very positively in the run-up to the September meeting (probably more than in [the mild scenario from the ECB]), [the ECB] could be done at that point. Where do we go next? As a reminder, [the author's] expectation, with low conviction, is that the ECB will deliver another hike in July. And the conclusion from today is that July is live, but [another hike in July] will depend on the urgency generated by energy prices when [the author gets] there; otherwise, [the ECB] will deliver in September. At the same time, the latest inflation print increases the likelihood of July if some of the move in Services proves not to be temporary. [The author thinks] that cuts will need to follow, eventually. [The author's] macro view holds firm: the path to sub-2% inflation is delayed, but all macro ingredients remain in play: a shallow recovery at unspectacular levels, with a very fragile and non-inflationary labour market, suboptimal fiscal policy, and central bank policy that always errs on the tight side. That still implies ECB policy rates returning to current levels at most by end-2027 (depo at 1.75% in [the author's] base case). EUR: limited impact, measured would be good The ECB meeting’s EUR impact was limited, with the FX price also somewhat affected by Iran headlines and the US PPI numbers. Most clients perceive ECB hikes as either risk management or a policy mistake, which has been in line with the downward move in belly real rates since the start of the war. Balanced guidance and a measured response would be the “sweet spot” for the EUR. In the near term and beyond the ECB, [the author maintains] a bearish EUR bias (vs. USD and GBP), viewing energy futures as overly optimistic and expecting Euro area data to remain weak over the summer. A more positive medium-term scenario for EUR is plausible if inflation proves persistent due to second-round effects. In [the case that inflation proves persistent due to second-round effects], a pre-emptive ECB may reinforce [the ECB's] credibility vs. other, more ambivalent, central banks. Monetary policy aside, ECB efforts to strengthen EUR’s international role support [the author's] constructive medium-term EUR view.
