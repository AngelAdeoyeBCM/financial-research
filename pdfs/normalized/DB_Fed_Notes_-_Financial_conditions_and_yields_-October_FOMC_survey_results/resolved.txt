# Fed Notes - Financial conditions and yields -October FOMC survey results

Economics Fed Notes Date 26 October 2026

This note summarizes responses to [the author's] pre-FOMC investor survey. Responses to the survey were submitted between Mon 10/19 and Thu 10/22. Views on current financial conditions and 10y yield expectations (Figures 1-3)

- Respondents generally viewed current financial conditions as accommodative, with 42% describing [current financial conditions] as “moderately accommodative” and 29% describing [current financial conditions] as “slightly accommodative”. Just 15% of respondents saw financial conditions as restrictive to any degree, all at only “slightly”.
- With respect to the level of the 10y UST yield that would lead to sustainably lower equity prices, 42% of respondents put [the level of the 10y UST yield that would lead to sustainably lower equity prices] at 5.5-5.75% while 29% placed [the level of the 10y UST yield that would lead to sustainably lower equity prices] at 6.0-6.25%.
- Regarding expectations of 10y UST yield one year from now, the highest probability on average was assigned to the 5.0 -5.5% bucket, with the balance of risks skewed toward lower yield levels. - Respondents on average viewed the probability of the Fed keeping rates on hold at [the October meeting] at 78%, though many responses came in prior to the sharper downturn in AI sentiment last week.
- Most respondents (63%) expected th e FOMC statement and Chairman Warsh’s press conference to sound neutral with respect to market pricing of h ikes through the end of this year. The remainder skewed slightly towards more hawkish communications. - In the event the policy rate is left unchanged this week, m ost respondents (86%) anticipated a 25bp hike in December. In the event of a surprise hike this week, most anticipated an additional hike in December while 34% saw the Fed as staying on hold then.
- In terms of t he economic impact of the policy action this week, respondents did not see [the October meeting outcome] as leading to a significantly different inflation trajectory, generally anticipating headline inflation at 3.2% over the next 12 months and around 2.4% over the longer run. Respondents viewed the probability that headline CPI (YoY) exceeds 3% in the longer run at around 25%, with the outcome of [the October meeting] having minimal impact on that assessment.
- Respondents expected a 25bp hike this week to lift the unemployment rate slightly (0.2ppt) over the next 12 months. The perceived probability of a recession over the next 12 months would increase by 7ppt to 22% with a hike, indicating that a surprise rate increase would push recession risks somewhat above the unconditional historical probability. - In the event of a rate-hold at [the October meeting], the median expectation was that the curve would be roughly unchanged, with 2y UST decreasing by 1bp and 10y UST unchanged, and that the S&P would rise slightly.
- In the 25bp-hike scenario, the median expectation was for a bear-flattening of the curve, with the 2y UST rising 10bps and 10y UST up 5bps. In [the 25bp-hike scenario], the S&P 500 would be expected to fall 1.5%.
- When asked to rate the importance of various channels for the reaction of 10y UST to a surprise hike, respondents saw the Fed’s responsiveness to inflation as by far the most important driver of higher long-term yields.
