# FX: Fade AUDUSD strength

Joshua Wilcock, G10 FX Strategist | Alex Jekov, Head of G10 FX Strategy, Europe | BNP Paribas London Branch. [The author] came into the year long AUD as part of [the author's] consensus trades basket, which [the author] closed today (FX: Closing consensus trades basket for a small gain), as historically consensus trades no longer perform in FX after the first week of the year. Instead, [consensus trades] tend to underperform in the coming two weeks, with hit ratios of 70% and 60% in weeks 2 and 3, respectively (see FX: Monetising year-ahead consensus, 11 December). The AUD is not only a consensus trade but also well positioned, with [the author's] positioning metrics showing AUD longs at a score of +43 (−/+ 50 scale; see Figure 1). BNP Paribas STEER suggests that AUDUSD is trading rich to other asset classes, in particular global equities, which [BNP Paribas STEER] views as the primary driver of [AUDUSD] (see Figure 2). [BNP Paribas STEER] does not currently view front-end rate differentials as important, but [the author notes] that relative to front-end rate differentials, the move in AUDUSD looks less stretched. Stretched valuations and positioning aside, [the author is] comfortable trading AUD from the short side.

- [The author sees] Australian superannuation funds as an ongoing source of AUD outflows (see Superannuation fund flows still weigh on AUD, dated 9 December 2025).
- One part of the bullish consensus year-ahead outlook on the AUD is the view that [the AUD] stands to benefit from higher commodity prices, particularly in metals. That said, when [the author looks] at the weights and prices of Australia’s exports, [the author does] not see a pickup in Australia’s terms of trade despite some metals prices rising recently.
- While [the author sees] scope for the RBA to deliver rate hikes this year, [RBA rate hikes this year] may take time to play out (see AUD rates: Pay Feb/ May RBA spread, 5 December 2025), and given flow considerations, [the author sees] [RBA rate hikes this year] as better expressed in rates than FX.
