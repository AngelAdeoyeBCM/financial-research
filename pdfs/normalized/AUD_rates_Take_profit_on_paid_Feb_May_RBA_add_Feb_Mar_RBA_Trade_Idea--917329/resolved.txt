# AUD rates: Take profit on paid Feb [or] May [Reserve Bank of Australia’s], add Feb [or] Mar [Reserve Bank of Australia’s]

Joey Li, Markets Strategist, APAC | Chandresh Jain, EM Asia FX and Australia’s inflation continues to run above target [The author has] maintained a paid bias in front-end AUD rates since Q4 last year, as inflation continued to surprise to the upside and remains above Reserve Bank of Australia’s (RBA) target (Figure 1). [The author] initiated a paid position in Feb [or] May [Reserve Bank of Australia’s] ahead of the Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA December 2025 meeting, citing the risk of a more hawkish stance due to upside surprises in inflation (see AUD rates: Pay Feb [or] May [Reserve Bank of Australia’s] spread, dated 5 December 2025). [The Reserve Bank of Australia] did adopt a more hawkish tone at [the Reserve Bank of Australia's] December meeting as [the author] had expected, ruling out rate cuts and warning of potential rate hikes if the inflation momentum proved more persistent than expected. Since then, front-end AUD rates have exhibited asymmetric pricing behavior, with a greater tendency to price in additional hikes on strong data while responding in a more muted fashion to weak data, and [the Feb or May Reserve Bank of Australia’s spread] has widened over time (Figure 2). Q4 CPI print to decide Feb [Reserve Bank of Australia’s]: With the Q4 CPI release scheduled for Wednesday (28 January), markets are expecting a 0.9% q/q print in the trimmed mean, exceeding the [Reserve Bank of Australia’s]’s November forecast of 0.8% q/q. However, economist estimates vary widely, ranging from 0.7% to 1.0%. As a result, Feb [Reserve Bank of Australia’s] remains only 60% priced, at 15bp. Currently, market consensus seems to be that the [Reserve Bank of Australia’s] will hold if [the Q4 CPI print] comes in at 0.8% q/q, but will hike if [the Q4 CPI print] reaches 0.9% q/q.
