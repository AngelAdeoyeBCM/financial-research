Resilient US macro: The January US employment data exhibited broad-based strength, with a 172k gain in private payrolls, a decline in the unemployment rate to 4.3%, and above-consensus average hourly earnings growth of 0.4% m/m. [The January US employment data] is likely to alleviate Fed concerns regarding the US job market and may influence the views of Fed officials for whom such doubts were underpinning potential support for near-term cuts. As discussed in [the author's] 10 February piece US: Sharp immigration decline to weigh on population growth, [the author has] revised down [the author's] breakeven rate for employment data to 0 -20k/month from 50k previously driven by sharp declines in immigration flows and slow population growth. Consequently, lower hiring rates can maintain low unemployment rates, as limited labour sup ply balances out weak hiring demand. Therefore, [the author believes] the risks to the unemployment rate are bidirectional, given the stimulative fiscal and monetary policy mix, declining trade policy uncertainties, and structurally lower labour supply in the economy. US January CPI on Friday was softer than expected at 2.4% y/y with the headline print helped by softer used car and shelter prices. Core CPI, on the other hand, was in line with expectations with non-shelter services remaining especially hot. Following the data, [the author reiterates] [the author's] view that inflation will remain sticky throughout the year with core CPI holding at around 3%, with two sided – but upwardly skewed risks at least in the near term (see US Inflation Outlook 2026: Sticky, not solved, dated 9 February). Overall, the backdrop from the labour market and inflation supports [the author's] view for a cautious Fed keeping rates unchanged throughout the year. Pressure points from US data strength: Despite the broad-based data strength, the reaction in rates was limited, with risk-off sentiment weighing on the front-end. The biggest move was the 6m point, and the market is reluctant to price out the 50bp of cuts anticipated for the second half of the year, reflecting expectations of a Fed with a one-sided reaction function to data. The limited move in front-end rates has kept a lid on FX impact. Still, [the author expects] the rates-sensitive complex to be the main pressure point in FX, while high-beta currencies continue to benefit from cyclical tailwinds of strong data and higher terms of trade. Hence, [the author maintains] [the author's] short EURBRL position given [the short EURBRL position's] attractive carry to vol ratio and the lack of election-related volatility in the short term.
