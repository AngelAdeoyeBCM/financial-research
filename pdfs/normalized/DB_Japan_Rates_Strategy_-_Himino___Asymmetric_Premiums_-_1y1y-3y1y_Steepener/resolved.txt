Himino [and] Asymmetric Premiums: 1y1y/3y1y Steepener

Monetising the Forward-Rate Ceiling Through Curve Steepening [The author believes] investors could consider a DV01-neutral forward steepener comprising a receiver in 1y1y and a payer in 3y1y. An entry around the prevailing spread of 38bp as of 27 August may be considered, with an indicative target of 65bp and a stop-loss at 30bp. Static carry and roll are estimated at approximately +13bp on an annualised basis. The position offers a potential means of expressing [the author's] rates outlook—a ceiling of 1.80% for the 2y yield and an increase in the 5y yield towards 2.50%—through the forward OIS curve. [The DV01-neutral forward steepener] may capture two themes within a single structure: a prospective unwinding of the market’s pricing for a September rate increase and a sustained term premium in the belly of the curve. The immediate catalyst was Deputy Governor Himino’s speech and subsequent press conference earlier today. Although [Deputy Governor Himino] provided no commitment to a September rate increase, [Deputy Governor Himino] stated explicitly that the BoJ must be “more mindful than before of upside risks to prices”, wording that did not appear in the July Outlook Report. [Deputy Governor Himino] also added orally that the Board would deliberate carefully at every policy meeting. [That the Board would deliberate carefully at every policy meeting] is not a timing commitment, but [that the Board would deliberate carefully at every policy meeting] is more accurately read as an explicit statement that every meeting is live rather than that the policy path is a blank slate—half a step closer to the language used ahead of the January 2025 hike. [The author therefore interprets] the remarks as an upward adjustment to the hawkish boundary of policy expectations, without an accompanying commitment on timing. The OIS market currently assigns a probability of slightly above 80% to a September increase. That pricing may still retrace, but the decline could be shallower than in the preceding three episodes. Valuation considerations likewise indicate that forward rates are already close to [forward rates'] ceiling. As of 27 August, 1y1y, 2y1y and 3y1y stood at 2.03%, 2.30% and 2.41%, respectively—28bp, 55bp and 66bp above [the author's] forecasts. In particular, 1y1y already exceeds the 2.00% level under [the author's] upside scenario, while 3y1y is only 9bp below the 2.50% level under [the author's] continuation scenario, implying that [the continuation scenario] is almost fully discounted. The initial market response to [Deputy Governor Himino’s speech] was led by front-end paying, which compressed the spread and improved the potential entry point. Himino Raises the Hawkish Ceiling Without Providing a Date Deputy Governor Himino’s remarks at today’s meeting with business leaders in Saitama were consistent with the base case set out in [the author's] 21 August preview, “A Hand on the Door to September”: [Deputy Governor Himino] provided no indication of the timing of a possible rate increase at the 17–18 September meeting. With respect to the timing and pace of policy adjustment, [Deputy Governor Himino] remained within the formulation used in the July statement, under which the BoJ would assess the degree of confidence in [the BoJ's] outlook and the associated risks. In [the Deputy Governor’s press conference], however, [Deputy Governor Himino] added meeting. The precise interpretation is therefore not that policy is a blank slate, but that every meeting is live—language half a step closer to that used ahead of the January 2025 hike. [Deputy Governor Himino] made no reference to long-term yields, JGB purchases or market-functioning considerations. [Deputy Governor Himino's] discussion of the underlying inflation trend was, however, more explicit. [Deputy Governor Himino] broadened the focus from whether underlying inflation would rise to and stabilise around 2% to whether [underlying inflation] could exceed 2%, adding that the Bank must be “more mindful than before of upside risks to prices”—a sentence absent from the baseline assessment in the July Outlook Report. The central message was therefore a firmer bias towards further policy normalisation, but without a commitment on timing: a higher hawkish boundary and a live-meeting signal, without a specified date. More than half of [Deputy Governor Himino’s speech] was devoted to a point-by-point rebuttal of criticism surrounding the June hike, which lifted the policy rate to 1.00%, [the policy rate's] highest in 31 years. Himino argued that “1% may in fact be a low interest rate in a non-deflationary economy”, that short- and medium-term real rates remain negative, and that policy effects take one to two years to reach maximum impact. [Deputy Governor Himino’s speech] and press conference have two principal implications for positioning. First, in the absence of a timing signal, the probability of slightly more than 80% assigned by the OIS market to a September rate increase remains vulnerable to the pattern observed in the preceding three episodes: expectations strengthened following a speech, subsequently retraced ahead of the meeting and were ultimately validated at the following meeting. However, the explicit statement meeting suggests that any retracement could be shallower than in [the preceding three episodes]. [The pattern observed in the preceding three episodes] still supports receiving positions in front-end forwards, but with less scope for repricing. Second, the explicit emphasis on upside inflation risk increases the weight of the right-hand tail in the distribution of future policy rates. Himino also acknowledged that yen weakness is contributing to inflation, while maintaining that the exchange rate is not a direct target of monetary policy. As discussed below, however, a substantial premium for that outcome is already embedded in current pricing. In [the author's] assessment, curve steepening therefore provides a more compelling relative-value expression than an outright payer position. Forward Rates Are Already Close to [Forward rates'] Ceiling Following [Deputy Governor Himino’s speech], 1y1y, 2y1y and 3y1y forwards stood at 2.03%, 2.30% and 2.41%, respectively. The expected policy rate consistent with [the author's] path—25bp hikes in October, January 2027 and April 2027, followed by a hold at 1.75%—is 1.75% across all three horizons. The cushions embedded in market forwards are therefore 28bp, 55bp and 66bp, respectively. The scenario comparison makes the valuation even clearer. Under [the author's] upside scenario, which adds a fourth hike in July 2027 and lifts the terminal rate to 2.00%, 1y1y is already trading slightly above the implied policy rate. Under [the author's] continuation scenario, in which rates rise towards the upper end of the neutral range at 2.50% by 2028, 3y1y is only 9bp below that level, meaning more than 90% of the scenario is priced. Current forward rates therefore reflect not simply expectations for the terminal rate but compensation for uncertainty around [the terminal rate]—that is, term premium, as captured by [the author's] 8 August “Policy Restraint” framework. In [the author's] view, the right-tail risk highlighted by today’s speech already carries a meaningful price. The realised outcome therefore depends on how the term premium embedded in each forward evolves. The 28bp premium in 1y1y should be relatively quick to compress as uncertainty around the policy path declines. By contrast, the 66bp premium in 3y1y is supported less by policy expectations than by structural supply-demand forces, including the larger JGB free float as the BoJ reduces purchases and higher short- and intermediate-sector issuance following the budget-request process. [The 66bp premium in 3y1y] should therefore prove stickier. In a plateau-confirmation or policy-stall scenario, the front-end premium compresses while the belly premium remains supported, widening the spread. [That the front-end premium compresses while the belly premium remains supported, widening the spread] is the core of [the DV01-neutral forward steepener]: [the DV01-neutral forward steepener] is not a directional call on rates, but a relative-value trade on asymmetric term-premium compression. [The author's] estimated returns under [the assumptions that the front-end premium compresses while the belly premium remains supported, widening the spread] (Figure 1, right) are favourable relative to the current 38bp spread. The position generates an estimated gain of 27bp under the pace-confirmation scenario and 32bp under the policy-stall scenario, creating meaningful upside when rates are held unchanged. Even if the upside scenario materialises and the terminal rate reaches 2.00%, the 1y1y entry level is already above that rate, leaving a 7bp gain. The loss scenario is a front-end-led bear flattening in which an early hike, including one in September, is followed by further tightening and the front-end forward rises above the expectations-consistent level. Even then, the estimated loss is limited to 3bp, with the spread ending at 35bp. On [the author's] assumptions, the position remains positive across all scenarios after annualised carry of 13bp. Nevertheless, the term-premium assumptions should be regarded as uncertain by approximately ±10bp, and the 30bp stop-loss should therefore be applied mechanically. Because the explicit live-meeting language could result in a shallower retracement and earlier policy action, adherence to the 30bp stop takes precedence over adding risk. A compression towards 34bp, the August low, may offer an opportunity to add only if the underlying thesis remains intact, while 65bp is the profit-taking level. An outright receiver in 1y1y, entered at 2.03% with a 1.80% target, is a more directional expression of the same view and may be considered by investors seeking greater exposure to the receiving leg. [The author remains] neutral on 2y1y, which lies between the upside and continuation scenarios and offers less compelling directional value.
