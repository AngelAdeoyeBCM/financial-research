# EM rates: Stopping out of 3y INR NDS receiver

[The author] close [the author's] receiver position in the INR z26, 2y NDS at 7.20%, realising a 29bp loss (USD290k) after hitting [the author's] stop-loss level. [The author] initiated [the receiver position in the INR z26, 2y NDS] earlier this month, expecting recent RBI measures to support a rally in the NDS curve (see India: RBI measures to push NDS rates lower, dated 3 July). While inflows into Indian government bonds (IGBs) have continued (Figure 1), market scepticism grew regarding the scale of FCNR inflows due to mixed reports and recent meetings between the RBI governor and various banks. Additionally, re-escalating tensions in the Middle East have pushed oil prices towards the USD80–90 range, exerting upward pressure on INR rates (Figure 2). However, [the author still believes] that significant FCNR inflows will eventually materialise, as the RBI is incentivised to use [the recent RBI measures] to curb currency depreciation. Furthermore, the RBI’s subsidised hedging facilities for external commercial borrowings (ECBs) should reduce hedging flows and support the NDS curve, in [the author's] view. That said, [the author] respect [the author's] stop-loss level and will seek better opportunities to re-engage.
