# EM rates: Take profit on paid THOR 5y

Chandresh Jain, EM Asia FX and Rates Strategist | MAR | Joey Li, Markets Strategist, APAC | BNP Paribas Singapore Branch. Sources: Bloomberg, Macrobond, BNP Paribas Fig. 1: Rates initially rallied due to the BoT's surprise rate cut, but have since sold off as oil prices surged higher We take profit on our paid position in M26, 5y THOR at 1.75%, capturing 27bp (USD405k) on the trade. [The author] had initiated [the paid position in M26, 5y THOR] after the Thai general elections, which resulted in a win for the Bhumjaithai Party (BJT) and Anutin Charnvirakul. [The author] expected [the win for the Bhumjaithai Party (BJT) and Anutin Charnvirakul] to bring some stability to the Thai economy and a return to fiscal spending (see EM rates: Paying THOR 5y after Thai election results, dated 9 February). Despite the Bank of Thailand's surprise rate cut in February, which initially triggered a sharp rally in Thai rates, the ongoing escalation in the Middle East has driven oil prices higher, prompting a renewed sell-off in Asian rates, with Thailand being particularly affected due to [Thailand's] large net oil imports. While markets are primarily focused on the inflationary impact of rising oil prices, [the author believes] Thailand's situation may differ somewhat from other large oil-importing countries in Asia such as India or South Korea. Although higher oil prices do contribute to inflation, [the author thinks] growth concerns take precedence in Thailand, which does not have an inflation problem – both headline and core inflation have largely undershot [the Bank of Thailand]'s target of 1–3% over the last few years. Instead, the surge in oil and fuel prices, combined with concerns over [the ongoing escalation in the Middle East], has led to a decline in air traffic, which will likely weigh heavily on tourism flows, a key driver of Thailand's economy. The BoT rate cut last month increases the risk that the Monetary Policy Committee may have a lower bound for [the Monetary Policy Committee's] policy rate in mind, particularly if tourism is negatively impacted, in [the author's] view. Given [that the BoT rate cut last month increases the risk that the Monetary Policy Committee may have a lower bound for the Monetary Policy Committee's policy rate in mind, particularly if tourism is negatively impacted], [the author] deem it prudent to take profit and potentially re-enter at more favorable levels. [The author] close [the author's] paid M26, 5y position at 1.75%, capturing a profit of 27bp (USD405k). [The author is] taking profit on [the author's] paid position in M26, 5y THOR at 1.60%, capturing 12bp (USD180k) on the trade. [The author] had initiated [the paid position in M26, 5y THOR] after the Thai general elections, which resulted in a win for the Bhumjaithai Party (BJT) and Anutin Charnvirakul, expecting the outcome to bring stability to the Thai economy and a return to fiscal spending(see EM rates: Paying THOR 5y after Thai election results, dated 9 February). Initially, the Bank of Thailand's surprise rate cut in late February, which was not priced in by the market, led to a sharp rally in THOR rates. However, the recent escalation of Middle East tensions has caused oil prices to surge, resulting in a significant selloff across Asian rates, with Thailand being particularly affected due to [Thailand's] large net oil imports. While markets are primarily focused on the inflationary impact of rising oil prices, [the author believes] Thailand's situation may differ somewhat from other large oil-importing countries in Asia such as like India or South Korea. Although higher oil prices do contribute to inflation, [the author thinks] growth concerns take precedence in Thailand, which does not have an inflation problem – both headline and core inflation have largely undershot [the Bank of Thailand]'s target of 1–3% over the last few years. Instead, the surge in oil and fuel prices, combined with concerns over war and strikes, has led to a decline in air traffic, which will likely weigh heavily on tourism flows, a key driver of Thailand's economy. The Bank of Thailand's surprise rate cut last month increases the risk that the Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) may have a lower bound for [the Monetary Policy Committee's] policy rate in mind, particularly if tourism is negatively impacted, in [the author's] view. Given [that the Bank of Thailand's surprise rate cut last month increases the risk that the Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) may have a lower bound for the Monetary Policy Committee's policy rate in mind, particularly if tourism is negatively impacted], although [the author maintains] [the author's] view that [the win for the Bhumjaithai Party (BJT) and Anutin Charnvirakul] will be supportive for the Thai economy and expect this year's increase in net bond supply to put upward pressure on rates, [the author thinks] it's prudent to take profit at [1.60%] and re-enter at more favorable levels. [The author] close [the author's] paid M26, 5y position at 1.60%, capturing a profit of 12bp (USD180k) on [the paid position in M26, 5y THOR].
