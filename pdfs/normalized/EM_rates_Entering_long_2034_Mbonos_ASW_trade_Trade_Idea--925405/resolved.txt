Mbonos typically rebound after bond switches: [The author expects] the Mbonos asset-swap spread to tighten after the government’s latest bond-switching activity, following the historical pattern. Banxico’s most recent bond operation (buying back short-tenor paper while selling longer tenors, switch date 29 May) adds supply of Mbonos but does not appear to pose any structural risk to local bonds, [the author argues]. Rather, [Banxico’s most recent bond operation] simply offers a better level to capture a potential spread compression. [The author's] analysis of 10 such switches since January 2025 shows that the 10y Mbonos asset swap spread widened by a median 2bp in the week preceding each switch. After completion, [the 10y Mbonos asset swap spread] narrowed by a median 2bp. Coupon payment to aid Mbonos: The upcoming Mbonos coupon payment on 4 June should buoy the bond curve, as investors will likely reinvest the receipts into the debt market. In the two most recent Mbonos coupon events (5 March 2026, 4 December 2025), the 10y asset swap spread first tightened by roughly 5bp on average in the two weeks leading up to each payment, and then narrowed by another 12bp in the two weeks following the payment (Figure 2). If a similar flow of funds into Mbonos occurs around the June 4 coupon payment, [the author anticipates] a comparable compression of the asset swap spread. Middle East deal still the key risk: A Middle East peace accord is a risk, as [a Middle East peace accord] could trigger a renewed rally in global fixed income (ASW widening). However, [the author sees] [the risk that a Middle East peace accord could trigger a renewed rally in global fixed income (ASW widening)] as contained because TIIE-F has recovered a third of [TIIE-F's] initial losses, while the bond market has trailed. Consequently, even if a deal materialises, TIIE-F has limited room to rally further relative to bonds, curbing potential for additional spread widening.
