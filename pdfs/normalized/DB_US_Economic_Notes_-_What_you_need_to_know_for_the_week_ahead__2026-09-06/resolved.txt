# US Economic Notes - What you need to know for the week ahead

Economics US Economic Notes Date 6 September 2026

Post CPI Conference Call: Friday, Sep 11, 9:00 am EST PROD/PROD0000000000641142.link Commentary for Tuesday: With the August employment report in the rear-view mirror, this week’s CPI (Friday) and PPI (Thursday) may very well determine whether the Fed hikes at the September 16 FOMC meeting. Briefly recapping last Friday’s release (see “August employment: Late summer labor market heatwave”), the August employment report outperformed expectations on almost all dimensions. Headline payrolls posted a 162k gain, with 55k of upward revisions to the prior two months. While there were slight declines in the information and financial activities sectors private payrolls increased by 127k, increasing the 3- and 6-month averages to +75k and +106k respectively. Payroll gains continue to broaden out beyond health care. Indeed, diffusion indices across all time horizons continued to rise, with the six-month diffusion rate (55.8) the highest since January 2024 (57.2). Though the unemployment rate ticked up a couple basis points to 4.14%, the broader measure of labor market underutilization, the U-6 rate, fell by two-tenths to 7.7%. Importantly, [that the broader measure of labor market underutilization, the U-6 rate, fell by two-tenths to 7.7%] occurred amidst a two-tenths increase in the participation rate (to 61.6%). However, [the two-tenths increase in the participation rate] was mostly due to reversing July’s participation declines for both older and younger workers; prime age participation remained steady at 85.4%. In short, while July may have painted an overly pessimistic view of the labor market, August’s data should largely correct that perception. [The author views] the labor market data as consistent with a broadly stable picture with a low monthly breakeven rate (likely in the 0-50k range). Indeed, there are potential early signs that payroll gains may even be slightly stronger than the breakeven pace, as evidenced by some measures of labor market slack tightening on the margin. As Chair Warsh emphasized “Inflation is running above our 2 percent target. So, the Fed’s predominant focus right now should be on prices.” Additionally, Fed Governor Waller elevated the importance of this week’s inflation prints in a speech last week noting that if the upcoming inflation data were to come in “hot” [Fed Governor Waller] could support a hike as soon as the September 16 meeting. As [the author] wrote in [the author's] preview (see “Webinar: August CPI preview [and] webinar registration “), a 4.4% increase in seasonally adjusted gas prices should boost August’s headline CPI (+0.38% forecast vs. +0.07% previous) relative to core (+0.21% vs. +0.22%). Should our forecasts hit the mark, the year-over-year rate for [August’s headline CPI] would remain roughly steady (3.38% vs. 3.36% in July) while that for the latter would fall a tenth from 2.48% to 2.38%. In terms of our component-level forecasts, we are Matthew Luzzetti, Ph.D. looking for an 8bp gain in core commodities prices (vs. +0.20%). On the core services (+0.25% vs. +0.23%) side, we are looking for a similar top line to last month, though some of the underlying components could differ. With respect to rents, we are looking for both primary (+0.21% vs. +0.26%) and owners’ equivalent rents (+0.24% vs. +0.26%) to post slightly lower readings relative to last month. Thursday’s PPI data, which comes ahead of the CPI release this month, will also be important to get a sense of the readthrough to core PCE, the Fed’s preferred inflation measure. Given our component-level forecasts, our ex-ante prediction is for a 0.21% increase in August (vs. +0.25% in July), which would have the year - over-year rate remain steady at 3.3%. As we also discussed last week (see “Fed Notes: Factions of the Fed’s family fight “), an implied core PCE print of 21bps, while not a automatic catalyst for a rate hike next week, would likely lean in that direction - though to be sure it would be a close call. As we detailed in our Jackson Hole recap (see “Working 9 to 5 to return inflation to target”), [the author thinks] Chair Warsh lowered the bar such that as long as incoming data do not surprise meaningfully to the downside, a rate hike at [the September 16 FOMC meeting] is the most likely policy outcome.
