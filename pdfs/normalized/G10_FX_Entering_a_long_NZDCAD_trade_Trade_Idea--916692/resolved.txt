NZDCAD as an RV expression: [The author] would characterize Canada and New Zealand as “late-cycle economies” given aggressive easing by [Canada and New Zealand's] central banks thus far, and markets now pricing the next move as a rate hike for both. The RBNZ is priced for 44bp by year end, and the BoC for 16bp. Easier financial conditions appear to be feeding through well to soft data in New Zealand. Forward-looking indicators are rebounding, suggesting that the worst might be in the rear-view mirror for the economy, and thus [the author] approach the NZD with cautious optimism. In contrast, despite easier financial conditions in Canada as well, [the author is] more doubtful about seeing a similar economic rebound. [That the author is more doubtful about seeing a similar economic rebound] is because of trade uncertainty acting as a drag, which [the author suspects] will be evident through to the USMCA review on 1 July at the very least. In [the USTR Ambassador Greer's] preliminary assessment of USMCA, the USTR Ambassador Greer said that “the shortcomings are such that a rubberstamp of the agreement is not in the national interest” and President Trump said [USMCA] is “no real advantage to us, it’s irrelevant really.” [The author interprets] [the USTR’s preliminary assessment of USMCA] as political posturing ahead of negotiations, but when assessing rhetoric about withdrawing from [USMCA] versus splitting [USMCA] into bilateral treaties, [the author thinks] this period of uncertainty will be reflected in future Canadian data. [That this period of uncertainty will be reflected in future Canadian data] suggests continued CAD underperformance on crosses, and [the author thinks] the contrast with the data in New Zealand sets up an attractive RV expression. Finally, [the author remains] on the lookout for any news on the US Supreme Court IEEPA tariff decision. If tariffs are struck down, [the author expects] the Trump administration to swiftly proceed with alternative levies, which [the author's] prior analysis highlights disproportionately hurt Canadian exports (see US: SCOTUS vs. IEEPA, dated 5 November 2025).
