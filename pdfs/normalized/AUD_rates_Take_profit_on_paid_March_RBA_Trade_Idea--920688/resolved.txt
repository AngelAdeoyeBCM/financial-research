# AUD rates: Take profit on paid March RBA

Joey Li, Markets Strategist, APAC | Chandresh Jain, EM Asia FX and Rates Strategist | MAR | BNP Paribas Singapore Branch. [The author takes] profit on [the author's] remaining paid position in March RBA trade idea at 4.105%, capturing a profit of 21bp (USD210k). [The author's paid position in March RBA] is part of [the author's] ongoing paid bias in front-end AUD rates, a view [the author] had since Q4 last year due to upside inflation surprises that exceeded the RBA’s target (see AUD rates: Pay Feb [or] May RBA spread, dated 5 December 2025). Ahead of the Q4 CPI release in January, [the author] took profit on [the author's] Feb [or] May RBA payer and added a Feb [or] Mar RBA payer, as [the author] believed [the Feb or Mar RBA payer] offered better risk–reward (see AUD rates: Take profit on paid Feb [or] May RBA, add Feb [or] Mar RBA, dated 27 January 2026). [The author] anticipated markets would continue to exhibit an asymmetric reaction function, with a greater tendency to price in rate hikes on strong data and hawkish rhetoric from the RBA than to remove [rate hikes] in response to weaker data or dovish signals. Since then, strong macro data and hawkish comments from officials continued to support [the author's] trade. In early March, [the author] took partial profit on [the author's] paid position in March RBA, as key macroeconomic data had been released, and pricing seemed somewhat fair for a meeting that does not coincide with the release of the Statement on Monetary Policy. [The author] chose to retain some paid exposure, however, as [the author] expected high oil prices could increase the risk of a back-to-back rate hike (see AUD rates: Taking partial profit on paid March RBA, dated 3 March 2026). Indeed, oil prices have remained elevated around USD100/bbl. Moreover, RBA Deputy Governor Andrew Hauser last Tuesday said that the recent flow of data reinforces [the RBA]’s view that the economy has limited supply capacity and affirmed [the author's] concern that elevated oil prices present an upside risk to inflation. Consequently, the consensus from market economists began to shift towards a rate hike in March, rather than May. On Tuesday, the RBA delivered a rate hike, citing a material risk that inflation may remain above target for longer, as short-term inflation expectations have already risen due to rising fuel prices. [The author] close the rest of [the author's paid position in March RBA] for a profit of 21bp (USD210k), bringing the total profit for [the author's] paid Feb [or] Mar RBA trade to USD330k. Although [the author maintains] [the author's] paid bias in front-end AUD rates, the recent sell-off across global rates has led markets to price in a significant amount of rate hikes from the RBA this year.
