# FX: Extending target and trailing stop on long USDTHB

[The author] extend the target and trail the stop on [the author's] long USDTHB (15Dec26 forward) trade idea, which [the author] initiated in FX: Long USDTHB as rising oil prices poses upside risks on 11 September. To recap, [the author's] rationale was predicated on elevated oil prices shifting momentum in USDAsia pairs to the upside, weighing on the trade balance and raising fiscal concerns; elevated capital goods imports needed for data centres; widening US-TH rate differentials with the Bank of Thailand likely to remain on hold while the Fed begins raising rates; and Fed hikes weighing on gold prices in the near-term, to which the currency is closely correlated. Additionally, dividend seasonality in September, rich THB valuations and heavy short USDTHB positioning made it attractive to build USDTHB longs. Since then, [the author's long USDTHB (15Dec26 forward) trade idea] has performed in [the author's] favour – the THB has been the second worst-performing currency (behind the IDR) in EM Asia, reflecting the aforementioned factors, which have since materialised, in [the author's] view. [The author sees] scope for [USDTHB] to rise further, and favour extending the target on [the author's] trade idea to 34.50, from 33.94, for four reasons:

- From a policy perspective, [the author anticipates] two further Fed rate hikes in December and January, and see a material risk of an October hike should upcoming non-farm payrolls and US CPI data remain strong.
- [The author expects] a seasonal uptick in demand for crude and refined products in Asia. With prices likely to remain elevated (see Chinese product export curbs to support USDAsia pairs, dated 1 October), Thailand’s trade and fiscal balances are likely to stay under pressure.
- Recent flooding in Thailand could act as an additional drag on domestic economic activity and the government’s fiscal position. Combined with the elevated oil prices, [Recent flooding in Thailand] may also weigh on tourism, just as the season is due to get underway in Thailand.
- While short positions in the THB have built to a score of -32, according to BNP Paribas Global FX Positioning Tracker (on a +/ -50 scale), [the author sees] scope for [short positions in the THB] to turn more extreme against this backdrop. Given the strong performance of [the author's] short THB position to date, [the author believes] it is prudent to protect current gains. With dividend seasonality now over and the risk of a pullback in capital goods imports following the government’s pause on data-centre project approvals and construction, [the author favours] trailing [the author's] stop from 32.20 to 33, locking in a minimum gain of 0.15% on [the author's long USDTHB (15Dec26 forward) trade idea].
