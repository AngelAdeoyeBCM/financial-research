Rates Strategy View: Hiking Without Hiking The BoE’s hawkish rhetoric delivered a 30bp (at one point 40bp+) effective rate hike today alone (to 14:15 GMT) via the market reaction. Moreover, end-2026 pricing has now increased 110bp since the conflict began such that a significant tightening in financial conditions is underway. The curve reaction was also staggering, with 30yr yields relatively stable (with 2s30s GBP swaps 26bp flatter as of 14:15GMT). The BoE had little choice but to sound hawkish into a fresh inflation shock given recent history, and jaw-boning the market is an effective strategy in times of great uncertainty. However, [the Bank of England] might not have intended a re-pricing of quite this magnitude. [That the Bank of England might not have intended a re-pricing of quite this magnitude] may be one price of the new individual paragraphs (which have never been tested in a shock having only been used since November). While offering greater transparency, [the new individual paragraphs] arguably means less control of the overall messaging as the market reacts to individual comments (in this case referencing hike possibilities). [The author's] read of the overall tone was that [the overall tone] was consistent with a base case to hold for the next few meetings to await more information on the scale, duration, and second-round effects of the Middle East conflict. Governor Bailey later cautioned against “any strong conclusions” about rate hikes (see Bloomberg, 19Mar26). Against that backdrop, the near 50bp of hikes priced over the next three meetings looks an overshoot. Jamie Searle
