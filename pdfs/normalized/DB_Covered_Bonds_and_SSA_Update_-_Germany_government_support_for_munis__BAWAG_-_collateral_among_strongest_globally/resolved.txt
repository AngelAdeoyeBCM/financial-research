# Covered Bonds and SSA Update - Germany government support for munis, BAWAG - collateral among strongest globally

Credit [or] Covered Bonds Covered Bonds and SSA Update Date 29 June 2026

Support for German municipalities – limited impact on federal state supply From a constitutional perspective, German municipalities belong to the respective federal states. Federal states are typically under pressure to support [German federal states'] municipalities in case of significant budget deficits. Last week, after long discussions in recent months, the prime ministers of German federal states and the central government (Bund) agreed on relief measures for federal states and municipalities. [Relief measures for federal states and municipalities] are planned to become effective on 1 Sept. According to [relief measures for federal states and municipalities], the Bund will cover 80% of the costs or lower revenues arising from new federal regulations (excluding tax laws) for federal states and municipalities. The cost coverage is set to apply from a minimum threshold of EUR 200m. [That the Bund will cover 80% of the costs or lower revenues arising from new federal regulations (excluding tax laws) for federal states and municipalities] means the Bund has broadly met the earlier demands by the federal states, which had requested an 80% share and a minimum threshold of EUR 125m. According to the prime minister or the German federal state or Rhineland-Palatinate (RHIPAL), [relief measures for federal states and municipalities] wil l lead to a relief for municipalities of over EUR 1bn in 2027 already, suggesting that the impact in H2 will be very limited. Moreover, in [the author's] understanding, [relief measures for federal states and municipalities] will mainly prevent be a further deterioration of already stretched budgets of German municipalities. Hence, at this stage, [the author] struggle to see that [relief measures for federal states and municipalities] will lead to a significant decline of gross supply by federal states particularly not in H2 (as [relief measures for federal states and municipalities] come info effect on 1 Sept only). Either way, with the credit quality of Bunds likely to deteriorate going forward, [the author expects] federal state bonds to outperform Bunds in the medium to longer term, ultimately trading at similar levels to Bunds, as for example, Spanish regions versus Spanish government bonds. CB cover pool quality – no driver for spreads, BAWAG with very strong CB quality Cover pool quality of CB seems to have no impact on pricing. Instead, country and particularly the assumed issuer credit quality and systemic importance of the respective issuer seem the key drivers. [That Cover pool quality of CB seems to have no impact on pricing] is nothing new and there are also arguments for [that Cover pool quality of CB seems to have no impact on pricing], e.g., CB frameworks guarantee a minimum pool credit quality, minimum and voluntary OC provide compensation for weaker collateral, and no CB cover pool was ever accelerated. However, given that CB are bank bonds with additional protection provided by a claim against a cover pool, strong differences of cover pool quality being quasi-irrelevant for spreads seems not appropriate. Moreover, from a macro perspective, [that Cover pool quality of CB seems to have no impact on pricing] could lead to a misallocation of resources. [The author highlights] that - for example- Austrian BAWAG, Bausparkasse Wuestenrot and Raiffeisen Bausparkasse have Moody's collateral scores of 4.1%, 4% and 4% regarding [Austrian BAWAG, Bausparkasse Wuestenrot and Raiffeisen Bausparkasse's] Austrian CB respectively. Collateral scores at or close to 4% are among the lowest for all CB globally. Broadly speaking, the Moody's collateral score is a theoretical junior tranche which would allow the highest rating for the respective cover pool as ABS. Hence, the lower the collateral score, the better the credit quality of the cover pool. Given the likely further increasing share of Dutch residential mortgage loans in BAWAG's Austrian CB (currently 41%, see link here), the collateral score could improve slightly further to 4% going forward (in line with the 4% collateral score for BAWAG's Dutch CB). The average collateral score of Austrian mortgage CB is 7.7%, with four Austrian mortgage cover pools even having a collateral score of above 10%. The average collateral score of German mortgage CB is 8.6%. In both cases, the relatively high average collateral scores are due to relatively high average shares of CRE loans in the cover pools. At the end of Q2 2026, BAWAG will have a share of 100% residential mortgage loan [BAWAG's] Austrian cover pool. Despite the much stronger cover pool quality Austrian mortgage CB issued by BAWAG, Raiffeisen Bausparkasse and Wuestenrot do not trade significantly tighter than mortgage CB issued by other Austrian CB with higher collateral scores. While [that Austrian mortgage CB issued by BAWAG, Raiffeisen Bausparkasse and Wuestenrot do not trade significantly tighter than mortgage CB issued by other Austrian CB with higher collateral scores] is unlikely to change significantly in the short term, from a fundamental p erspective, [that Austrian mortgage CB issued by BAWAG, Raiffeisen Bausparkasse and Wuestenrot do not trade significantly tighter than mortgage CB issued by other Austrian CB with higher collateral scores] seems like a mispricing. [The author highlights] that the low average collateral score of 4% for [Norwegian, Singaporean, Korean and Slovakian CB's] mortgage cover pools is also the reason why [the author likes], for example, Norwegian, Singaporean, Korean and Slovakian CB from a fundamental perspective.
