# FX: Long SGDNEER ahead of April [Monetary Authority of Singapore’s] meeting

Chandresh Jain, EM Asia FX and Rates Strategist | Joey Li, Markets Analyst, APAC | Parisha Saimbi, EM Asia FXLM Strategist | BNP Paribas Singapore Branch. 1:: S$NEER vs Input Prices [The author favours] adding long SGDNEER exposure ahead of the Monetary Authority of Singapore’s meeting in April, via a proxy NEER basket optimised for accessibility and carry. Risks for hawkish [Monetary Authority of Singapore’s] shift: Singapore’s growth has remained robust, while inflation has bottomed out from 2025 lows. Since January, [the author has] flagged a risk of the [Monetary Authority of Singapore’s] reducing [the Monetary Authority of Singapore’s'] policy accommodativeness this year, as Singapore goes through a reflationary phase (see Singapore: Reflation set to change [Monetary Authority of Singapore’s] and market views, dated 7 January). Historically, the [Monetary Authority of Singapore’s] has usually done [reducing its policy accommodativeness] by increasing the SGDNEER slope (Figure 1), which [the author estimates] has a long-term average of 1.0% and is currently at 0.50%. The recent Middle East conflict has added to inflation concerns, given Singapore’s reliance on oil imports and limited reserves (FX: Asia’s sensitivity to supply-driven energy shocks, 10 March), increasing the likelihood of a hawkish move from the [Monetary Authority of Singapore’s] in April. Recentering risk: Historically, the [Monetary Authority of Singapore’s] has responded to prolonged periods of high inflation, caused by high oil prices in 2011 and 2022, by re-centering the SGDNEER mid-point upwards. [Re-centering the SGDNEER mid-point upwards] allows scope for the SGD to appreciate quickly, mitigating the impact of high inflation (Figure 2). While re-centering the policy band is typically a measure [Monetary Authority of Singapore’s] reserves for extreme circumstances, given the current sharp increase in oil prices, [the author believes] [Monetary Authority of Singapore’s] may consider re-centering the band upwards. [Re-centering the SGDNEER mid-point upwards] would provide a faster means of reducing imported inflation compared to adjusting the SGDNEER slope. [The author thinks] the market is underestimating the likelihood of a re-centering from [Monetary Authority of Singapore’s] and therefore recommend running long SGDNEER. If oil prices were to remain high, exacerbating growth concerns, [the author expects] the government to respond with fiscal measures to support the economy, allowing monetary policy to focus on maintaining price stability. The current risk-off sentiment driven by the Middle East conflict has led to broad USD strength, which saw the SGDNEER initially decline, as the USD holds a significant weight in the index.
