1. Policy decision: Status quo maintained, but Takata proposed a rate hike The BoJ decided at today's Monetary Policy Meeting (MPM) to maintain the short-term policy interest rate (the uncollateralized overnight call rate target) at 0.75%, as widely expected. However, in the vote on the proposal, policy board member Takata cast a dissenting vote and submitted a counter-proposal of a rate hike. As noted in [the author's] preview memo, Takata has consistently indicated [policy board member Takata's] view that the 2% price stability target has been largely achieved. From [the perspective that the 2% price stability target has been largely achieved], it is a natural logical conclusion to reduce the degree of monetary accommodation and raise the policy interest rate toward at least 1%, which is estimated to be the lower bound of the nominal neutral interest rate. [Takata's proposal for a rate hike] represents putting that idea into action and can be seen as a clear indication of [policy board member Takata's] intention to lead future discussions on rate hikes. In addition to Takata, board member Tamura also dissented from the contents of the Outlook Report. Since Tamura anticipates achieving the price stability target at an earlier timing, it is highly likely that [board member Tamura] will propose an interest rate hike in the near future.
2. Outlook report: Economic and inflation forecasts generally turn more hawkish The revised Outlook Report presented a generally stronger outlook than anticipated. Overall, both economic activity and inflation have clearly moved away from the "modest growth" phase seen in the previous report (October 2025) and shifted towards a more positive outlook. 2.1 Improvement in economic outlook Improved assessment of economic prospects: The assessment of the economic outlook improved from "modest growth," stemming from a slowdown in overseas economies, to "continued moderate growth," supported by the government's economic measures and "a virtuous cycle from income to spending." [The Improved assessment of economic prospects] represents an even more positive judgment compared to the phrase "likely to remain moderate" used in the December statement last year. Notably, the reappearance of the keyword "a virtuous cycle from income to spending" for the first time since January 2025 is symbolic, indicating a return to the optimistic economic view that prevailed before the emergence of uncertain factors such as the U.S. tariff hikes. Increased robustness of individual components: Reflecting [the Improved assessment of economic prospects], individual economic indicators also showed increased robustness across the board. Corporate profits shifted from "remaining at high levels, but decreasing" to "remaining at high levels," and the description of "deceleration in the growth rate" for business fixed investment was removed. Regarding nominal wages, consistent with the changes made in the December statement, the view that "firms are highly likely to continue to steadily raise wages in this year's annual spring labor-management wage negotiations, leading to relatively high growth" has been maintained, demonstrating confidence in sustainable wage increases. Upward revision of real GDP forecasts: The median forecasts of the policy board members for real GDP growth were also revised upward by a larger margin than [the author's] expectations. While the forecast for fiscal 2027 was revised downward from +1.0% to +0.8%, [that the forecast for fiscal 2027 was revised downward from +1.0% to +0.8%] can be interpreted as a reactionary decline from the upward revisions in fiscal 2025-26. 2.2 Improvement in inflation outlook Confirmation of stronger underlying inflation: The timing for the year-on-year rate of increase in the consumer price index (CPI, all items less fresh food, i.e., core CPI) to fall below 2% was slightly brought forward from "the first half of fiscal 2026" to "the first half of 2026," primarily due to the effects of government measures to address rising prices. However, more significant is the outlook for core-core CPI, which excludes the effects of energy prices. [The outlook for core-core CPI] was revised upward from the October assessment of "temporarily falling below 2%" to "decelerating to around 2% and then remaining at around the same level." [That the outlook for core-core CPI was revised upward from the October assessment of "temporarily falling below 2%" to "decelerating to around 2% and then remaining at around the same level."] suggests that the BoJ judges core-core CPI inflation will not clearly fall below 2%, which is consistent with [the author's] inflation outlook. Strengthening of upward factors: The factors supporting the inflation outlook also showed overall improvement. Underlying CPI inflation shifted from "sluggish" to "continuing to rise moderately," and the macroeconomic output gap moved from "remaining at around the current level" to "moderately widening in positive territory." Medium- to long-term inflation expectations also changed from "rising again" to "continuing to rise moderately," indicating a stronger focus on more sustained momentum. Upward revision of core-core inflation outlook and consensus: In the median forecasts of the policy board members, core-core CPI inflation for fiscal 2025 was revised upward from +2.8% to +3.0%, and for fiscal 2026, from +2.0% to +2.2%. Furthermore, the fact that the lower bound of the forecast range for fiscal 2026 shifted from 1.8%-2.2% in October to 2.0%-2.3% is very important. As [the forecast range for fiscal 2026] excludes the highest and lowest figures, [that the lower bound of the forecast range for fiscal 2026 shifted from 1.8%-2.2% in October to 2.0%-2.3%] implies that at least eight out of the nine board members expect core-core CPI inflation to reach 2% or higher in fiscal 2026, strongly suggesting a strengthening consensus within the BoJ regarding the achievement of the price stability target. Of course, the possibility that the remaining one member is Governor Ueda cannot be ruled out. 2.3 Improvement in risk balance The risk balance for the economy improved from "skewed to the downside" in the previous report to "generally balanced." [That the risk balance for the economy improved from "skewed to the downside" in the previous report to "generally balanced."] indicates a shift in judgment that risks for both economic activity and inflation are now balanced. However, a closer look reveals that the number of members who responded that "upside risks are larger" for both the growth rate and inflation rate in fiscal 2025-26 exceeded the number of members who responded that "downside risks are larger" by one. [That the number of members who responded that "upside risks are larger" for both the growth rate and inflation rate in fiscal 2025-26 exceeded the number of members who responded that "downside risks are larger" by one] suggests that the BoJ's stance, strictly speaking, is leaning slightly towards being wary of upside risks. 3. Future monetary policy outlook: [The author maintains] [the author's] April rate hike forecast Based on the contents of [the Monetary Policy Meeting] and the hawkish revisions in the Outlook Report, [the author maintains] [the author's] forecast that the next rate hike will occur in April. Takata's proposal for a rate hike indicates that discussions toward normalization are already active within the BoJ, and [Takata's proposal for a rate hike] can be seen as paving the way for an early rate hike. At the April [Monetary Policy Meeting], the BoJ will be able to incorporate the results of the 2026 annual Page 4 Deutsche Securities Inc. spring labor-management wage negotiations (shunto) to some extent. If strong wage growth is confirmed, the certainty of a virtuous cycle from wages to prices will further increase. Furthermore, in April, the projection period of the Outlook Report will shift from the current fiscal 2025-27 to fiscal 2026-28. [The author anticipates] that [that the projection period of the Outlook Report will shift from the current fiscal 2025-27 to fiscal 2026-28] will indicate the stable achievement of the 2% price target at an earlier stage of the projection period, thereby providing a basis for justifying a rate hike path towards 1%, the lower bound of the nominal neutral interest rate. Regarding the specific pace of rate hikes, after implementing a 0.25% hike in April (policy interest rate at 1.0%), [the author expects] an additional hike in October, bringing the policy interest rate to 1.25%. Depending on the results of the shunto and the progression of the yen's depreciation, a faster pace of rate hikes could be possible. Subsequent rate hikes will likely depend on developments in the global economy, particularly the U.S. economy. If the Federal Reserve (Fed) were to re-enter a rate hike cycle earlier than anticipated, the BoJ would likely follow suit with further hikes, also from the perspective of addressing upward pressure on import prices due to a weaker yen.
