# Lift-off Sunday Tea with BNP Paribas

Sunday Tea with BNPP: Lift-off

## OUR KEY VIEWS

- The rate hike delivered by the Fed looks like the start of a wider effort to assert [the Fed's] credibility and recalibrate monetary policy towards a more restrictive stance. [The author expects] the FOMC to raise rates again at the December and January meetings.
- That said, [the author] still [the author still sees] the balance of risks tilted towards higher back-end yields and remain short 30y Treasuries.
- The BoJ failed to meet hawkish market expectations which [the author thinks] sets the stage for USDJPY to grind back above 160. The FOMC did as much as [the FOMC] reasonably could. Accompanying Wednesday’s rate hike was a pound - the-podium press conference from Fed Chair Kevin Warsh – and a show of confidence that the FOMC would achieve the 2% inflation target. Warsh aimed to present [Wednesday’s rate hike] as a decision [Fed Chair Kevin Warsh] believed in, pushed for and owns, thereby addressing lingering doubts about [Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's] credibility on inflation and ability to withstand political pressure. As part of a discussion around economic realities, Warsh notably flagged “geopolitics” as the driver of inflation, a comment that may not sit well in some corn ers of Washington, but one which helped Warsh achieve a wider goal – to portray himself as the leader of a unified, independent FOMC that has conviction to move whatever the political weather. [The author still expects] the FOMC to raise rates in December and January, unwinding the 2025 ‘insurance cuts’ and returning the real policy stance to a roughly neutral level. While the Summary of Economic Projections ’ call for two 2026 hikes is consistent with a hold in October, Warsh’s stridently hawkish remarks, both on inflation and on the current policy setting, provided little to support expectations for such a hold. [The author] may reconsider [the author's] forecast if signals from policymakers in the coming weeks appear to signal a follow-up hike. In general, [the author sees] upside risk to the length and size of the hiking cycle. The most striking feature of Warsh’s press conference was [Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's] description of [Wednesday’s rate hike] as removing a “dose of accommodation.” The word ‘accommodation ’ means ‘stimulus ’ at the Fed; [the description of the hike as removing a “dose of accommodation”] implies that the current monetary policy stance is meaningfully stimulative. With policy starting at a stimulative stance, alongside a strong cyclical impulse and persistent inflation, [the author thinks] significant rate increases – perhaps more than the three [the author expects] – may be necessary to stabilize the unemployment rate from below and prevent overheating next year. In rates [the author] took profit on [the author's] 1x1.5 6m30y payer spread and entered an outright short 30y Treasuries (Entry: 5.36%, target 5:60%, stop: 5.20%, size: 50k/01). [The 1x1.5 6m30y payer spread] was near [the 1x1.5 6m30y payer spread's] peak strike, and [the author does] not think buybacks and Fed hikes are sufficient to stem the momentum towards higher yields (see September FOMC: Removing a ‘dose of accommodation’, dated 16 September). In FX, [the author is] not immediately inclined to chase the USD higher given a lack of follow-through on recent positive data releases, and on historical precedent, which suggests that USD returns tend to flatten out once the Fed begins [the Fed's] tightening cycle. Unlike the Fed, the Bank of Japan failed to ‘out-hawk’ market expectations at Friday’s monetary policy meeting. [The Bank of Japan] raised interest rates by 25bp as widely expected, accelerating the pace of hikes from about every six months to once a quarter. Markets, however, appeared to view [Friday’s monetary policy meeting] as dovish due to dissenting votes from the two board members appointed by the Takaichi administration. [The author acknowledges] that the government’s bias for low interest rates remains fundamentally unchanged, but [the author does] not expect [the Takaichi administration] to exert strong pressure on the BoJ, at least for the time being, given the risk of reigniting yen weakness. Although Governor Kazuo Ueda’s press conference was not particularly hawkish overall, the BoJ’s policy stance has shifted to prioritize upside risks to underlying inflation; should significant yen depreciation occur, [the author does] not rule out a rate hike in October. [The author notes] that the reaction in FX was stronger than in rates, with USDJPY rising back towards 158 which likely reflects that positioning was long JPY going into the BoJ as investors sought to capitalise on a decisively hawkish shift from the BoJ. By [the author's] metrics, JPY longs were the largest position in G10 with a score of +27/50 (Figure 1), with a broad range of components showing bullish positioning. [The author continues] to forecast a quarterly pace of hikes until the policy rate reaches 1.75% (which [the author views] as nominal neutral) in March 2027, at which point the JPY may stop weakening and the BoJ may no longer be seen as behind the curve. [The author also thinks] onshore flow dynamics continue to lean bearish for the JPY (see Japan: Domestic flows not bullish for the JPY, dated 17 September). As such [the author sees] room for USDJPY to grind back above USDJPY back to 160 In energy markets, crude prices rose sharply in the last week after news of an attack on a large Saudi pipeline, before coming off on news that repairs may be forthcoming and on a dramatic impact on refining margins from the rally in physical crude and freight, particularly in Asia. [The author expects] Brent crude to remain in a USD100 –110/bbl range: further escalation could send [Brent crude] higher; faster progress on restoring pipeline volumes could push [Brent crude] lower. Diesel cracks are still extremely elevated, driven by genuine tightness, inelastic demand and a lack of progress in the Middle E ast or between Russia and Ukraine. Politicians in the US have also signalled a possible export ban, which [the author thinks] is not imminent, but could be in play later as a measure of last resort. Finally, although TTF took a pause at the end of last week, [the author believes] [TTF's] ascent will continue as German storage injections need to pick up. See Positioning for near-term upside as injections need to accelerate, dated 17 September. The Bank of England made clear on Thursday that the decision to keep Bank Rate on hold is becoming an increasingly close call for the MPC, opening the door to a reluctant rate hike later this year. In line with [that the decision to keep Bank Rate on hold is becoming an increasingly close call for the MPC, opening the door to a reluctant rate hike later this year], [the author still expects] the BoE to adopt a risk management approach and deliver a single ‘insurance ’ hike in November, consistent with the conditional guidance laid out in the minutes and the framework outlined by MPC hawks. That said, the focal point of the meeting was the QT decision, which delivered a complete overhaul of the programme. Long-dated gilts outperformed the most as the MPC surprised the market with the unveiling of a multi-year plan to unwind the APF, thus removing the uncertainty created by [the MPC's] annual decisions unveiled at each September meeting. Other bullish factors were: a pause in active sales through and beyond the next budget (to resume by April 2027); the removal of very long-dated gilts (>2049) from active sales, and; a decision to sell to the government rather than to the market, allowing better coordination with the Debt Management Office on the timing and strategic allocation of the regular bond issuance programme (see Bank of England: Laying the groundwork for a reluctant rate hike, dated 17 September). The BoE’s divergence from other major central banks by staying on hold in September led the GBP to weaken moderately after the meeting but [the author doubts] that weakness will continue. Assuming t he BoE proceeds with a rate hike in November, only marginally later than other central banks, [the single ‘insurance ’ hike in November] should mean the GBP can still benefit from [the GBP's] yield advantage, in [the author's] view. [The author projects] EURGBP at 0.85 by year - end (at the low end of [EURGBP's] post-Brexit range) due to [the GBP's yield advantage], as well as on [the author's] expectation for ongoing resilience in UK growth, and low fiscal-risk premium in [the author's] base case. While the Swiss National Bank is widely expected to stay on hold on 24 September, keeping the policy rate steady at 0.00%, [the author sees] scope for [the Swiss National Bank] to present a more cautious tone. The SNB is likely to acknowledge the resilience of the global economy despite the prolonged nature of the energy price shock, and in turn, could deliver some hawkish tweaks to the guidance alongside upward revisions to [the Swiss National Bank's] growth and inflation projections. Implicitly, [that the SNB is likely to acknowledge the resilience of the global economy despite the prolonged nature of the energy price shock, and in turn, could deliver some hawkish tweaks to the guidance alongside upward revisions to the Swiss National Bank's growth and inflation projections] would begin to signal that an interest rate hike is now more likely than a cut, though [the author does] not expect the SNB to act until late 2027. Market pricing has moved to discount 50bp of tightening through to June. In addition, the sustained depreciation of the CHF and fading deflation risks could encourage the SNB to relax guidance around FX intervention from the current, intensified, level, reverting to [the guidance around FX intervention's] steady-state form (see SNB preview: Stable stance, cautious outlook, dated 16 September).
