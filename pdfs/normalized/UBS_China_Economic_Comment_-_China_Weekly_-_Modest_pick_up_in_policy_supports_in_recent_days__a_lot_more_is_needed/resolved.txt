# China Economic Comment China Weekly: Modest pick up in policy supports in recent days, a lot more is needed

There are no signs that August conditions are better than they were in July. Bad weather conditions again contributed to the weakness, given the focus on personal safety amid uncertainties, but [bad weather conditions] is not the most important cause of growth weakness. There is a significant chance that the growth target (4.5-5.0%) will be missed if no meaningful loosening measures are taken. [The author believes] the government is committed to the target. There have been some signs of pick up in policy initiatives in recent days (see below for details). But [the author needs] a lot more both in terms of policy signals and actions. Deputy Minister of Finance Liao Min will host a press conference on fiscal policy tomorrow. [The press conference on fiscal policy] will be an informative meeting as fiscal policy needs to play a key role and Minister Liao is a technocrat who commands a lot of respect in the market. What the market wants to know is whether the focus is on implementation of existing policy plans or new measures. But it is clear this is not quite September 2024 yet when the press conferences were held by ministers instead of their deputies. To reiterate [the author believes] [the author needs] to see broad based loosening measures to generate a reversal of growth momentum. But not all policy measures will have equal importance. Household consumption will receive some policy supports, but the more meaningful delta will come from fixed asset investments, especially high tech and related infrastructure, and government consumption. Within FAI, [fixed asset investments] related to strategic competition will receive more tangible supports. Beijing and Shanghai property loosening are important as [Beijing and Shanghai] are politically important and [Beijing and Shanghai] set examples of the kind of measures that can be taken. One commonly held misconception in the market is officials have a tendency to be inactive ahead of the government reshuffle. [That officials have a tendency to be inactive ahead of the government reshuffle] is not true since if there are strong orders from the top to do more it would be unwise politically to go against the wind. The recent cautious tendency was the result of the policy stance since 2Q, which focused on avoiding doing too much at the expense of other goals such as debt control. Not that long ago in 1Q the momentum for doing more to ensure a good start to the Five Year Plan period was strong. Since the July Politburo meeting the tone has changed again to a pro-growth one, though in [the author's] view the tone is not strong enough. But [the author expects] [the tone since the July Politburo meeting] to be stepped up in the coming weeks. By late September, there will be questions: if the situation is becoming similar to that at the same time in 2024, shouldn’t there be more policy support? [The questions by late September] could lead to another step-up in policy support even though there are no important regular top-level meetings scheduled. [The author] would note however that policy making faces a lot of uncertainties and investors should recognize [the reality that policy making faces a lot of uncertainties] whether they believe policy will be stepped up further or not. Externally, China made a strong statement on the actions taken by the EU on JD. With the China-US relationship largely stable, China’s tension with EU is on the rise. [The author expects] China to take a firm stance on [the actions taken by the EU on JD], posing some downside risks on exports towards 4Q and beyond. Recent policy measures: Property: On August 7th, Beijing relaxed home purchase restrictions and raised the housing provident fund loan caps. On August 20th, Shanghai announced an additional round of easing following the measures introduced in February. Key changes included a lowering down payment ratio for 2nd home outside the Outer Ring, housing subsidies, broader HPF withdrawal requirements, and measures supporting housing inventory destocking. Nationally, China released the amendments to the rules managing housing provident fund on August 18th. [The amendments to the rules managing housing provident fund] expand the permitted use of HPF funds, streamline the withdrawal process, while assign State Council authority to set HPF rate adjustments. Fiscal policy: The NDRC called for faster implementation of the RMB800bn policy financing tool announced at the March NPC, compared with RMB500bn in 2025. Reportedly, the 2026 implementation guidelines have been circulated to local governments with fiscal interest subsidies for eligible projects. Consumption: Nine ministries together issued new guidelines aimed at boosting consumption in county-level markets. [The new guidelines aimed at boosting consumption in county-level markets] focus on upgrading commercial infrastructure, improving services, expanding supplies, and enhancing logistics networks. That said, [the new guidelines aimed at boosting consumption in county-level markets] did not announce a quantified fiscal package. Monetary policy: The PBOC released [the PBOC's] Q2 Monetary Policy Report, reiterating [the PBOC's] accommodative policy stance. However, [the Q2 Monetary Policy Report] contained no new easing measures and removed the previous reference to lowering banks’ funding costs, suggesting a reduced likelihood of an RRR cut in the near term. Consistent with [the signal that the Q2 Monetary Policy Report contained no new easing measures and removed the previous reference to lowering banks’ funding costs], the August LPR fixing was left unchanged.
