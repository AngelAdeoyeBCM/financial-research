# European Daily: ECB Recap—Higher Rates Ahead

10 September 2026 | 10:02PM BST The Governing Council hiked policy rates by 25bp at today’s meeting, as widely expected, and President Lagarde described the decision as a “no brainer.” The new staff projections showed stronger growth and a more lasting inflation overshoot, with the Council stating that inflation would remain “well above target for an extended period”. Lagarde gave no indication on the path ahead during the press conference, suggesting that the next move was still undecided. Given the economy’s recent resilience, the renewed spike in energy prices and today’s hawkish message, [the author now expects] the Governing Council to hike the deposit rate by a further 25bp in December to 2.75%, taking rates into mildly restrictive territory. While the ECB staff projections—especially if updated with the latest energy forwards, which are close to the adverse scenario—would likely require tightening beyond [a further 25bp in December to 2.75%], [the author's] forecasts for growth, inflation and commodity prices are somewhat softer than the staff’s baseline. That said, [the author sees] a low hurdle for hikes to 3% with stronger data and see the risks skewed in [hikes to 3%]. In particular, a hike is quite possible already in October with continued energy price pressures, an upside surprise in the September HICP data, or early signs of second-round effects in the ECB’s surveys, especially if stronger US inflation tilts the Fed towards hiking. Looking further ahead, [the author maintains] [the author's] view that the ECB will normalise the policy rate when inflation slows but push back the anticipated timing of cuts to 2027Q4 and 2028Q1 (from 2027Q2 and 2027Q3 previously). [The author] also nudge up [the author's] terminal rate forecast to 2.25% (from 2% before) given recent signs of a higher neutral rate. More Tightening Ahead Given the economy’s recent resilience, the renewed spike in energy prices and today’s hawkish message, [the author now expects] the Governing Council to hike the deposit rate by a further 25bp in December to 2.75%, taking rates into mildly restrictive territory. As discussed above, the ECB staff projections—especially if updated with the latest energy forwards, which are close to the adverse scenario—would likely require tightening beyond [a further 25bp in December to 2.75%] given the projected resilience in growth and persistence in inflation pressures. The early Q3 growth data, however, have been softer (with [the author's] tracking at 0.19%), and [the author forecasts] weaker pass-through pressures into underlying inflation (given the weaker run-rate of inflation so far) and [the author's] commodities team looks for some relaxation in energy prices compared to the forwards.
