A dovish premium is embedded in the Fed curve relative to other G10 central banks Source: Citi Research, Bloomberg Source: Citi Research, Bloomberg ECB Citi Econ Take: European Economics - ECB: Wait and Warn now, Hike Later Citi Economics has increased [Citi Economics'] modal expectation for the ECB, from staying on hold this year to seeing two 25bps hikes, in June and July respectively. [Citi Economics's increased modal expectation for the ECB] is a function of an increasing risk of a prolonged energy disruption combined with communication by members of the Governing Council which points to a very low level of patience should the supply shock last a bit too long. [The author expects] language at this meeting to focus on uncertainty, with the ECB remaining patient as [the ECB] awaits more inflation data before making any decisions. However, there could be a hawkish lean to the commentary related to inflation expectations in light of multiple inflation shocks (a similar comment was made by Waller; see Fed section). Last meeting, Lagarde noted: “inflation expectations have a lot to do with the memory... now the memory is rather fresh because people have seen inflation. So the reaction function that they will have in terms of investment, in terms of wage negotiations and in terms of consumption is going to be informed by a fresher memory of inflation” (ECB 03/19/26). [The author has] been flagging that global central bankers may remain more hawkish than expected—despite knowing that hiking into a commodity supply shock could be a policy error—due to concerns around de-anchoring inflation expectations as [the author moves] into yet another inflation shock (i.e., do consumers begin to worry that there is something structurally driving inflation higher, rather than [the inflation shock] being another idiosyncratic inflation shock?). In that context, and given the Strait of Hormuz remains closed, there is some marginal hawkish risk for the ECB. But again, [the author does] not think [the ECB] would go so far as to commit to action in June, which would limit how much markets can really adjust already hawkish June meeting pricing (which would limit EUR upside). In line with Econ and Rates views above, [the author sees] marginal dovish risk into the BoE meeting this week also given the weaker starting point for the UK pre-conflict (BoE was already being priced dovishly) and higher starting point for policy rates (e.g., more room to cut into neutral territory). On the margin, [the marginal dovish risk into the BoE meeting this week] could see a weaker GBP, but [the author also sees] limited market impact from guidance ahead of clarifying inflation data releases in the coming months (a BoE staying on hold in light of new higher inflation data would be a clearer, market-moving dovish signal, in [the author's] view). BoJ Citi Econ Take: BoJ Preview – Expecting hawkish hold, voting behavior again in focus The long-held base case of Citi Economics has been a rate hike once every six months, with the next hike coming in July. The two most recent public appearances by Governor Ueda offered no clear signal for an imminent rate hike, and market pricing for a hike has fallen sharply. That said, even if the policy rate is unchanged at this meeting, the hold is likely to be hawkish given concerns about further JPY depreciation and the risk of falling behind the curve. [The author] will be closely watching the voting behavior of board members to gauge the degree of hawkishness. At the March meeting, the number of dissenting votes did not increase, which [the author] interpreted as a signal that the BoJ intends to maintain [the BoJ's] wait-and-see stance. An increase in the number of dissenting votes increase at this meeting could be interpreted as the BoJ laying the groundwork for a rate hike in the near future. Notably, despite recent USD weakness during the deescalation phase of the US- Iran conflict, USDJPY has remained elevated and EURJPY has climbed to a new all-time high. If the BoJ leaves [the BoJ's] policy unchanged at this week’s meeting, USDJPY could well climb past 160. In [the case that the BoJ leaves the BoJ's policy unchanged at this week’s meeting], [the author thinks] the Japanese government would likely intervene to buy the JPY and try to push USDJPY back to around 155. BoC Citi Econ Take: Canada Economics Weekly – Core inflation subdued with soft demand While [the author] had forecast modest upside for USDCAD in the coming months, the pair has primarily traded to US equities (risk-on) and relative data momentum, which have pushed USDCAD lower along with optimism around PM Carney’s push to attract investment (with the latest development being the creation of a sovereign wealth fund; see Bloomberg, 4/27 /26). Such momentum could continue with tomorrow’s Spring Economic Update (e.g., to the extent higher oil prices improve Global FX Strategy the revenue outlook or a new stimulus is announced) and with Citi Economics expecting modest hawkish risks for the BoC compared to communications in March. Growth and employment data since then have improved, and the latest CFIB survey showed a jump in average price plans, typically a leading indicator for Canadian inflation. Still, Citi Economics maintains [Citi Economics'] base case for cuts over H2 (September [or] October), in contrast to current market pricing of roughly 30bps in hikes by year-end. One dovish risk is if the April MPR indicates the neutral rate could be lower (e.g., around updated estimates of potential growth), which would mean current policy is less accommodative than originally thought. [The author expects] any subsequent repricing could eventually weigh on CAD, as would [weigh on CAD] focus on more structural headwinds facing Canada’s economy that weigh on the outlook for growth. But these may take time to materialize, and BoC communication for now is unlikely to be a CAD headwind, in [the author's] view.
