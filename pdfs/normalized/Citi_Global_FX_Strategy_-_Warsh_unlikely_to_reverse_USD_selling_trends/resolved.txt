# Global FX Strategy Warsh unlikely to reverse USD selling trends

## CITI'S TAKE

[The author does] not think Chair Warsh's remarks at Jackson Hole will reverse USD selling trends. Risks to [the outcome that Warsh unlikely to reverse USD selling trends] are more around valuation and positioning, in [the author's] view, than fundamental reasons for a hawkish surprise. Several directions Chair Warsh could go at Jackson Hole. At the July FOMC presser, Warsh noted several options for how [Chair Warsh] might approach [Chair Warsh's] Jackson Hole speech. One is a more traditional 'setup' speech for upcoming FOMC meetings, while another is a speech framing the 'big questions' policymakers are facing. The reality might fall somewhere in the middle, i.e., remarks on medium-term themes the FOMC is currently grappling with. Increased discussion of productivity and AI in the Minutes would support such an approach, as would any early results from task force discussions relevant for the direction of policy [support such an approach]. But [the author] don't see a strong incentive for Warsh to be hawkish. There are three reasons Warsh might have stronger incentive to be hawkish, but which are not at play in [the author's] view. The first is if there was not enough hawkish premium in the curve, but with 10bps priced for September, there is likely enough premium to keep Warsh satisfied from an optionality or financial conditions perspective. The second is the view that a hawkish surprise is needed to contain the back-end of the yield curve. But [that a hawkish surprise is needed to contain the back-end of the yield curve] is not the view of [the author's] rates strategists, who note a rate hike (at the extreme) is unlikely to reverse the recent rise in term premium. The third is if data was pointing to some form of reacceleration that reflected inflationary pressures in the pipeline. But the data has broadly come in softer over recent months. Thus [the author] don't see fundamental reasons to surprise hawkish. [The author remains] bearish USD into Jackson Hole. In [the author's] latest FX forecast update, [the author] flipped from neutral to bearish USD (Foreign Exchange Forecasts - August 2026). [The author's] real rates model for EURUSD -- under assumptions the Fed will not hike, the ECB will hike once more, and oil prices will gradually normalize -- can see the pair move towards 1.18 after factoring in overshoot potential. Risks to the view stem less from a 'hawkish surprise' via Warsh, but rather some combination of positioning [or] valuation and the US [or] Iran conflict. On [positioning or valuation], EURUSD overvaluation is starting to look more stretched; with the leveraged segment short USD, it's possible [the author sees] position trimming around Jackson Hole. If there is any area that could come across more hawkish, in [the author's] view, it's presenting task force results on the balance sheet. But there seems very little incentive to do so, especially if there is indeed some coordination behind the scenes between Treasury Secretary Bessent and Warsh. More likely, in [the author's] view, is if [the author does] get any task force result previews, [task force result previews] will be around AI and productivity -- which should lean dovish [or] disinflationary.
