# Japan: BOJ’s April Branch Managers’ Meeting: Confirms High Wage Settings, While Citing Concerns over Middle East Conflict

Co., Ltd.

6 April 2026 | 5:31PM JST BOTTOM LINE: The Bank of Japan (BOJ) held [the Bank of Japan's] quarterly branch managers’ meeting on April 6, and subsequently released [the Bank of Japan's] report on the current state of the economy (based on a summary of views from each region discussed at the April 2026 meeting) and the more detailed Regional Economic Report (Sakura Report). The content of [the report on the current state of the economy] supported the BOJ’s view of continued solid economic development, higher wage hikes, and price pass-throughs from a micro information perspective. At the same time, [the report on the current state of the economy] introduced voices of concern regarding downward pressure on the economy from the Middle East conflict and rising crude oil prices. MAIN POINTS: Regarding regional economic assessments, all regions reported that [all regions'] respective economies had been “recovering moderately,” “picking up,” or “picking up moderately,” although “some weakness has been seen in part”. [The regional economic assessments] of economic conditions based on micro information aligns with the BOJ’s macro assessment from the March Monetary Policy Meeting statement (“Japan’s economy has been recovering moderately, although some weakness has been seen in part.”). At the same time, regarding the Middle East situation, [the report on the current state of the economy] noted that reduction in operating rates due to rising input costs and supply constraints on raw materials has begun to be observed. Going forward, [the report on the current state of the economy] noted voices of concern over negative impact on corporate profits and personal consumption, and the possibility of supply constraints spreading throughout the entire supply chain. [The report on the current state of the economy] summarized that [the Middle East situation] will be monitored carefully, considering the potential for downward pressure on regional economies. Regarding the major demand components, on personal consumption (including inbound demand), the BOJ noted that spending for special occasions remains firm, while also reporting consumers’ persistent thrifty stance, mainly at super markets. On capex, [the report on the current state of the economy] noted that positive investment stance is being maintained. Regarding this year’s wage hikes, the BOJ noted there were many companies planning to provide FY2026 wage hikes at a similar level to FY2025, from the perspective of securing and retaining personnel, and given the spread of high-level wage hikes comparable with FY2025 at large corporations. On the other hand, [the report on the current state of the economy] introduced voices noting the possibility of a more cautious wage-setting stance depending on the future development of the Economics Research Middle East situation. On corporate price setting, the BOJ reported that many companies continue to pass on rising input, labor, and logistics costs to output prices, implying that price hikes will likely broaden for the April price revision season. [The Bank of Japan] also reported voices from some companies that have announced or are considering future price hike due to the recent depreciation of the yen and the surge in crude oil prices. As mentioned above, the results of the April branch managers’ meeting, from a micro information perspective, supported the BOJ’s view of a continued solid economic development, higher wage hikes, and price pass-throughs, while at the same time, [the report on the current state of the economy] introduced voices of concern regarding downward pressure on the economy from the Middle East conflict and rising crude oil prices.
