Regarding [Fed Chair Warsh’s appearance at the ECB’s annual Central Bank forum in Sintra], as [the author] noted in the wake of the June FOMC meeting (see “US Economic Perspectives: Yield to Warsh: 50bps of hikes in 2026 “), [the author now expects] the Fed to act against sticky inflation, which [the author has] revised up to 3.2% and 2.5% for core PCE for end-2026 and 2027, respectively. [The author's] base case is that the Fed raises rates twice this year, in September and December, bringing the fed funds rate to 4.1%. [The author's] forecast then sees the Fed on hold in 2027 before returning policy to neutral (3.5 -3.75%) with 50bps of rate cuts in H1 2028 (penciled in for March and June). Since Warsh’s press conference a couple of weeks ago, the Fed has gotten another data point to inform [the Fed's] inflation outlook (see “US Economic Chartbook: US Inflation Outlook: (CPI-PCE) Wedge-ing the door open for hikes“). Though Warsh has played down the importance of any one data point, the recent trends in core PCE inflation – the Fed’s preferred inflation metric – remained well-above the Fed’s 2% target. Though the three-month annualized rate for core PCE slowed 30bps to 3.5%, the six-month rate increased by the same amount to 4.1% while the year-over-year rate ticked up 19bps to 3.41%. While the latest core PCE data would argue for little, if any, change to the hawkish message Warsh delivered at the June FOMC meeting (see “Fed Notes: June FOMC recap: Rock Chalk, Warsh-hawk “), the ongoing decline in energy prices since the MOU signed by the US and Iran is a tentative positive development on the inflation front. To be sure, whatever the message Warsh gives on the inflation outlook, [the author expects] [Fed Chair Warsh] to continue to avoid any hints on the potential near-term path for monetary policy. Indeed, the lack of any other appearances by Fed officials this week will leave financial markets to interpret the implications of this week’s labor market data— namely, the June employment report which will be released Thursday due to the July 4 th holiday.
