# US Investor Visit: Post Ruling Party Landslide in Lower House Election

Japan Economics | Japan

Visit to US investors after ruling party landslide: In the week of Feb 9, right after Japan’s Lower House election, Masayuki Inui from the Japan Economics team visited US investors. Numerous meetings were held with equity and fixed income investors, including senior participants such as fund CIOs, CEOs, and founders. Joint roundtable discussions were also hosted with the US economics and rates teams. Overall, the visit was very well attended and highly constructive. Following the Takaichi administration’s victory in securing more than two-thirds of seats in the Lower House, investor interest intensified around the implications of future policy management for the broader macroeconomic outlook. Narrative shift on the Takaichi administration’s fiscal policy stance: While uncertainty remains regarding the framework and timing of a potential consumption tax cut—including whether [a potential consumption tax cut] could be implemented as a two-year temporary measure—[the author] sensed a shift in investor narrative. Views have moved from expectations of excessive fiscal expansion toward an assessment that fiscal easing would likely be pursued selectively.[The perspective that fiscal easing would likely be pursued selectively] is consistent with [the author's] view that Japan’s fiscal fundamentals are solid (see [the author's] report). That said, offshore investors play a significant role in the super long JGB market, and market interest in fiscal sustainability would remain. Near-term attention is likely to be on updates at the national council on consumption tax cuts, with emphasis on gauging market perceptions of fiscal management and the feasibility of implementation. Rising focus on economic upside amid a strengthened political foundation: Against the backdrop of a strengthened political base for the Takaichi administration, discussions around economic upside became more prominent. Beyond counter-inflation measures, there was substantial interest in policy implications related to national security and strategic investment. Looking ahead, investors broadly expect security-related budgets to expand. Attention centered on “crisis-management and strategic investment” areas—including economic security; food, energy, resources, healthcare, and national resilience; and cybersecurity—as well as 17 strategic sectors such as AI, semiconductors, shipbuilding, and space. There was also strong interest in newly debated defense spending targets, with details on the use of non-core defense expenditure items, including infrastructure, drawing attention. Intensifying debate over early BoJ rate hikes: Questions around early rate hikes were frequent. Some investors indicated a view that a rate hike could occur in April, or potentially as early as March. Some suggested that FX rate checks conducted by Japan and the US in January, along with the March Japan–US summit, could act as external pressure pushing the BoJ toward a March move. That said, while the “Summary of Opinions” from the January meeting included several comments supportive of rate hikes, [the “Summary of Opinions” from the January meeting] also emphasized that “the pass-through of personnel expenses to prices has so far been moderate in areas other than food, dining-out, and accommodations, partly because of households’ lack of financial leeway”. The April nationwide CPI (May 22) and April Tokyo CPI (May 1), which will be key in assessing price revisions, will be released after the April policy meeting. [The author thinks] it is important to watch implicit signals from supplementary data such as the BoJ Tankan and branch managers’ meetings. Interest is high in upcoming speeches by policy board members, including Deputy Gov. Himino (Mar 2) and Ms. Koeda (Feb 24). Other key topics: Inflation outlook, wealth effects on consumption, repatriation trends, Japan–China relations, and [the author's] FX team’s USD [or] JPY outlook.
