# Fed Notes - R-star update lends some weight to Fed hawks

Economics Fed Notes Date 15 January 2026

A longstanding feature of [the author's] Fed forecast has been that the nominal neutral rate is higher than the assumptions reflected in the Fed’s SEP. Based on [the author's] dashboard, which consolidates estimates from thirteen different indicators, [the author has] built [the author's] Fed outl ook around a view that r-star has been in a relatively tight 1.5-1.75% range over the past two years, consistent with a nominal neutral rate of 3.5 -3.75% over the long run when inflation is at target. That estimate is meaningfully above the 3.0% median nominal neutral rate in the Fed’s SEP and all but 4 of the 19 individual projections. One compelling pushback to [the author's] view has been that the most widely cited estimates of r-star – estimates from NY Fed President Williams and co-authors that are known as LW and HLW -- have been substantially lower than the average [the author publishes]. 1 HLW, in particular, has been the lowest estimate [the author tracks], stuck below 1% in recent years. However, recent updates and revisions to LW and HLW move [the LW and HLW estimates] closer to [the author's] view. Updated for Q3 2025 (the last available), estimates of HLW and LW rose to 1.1% and 1.6%, respectively. In addition, recent estimates of HLW have been revised higher. [The “other determinants” component] are the factors tied to the balance of supply versus demand for savings that are unrelated to potential growth. Indeed, the average revisions between the initial and current vintage of LW and HLW estimates have been +61bp and +50bps over [the LW and HLW estimates'] respective available samples (since 2005 for LW and 2015 for HLW). [The author], therefore, would not be surprised to see further upward revisions to [the LW and HLW estimates] over the coming quarters if the economy evolves broadly in line with [the author's] forecasts – solid growth, labor market stabilization, and lower but still above-target inflation. For Fed officials, the level of neutral has become an increasingly important determinant of near-term policy decisions after the Fed completed another round of rate cuts in December. Dovish-leaning officials point to a 3% estimate of the nominal neutral fed funds rate as an argument for why further rate cuts should be expected over time. Conversely, hawkish-leaning officials have argued that policy is already close to neutral, providing minimal scope for further rate cuts, particularly with inflation still above target. [The recent updates and revisions to LW and HLW] provide some support for the latter view.
