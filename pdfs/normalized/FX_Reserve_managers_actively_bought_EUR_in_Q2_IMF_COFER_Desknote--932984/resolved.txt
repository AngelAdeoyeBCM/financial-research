# FX: Reserve managers actively bought EUR in Q2 (IMF COFER

## OUR KEY VIEWS

- IMF COFER data for Q2 shows an active rebalancing of non-USD reserve allocations.
- The largest beneficiary was the EUR, with active buying of USD94bn.
- Reserve managers actively sold JPY for the second consecutive quarter, bringing allocations down to 5%.
- There was little sign of active diversification away from the USD in Q2, but [the author thinks] that reserve managers are likely to resume making reductions to [reserve managers'] USD allocations in line with longer-term trends. IMF COFER (Currency Composition of Official Foreign Exchange Reserves) data shows that reserve manager USD allocations fell slightly, from 57.2% in Q1 to 56.7% in Q2. [That reserve manager USD allocations fell slightly, from 57.2% in Q1 to 56.7% in Q2] appears consistent with longer-term trends, but with little sign of active diversif ication away from the USD during the quarter. Nonetheless, anecdotal evidence (such as the OMFIF survey) suggests that reserve managers are likely to resume making reductions to [reserve managers'] USD allocations (see Global Outlook Q4 2026). More notable, in [the author's] view, is an active rebalancing of currency reserves, driven by active EUR buying and active JPY selling. The data shows active buying amounting to USD94bn of EUR, taking [EUR]’s share of reserve holdings from 20% to 20.6%. Reserve manager decisions tend to offset valuation effects, i.e. [reserve managers] would buy EUR into weakness and sell into strength to keep holdings steady. If [the historical trend that Reserve manager decisions tend to offset valuation effects] was all that lay behind the Q2 numbers, then the necessary valuation adjustments imply reserve managers would have bought USD21bn EUR. With buying at more than 4x that figure, [the author] conclude there was a meaningful active effort to boost EUR holdings. While the EUR is the largest beneficiary of [the active rebalancing of currency reserves], [the author notes] that the same also applies (to a smaller degree) for the GBP, AUD, CHF and CNY. The JPY stands out as a target of reserve managers ’ active sales. The JPY weakened in Q2, with valuations contributing to a USD16bn fall in reserve holdings. But rather than buying into weakness, reserve managers actively sold USD46bn of JPY, taking the JPY share of reserves from 5.4% to 5%. [Q2] was the second consecutive quarter where JPY selling occurred alongside a valuation-driven drop.
