# US Economic Notes - What you need to know for the week ahead

Economics US Economic Notes Date 23 August 2026

Commentary for Monday: This week is headlined by Chair Warsh’s keynote at the Kansas City Fed’s Jackson Hole Symposium on Friday morning at 10am ET. This year’s conference theme is “Financial Innovation: Implications for Payments and Policy”, but it is unlikely [Chair Warsh] focuses [Chair Warsh's] comments on [“Financial Innovation: Implications for Payments and Policy”]. Instead, Warsh noted at the July FOMC meeting that [Chair Warsh's] remarks could go in one of two directions: a “big-picture speech” or a “more traditional set up for all the action we’re going to have between September and December.” See our full preview here: Fed Notes: Jackson Hole preview: Staying on the summit or trekking a path back to target? As [the Kansas City Fed’s Jackson Hole Symposium] often seems to do, this year’s gathering occurs against a challenging policy backdrop for the US central bank. Inflation remains elevated for the sixth consecutive year, and officials have begun to actively debate whether tighter policy is required t o bring [inflation] back to target. [The “good family fight” that officials have begun to actively debate whether tighter policy is required t o bring inflation back to target], in Warsh’s words, resulted in three dissents at [the July FOMC meeting] in favor of a rate hike. In addition, Warsh’s comments during the July presser raised questions about the Committee’s willingness to raise rates in response to persistent inflationary pressures, triggering a rise in longer-term inflation expectations and bond yields. If Warsh sticks to big picture topics, [the author sees] the task forces and AI as most likely focal points for [Chair Warsh's] comments. On [the task forces], uncertainty remains about many of the specifics of the task forces: the scope within each topic, how the task forces interact with the FOMC and staff, when specifically [the task forces] will report [the task forces'] findings [or] recommendations, and how [the findings or recommendations] will ultimately be translated into framework changes for the Fed. On [AI], Warsh could repeat some of [Chair Warsh's] optimistic views around the medium-term economic implications of w hat [Chair Warsh] has called “as important a change in the economy and business and households that we’ve had in my adult lifetime.” If, instead, [Chair Warsh] decides to delve into more policy-relevant topics – less likely in [the author's] view -- [the author sees] a few possibilities. First is to clean up remaining uncertainties around the inflation objective (2% PCE inflation) and tools (policy rate versus balance sheet) that would be used to achieve [the inflation objective]. Second, is a broad description of how officials are viewing inflation dynamics, given that [inflation dynamics] is the primary driver of policy decisions in the near-term. Within [a broad description of how officials are viewing inflation dynamics] Warsh could reference the two scenarios detailed in the minutes to the June FOMC meeting, namely one in which inflation “soon” dissipates and another in which inflation remains elevated. Third, how the Committee views the implications of evolving financial conditions and recent volatility in long-term interest rates for monetary policy, particularly given [Chair Warsh's] focus on signals from bond markets for monetary policymakers. Related to [how the Committee views the implications of evolving financial conditions and recent volatility in long-term interest rates for monetary policy], the market will continue to focus on any news out of Treasury related to debt management policies after l ast week’s surprise announcement of increased buybacks (see a note from [the author's] colleague Steven Zeng: Fixed Income Blog: The element of surprise). Matthew Luzzetti, Ph.D. Prior to Warsh’s speech, [the author] will receive important information on inflation that could influence the outcome of the September FOMC meeting. Recall that the July FOMC meeting minutes revealed that “many” participants anticipated a need to tighten policy if inflation fails to quickly decline. Given [the backdrop that “many” participants anticipated a need to tighten policy if inflation fails to quickly decline], market participants will heavily scrutinize Wednesday’s core PCE print. Based on the recent CPI and PPI reports, [the author is] tracking 0.18% month-over-month for July core PCE, which would correspond to a 3.22% year-over-year growth rate for the Fed’s preferred inflation metric. If [the author's] forecast is correct – and there appears to be greater disagreement /uncertainty around [the translation that the author is tracking 0.18% month-over-month for July core PCE] than normal – [0.18% month-over-month for July core PCE] would lower the probability of a rate hike in September, which remains [the author's] baseline expectation. Beyond the PCE inflation readings, Wednesday and Thursday’s data releases should collectively sharpen expectations for Q3 growth by providing a more complete read on consumer demand, business investment, and trade flows at the start of the quarter. [The author forecasts] personal income and consumption to rise by 0.1% and 0.2% in July, respectively. Meanwhile, durable goods orders should post strong gains, growing by 0.7% in July (versus +0.5% previously), with both ex - transportation orders and core capital goods orde rs also rising by a similar magnitude, pointing to a continued resilient investment trend. Released alongside [Wednesday and Thursday’s data releases] on Wednesday will be the second estimate of Q2 GDP, which will update the baseline against which current-quarter activity is compared, with growth for Q2 potentially revised up slightly on better consumer spending. Thursday’s advanced goods trade bal ance should narrow modestly to -$100.4bn from -$101.5bn, offering an early indication of the contribution of net exports to current-quarter growth. Taken together, [Wednesday and Thursday’s data releases] should provide a clearer assessment of whether the economy carried [the economy's] strong demand and investment momentum into the current quarter commensurate with [the author's] Q3 real GDP growth forecast of 3.3% annualized. Warsh’s Jackson Hole speech provides a timely opportunity for the Fed’s new leader to clarify [Chair Warsh's] vision for the central bank, either through a “big picture” talk focused on the task forces or through a policy-relevant discourse that cleans up some missteps in recent communications and presents scenarios for the outlook. Given [Chair Warsh's] overall desire to provide limited information about the policy outlook, [the author thinks] [Chair Warsh's] comments will most likely skew to [a “big picture” talk focused on the task forces], though [the author] will be attentive to any additional signals on [a policy-relevant discourse that cleans up some missteps in recent communications and presents scenarios for the outlook].
