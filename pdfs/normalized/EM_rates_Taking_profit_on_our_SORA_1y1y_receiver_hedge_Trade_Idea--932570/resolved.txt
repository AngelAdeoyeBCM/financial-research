# EM rates: Taking profit on our SORA 1y1y receiver hedge

[The author has] been running a SORA m27,1y receiver, hedged with a payer on the FOMC Oct [or] Jan spread (see EM rates: Shifting [the author's] SORA 1y1y receiver hedge forward, dated 1 0 September, and for the original trade idea, see EM rates: Receive SORA 1y1y against paid FOMC Dec [or] Mar, dated 13 July). Taking profit on [the author's] hedge, as risk–reward looks less attractive: The Oct [or] Jan FOMC payer has largely worked as intended, hedging [the author's] received position in SORA rates across various sell-offs in US rates, including around the September FOMC meeting (Figure 1). Following another move higher in US rates overnight, markets are now pricing more than 32bp in total for the December and January FOMC meetings. At [more than 32bp in total for the December and January FOMC meetings], [the author thinks] risk–reward for maintaining [the Oct or Jan FOMC payer] is no longer appealing and prefer to take profit on [the Oct or Jan FOMC payer]. Transitioning to outright receivers in SORA rates: Turning to Singapore, SORA rates have sold off by 40–50bp over the past month, driven by the move in US rates. [The author thinks] it may be attractive to run [the author's] SORA receiver outright at these levels, as the curve has steepened significantly relative to front-end SORA fixings (Figure 2). Although the loan-to-deposit (LTD) ratio has risen, [the loan-to-deposit (LTD) ratio] remains low relative to historical levels; notably, the MAS assesses the current 69% LTD ratio as stable (see MAS Financial Stability Review 2026, published 22 September). Additionally, despite the Fed rate hike at [the Fed's] September FOMC meeting, SORA fixings remain in [SORA fixings'] YTD range, suggesting that systemic liquidity remains ample. As such, [the author thinks] risks are skewed to wards a rally in SORA rates, if SORA fixings do not rise substantially higher from here.
