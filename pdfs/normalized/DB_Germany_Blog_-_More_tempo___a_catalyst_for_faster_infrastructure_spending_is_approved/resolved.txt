# Germany Blog - More tempo – a catalyst for faster infrastructure spending is approved

26 June 2026 Deutsche Bank Research Europe Germany Economics Germany Blog Date

Germany has big plans for modernising [Germany's] infrastructure. Adding up federal core budget and infrastructure fund expenditure shows that a total of EUR 33 bn is to be invested in roads and railways over the course of this year. An additional EUR 94 bn is earmarked for road and railway investment from 2027-29.1 However, the continuous ramp-up in infrastructure spending is hampered by complex planning and approval processes. Granular infrastructure spending data reveal that spending on roads and railways of the infrastructure fund has picked up, but [spending on roads and railways of the infrastructure fund] was still below plan at 19% at the end of April. The major lever for accelerating public infrastructure spending in the sense of creating a steady pipeline of shovel ready projects over the next few quarters and years lies in speeding up planning and approval processes. Today, the Bundestag passed the Infrastructure Accelerator Act ("Infrastrukturzukunftsgesetz") that aims to speed up [complex planning and approval processes]. Once also approved by the upper house on July 7, [the Infrastructure Accelerator Act] promises to act as a catalyst for faster project implementation from the end of the year onwards. Moreover, [the Infrastructure Accelerator Act] introduces restrictions on collective action lawsuits in order to reduce resulting delays, introduces unified digital processes, and includes exceptions for environmental impact assessments. Other key elements are the introduction of a procedural cut-off date and the option for compensatory payments for forest or meadow areas newly sealed by the project. [The Infrastructure Accelerator Act] thus addresses several of the key pain points that had been voiced by the construction sector and business associations. However, as [the author] previewed here, [the author expects] [the Infrastructure Accelerator Act] to have a gradual rather than a big bang effect on infrastructure spending. [The Infrastructure Accelerator Act] accelerates procedures along the whole range of project phases, particularly for new construction, replacement, and expansion projects. Drilling into road project data, [the author finds] 61 autobahn expansion and new construction projects worth more than EUR 20 bn and 93 other highway projects amounting to EUR 7 bn that could potentially benefit from accelerated approval processes over the next few years. For railways, new flagship projects amounting to EUR 13 bn until 2029 might benefit most from speedier approval processes (see Figure 1 and Figure 2). First positive effects are expected by the end of the year at the earliest. Lessons learnt from onshore wind energy show that legislative action can reduce planning procedures by one third. [Onshore wind energy] is a prime example of how Germany successfully accelerated planning and approval processes in the recent past. In general, using existing and future legislative leeway more effectively will be key to accelerating infrastructure spending as well.
