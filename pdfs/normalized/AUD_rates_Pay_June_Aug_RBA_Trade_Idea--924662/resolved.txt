# AUD rates: Pay June [or] Aug [Reserve Bank of Australia]

Joey Li, FX and Rates Strategist, APAC | MAR | Chandresh Jain, EM Asia FX and Rates Strategist | BNP Paribas Singapore Branch. 1: Hours worked increased in April [The author is] adding a paid position in the June [or] Aug [Reserve Bank of Australia] spread, following today’s rally in AUD rates, as [the author believes] inflation concerns will keep the Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA) in [the Reserve Bank of Australia's] hiking cycle. Rally in AUD rates driven by labor force data: AUD rates rallied significantly today, driven by the release of April’s labor market data, which showed a slight decline in employment numbers and a rise in the unemployment rate to 4.5%. [April’s labor market data], combined with lower oil prices overnight and softer inflation expectations, led to an 8- 10bp move lower in front-end AUD swaps. Besides the inherent volatility in the monthly series, [the author is] not convinced that the recent labor market data represents a significant loosening in the labor market, and prefer to fade [the recent rally in AUD rates] for several reasons: Firstly, the underemployment rate tightened from 5.9% to 5.8%, while hours worked per employed person increased (Figure 1), suggesting a more nuanced picture rather than a broad-based loosening of the labor market. Secondly, the rise in the unemployment rate is also due to seasonal adjustments, as April has historically been a strong month for the labor market. The trend measures of employment and unemployment rate remained steady from March to April, indicating no significant change in the labor market (Figure 2).
