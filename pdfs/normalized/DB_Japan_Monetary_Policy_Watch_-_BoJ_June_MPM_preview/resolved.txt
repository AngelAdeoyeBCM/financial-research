# Japan Monetary Policy Watch - BoJ June [Monetary Policy Meeting] preview

8 June 2026 [The author expects] a 0.25% rate hike at the June meeting The Bank of Japan (BoJ) will hold [the Bank of Japan's] Monetary Policy Meeting (MPM) on June 15-16. [The author expects] the BoJ to decide on an additional 0.25% rate hike, raising [the Bank of Japan's] target for the uncollateralized overnight call rate to around 1.00%.

- The primary reason for [the 0.25% rate hike at the June meeting] is the strong sense of urgency Governor Ueda expressed in [Governor Ueda's] June 3 speech about the risk of "getting behind the curve." The Governor explicitly stated [Governor Ueda's] desire to avoid a situation where a delayed response would necessitate sharp rate hikes later. [The author believes] [Governor Ueda's sense of urgency about the risk of "getting behind the curve"] shortens the timeframe [the author] had previously assumed the BoJ would take to "carefully scrutinize data," thereby increasing the probability of a preemptive rate hike. Furthermore, the domestic corporate goods price index and inflation expectations, which the Governor is closely watching, are already indicating upside risks. [The objective facts that the domestic corporate goods price index and inflation expectations, which the Governor is closely watching, are already indicating upside risks] support the Governor's sense of urgency and could serve as a rationale for a rate hike decision at the June meeting.
- [The 0.25% rate hike at the June meeting] will be a significant step, bringing the policy rate closer to the lower bound of the neutral rate. A critical focus will be whether [the 0.25% rate hike at the June meeting] is accompanied by a revision to the forward guidance, as [the revision to the forward guidance] will be pivotal in determining the flexibility and direction of future policy management. 1. The vote count If a rate hike is decided, the vote count will be a key focus. It is important whether the decision is unanimous or if there are dissenters. In particular, whether board member Asada, appointed by Prime Minister Takaichi, will vote in favor of [the 0.25% rate hike at the June meeting] will be a crucial piece of information for gauging the future course of monetary policy.
2. Possibility of a 50bp rate hike proposal Another point of interest is whether any board member will propose a 50bp hike. The minutes of the March [Monetary Policy Meeting] revealed that one member wished to consider the magnitude of rate hikes when discussing them. Amid growing concerns about upside inflation risks, the possibility of opinions favoring a larger hike cannot be dismissed.
3. Publication of a "mini" outlook report [The June meeting] is an interim meeting without the release of the full "Outlook for Economic Activity and Prices" (Outlook Report). However, if the BoJ decides to raise rates citing heightened upside risks to inflation, [the Bank of Japan] may publish a concise analysis (a so-called "mini" outlook report), similar to the December 2025 meeting. In such a case, it would also be important to see if there is any difference between the members who approve [the so-called "mini" outlook report] and those who vote for [the 0.25% rate hike at the June meeting] itself, as [the difference between the members who approve the mini-report and those who vote for the rate hike itself] would offer insights into the range of views within the Policy Board. The focus is on the revision of forward guidance [The author believes] [the 0.25% rate hike at the June meeting] could be a significant turning point, meaning more than just raising the policy rate to 1.0%. The key question is whether the forward guidance will be revised simultaneously, and specifically, whether the BoJ will alter [the Bank of Japan's] "pre-committed" stance on future hikes.
1. The limits of "adjusting the degree of accommodation" The BoJ has so far maintained [the Bank of Japan's] stance of "continuing to raise the policy rate and adjust the degree of monetary accommodation." However, with [the 0.25% rate hike at the June meeting], the phase of "adjusting the degree of accommodation" will effectively reach [the phase of "adjusting the degree of accommodation"'s] final stage, weakening the rationale for maintaining [the BoJ's stance of "continuing to raise the policy rate and adjust the degree of monetary accommodation"]. A policy rate of 1.0% would be nearly aligned with the 1.1% lower-end estimate of the nominal neutral rate calculated by the BoJ. While [the policy rate of 1.0%] is strictly 0.1 percentage point lower, considering the uncertainty of the estimate and that underlying inflation is slightly below 2%, it is reasonable to assess that the rate has almost reached the lower bound of the neutral rate. The crucial part is what comes next. If the BoJ maintains [the Bank of Japan's] current forward guidance, it would imply that the next rate hike is also an "adjustment of the degree of accommodation." However, the subsequent hike would bring the policy rate to 1.25%, exceeding the 1.1% lower bound of the neutral rate. At that point, monetary policy could hardly be described as "accommodative," and the phrase "adjusting the degree of accommodation" might begin to seem inconsistent with reality. Here, the BoJ faces a crucial communication crossroads. If [the Bank of Japan] maintains the guidance of "continuing to adjust the degree of accommodation," [the Bank of Japan] will face the new challenge of justifying a future hike to 1.25%. On the other hand, revising the guidance now would increase future policy flexibility but also risks being perceived by the market as an unintendedly dovish signal. How the BoJ navigates [the BoJ's crucial communication crossroads] will be a major focus of [the June meeting].
2. Expecting a shift to a data-dependent stance [The author anticipates] that the BoJ, considering future communication costs, will choose to secure policy flexibility in advance and will revise [the Bank of Japan's] forward guidance, which currently presumes continued rate hikes. Specifically, [the author] envision the following change: [April Outlook Report] "As for the conduct of monetary policy, given that underlying CPI inflation has been approaching 2 percent and real interest rates are at significantly low levels, the Bank will continue to raise the policy interest rate and adjust the degree of monetary accommodation, in response to developments in economic activity and prices as well as financial conditions. In this regard, it will consider the timing and pace of adjustment, while closely monitoring the impact of the future course of the situation in the Middle East on Japan's economic activity and prices and examining the likelihood of realizing the baseline scenario of the outlook for economic activity and prices and the risks to the outlook. With the price stability target of 2 percent, the Bank will conduct monetary policy as appropriate from the perspective of sustainable and stable achievement of the target." [Envisioned new guidance at the June meeting] "Regarding the conduct of monetary policy, the Bank will appropriately adjust the policy interest rate in accordance with economic activity and price and financial conditions. In doing so, the Bank will examine the timing and pace of adjustments while watching developments in the Middle East situation and their impact on Japan's economic activity and prices, and checking the likelihood of achieving the Bank's baseline scenario and the risks. The Bank of Japan will appropriately conduct monetary policy from the perspective of the sustainable and stable achievement of the 2 percent "price stability target." This shift—deleting the "pre-committed" wording and moving to a data-dependent stance where decisions are made on a case-by-case basis—is perfectly consistent with Governor Ueda's intention, expressed in his June 3 speech, to carefully scrutinize data such as the "second-round effects" on inflation. It is important to note that the market impact of this forward guidance revision is ambiguous. The removal of the pre-committed wording on rate hikes could be interpreted by the market as a dovish signal that the hiking cycle is nearing its end, and the risk of a temporary yen depreciation and lower interest rates cannot be denied. However, we place more importance on the fundamental implications of this change rather than its superficial interpretation. We consider this change to be hawkish for two main reasons. First, the phrase "adjusting the degree of monetary accommodation" implicitly suggested that policy would remain in "accommodative" territory. Removing this wording is a clear signal that this implicit constraint is gone, indicating that future policy may not necessarily be accommodative. Second, it enhances the BoJ's "policy flexibility," enabling it to respond swiftly and flexibly—at times even exceeding market expectations (e.g., by accelerating the pace of rate hikes)—depending on future data. These two fundamental shifts—namely, the "de-commitment from an accommodative stance" and "enhanced policy flexibility"—are the hawkish aspects, and [the "de-commitment from an accommodative stance" and "enhanced policy flexibility"] will be a critical focal point for gauging the flexibility and direction of future policy management. Key issue: Interpretation of the "price stability target" and the asymmetry of the policy reaction function The Governor currently states that underlying inflation is "approaching" 2%. The recent rise in crude oil prices could push [underlying inflation] up, leading to [underlying inflation] reaching 2% while also posing an upside risk. In [the context that the recent rise in crude oil prices could push underlying inflation up, leading to underlying inflation reaching 2% while also posing an upside risk], answers to the following long-standing questions [the author has] are awaited:
1. If underlying inflation reaches 2%, can it be considered consistent with achieving the "price stability target"?
2. If underlying inflation exceeds 2%, does it become inconsistent with the target?
3. Alternatively, is the target considered consistent if underlying inflation averages 2% with some fluctuations, meaning temporary overshoots are permissible? While it may have been premature to discuss [the interpretation of the "price stability target"] before, now is the time for a full-fledged debate, as the BoJ anticipates reaching a state consistent with the target from the second half of fiscal 2026 onwards. [The interpretation of the "price stability target"] is important for understanding the asymmetry of the policy reaction function. In the past, during the process of exiting deflation, a deliberate "behind-the-curve" strategy while underlying inflation was below 2% had a certain rationale, as [the deliberate "behind-the-curve" strategy] helped push inflation toward 2%. However, now that Governor Ueda has clearly signaled [Governor Ueda's] concern about the risk of "getting behind the curve," [the author] should consider [the deliberate "behind-the-curve" strategy] as having already shifted. [The author believes] that once underlying inflation reaches 2%, or even when [underlying inflation's] path toward 2% becomes foreseeable, a similar strategy would risk an inflation overshoot and threaten the achievement of the "price stability target." In other words, [the author] understand that the BoJ's policy reaction function is changing to become more forward-looking as inflation approaches 2%, and the need to respond more swiftly to upside risks is increasing. A clear explanation from the Governor on [the point that the BoJ's policy reaction function is changing to become more forward-looking as inflation approaches 2%, and the need to respond more swiftly to upside risks is increasing] is essential for fostering the market's understanding of the BoJ's policy reaction function and for stabilizing expectations regarding future monetary policy. Enhancing the transparency of dialogue with financial markets is, by extension, a textbook example of central bank communication that contributes to economic and price stability. Future policy path and JGB purchases
1. Rate hike path: Maintaining a quarterly pace Regarding the subsequent rate hike path, [the author expects] further 0.25% hikes in October 2026, and January and April 2027, bringing the policy rate to 1.75% in April 2027. 5 [The quarterly pace of the rate hike path] is supported not only by the reasons [the author] pointed out in [the author's] previous report (ensuring tightening effects, controlling inflation expectations, coordinating with overseas central banks, and changes in the Policy Board's composition) but also from the perspective of achieving the price stability target. As discussed above, from October onwards, when the situation could become consistent with the "price stability target," the need to raise rates will increase in response to upside risks to underlying inflation.
2. JGB purchase plan: Expecting purchases to remain at JPY 2.1 trillion per month Regarding the JGB purchase plan, which will be discussed concurrently, [the author expects] no changes to the plan already decided for the period up to Q1 2027. Altering [the plan already decided for the period up to Q1 2027] would reduce the predictability of JGB purchases and could introduce unnecessary volatility into the JGB market. The focus will be on the plan from Q2 2027 onwards, for which [the author expects] no further reduction in purchases; that is, the monthly purchase amount will be maintained at JPY 2.1 trillion. [That the monthly purchase amount will be maintained at JPY 2.1 trillion] is inferred from the minutes of the Bond Market Group Meeting and Governor Ueda's June 3 speech. However, [the author] recognize that at the Bond Market Group Meeting, requests from market participants varied widely, from accelerating [the reduction of JGB purchases] to continuing [the reduction of JGB purchases] or halting [the reduction of JGB purchases], with the latter being a minority view. In such circumstances, if the BoJ decides to halt [the reduction of JGB purchases], [the decision to halt the reduction] will be accompanied by a responsibility to provide a very thorough explanation. A decision to halt [the reduction of JGB purchases] can find [the decision to halt the reduction's] own economic rationale. [That a decision to halt the reduction of JGB purchases can find the decision to halt the reduction's own economic rationale] is because, in a rate hike phase, curbing excessive rises in long-term interest rates and heightened volatility can be considered a legitimate policy objective to ensure financial market stability. However, the timing is the problem. Amid media reports suggesting the government would condone a rate hike on the condition that the BoJ halts the reduction of [the Bank of Japan's] JGB purchases (e.g., Nikkei on May 28, TV Tokyo's "Morning Satellite" on June 4), a simultaneous decision to raise rates and halt [the reduction of JGB purchases] could, regardless of [the simultaneous decision to raise rates and halt the reduction's] true intent, be perceived by the market and the public as a "political deal with the government." [The simultaneous decision to raise rates and halt the reduction] carries a serious risk of creating doubts about the BoJ's independence, which is the cornerstone of monetary policy credibility. Thus, while halting the reduction of JGB purchases has the legitimate objective of "market stabilization," a simultaneous decision with a rate hike presents a dilemma with the serious side effect of "doubts about independence." [The author considers] [the dilemma with the serious side effect of "doubts about independence"] a non-negligible hurdle for a June rate hike and would urge the BoJ to approach [the dilemma with the serious side effect of "doubts about independence"] with extremely careful communication.
