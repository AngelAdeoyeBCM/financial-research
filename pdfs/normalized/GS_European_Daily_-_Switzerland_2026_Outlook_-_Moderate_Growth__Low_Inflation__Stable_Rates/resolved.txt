# European Daily: Switzerland—2026 Outlook: Moderate Growth, Low Inflation, Stable Rates

25 January 2026 | 6:32PM GMT Growth in Switzerland was characterised by substantial trade distortions last year. The trade deal reached with the US in November should support Swiss activity going forward and [the author expects] growth close to potential throughout 2026 and 2027. On an annual basis [the author sees] growth at 1.2% this year, slightly above the SNB’s forecast, and 1.6% next year. Inflation has stabilised slightly above 0%, and [the author expects] [inflation] to remain subdued but positive throughout this year. Towards the end of this year and into 2027 [the author expects] headline inflation to move up towards 0.6%, in line with the SNB’s forecast. This inflation outlook is supported by [the author's] FX strategists’ expectation of a slight depreciation of the Swiss Franc against the Euro. That said, [the author sees] risks to the inflation outlook tilted towards the downside if the Franc remains stronger than [the author expects] and if labour market weakness weighs more strongly on domestic inflation. Against this backdrop of moderate growth, [the author expects] the SNB to remain on hold at 0% for the foreseeable future. While [the author views] risks to [the path of the SNB to remain on hold at 0% for the foreseeable future] as tilted to the downside, the SNB has repeatedly communicated that the hurdle for negative rates is high. [The author thus thinks] that a persistent undershoot of inflation below 0% and a meaningfully lower medium-term inflation forecast would be required for the SNB to introduce negative rates. The growth picture in particular contrasts with last year, which was characterised by substantial trade distortions. 2025 saw strong growth due to the frontloading of exports ahead of tariffs in Q1, followed by below potential growth in Q2 and a strong contraction in Q3 after the US imposed tariffs of 39% on Swiss exports to the US. That said, activity indicators stabilised in autumn (Exhibit 1, left) and the trade deal reached with the US in November—which caps tariffs at 15%, similar to the EU-US deal—should be a tailwind for growth going forward. In 2025Q4 [the author expects] growth to have picked up to 0.3%qoq, only slightly below potential. This year [the author expects] growth around potential, which [the author estimates] at 1.6% (or 0.4%qoq), as the tariff drag fades and monetary policy remains supportive (Exhibit 2, left). With growth returning towards potential, [the author expects] the unemployment rate to stabilise slightly above [the unemployment rate's] long-run average and the output gap to settle at a slightly negative level, after being positive between 2021 and 2025 (Exhibit 1, right). On an annual basis [the author expects] growth of 1.2% this year—as low growth from 2025H2 pulls down the annual average—slightly above the SNB’s forecast (Exhibit 2, right). For next year [the author upgrades] [the author's] forecast slightly as [the author's] US colleagues no longer expect pharma tariffs to be implemented after the US midterm elections. [The author expects] growth close to potential, resulting in average growth of 1.6% next year vs. 1.4% previously. Core inflation should run at around 0.5% in 2026. Towards the end of this year and into 2027 [the author expects] headline inflation to move up towards 0.6%, in line with the SNB’s forecast. A stabilization of inflation at low but positive levels is consistent with inflation and wage expectations (Exhibit 3, right). Short-term inflation expectations have not moved down further since 2025Q2 and firms’ wage growth expectations, at 1.3%, are consistent with inflation around 0.5% and labour productivity growth slightly below 1%. That said, [the author's] inflation forecasting framework points to risks to both headline and core inflation being tilted to the downside, particularly if the Swiss Franc remains stronger than [the author expects] and if labour market weakness weighs more strongly on domestic inflation. Against a backdrop of moderate growth—broadly in line with potential—some relief from a slight depreciation of the Franc against the Euro and low but slightly increasing inflation within the SNB’s target range, [the author expects] the SNB to remain on hold at 0% for the foreseeable future (Exhibit 5, left). In line with [the author's] risk assessment for inflation, [the author sees] risks for the SNB policy path tilted to the downside. That said, the SNB has repeatedly communicated that the hurdle to cut the policy rate below 0% is high and SNB chairman Martin Schlegel noted recently that a temporary undershoot of inflation below 0%—the lower limit of the SNB’s target range—would be tolerated. A move towards negative rates would thus require a persistent undershoot of inflation below 0% and the SNB meaningfully lowering [the SNB's] medium-term inflation forecast in [the author's] view. In [a persistent undershoot of inflation below 0%] [the author] would expect the SNB to cut by more than 25bp to bring inflation back into the price stability range.
