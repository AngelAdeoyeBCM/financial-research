# EM FX and rates: Portfolio adjustments due to Middle East events

As the outbreak of military action in the Middle East over the weekend has triggered volatility and uncertainty for markets, [the author is] adjusting [the author's] portfolio of trades to better reflect the risks ahead. In particular, the heavier-than-expected attacks on Iran suggest the possibility of oil and gas prices staying elevated for longer than anticipated, while Tehran could deliver a more disruptive-than-anticipated response directed at energy commodities. [A more disruptive-than-anticipated response directed at energy commodities] could entail some broader de-risking across markets. [The author is] making the following adjustments to [the author's] portfolio:

1. Taking a partial profit on [the author's] short EURBRL position. [The author is] locking in a profit on half of [the short EURBRL position] ($5m), booking a gain of 5.38% (USD269k). The remaining half stays open with a stop-loss revised from 6.321 to 6.145 and a profit-taking level adjusted from 6.041 to 5.840. [The author] entered [the short EURBRL position] on 13 January in anticipation of a supportive global macro environment for high-yielding EM currencies (see Brazil: Adding to [the author's] short EURBRL position).
2. Closing [the author's] Jan29 DI receiver trade. [The author] exit and book a loss of -1.1bp (USD11k). [The author] entered [the Jan29 DI receiver trade] on 28 January on the back of two coinciding factors:
1. a backdrop of USD weakness which will probably continue to filter into downward pressure for local rates and
2. the BCB likely softening [the BCB's] tone today, signaling the start of the easing cycle could be approaching (see: EM rates: Enter a Jan 29 DI receiver).
3. Closing [the author's] EURPLN put seagull trade. [The author buys] the 17 April 4.30 call in EUR20mn and sell the 17 April 4.22-4.15 put spread in EUR20mn x 30mn for net 2.8bp (4.24 spot ref; zero loss). [The author] entered [the EURPLN put seagull trade] as a put seagull on 19 January (see EURPLN: EU fund flows support PLN; add 3m seagull), owning carry and selling elevated vol surface. However, [the author is] following the path of prudence and reducing tail risk exposure here due to Poland’s potential exposure to energy price spikes.
4. Closing [the author's] short CADMXN trade for a small gain. [The author] exit for a profit of 0.76% (USD38k). [The short CADMXN trade] reflected the view that the CAD would underperform the MXN during trade negotiations ahead of the summer’s USMCA review. [The author thinks] [that the CAD would underperform the MXN during trade negotiations ahead of the summer’s USMCA review] will remain the case and will look to re-enter short positions in the future, but given risks of carry positions being unwound, [the author] opt to move to the sidelines for now (see FX: Short CADMXN, dated 2 February).
