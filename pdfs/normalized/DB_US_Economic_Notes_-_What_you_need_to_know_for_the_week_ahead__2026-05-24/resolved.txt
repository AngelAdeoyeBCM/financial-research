Commentary for Tuesday: After Monday’s Memorial Day holiday, market participants will come back to a steady flow of data and Fedspeak, both of which will be evaluated in the context of Governor Waller’s hawkish speech last week. There [Governor Waller] made known that the recent labor market and inflation data had caused [Governor Waller] to reevaluate the balance of risks with inflation becoming the “driving force” behind monetary policy in the near term. In particular, [Governor Waller] noted that [Governor Waller] would support changing language in the statement to remove the easing bias and make it clear that “a rate cut is no more likely in the future than a rate increase”. Indeed, after [Governor Waller's hawkish speech], the market fully priced in the possibility of a rate hike by year-end. [The possibility of a rate hike by year-end] seems to be shared by consumers as well (see “Consumers think the Fed might hike rates”), which could weigh on Tuesday’s consumer confidence (91.9 forecast vs. 92.8 previous). As to the rest of the week’s data, Thursday’s personal income and spending data will be the highlight. Released alongside [Thursday's personal income and spending data] will be revisions to Q1 GDP (+2.0% forecast vs. +0.5% previous) which will update the baseline for which to compare readings on current quarter activity. [The author's] expectation is that both income and consumption rose +0.4% in April (versus prior readings of +0.6% and +0.9% respectively), pointing to a more moderate pace of real activity after a strong prior month. Also in [Thursday's personal income and spending data] will be the latest reading on core PCE, which, based on the CPI and PPI data, [the author is] tracking the April print to be +0.28% (vs. +0.3%). [The latest reading on core PCE] would have the year-over-year rate rise a tenth to 3.3%, [the year-over-year rate's] highest reading since late 2023, and consistent with what Waller mentioned in [Governor Waller's] speech. Based on recent signals from relatively upbeat business sentiment surveys, [the author expects] headline orders to rebound sharply, though ex transportation (+0.5% vs. +0.9%) and core (+0.5% vs. +3.4%) orders should suggest a more tempered but still solid underlying signal. [Thursday's new home sales] will be scrutinized for any early signs that the recent rise in mortgage rates is denting activity, while [Friday's advance goods trade balance] will give [the author] a better sense of the extent to which trade flows have been disrupted. Thus far, economic activity has largely held up in response to the energy shock, helping to shore up any downside risks to the labor market. Collectively, the balance of the week’s data will help to clarify whether the recent firmness in activity is sustainable or beginning to soften at the margin. Thursday’s weekly jobless claims (215k vs. 209k) thus remain a perennial barometer for [the author] as [Thursday's weekly jobless claims] can provide early signs of labor market slowing.
