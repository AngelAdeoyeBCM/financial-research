First batch of China onshore active ETFs to benefit the dividend theme: Following China Securities Regulatory Commission chair’s proposal of new reforms – including the launch of active ETFs at the Lujiazui Forum (see Key takeaways from China’s Lujiazui Forum, dated 18 June) – the first batch of 18 active ETFs was already filed with the regulator this summer, with the market expecting [the first batch of 18 active ETFs'] launch in Q4. [The author argues] that the rollout of active ETFs marks a major milestone for the ongoing capital market reforms, as [the rollout of active ETFs] addresses a critical product gap in China onshore equity ETF market, providing domestic investors with a more flexible and efficient way to pursue alpha at a lower cost. Specifically, two-thirds of [the first batch of 18 active ETFs] follow value, balanced [or] conservative, or dividend strategy, while those focusing on growth [or] quality and other strategies account for the remaining one-third. [That two-thirds of the first batch of 18 active ETFs follow value, balanced or conservative, or dividend strategy] suggests that the first batch of active ETFs will particularly drive incremental funds into stocks with attractive valuations and high dividend yields. Low interest rates likely to prompt further domestic buying of dividend stocks: The 12-month forward dividend yield of the CSI 300 remains elevated at 3.0%, 132–178bp higher than the 10-year and 1-year CGB yields, indicating the relative appeal of A-shares domestically. In H1, the proportion of equity and securities funds holdings within the total AUM of domestic insurance funds rose 84bp to 15.7%. Amid the low-interest-rate environment in the onshore market, [the author anticipates] domestic investors, including insurance funds, will continue to allocate to dividend stocks and related products. Meanwhile, for long-term institutional investors, dividend-oriented active ETFs offer greater appeal, as [dividend-oriented active ETFs]’ daily Portfolio Composition File (PCF) disclosure solves the information lag in traditional quarterly fund reporting, providing real-time transparency for effective risk management.
