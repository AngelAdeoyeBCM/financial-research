# European Economics Eurozone: August Inflation Set to Jump, But Core to Soften

## CITI'S TAKE

The national inflation data releases today and on Friday last week do not move the needle for the ECB, in [the author's] view, ahead of the September meeting: headline inflation is being lifted well above target by the energy shock, but signs of spillovers into other prices remain mostly absent.

- Eurozone HICP update – National data so far point to Eurozone headline HICP tomorrow printing at 3.3% YY in August, in line with consensus (Citi 3.4%), driven higher by energy prices, while core is likely weaker than expected at 2.4% (consensus and Citi 2.5%).
- Relevant details – German HICP today came in 20bp lower than expected at 2.9% YY (vs 2.8% in July), with core CPI inflation stable at 2.4% but core HICP probably easing by 10bp to 2.4%. Softening core inflation was also recorded in the French and Spanish data on Friday. Food inflation softened again in Germany but rose in France and Spain, possibly affected by the summer drought. Services inflation softened both in Germany and France. Overall, [the author finds] very limited evidence of second-round effects of energy into other parts of the basket.
- Implications for the ECB – Overall, the August preliminary inflation data out tomorrow do not seem able to move the needle for the ECB ahead of the September meeting. If indeed confirmed at 3.3%, headline inflation would be 10–20bp lower in August than implied in the ECB June Staff projections and core roughly in line. However, higher gas prices compared to June will likely imply an upward revision to the ECB's 2027 inflation forecast.
