Riksbank Will Stay on Hold in September At [the Riksbank's] last meeting in August, the Riksbank’s Executive Board left the policy rate unchanged at 1.75%, in line with [the author's] and consensus expectations. The communication appeared balanced, which, in the context of recent data surprises, sounded somewhat dovish. The Monetary Policy Update (MPU) stated that while “both growth and inflation have been higher than was forecast in June, and there is still a risk of underlying inflation becoming too high,... companies’ pricing plans have been subdued, disruptions in global supply chains have declined and the labour market has been somewhat weaker than expected”. Riksbank’s August meeting minutes, however, sounded more hawkish than what the [Monetary Policy Update] suggested. The focus of the Board remained on the upside risk for inflation, which had broadened from external supply risks to stronger domestic demand and firmer underlying inflation. Still, no one called for any immediate action, which suggests a move as early as September (or even November) remains unlikely. Since [the Riksbank's August meeting], Swedish core inflation (CPIF ex. energy) surprised somewhat to the downside, printing at 0.5%yoy in August, closing the gap to Riksbank’s latest forecast. At the same time, most of the surprise relative to [the author's] forecast was driven by a weaker rebound in goods, while services remained elevated at 2.85%yoy, with package holidays climbing to 12.9%yoy. While headline inflation (CPIF) printed at 0.7%yoy in August, September will likely see a notable rebound, driven by energy-related pressures, as petrol and diesel pump prices climbed more than 10%mom in the first two weeks of September. Besides, European TTF gas prices have surged again in recent weeks, almost doubling since the June lows. Given further delays to the normalisation of Persian Gulf LNG exports, [the author's] commodities team raised [the author's commodities team's] Q4 TTF gas price forecast to 70 EUR [or] MWh (from 53 EUR [or] MWh before). At the same time, [the author's commodities team] only raised [the author's commodities team's] oil forecast modestly— with [the author's commodities team's] Brent price projection up $5 to $85 in Q4—which matters more for Sweden, which remains relatively insulated from the gas price swings. Norges Bank Will Likely Hike, Driven by a Hawkish Reaction Function At the August meeting, Norges Bank’s Monetary Policy Committee decided to keep the policy rate on hold at 4.25%, in line with [the author's] and consensus expectations. The Committee did not reiterate the June policy rate path, which indicated a 100% chance of another hike this year. Instead, Governor Wolden Bache guided that “inflation has been lower than projected, but it is too early to conclude that the inflation outlook has changed materially since June... [so] it may still become necessary to raise the policy rate”. Since the August meeting, inflation data printed close to consensus expectations, with core inflation (CPI-ATE) coming in at 3.0%yoy, and headline CPI inflation at 3.3%yoy. The gap with Norges Bank’s forecast, that opened up in June, remains but has closed to 30bp (from 60bp before) on core inflation as [Norges Bank] expected a 3.3%yoy print in August. Services inflation went up to 3.9%yoy in August, as consumer goods CPI-ATE recovered to 1.9%yoy, still notably below the recent run rate, most likely driven by stronger Krone pushing imported goods inflation lower.
