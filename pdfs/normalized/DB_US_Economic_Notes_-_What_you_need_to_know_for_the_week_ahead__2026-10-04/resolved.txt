# US Economic Notes - What you need to know for the week ahead

Economics US Economic Notes Date 4 October 2026

Commentary for Monday: This week’s economic calendar slows a bit following a busy week capped off by the September employment report. Recapping last Friday’s labor market data (see “US Economic Chartbook: September employment: Not one to remember (good or bad), the September employment report exhibited slightly more payback on headline (+29k vs. +133k) and private (+46k vs. +127k) payrolls than we had anticipated, along with 60k of downward revisions to headline payrolls over the prior two months. In addition, av erage hourly earnings growth (+0.1% vs. +0.3%) disappointed, though this was likely due to a still elevated work week (34.4hrs). However, despite the aforementioned misses, the implied annual growth rate of labor income from our payroll proxy slipped only a tenth to 4.2% - well within its recent range and supportive of consumer spending. Regarding the household survey, though the unemployment rate (4.175% vs. 4.141%) edged up slightly, the U -6 rate fell by a tenth to 7.6%. The 78k rise in household unemployment alongside a 406k increase in employment had the effect of lifting the labor force participation rate (LFPR) a couple of tenths to 61.8%, its highest level since last May. Importantly, the employment [or] population ratio (80.7 vs. 80.4) and [labor force participation rate] (83.7 vs. 83.4) for prime age workers (25 -54 years) continued to recover from unusually large declines witnessed in June. In short, though the September employment report slightly disappointed, the picture of a stable labor market remains very much intact, particularly when coupled with recent readings from other key indicators such as ADP and jobless claims. Indeed, there ar e even some tentative signs of tightening in certain sectors. In turn, Fed officials are likely to remain squarely focused on inflation. We continue to expect two more 25bp rate hikes over the next couple of quarters. Wednesday’s release of the minutes to the September FOMC meeting may provide more color on the near-term rate outlook. Fed communications following the September meeting largely reinforced the quarterly pace of rate hikes, as signaled by the September SEP and consistent with our baseline expectation for the Fed (see US Economic Perspectives: Core PCE revisions reduce the urgency, but not the need, for hikes and “Who’s who in the September 2026 dot plot”). Most notably, both Vice Chair Jefferson and NY Fed President Williams expressed a clear preference to take some time and gather more data before determining next policy move, which likely reflected the consensus view of the center of the Committee. That said, many officials have argued for additional tightening and Wednesday’s minutes could offer useful insights on the framing of the current tightening cycle within the broad Committee, as well as the assessment of the neutral rate whose distribution of es timates has drifted higher in the September SEP. Both should have strong implications on how far the Fed has to go to bring rates to the appropriate restrictive level (see “How high might they go?” and “US Matthew Luzzetti, Ph.D. Economic Perspectives: Two charts that will determine how hawkish the Fed will be in 2027 “).
