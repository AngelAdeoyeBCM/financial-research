# EM rates: Closing SORA 5s10s flattener

Chandresh Jain, EM Asia FX and Rates Strategist | MAR | Joey Li, FX and Rates Strategist, APAC | BNP Paribas Singapore Branch. Sources: Bloomberg, Macrobond, BNP Paribas Fig. 1: Change in 2y swaps vs fixing across EM Asia We close our m25, 5s10s flattener position in SORA rates for a 7.7bp (USD115.5k) loss. The position didn't move much against [the author], but the negative carry and roll-down costs of holding [the m25, 5s10s flattener position in SORA rates] resulted in a loss. [The author] initiated [the m25, 5s10s flattener position in SORA rates] on 23 April 2025, following a steepening of the SORA curve in tandem with US rates after a series of US Treasury sell-offs triggered by tariff announcements and concerns about the Fed's independence (see EM rates: Enter 5s10s SORA flattener). At the time, [the author] viewed [the steepening of the SORA curve] in SORA rates as overdone, given Singapore's AAA sovereign credit rating. Since then, back-end SORA rates have continued to track the move in US rates, as shown by how the box has largely traded in a range-bound fashion (see Figure 1). Looking ahead, [the author now expects] the US Treasury curve to steepen due to fiscal concerns resurging (see US rates: The fiscal gulf asymmetry, dated 1 April). Similarly, [the author expects] Asian rates to steepen, as rate-hike pricing in the front-end may be removed in a de-escalatory scenario, while subsidies across EM Asian economies will continue to weigh on the fiscal picture over time, driven by elevated energy prices. Hence, [the author considers] it prudent to close [the author's] SORA flattener. [The author] close [the m25, 5s10s flattener position in SORA rates] at 39.6bp for a loss of 7.7bp (USD115.5k), including carry and roll. [The author is] closing [the author's] m25, 5s10s flattener position in SORA rates for a loss of 8bp (USD120k). [The author] initiated [the m25, 5s10s flattener position in SORA rates] in April 2025, following a steepening of the SORA curve in tandem with US rates, after a series of US Treasury selloffs triggered by tariff announcements and concerns about the Fed's independence (see EM rates: Enter 5s10s SORA flattener, dated 23 April 2025). At the time, [the author] believed [the steepening of the SORA curve] in SORA rates was overdone, given Singapore's AAA rating. Since then, the back-end SORA rates have continued to track the move in US rates, as seen by how the box has traded in a range-bound fashion (Figure 1). However, [the author's] outlook has changed, and [the author now expects] the US Treasury curve to steepen due to resurgent fiscal concerns (see US rates: The fiscal gulf asymmetry, dated 1 April 2026). Similarly, [the author anticipates] Asian rates to steepen, as the pricing of rate hikes in the front-end may be removed in a de-escalatory scenario, while subsidies across EM Asian economies will continue to deteriorate the fiscal picture over time, driven by elevated energy prices. Given [that the author's outlook has changed], [the author believes] it is prudent to close [the author's] m25, 5s10s SORA flattener – [the author] close [the m25, 5s10s flattener position in SORA rates] at 39.5bp for a loss of 8bp (USD120k).
