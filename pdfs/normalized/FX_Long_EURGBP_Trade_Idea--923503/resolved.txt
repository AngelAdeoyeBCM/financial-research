[The long EURGBP ahead of the 7 May local elections] is in addition to [the author's] existing EURGBP higher/ SX5E higher dual digital trade idea (see Hybrid insights: SX5E and EURGBP higher, dated 29 January). After a period of resilience amid the energy shock, [the author thinks] valuations do not reflect ongoing political uncertainty (Figure 1) and the FX market is positioned flat EURGBP according to the BNP Paribas FX Positioning Tracker (Figure 2). Given FX valuations and positioning, [the author therefore thinks] GBP is vulnerable to an election outcome seen as negative for GBP. A change of leadership presents the tail risk of a shift to a less fiscally prudent government (see UK politics: Succession scenarios, dated 13 February). Past episodes of political risk premium being embedded in the GBP have been sharp. Recent examples include fiscal and political concerns in the lead-up to November’s budget and February’s renewed pressure on the prime minister over Peter Mandelson’s appointment as US ambassador. In [the fiscal and political concerns in the lead-up to November’s budget and February’s renewed pressure on the prime minister over Peter Mandelson’s appointment as US ambassador], EURGBP rallied more than 2% and went above fair value (Figure 1). [The author thinks] rate differentials have been traded by the market as a source of support for the GBP during the energy shock (see FX and energy Q&A: Strait answers, dated 12 March). That said [the author does] not foresee rate differentials as a durable source of support for the GBP, as [rate differentials] is also consistent with weaker UK growth amid the energy shock.
