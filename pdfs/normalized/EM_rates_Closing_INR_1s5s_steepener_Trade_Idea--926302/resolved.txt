# EM rates: Closing INR 1s5s steepener

Chandresh Jain, EM Asia FX and Rates Strategist | MAR | Joey Li, FX and Rates Strategist, APAC | BNP Paribas Singapore Branch. [The author] close [the author's] M26 1s5s INR NDOIS steepener trade idea, as [the author thinks] recent announcements from the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has changed the near-term outlook for INR rates. [The author] initiated [the M26 1s5s INR NDOIS steepener] in March, based on the expectation that rising oil prices would put upward pressure on rates, while fiscal subsidies and a mismatch in bond demand and supply would steepen the curve (see EM rates: Take profit on INR 5y payer, enter 1s5s steepener, dated 11 March). [The author's] view was also supported by the view that front-end pricing of rate hikes seemed extreme relative to core inflation, which remained under control in India. Although there was some chatter that [the Reserve Bank of India (RBI)] may hike rates to curb FX depreciation, the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) voted unanimously to keep policy rates unchanged in June. Instead, [the Reserve Bank of India (RBI)] introduced several FX measures—including 100% hedging-cost support for foreign-currency non-resident (FCNR) deposits and subsidized hedging for external commercial borrowings (ECB) and overseas foreign-currency borrowings (OFCB), with tenors ranging from 3-5 years. [The author estimates] [the FX measures] could attract USD 70-80 bn of inflows (see FX: [Reserve Bank of India], BI try different paths to fight depreciation, dated 12 June 2026), which will add INR liquidity in the banking system once banks convert USD to INR with the help from [Reserve Bank of India]. [The FX measures] has triggered a pronounced rally in INR rates, especially in the belly of the curve. Looking ahead, [the author thinks] fiscal concerns may take a back seat for now and expect the belly (3-5y) to outperform further on the back of [USD 70-80 bn of inflows], which can continue over the next few months and flatten the curve. [The author] close [the M26 1s5s INR NDOIS steepener] at 37.5bp, for a 12.5bp (USD250k) loss. [The author] close [the author's] M26 1s5s INR NDOIS steepener for a loss of xxbp, as [the M26 1s5s INR NDOIS steepener] has traded through [the author's] stop loss level. Since Q1, [the author has] held a paid bias on INR rates, expecting India's economic growth to pick up over the medium term, driven by recent trade deals and a large Union Budget for FY2026/27 (see EM rates: Pay 5y INR as growth picks up, dated 5 February). [The author] also anticipated the mismatch in bond demand and supply to put upward pressure on rates. Following the surge in oil prices due to the Middle East conflict, [the author] took profit on [the author's] outright long position in 5y NDOIS and converted [the outright long position in 5y NDOIS] to a 1s5s steepener (see EM rates: Take profit on INR 5y payer, enter 1s5s steepener, dated 11 March), as the front-end was already pricing in two rate hikes from the Reserve Bank of India (RBI). Since then, the Middle East conflict has continued, while oil prices have remained elevated, as it remains unclear when the Strait of Hormuz could be reopened. As such, INR rates have continued to sell off (Figure 1), with the 2y underperforming as markets have begun to price in a deeper hiking cycle (Figure 2). Meanwhile, the rest of the curve (e.g., 2s5s) has flattened, as the risk of a negative growth shock and the potential need for future rate cuts to support growth have increased. [That the rest of the curve (e.g., 2s5s) has flattened] is a risk [the author] flagged, where rising growth concerns due to prolonged elevation in oil prices would weigh on the 5y point of the curve (see EM rates: Trading EM rates in an oil shock – and after, page 3-4, dated 26 March). Although [the author maintains] [the author's] stance that the market's pricing in the very front-end (4-5 rate hikes over the next 2 years) appears excessive, and that the bond demand-supply mismatch will ultimately steepen the curve over the medium term, [the author] recognize that near-term price action will be driven by developments in the Middle East. Specifically, if markets' expectations for a de-escalation and reopening of the Strait of Hormuz continue to be pushed back, the very front-end (i.e., up to 2 years) can continue to underperform, while the rest of the curve flattens. As such, [the author] close [the author's] M26 1s5s steepener at xxbp for a loss of xxbp (USDxxxk).
