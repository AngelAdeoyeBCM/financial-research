## KEY MESSAGES

We expect net supply net of QT of European government bonds to reach a record high of EUR712bn in 2025, up from EUR625bn in 2024.

The increase is largely driven by QT which we expect to accelerate to EUR261bn in 2025 from EUR186bn in 2024.

We forecast gross supply to stay flat at EUR1.27trn in 2025 versus EUR1.28trn in 2024.

Net issuance would be slightly higher at EUR451bn versus EUR439bn.

Heavy issuance in 2025 is consistent with our expectation that term premia will remain high in 2025, thus allowing curves to steepen.

A year of record net supply, net of QT: We forecast that EUR712bn of EGB net supply, net of QT, will hit the market in 2025, up from EUR625bn in 2024.

We estimate QT will accelerate to EUR261bn in 2025 from EUR186bn in 2024, given that PEPP reinvestments will fully cease this year.

Gross supply should reach EUR1.27trn this year, we think, marginally lower than 2024's EUR1.28trn. We estimate net supply will rise slightly to EUR451bn in 2025 from EUR439bn in 2024.

Heavy net supply, net of QT, this year is consistent with our expectation that term premia will remain high in 2025 (see Global Outlook 2025: Navigating unpredictability), limiting the propensity for 10y rates to rally, thus allowing curves to steepen. Issuance patterns will likely remain similar to 2024, with the weighted average maturity (WAM) of issuance likely to remain stable, if not a little above 2024 levels.

Net supply y/y is still higher in 2025 vs 2024, even though governments are broadly targeting a small degree of fiscal consolidation (when looking at country budget deficits). Whilst most countries below have lower net funding needs y/y, country specific differences, especially in Italy and the Netherlands, push net supply higher in 2025 vs 2024.

## Overview of 2025 supply versus previous years

We expect net supply, net of QT, to reach a record high of EUR712bn in 2025 versus EUR625bn in 2024. We forecast gross and net supply to fall slightly y/y (Figures 5 and 6), but this would be more than offset by the increase in QT next year as PEPP reinvestments will cease completely (Figure 4).

In terms of the distribution of supply, we expect January and September to be peaks in net supply, net of QT (Figure 8), compared to 2024 where January and June were the peaks (although this distribution could change if syndications are delayed).

## Weighted average maturity of issuance in the eurozone

The WAM of eurozone issuance increased slightly in 2024 to 10.4 years from 10.2 years in 2023 (see Figure 9). The increase in WAM was broad based, rising in Germany, France, Italy and Spain (alongside several other countries). Notably the WAM of issuance reached a new high for Germany at 9.63 years, helped by heavy issuance in the 30y sector with three 30y syndications. Looking at sector-level issuance in the eurozone overall, the weight of supply fell in the 2/3y, 10y, and to a small extent in the 30y+ sector. This was offset by increases in the 5y, 7y, 15y, and (marginally) in the 20y30y segment.

Guidance remains limited from national Treasuries on this matter, however, we think the WAM of issuance in the eurozone could extend slightly from current levels.

In Germany, we think the WAM of issuance will probably rise further to around 9.9y (from 9.6y), under the assumption that Germany syndicates a new 30y and a new 10y green, while green auctions patterns remain close those seen in 2024.

The WAM of overall issuance could also extend in Italy. The Italian Treasury aims to lower the weight of front-end issuance (3y-7y), maintain a similar weight on the 10y and increase issuance on the BTPei curve further out (15y, 30y segments). Meanwhile, supply on the 20y and 50y segment could rise y/y as stated by the treasury, though issuance on the 15y and 30y segments may fall y/y.

In France, we expect four long-end syndications this year compared to three in 2024, which opens up the possibility for an extension of the WAM of debt, although details beyond this are limited.

In Spain, the WAM of issuance may stabilise, or reduce at the margin, based on our syndication assumptions (we indicatively assume 10y-20y syndications may occur in 2025, instead of a 30y).

## SSA supply - EU leading the way

In the SSA space, the EU is already leading the way in terms of the increase in supply for 2025, similar to what was seen in 2024. Based on announcements made so far, y/y gross supply is set to be around EUR14bn higher across SSA issuers, with most of the supply increase coming from the EU itself given that KfW and CADES expect falls in supply of EUR10.6bn and EUR8.1bn respectively compared to the previous year.

The net supply picture shows the opposite, where we expect EUR8.1bn less net supply compared to 2024, given more redemptions relative to gross supply in 2025.

Overall, 2025 will continue to mark a heavy year of SSA issuance, similar to EGBs.
