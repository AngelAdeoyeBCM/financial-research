## 2025 copper outlook: Bigger surpluses, bigger downside price risks

Second part of report title KEY MESSAGES

2025 downgrade on slower expected demand growth: We leave our current Q4 2024 copper price forecasts unchanged, but see a likelihood of slower 2025 global copper demand and dampened prices as a result of growing trade restrictions. As a result, we have cut our end-2025 price by about 9%.

Less trade signals slower demand growth: We have moderately cut our 2025 demand growth expectations from 3.3% to 2.9%. The principal drivers of this revision are our expectations of further contraction in European construction, and in European manufacturing, due to export headwinds. We also expect slower demand growth in China on trade constraints and continued sluggish domestic demand. We remain sceptical that China stimulus measures will offer any meaningful support to copper demand.

Copper supply side - robust in 2025: We think strong mine supply growth in 2025, and the commissioning of more smelter capacity, will drive up refined copper supply growth by 3.7% next year, running well ahead of our new demand growth projections. Indeed, we now see an around-500-kt surplus for the copper market next year, roughly a 320 kt increase over our previous 2025 surplus expectations.

## Copper market showing no signs of short-term tightening

Second part of report title Chinese refined copper production continues to accelerate: Having stuttered in terms of growth through the middle of 2024 on concentrate availability concerns, China's refined copper has started to pick up strongly on a month-on-month basis through Q3. We expect this trend to continue through Q4 2024 and 2025 backed by smelter capacity growth and rising copper concentrate supply.

China prices trading at sustained deep discounts to LME: The extent of growth in China's domestic refined copper production has sustained the trend of Chinese physical copper trading at consistently deep discounts to ex-China physical pricing. We see this as a key reason why China's YTD net copper imports have been flat on 2023 volumes, a trend that has effectively pushed the ex-China copper market into oversupply.

LME contango spreads deepest on record: The clearest indication of copper's oversupply has been the trends in the LME forward curve structure, which through October was seeing record contangos, whether looking at cash-3m or longer-dated spreads. Given our expectations of rising surpluses in the global copper market in 2025, we expect deep contango pricing structures to remain a feature of copper over the next year.

## Demand signals a cause for concern, most notably in China

Second part of report title China's traditional metals demand drivers increasingly sluggish: Despite numerous policy attempts to stimulate the construction sector, China's property sales, plus commercial and residential property starts and completions, remain very weak, with activity in its third year of contraction. We think there are structural issues at play, namely an aging and shrinking population, the end of rural-urban migration, and the effect of property ceasing to act as a savings or investment vehicle.

Auto production for China's domestic market has continued to slow through 2024. In the year to September, with domestic demand effectively contracting by 3.5%, it is only Chinese vehicle exports, up 28% y/y, that are supporting China's auto sector output growth (+1.9% YTD) and thus auto sector copper demand. This reliance on exports is seen across most Chinese manufacturing sectors.

Renewables/new energy the only bright spot: The one bright spot in Chinese demand remains the renewable energy sector, with solar capacity production up 24% YTD. We see similar strong growth in other new energy sectors, ranging from wind turbines to electric vehicles (EVs). However, China is currently in transition, with new energy sectors only partially compensating for slowing demand from traditional construction/infrastructure sectors.

## Manufacturing remains sluggish, growing trade barriers a headwind

Second part of report title Trump victory a potential trade, global growth headwind. Global manufacturing has struggled for positive momentum in 2024. The potential implementation of tariffs between 60-100% on US imports from China and Mexico, and 10-20% duties on imports from all other regions, in our view, would act as a headwind to global growth, particularly if other regions impose reciprocal tariffs on US exports. In addition, implementation of tariffs by the US is, in our view, likely to lead to renewed USD strength, presenting a further headwind to USD-denominated metals. We also see a Trump driven partial defunding of the IRA program as a further copper headwind.

China increasingly reliant on exports for growth: Sluggish domestic demand growth for manufactured products in China has made many sectors more reliant on exports to drive production growth in 2024. Auto manufacturing for China's domestic market has slowed 3.5% YTD, while exports have jumped 28% (Figure 12 above). The same pattern is seen in China's aircon sector (Figure 15) and is a common trend across most manufacturing sectors, heightening tariff risks to China growth.

European manufacturing and construction in structural decline: European manufacturing activity has been in decline since July 2022, while construction activity has been shrinking since May 2022. The prospect of a US-instigated trade war in 2025 will, in our view, deepen this downtrend next year.

## Mine supply: Improving in 2024, accelerating in 2025

Second part of report title Accelerating global mine supply: After growth of about 2.0% y/y in 2023, mine supply continued to grow at around 2.4% over the first eight months of 2024. However, we expect copper mine supply to grow by 3.1% this year and about 5% in 2025.

Our forecast incorporates the closure of Cobre Panama and allows for Anglo American's copper output guidance cuts for 2024. For Chilean output, we see significant upside from the ramp-up of Teck's Quebrada Blanca II and modest upside from improvements at its Ramiro Tomic and Chuqui operations. We also expect continued mine production growth in the DRC and Peru from ongoing project ramp-ups.

Cobre Panama impact limited due to growth elsewhere: We do not expect Cobre Panama to restart for the foreseeable future, but do not see concentrate market deficits or refined-market tightness in 2024/25. This is in part due to significant oversupply in the copper concentrate market for most of 2024 - we estimate well over 300 kt - but also reflects likely mine supply growth in most key copper mining regions and a continuing surge in copper scrap processing in China.
