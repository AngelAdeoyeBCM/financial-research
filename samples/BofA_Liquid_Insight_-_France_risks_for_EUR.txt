In the first step of the process, we construct the components for risk reversals and FX positioning. For risk reversals we use an equally weighted average of the full-sample z-scores applied to EUR-USD and EUR-CHF risk reversals, 3 months and 1 year ahead. For FX positioning we use a simple average of the z-scores for the CFTC data (non-commercials, TFF Asset Managers, and TFF Leveraged Funds) and SDR FX options. In the second step of the process, we generate full-sample z-scores for all four components separately (re-standardising onto a common scale) and then take their simple average. In the third step of the process, we calculate the full-sample z-score of that average, which becomes our proxy for France-related risks. Throughout, the signs of individual components have been adjusted so that higher z-scores suggest higher France- related concerns. Our approach is inspired by Lina Fransson and Oskar Tysklind (2017), An index for financial conditions in Sweden, Sveriges Riksbank Economic Review 2017:1. 2 Liquid Insight | 08 September 2026 What to watch: EU fragmentation risks further out, budget approval near term While our economists are not particularly constructive on the macro side, and our rates strategists remain concerned about fiscal slippage and the Moody’s Oct 23rd rating decision, our main medium-term concerns for the EUR reflect: (1) the potential implications of the French Presidential Elections for European integration, and (2) parliamentary fragmentation risks after a potential 2027 snap parliamentary election following the presidential one (see also France cross-asset report 30 Jul '26). In the near term, political stability concerns around the 2027 budget approval process also pose some downside risks to the EUR (Exhibit 1). However, we work under the assumption that the government manages to present and pass a budget plan with sufficient corrective action to keep the planned deficit unchanged vs 2026, which would likely require c 60bp of measures (see also Euro Area Back to school 1 Sep '26 ). Liquid Insight | 08 September 2026 3 To some extent, we think the FX market has already been pricing in France-related risks. For instance, risks around the French presidential election seem already reflected in FX options pricing: forward volatility premia peak around the 6m3m tenor, suggesting investors expect a meaningful pickup in volatility during the April-June 2027 election window. This pattern is particularly evident in the EUR crosses most sensitive to European political risk, including EUR-GBP and EUR-CHF (Exhibit 4, Exhibit 5). In fact, z-scores of the forward volatility premium since 2015 suggest that the steepness of the curve in 6m3m space is 2 standard deviations above average for many EUR crosses. To us, this suggests that investors are already paying a historically large premium for volatility around the French election. In rates, 10y OAT-Bund spreads are near multi-year highs (Exhibit 8). As a rule of thumb, a 10bp widening in the OAT-Bund spread has historically been associated with a roughly 0.4% weaker EUR-USD for sufficiently large shocks (Exhibit 9).

This is also consistent with relative France vs. Euro area equity inflows (Exhibit 10, Exhibit 11 and European Equity Strategy 4 Sep '26 ). A simple proxy for France risks is indeed at its highest level since 2017 A simple proxy for France-related risks indeed suggests that they are at least partly priced in: our proxy stands one standard deviation above its post-2015 average, the highest level since the 2017 presidential election (Exhibit 12, Exhibit 13). We assume downside risks to the EUR continue to dominate over the medium term but expect them to become more relevant for markets once there is greater clarity on the presidential field. As such, France may prove more of a 2027 story than a late-2026 story for the EUR. We consider CHF or GBP to be “cleaner” expressions to hedge France- related risks than EUR-USD, which depends more on US policies and data.
