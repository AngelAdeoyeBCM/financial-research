# Research Text

Liquid Insight

Sept FOMC: hike or bond spike

## Key takeaways

- Fed likely to hike 25bp. SEP = 50bps total hikes in '26. Fed choice is simple: hike or bond spike. Bond likes orthodoxy.
- Warsh likely to deliver hawkish hike, which should support higher front end rates & twist flattening of UST curve.
- USD outcome will be determined by Fed ability to "out-hawk" mkt expectations & other G10 central banks.

## Sept FOMC choice: hike or risk large bond spike

The Fed is now widely expected to hike Wednesday by 25bps. Rates price this with ~85% likelihood, up from just 35% likelihood just 2 weeks ago. The sharp shift in Fed pricing was catalyzed by Warsh Jackson Hole (JH) speech + stronger labor & inflation data. It is the first part of a previously very out-of-consensus 75bps hike in ’26 call from BofA US Economics.

Fed faces simple choice at Sept FOMC: hike or risk large bond spike. If the Fed does not hike with current pricing, it risks a sharp and disorderly UST long end move. US 30Y could rapidly test 5.75%; UST buybacks would be futile to stop it. Bond price action, including post Friday’s CPI, shows the bond likes orthodoxy (Exhibit 2). Orthodoxy = use rate hikes to slow inflation & quell long end rate spike (not buybacks).

Sept FOMC communications likely lean hawkish. SEP will show 50bps of total hikes in ’26, Waller may dovish dissent. Big Q: which Warsh in presser? July FOMC or JH Warsh? We think JH, largely to support bond. If right, UST curve likely to twist flatten, USD to be supported, & risk challenged. If wrong, bigger Bessent “house” needed for bond. History note: Fed never held with market pricing such high hike likelihood, using FOMC OIS.

## US economics: Sept hike is a go

Friday’s CPI data all but lock in a rate hike this week. Chairman Warsh's Jackson Hole remarks, together with current market pricing, have raised the cost of disappointing expectations significantly. Warsh can also point to Friday’s twist flattening of the Treasury curve as evidence that tighter policy is reinforcing inflation-fighting credibility, providing further justification for a hike.

Beyond next week's meeting, uncertainty around the policy path remains. However, there will be only one additional employment report and one CPI release before the October meeting. Unless incoming data soften materially or Fed communication turns unexpectedly dovish, we expect additional rate hikes in both October and December.

## Statement: tweaks to justify the expected hike

We see a few potential tweaks to the FOMC statement. If the Fed hikes, we think the language around inflation will be modified to rationalize the move. For example, the statement might note that progress on inflation has slowed or that the rate increase was aimed at delivering price stability. The comment that productivity growth is strong might also be altered due to soft productivity readings over the last three quarters. If the Fed raises rates, Gov. Waller might dissent, depending on his estimate of Aug core PCE.

## SEP: higher rates, lower inflation

While the SEP's days may be numbered, this meeting will still deliver an updated set of Fed projections. The forecasts will also include policymakers' first estimates for 2029, which are likely to be very similar to the longer-run projections. Assuming a hike, we expect the 2026 median dot to show 50bp of hikes this year, implying another rate increase in Oct/Dec. We think the 2027 median will likely show one cut (3.875%). Recall that the dots reflect the expected policy rate at year-end. It would be reasonable for the Fed to pencil in a cut at some stage next year, since many policymakers will likely assume that higher rates will help quell inflation.

One outstanding question is whether the Fed will incorporate the PCE methodology changes scheduled for September 30 into its projections. We think it will, although our conviction is low as it may be easier to reflect those changes ex-post. If the revisions are incorporated, we would expect both the headline and core inflation projections for 2026 to be marked down by roughly 30bp.

Beyond this year, we expect the Fed to lower its growth forecasts by 0.1pp, to 2.2% in 2027 and 2.1% in 2028, reflecting the somewhat higher expected rate path over this year and next. We also expect the headline and core inflation projections for 2027 to be revised down by 0.1pp. The u-rate, however, is already two-tenths below the median projections for the end of this year and next. So we expect those medians to move down as a mark-to-market, despite the higher policy trajectory. In sum, aside from changes to the policy path and the potential inflation revisions stemming from the PCE methodology update, SEP changes should be relatively modest.

## Press conference: what changed?

Assuming the Fed hikes, Chair Warsh will almost certainly field questions about the policy path going forward. In responding to these questions, he will face a balancing act. If he asserts that the Sep hike (if it happens) was the first in a sequence of rate increases, markets might quickly price in over 100bp of hikes, as they did with the ECB. If he attempts a "dovish hike" by reassuring markets that the Fed will proceed cautiously, markets might once again question the Fed's commitment to the 2% inflation target.

We also expect Warsh to be asked why the Fed didn't just hike in July. What has changed? We think he will probably say that progress on underlying inflation has been insufficient. He might also point to the increase in oil as upside risk to inflation, though that would contradict his prior stance that oil shocks should be treated as one-offs.

We will also be looking for additional color on Warsh's reaction function. At Jackson Hole he finally cited a data point that he uses to gauge underlying inflation - the share of PCE components that are running above 3% over the last 12 months. Will he stick with this metric? Will he elaborate on his thinking? Will he move the goalposts? For example, it would be dovish if Warsh starts referring to 3m inflation trends, given the residual seasonality in the PCE inflation data. Or if he brings up the Dallas Fed's trimmed-mean PCE metric (running at 2.3% on a 12m basis), after having abandoned it at Jackson Hole.

We're curious whether Warsh will retain the tweaks he made to his communication style at Jackson Hole. We found that speech to be more transparent about his outlook and, as mentioned above, his reaction function. In our view, greater transparency decreases the risk of miscommunication. Even without forward guidance. If Warsh reverts to his prior stance of avoiding substantive discussions about the economic outlook and the stance of policy, he may field far fewer questions at the press conference.

## Rates: hike or bond spike

Rates price Sept FOMC hike with ~85% probability, up sharply in recent weeks (Exhibit 4). Rate pricing is firmly in the zone to support a hike (Exhibit 5). Rate hike + hawkish communication should support modest twist flattening of curve. Larger market move would likely come from dovish hike or Fed hold. We offer thoughts.

Fed hike: rate reaction will depend on mix of Fed communications. We expect Fed to deliver a hawkish hike. Key Q will be Warsh presser: we expect more JH than July FOMC.

Hawkish presser: If Warsh continues to sound inflation impatient & suggest recent data does not imply adequate disinflation trend, market likely to raise hike peak & pull forward timing. We might guess total hikes of 100bps & 60-65bps by end ’26. This would likely see 2Y rates rise 5-10bps & 30Y rates lower by 5-10bps. Bond yields lower due to downside growth & inflation risk + more credible Fed = return to orthodoxy.

Dovish presser: If Warsh implies unpopular hike, few hikes to follow, or improved signs in inflation trend, market is likely to reduce hike peak & push out timing. We might guess total hikes drop to 85 & 40-45bps by end ’26. This would likely see 2Y rates lower by 5bps & 30Y rates higher by 5bps. Bond yields higher due to dovish Fed action & confusion about Warsh policy direction / reaction function.

Fed hold: if Fed surprisingly holds, we expect a material front end rate decline & sharp twist steepening of the curve. Total hikes to decline to 65-75bps with 30-35bps by end ’26. Initially, we might guess 2Y lower by 15-20bps & 30Y bond increase of 10-15bps. Twist steepening would come from a lower near term policy path & perceived very high bar to hike + long end rates that would reflect higher inflation risk premium & Warsh credibility discount. Over time, we might expect the 30Y to increase towards 5.75% with limited investor confidence in the Fed. Treasury buybacks would likely prove futile to stop the move, especially with their stumble out the gate.

We think Warsh, others at Fed, & Trump admin understand bond risk with Sept hold. For Fed, their choice is simple: hike or risk bond spike. For Trump admin, their choice is likely: tolerate modestly higher front end rates or materially higher long end rates. We expect Bessent & Warsh to effectively explain this tradeoff to Trump. Trump won’t like the choice set but should understand risks of bond un-anchoring & market implications.

Our best guess is that Warsh delivers a hawkish hike. If right, this will challenge our prior bias for lower belly rates with higher front end rates & twist flattening on curve. It should support our constructive long end asset swap spread view as lower growth & inflation risk + enhanced Fed credibility should support UST investor confidence.

History note: if market pricing remains stable & the Fed does not hike, it would represent an unprecedented dovish hold (using FOMC OIS data back to ’07). In FOMC OIS data history, the market has never priced such elevated hike likelihood & have Fed hold. The 4 closest historic parallels include: Sept & Oct ’15, ahead of post GFC liftoff (8-9bps priced); June ’23, Fed hold before delivering last post COVID hike (8.5bps priced); July ’26, Warsh held steady despite 40% priced (10bps priced). Today = 22bps priced. Since ’07, there have been 74 meetings where market priced some hike likelihood & Fed held; the vast majority of these imply very low Fed hike likelihood (Exhibit 6).

Fed policy tools to shift parallel +25bps, no change in IORB or ON RRP within range. No balance sheet changes expected at this meeting.

## FX: USD limps into the FOMC

The dollar remains subdued going into the September FOMC, despite implied pricing rising sharply to ~85% probability of a hike, post CPI (Exhibit 7). Dollar sentiment has soured ever since Chair Warsh’s July FOMC press conference raised credibility concerns on lack of an articulated plan to address persistently above-target inflation. Since then, the market has received a mix of signals, including an attempted course correction from Chair Warsh at Jackson Hole (bullish), a strong August labor report (bullish), dovish comments from the Fed’s Williams and Waller (bearish), and the Treasury’s buyback gambit to suppress US yields (bearish).

Beyond US economic and policy specific factors, the dollar has also been unable to rally amid the recent reacceleration in oil, gas, and refined energy products; something typically associated with dollar appreciation. Part of the puzzle lies in the broader re-pricing of central banks and curves globally (Exhibit 8). Now with more than 3 hikes priced in for the Fed, the bar for this committee to “out-hawk” both expectations as well as other G10 central banks appears high. This is partly why despite the prospect of Fed hikes this year, we see the USD trading more-or-less sideways into year-end (ex-JPY) (see report: G10 FX back-to-school: dollar unloaded 08 September 2026).

## Range of outcomes for the USD

With these evolving expectations, there remains considerable two-way risk for the USD at Wednesday's meeting. At the extremes, we see the FOMC-related bull and bear case for the USD as the following:

Bullish USD case: The Fed hikes and some combination of the following are met:

1) The SEP shows broadening support for additional hikes (relative to the June SEP);
2) growth and inflation forecasts are elevated even in the context of further hike guidance;
3) Chair Warsh himself (intentionally or unintentionally) offers some indication that a more prolonged campaign is necessary to return inflation on the path to 2%.

This likely sees increased market-implied likelihood that the Fed hikes at least 100bp and/or brings current expectations forward. Chair Warsh’s aversion to forward guidance would make this challenging.

Bearish USD case: Of course, if the Fed opts to hold in the face of sticky inflation and market pricing, we expect the dollar to depreciate swiftly and significantly. But even in the event of a hike, the USD could soften with dovish or non-committal language from Warsh. Markets may not have the patience for anything that sounds like a “one and done” type event under the veil of “no forward guidance”. Any messaging that muddles the reaction function further (as was the case in July) that results in rising back-end yields would also likely see the USD fall in tandem. A more restrained SEP dot-plot could also weigh on the dollar if it appears the broader committee (outside of the already identified hawkish members) was reluctant to tighten policy in the current environment.

## Bottom line

Fed expected to hike 25bp. SEP to show 50bps of total hikes in ’26. Fed choice is simple: hike or bond spike. Warsh likely to deliver hawkish hike, which should support higher front end rates & twist flattening of UST curve. USD outcome will be determined by Fed ability to “out-hawk” market expectations & other G10 central banks.
