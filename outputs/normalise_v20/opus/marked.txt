## FX: USD limps into the FOMC

The dollar remains subdued going into the September FOMC, despite implied pricing rising sharply to {{~85% => approximately 85%}} probability of a hike, post CPI (Exhibit 7). Dollar sentiment has soured ever since Chair Warsh’s July FOMC press conference raised credibility concerns on lack of an articulated plan to address persistently above-target inflation. Since then, the market has received a mix of signals, including an attempted course correction from Chair Warsh at Jackson Hole (bullish), a strong August labor report (bullish), dovish comments from the Fed’s Williams and Waller (bearish), and the Treasury’s buyback gambit to suppress US yields (bearish).

Beyond US economic and policy specific factors, the dollar has also been unable to rally amid the recent reacceleration in oil, gas, and refined energy products; something typically associated with dollar appreciation. Part of the puzzle lies in the broader re-pricing of central banks and curves globally (Exhibit 8). Now with more than 3 hikes priced in for the Fed, the bar for {{this committee => the Fed}} to “out-hawk” both expectations as well as other G10 central banks appears high. {{This => That the bar for the Fed to “out-hawk” both expectations as well as other G10 central banks appears high}} is partly why despite the prospect of Fed hikes this year, {{we see => the author sees}} the USD trading more-or-less sideways into year-end (ex-JPY) (see report: G10 FX back-to-school: dollar unloaded 08 September 2026).
