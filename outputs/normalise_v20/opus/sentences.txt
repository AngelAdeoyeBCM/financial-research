[E042] SEP: higher rates, lower inflation
[E043] While the SEP's days may be numbered, [the Sept FOMC] will still deliver an updated set of Fed projections.
[E044] [The Fed projections] will also include policymakers' first estimates for 2029, which are likely to be very similar to the longer-run projections.
[E045] Assuming a hike, [the author expects] the 2026 median dot to show 50bp of hikes this year, implying another rate increase in [Oct or Dec].
[E046] [The author thinks] the 2027 median will likely show one cut (3.875%).
[E047] Recall that the dots reflect the expected policy rate at year-end.
[E048] It would be reasonable for the Fed to pencil in a cut at some stage next year, since many policymakers will likely assume that higher rates will help quell inflation.
[E049] One outstanding question is whether the Fed will incorporate the PCE methodology changes scheduled for September 30 into [the Fed's] projections.
[E050] [The author thinks the Fed will incorporate the PCE methodology changes into the Fed's projections], although [the author's] conviction is low as it may be easier to reflect [the PCE methodology changes] ex-post.
[E051] If [the PCE methodology changes] are incorporated, [the author] would expect both the headline and core inflation projections for 2026 to be marked down by roughly 30bp.
[E052] Beyond this year, [the author expects] the Fed to lower [the Fed's] growth forecasts by 0.1pp, to 2.2% in 2027 and 2.1% in 2028, reflecting the somewhat higher expected rate path over this year and next.
[E053] [The author also expects] the headline and core inflation projections for 2027 to be revised down by 0.1pp.
[E054] The u-rate, however, is already two-tenths below the median projections for the end of this year and next.
[E055] So [the author expects] [the median projections] to move down as a mark-to-market, despite the higher policy trajectory.
[E056] In sum, aside from changes to the policy path and the potential inflation revisions stemming from the PCE methodology update, SEP changes should be relatively modest.
[E057] Press conference: what changed?
[E058] Assuming the Fed hikes, Chair Warsh will almost certainly field questions about the policy path going forward.
[E059] In responding to [the questions about the policy path going forward], [Warsh] will face a balancing act.
[E060] If [Warsh] asserts that the Sep hike (if [the Sep hike] happens) was the first in a sequence of rate increases, markets might quickly price in over 100bp of hikes, as [markets] did with the ECB.
[E061] If [Warsh] attempts a "dovish hike" by reassuring markets that the Fed will proceed cautiously, markets might once again question the Fed's commitment to the 2% inflation target.
[E062] [The author also expects] Warsh to be asked why the Fed didn't just hike in July.

[E116] FX: USD limps into the FOMC
[E117] The dollar remains subdued going into the September FOMC, despite implied pricing rising sharply to [approximately 85%] probability of a hike, post CPI (Exhibit 7).
[E118] Dollar sentiment has soured ever since Chair Warsh’s July FOMC press conference raised credibility concerns on lack of an articulated plan to address persistently above-target inflation.
[E119] Since then, the market has received a mix of signals, including an attempted course correction from Chair Warsh at Jackson Hole (bullish), a strong August labor report (bullish), dovish comments from the Fed’s Williams and Waller (bearish), and the Treasury’s buyback gambit to suppress US yields (bearish).
[E120] Beyond US economic and policy specific factors, the dollar has also been unable to rally amid the recent reacceleration in oil, gas, and refined energy products; something typically associated with dollar appreciation.
[E121] Part of the puzzle lies in the broader re-pricing of central banks and curves globally (Exhibit 8).
[E122] Now with more than 3 hikes priced in for the Fed, the bar for [the Fed] to “out-hawk” both expectations as well as other G10 central banks appears high.
[E123] [That the bar for the Fed to “out-hawk” both expectations as well as other G10 central banks appears high] is partly why despite the prospect of Fed hikes this year, [the author sees] the USD trading more-or-less sideways into year-end (ex-JPY) (see report: G10 FX back-to-school: dollar unloaded 08 September 2026).
[E124] Range of outcomes for the USD
[E125] With these evolving expectations, there remains considerable two-way risk for the USD at Wednesday's meeting.
[E126] At the extremes, [the author sees] the FOMC-related bull and bear case for the USD as the following:
[E127] Bullish USD case: The Fed hikes and some combination of the following are met:
[E128] The SEP shows broadening support for additional hikes (relative to the June SEP);
[E129] growth and inflation forecasts are elevated even in the context of further hike guidance;
[E130] Chair Warsh himself (intentionally or unintentionally) offers some indication that a more prolonged campaign is necessary to return inflation on the path to 2%.
[E131] [The Bullish USD case] likely sees increased market-implied likelihood that the Fed hikes at least 100bp and/or brings current expectations forward.
[E132] Chair Warsh’s aversion to forward guidance would make [the Bullish USD case] challenging.
[E133] Bearish USD case: Of course, if the Fed opts to hold in the face of sticky inflation and market pricing, [the author expects] the dollar to depreciate swiftly and significantly.
[E134] But even in the event of a hike, the USD could soften with dovish or non-committal language from Warsh.
[E135] Markets may not have the patience for anything that sounds like a “one and done” type event under the veil of “no forward guidance”.
[E136] Any messaging that muddles [Warsh's reaction function] further (as was the case in July) that results in rising back-end yields would also likely see the USD fall in tandem.
[E137] A more restrained SEP dot-plot could also weigh on the dollar if it appears the broader committee (outside of the already identified hawkish members) was reluctant to tighten policy in the current environment.
